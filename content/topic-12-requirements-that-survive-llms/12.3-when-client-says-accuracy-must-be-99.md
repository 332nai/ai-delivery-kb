**Definition**

At some point in nearly every AI requirements conversation, a stakeholder will say "the AI must be at least 99 percent accurate" (or 95, or 99.9, or 100). The number is usually picked without analysis. The conversation that follows is one of the most consequential of the entire project. Handle it badly and the project is set up to fail. Handle it well and you have built credibility that lasts the engagement.

**Why it matters in delivery**

The 99 percent number, taken at face value, often makes the project impossible. The 99 percent number, taken as the start of a conversation, usually leads to a different and more achievable target that the client is happier with. The skill is being able to redirect from the number to the real underlying concern, without making the client feel they have been corrected or talked down to.

This conversation also reveals something deeper: the client has a model of AI as a deterministic system that "just needs to be tuned up to high accuracy". Part of the work in week one and two is helping the client update that mental model. Without an updated mental model, every conversation about quality will be frustrating.

**How it works**

When the 99 percent statement arrives, do not push back on the number directly. Ask three questions in sequence.

*Question 1: "Accurate on what, specifically?"* The 99 percent claim is usually about an undefined evaluation. Force specificity. "Accuracy on what dataset? Against what answers? Measured how?" Often, the client cannot answer this in detail, and the conversation naturally moves toward defining the evaluation, which is the real work.

*Question 2: "What would the impact be if the system is wrong 5 percent of the time, instead of 1 percent?"* This question forces the client to think about consequence, not target. Often, the answer reveals that 5 percent wrong is fine for most outputs, and only catastrophic for a small subset. That subset becomes the high-bar carve-out, while the rest of the system can target a more achievable accuracy.

*Question 3: "What is the current human accuracy on this task?"* If the AI is replacing or augmenting a human process, the human baseline is the most honest comparison. Humans are rarely 99 percent accurate at anything. A customer service agent answering policy questions might be right 85 percent of the time. A medical coder might be 92 percent accurate. Calling out the human number, kindly, repositions the conversation: the AI does not have to be perfect, it has to be better than the alternative.

The combined effect of these three questions is to move the requirements from "99 percent accurate" to something like: "92 percent overall accuracy on the agreed eval set, with a 100 percent requirement on the 20 critical edge cases, and human review for any input the system rates as low confidence". That is a buildable target. The original 99 percent was not.

**Common patterns underneath the 99 percent statement**

The 99 percent statement is rarely about 99 percent specifically. It is usually a proxy for one of these underlying concerns:

| Underlying concern | What it sounds like | How to address |
|---|---|---|
| Fear of public failure | "We cannot afford to look stupid in front of customers" | Reframe around catastrophic failure prevention, not overall accuracy |
| Regulatory exposure | "Our regulator will scrutinise every wrong answer" | Carve out the regulated subset for higher bar, accept lower bar on rest |
| Comparison with internal systems | "Our other systems are 99.9 percent reliable" | Note that those systems are deterministic, AI is statistical, different metric |
| Negotiating starting point | "If I ask for 99, maybe we will land on 95" | Engage with the underlying need, the negotiation collapses on its own |
| Genuine quality requirement | "Lives depend on this being right" | Probably true. Build for it explicitly, with humans in the loop |

Identifying which of these is in play changes the conversation. The same words from a regulator-anxious sponsor mean something different than the same words from a sponsor who is using 99 as an opening position.

**How to communicate the trade off**

A useful artifact is a one slide table showing three columns of target accuracy and what each implies. The 99 percent column is included, alongside more achievable columns, so the client can see the trade off directly.

| Target | What it requires | Cost implication | Time implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 99% on full task | Heavy human-in-loop, narrow scope, expensive model, extensive evals | High | Long |
| 92% overall, 100% on critical subset | Tiered approach, focused human review, achievable evals | Medium | Standard |
| 80% with full disclosure | Lighter touch, transparency to users about limitations | Lower | Faster |

When the client sees the columns side by side, the conversation almost always moves to the middle column. The 99 column was never really about 99, it was about not wanting to talk about trade offs. The middle column lets them have the trade off conversation on safe ground.

**PM decision points**

- When a high accuracy number is named, have I asked the three diagnostic questions, or did I accept the number?
- For the accepted target, is there a tiered structure (critical / important / nice to have) so that not everything is held to the highest bar?
- Have I documented the trade offs explicitly in writing, so they can be referenced later when the conversation reopens?

**Watch outs**

- Do not argue with the number directly. "99 is unrealistic" is technically correct and politically wrong. Use the three questions instead.
- Do not let the conversation be deferred. "We will figure out accuracy later" guarantees a painful conversation later. Have it now, while the project is still cheap.
- Beware sponsors who agree verbally and then write 99 percent in their internal communications. Make the agreed target part of the written record.

**Takeaway: when a client names a high accuracy number, do not push back on the number, ask three diagnostic questions. The conversation will move from a fictional target to a buildable one, and you will have updated the client's mental model in the process.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine kicking off an AI project at Qantas to automate part of the disruption communications process: when a flight is cancelled or significantly delayed, the system drafts the customer notification email for human review. In the first requirements workshop, the operations sponsor states the requirement: "the system must be 99 percent accurate, this is customer-facing communication and we cannot afford errors".

The temptation is to either agree and book a problem for week eight, or to push back and look like a difficult vendor. Neither is the right move. The right move is the three questions.

The first question, "accurate on what specifically", produces a useful pause. The sponsor reflects. After some discussion, the meaning of "accurate" gets unpacked into four sub-criteria: factually correct about the disruption, consistent with the published Qantas communication style, free of any inappropriate content (apologies that imply legal liability, for example), and personalised correctly to the recipient. The 99 percent claim was actually four claims, each of which deserves its own conversation.

The second question, "what is the impact of being wrong 5 percent of the time", produces another useful pause. The sponsor walks through it. On factual correctness, even 1 percent wrong would be unacceptable. On Qantas style, 5 percent stylistically off is fine, the human reviewer can catch it. On inappropriate content, even 0.1 percent is unacceptable, this is a hard rule. On personalisation, 5 percent slightly imperfect is acceptable. The sponsor has now done the trade off work themselves.

The third question, "what is the current human accuracy", is the most reframing. The sponsor honestly answers that today, when human agents draft these notifications under time pressure during a disruption, accuracy is around 90 percent on factual correctness, 70 percent on style consistency, 99 percent on no-inappropriate-content (a stable rule humans follow), and 80 percent on personalisation. The AI does not have to beat 99 percent on everything. It has to beat the human baselines on the dimensions that matter, and not get worse on the ones that already work.

The resulting requirement is not 99 percent. It is four separate targets: 98 percent factual correctness, 90 percent style consistency, 100 percent on the inappropriate-content rule (with hard blocks at the system level, not relying on the model), and 85 percent personalisation. The system is configured with human review on every output, so the final delivered quality to the customer is higher than the AI alone could provide.

This requirement is buildable. The original "99 percent accurate" was not. The conversation took 45 minutes in week two, and the result was a target that the team could engineer to, the sponsor was actually comfortable with, and the legal team could review and approve. Without that 45 minute conversation, the team would have spent eight weeks chasing an undefined number, and the project would have failed not on quality but on misalignment about what quality meant.

The lesson for AI delivery is that round numbers like 99 percent are a sign that the conversation has not yet started, not that the requirement has been set. The work in week one and two is to convert round numbers into specific tiered targets that map to actual consequences. The three questions are the cheapest tool to do this. The skill is asking them with curiosity and patience, not with the body language of someone correcting a misconception.

**定义**

几乎每一场 AI 需求对话里, 都会有某位 stakeholder 在某个时刻说出 "AI 必须至少 99% 准确" (或者 95、99.9、100)。这个数字**通常是没有分析就拍出来的**。接下来的那场对话, 是整个项目最关键的对话之一。处理得不好, 项目就被设置成会失败。处理得好, 你就建立了**贯穿整个合作的可信度**。

**为何在交付中重要**

99% 这个数字**按字面接受, 通常让项目变得不可能**。99% 这个数字**作为对话的起点, 通常会引向一个不同的、更可达成的目标, 而客户对那个目标更满意**。技艺是把对话从那个数字, 重新引向客户真正的底层担忧, 同时**不让客户觉得自己被纠正了或被居高临下**。

这场对话也揭示更深的东西: 客户脑子里的 AI 模型, 是一个 "调一调就能调到高准确率" 的确定性系统。第一周和第二周的一部分工作, 是**帮客户更新这个心智模型**。没有更新过的心智模型, 后续每一场关于质量的对话都会让人受挫。

**运作原理**

99% 那句话出现时, 不要直接 push back 那个数字。依次问三个问题。

*问题一: **"准确, 具体是指什么上的准确?"*** 99% 这种说法通常基于一个**没定义的评估**。**强制具体化**。"在什么数据集上的准确率? 对照什么答案? 怎么衡量?" 大多数时候**客户答不出细节**, **对话自然就转向定义评估**, 而那才是真正的工作。

*问题二: **"如果系统错的不是 1%, 而是 5%, 后果会怎样?"*** 这个问题**强迫客户去想后果, 而不是目标**。**通常**, 答案会揭示 **5% 错误对大多数输出来说是 fine 的, 只对一小部分是灾难性的**。**那一小部分就成为高标杆的 carve-out**, 系统其他部分**可以瞄一个更可达的准确率**。

*问题三: **"今天人做这件事的准确率是多少?"*** 如果 AI 在替代或增强一个人工流程, **人工基线是最诚实的对照**。**人在任何事上很少 99% 准确**。一个客服坐席回答政策问题可能是 85% 对的。一个医疗编码员可能是 92% 准确。**温和地把人的数字摆出来, 重新定位对话**: **AI 不必完美, 它只要好过替代方案就行**。

这三个问题合起来的效果, 把需求从 "99% 准确" 移到: "在商定的 eval set 上整体 92% 准确率, 20 条 critical 边缘情况上 100% 要求, 系统判定为低置信的任何输入走人工 review"。那是可建造的目标。原始的 99% **不是**。

**99% 这句话下面常见的几种 pattern**

99% 那句话**很少是真的关于 99%**。它通常是某种底层担忧的代理:

| 底层担忧 | 听起来像 | 怎么应对 |
|---|---|---|
| 害怕公开失败 | "我们没法在客户面前显得蠢" | 围绕 "防止灾难性失败" 重新框架, 而不是整体准确率 |
| 监管暴露 | "监管机构会审视每一个错误答案" | 把受监管的子集分出来, 用更高标杆; 其余接受较低标杆 |
| 跟内部系统对照 | "我们其他系统是 99.9% 可靠的" | 指出**那些系统是确定性的, AI 是统计性的, 不同 metric** |
| 谈判起手价 | "我要 99, 也许我们能落在 95" | **engage 底层需求, 谈判自己就崩了** |
| 真实的质量要求 | "人命取决于这个对" | **大概率是真的**。**显式地为它建, 带 human-in-the-loop** |

识别现在在场上的是哪一种, 会改变整场对话。同样的话, 来自一个怕监管的 sponsor, 跟来自一个把 99 当作起手价的 sponsor, 意味着完全不同的东西。

**怎么沟通这个 trade off**

一个有用的产物: 一张 slide 上的表, 显示三栏目标准确率, 以及每一栏意味着什么。99% 那一栏被包含进去, 跟更可达的几栏并列, 这样客户**直接看到 trade off**。

| 目标 | 需要什么 | 成本含义 | 时间含义 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 整任务 99% | 重度 human-in-loop、窄范围、贵模型、广泛 evals | 高 | 长 |
| 整体 92%、critical 子集 100% | 分层方式、聚焦人工 review、可达成的 evals | 中 | 标准 |
| 80% 加充分披露 | 较轻的处理、对用户透明说明局限 | 低 | 快 |

客户看到三栏并排时, 对话几乎总是移到中间那一栏。99 那一栏从来不是真的关于 99, 是关于不想谈 trade off。中间那一栏让他们在安全的地面上谈 trade off。

**PM 决策点**

- 高准确率数字被说出来时, 我问了那三个诊断问题, 还是接受了那个数字?
- 对于商定的目标, **有没有分层结构** (critical / important / nice to have), 这样不是所有东西都被同一个最高标杆约束?
- 我有没有**把 trade off 明确写下来**, 这样以后那场对话重启时, 可以引用?

**注意事项**

- **不要直接 argue 那个数字**。"99 不现实" **技术上对、政治上错**。**用那三个问题代替**。
- **不要让对话被推迟**。**"我们以后再搞清楚准确率"** 保证后面会有一场痛苦的对话。**现在谈, 趁项目还便宜**。
- 警惕**口头同意了、然后在自己的内部沟通里写 99%** 的 sponsor。**把商定的目标写进书面记录**。

**要点总结: 客户说出一个高准确率数字时, 不要 push back 那个数字, 问三个诊断问题。对话会从一个虚构目标移到一个可建造目标, 你在过程中还更新了客户的心智模型。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你启动一个 Qantas 的 AI 项目, 自动化中断通讯流程的一部分: 当航班取消或严重延误时, 系统起草客户通知邮件, 由人工 review。第一次需求 workshop 上, 运营 sponsor 提出需求: "系统必须 99% 准确, 这是面向客户的沟通, 我们承担不起错误"。

诱惑是要么同意、给第八周埋个雷, 要么 push back、显得自己是个难搞的 vendor。**哪个都不对**。对的动作是那三个问题。

第一个问题, "准确, 具体是指什么上的准确", 制造一个有用的停顿。Sponsor 反思。讨论一会之后, "准确" 的意思被拆成四个子标准: 关于中断的事实正确、符合已发布的 Qantas 沟通风格、不含任何不合适的内容 (例如**暗示法律责任的道歉**)、正确个性化到收件人。99% 那句话**其实是四句话**, 每一句都值得自己的对话。

第二个问题, "如果错的是 5% 而不是 1%, 后果是什么", 制造另一个有用的停顿。Sponsor 走一遍。事实正确上, 哪怕 1% 错都不可接受。Qantas 风格上, 5% 风格上偏差是 fine 的, 人工 review 能抓。不合适内容上, 哪怕 0.1% 都不可接受, 这是硬规则。个性化上, 5% 略不完美是可接受的。Sponsor 自己已经把 trade off 工作做完了。

第三个问题, "今天人的准确率是多少", **最重塑认知**。Sponsor 诚实回答: 今天, 当人工坐席在中断期间的时间压力下起草这些通知, 事实正确大约 90%, 风格一致性 70%, 不含不合适内容 99% (这是人能稳定遵守的硬规则), 个性化 80%。AI 不必在所有维度上都打败 99%。它要在重要维度上打败人的基线, 在已经能跑通的维度上不变差。

结果出来的需求不是 99%。是四个独立目标: 事实正确 98%、风格一致 90%、不含不合适内容 100% (在**系统层硬阻断**, **不依赖模型**)、个性化 85%。系统配置成**每一个输出都有人工 review**, 这样**最终交付给客户的质量, 比 AI 单独能提供的更高**。

这个需求是可建造的。原始的 "99% 准确" 不是。对话花了 45 分钟, 在第二周, 结果是一个团队能工程化、sponsor 真的舒服、法务能审阅和批准的目标。没有那 45 分钟的对话, 团队会花 8 周追一个没定义的数, 项目失败的原因不是质量, 而是 "质量是什么" 没对齐。

AI 交付的启示: 像 99% 这种圆整数字, 是 "对话还没开始" 的信号, 不是 "需求已经定了" 的信号。第一周和第二周的工作, 是把圆整数字转换成具体的分层目标, 映射到实际后果。那三个问题是做这件事最便宜的工具。技艺是带着好奇和耐心问出来, 不是带着 "我在纠正一个误解" 的肢体语言。
