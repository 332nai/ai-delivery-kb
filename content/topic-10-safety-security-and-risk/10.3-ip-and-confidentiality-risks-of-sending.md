**Definition**

IP and confidentiality risks are the legal and commercial exposures from sending proprietary, confidential, or third party owned content through an AI API. These include risk of training on your data, risk of leakage to other users, risk of breach of client confidentiality obligations, and risk of inadvertently using copyrighted or licensed material.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Many enterprises have NDAs, client contracts, or regulatory obligations that restrict where confidential information can be processed. Sending client data to a public AI API can breach those obligations even if the data never reaches another user. Procurement and legal will block any project that has not addressed this. PMs who frame the issue early get the right approvals. PMs who do not face surprise vetoes from general counsel.

**How it works**

| Risk | Description | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Training on your data | Provider uses your inputs to train future models | Zero retention tier, no training contractual clause |
| Leakage across users | Your data appears in another user's output | Vanishingly rare with modern providers but legally untested |
| Breach of confidentiality obligation | Sending client data violates NDA or master agreement | Get explicit consent, or use approved sub processor list |
| Third party IP in inputs | User uploads copyrighted material to your AI product | Terms of service, content filters |
| Third party IP in outputs | Model generates text similar to copyrighted material | Output filtering, citation of sources |
| Trade secret exposure | Internal proprietary info leaves the firm | On premise hosting, self hosted models |
| Licensed content boundaries | Your enterprise has a licence that restricts use | Audit usage against licence terms |

**PM decision points**

- What client and regulatory contracts govern the data being processed?
- Is the AI provider an approved sub processor under those contracts, or is an addendum needed?
- Are there specific data classes (trade secrets, sealed legal matters) that must not leave the firm at all?

**Watch outs**

- "It is just an internal pilot" still triggers contractual obligations if real client data is used
- M&A and litigation matters often have stricter rules than general business operations
- Sub processor lists are usually annexes to master agreements, easy to miss

**Takeaway: IP and confidentiality are contract issues first, technology issues second. Map obligations to architecture before building.**

**Qantas story**

IP and confidentiality considerations come up in unexpected ways at Qantas, particularly given the network of partner airlines, codeshare relationships, and supplier arrangements that the airline operates within.

Consider a hypothetical AI system that processes inbound supplier proposals and contract drafts. The system reads documents, extracts key terms, summarises risks, and flags departures from standard terms. The documents themselves are confidential between Qantas and the supplier. Many of them are marked "commercial in confidence" or covered by mutual NDAs.

The question is whether sending those documents through a third party AI API satisfies Qantas's confidentiality obligations to its suppliers. The answer depends on the specifics. If the NDA explicitly permits Qantas to use such confidential information through approved processors, and the AI provider is on the approved processor list (or can be added with the supplier's consent), the answer is yes. If the NDA is silent or restrictive, the answer might be no, even with zero retention contractual commitments from the AI provider.

The mature approach is to map the supplier and partner confidentiality landscape before designing the AI system. For most suppliers, an addendum to the standard NDA can authorise use of named AI processors. For sensitive negotiations or strategic suppliers, the addendum might not be appropriate, and a different architecture (on premise or self hosted) becomes the right answer.

The codeshare partner consideration is similar. Qantas operates codeshares with many other airlines, and the operational data exchange includes confidential information under partnership agreements. An AI system that processes this data needs to satisfy the partnership agreement obligations, not just Qantas's own privacy standards. The partnership agreements may have data handling requirements that are more restrictive than Qantas's general approach.

The third party IP consideration also matters in AI output. If a Qantas AI system generates marketing copy that incorporates phrasing from licensed sources (stock photo descriptions, partner content libraries, advertising platforms), the output might inherit the licensing constraints of those sources. A naive deployment could result in licensed material being repurposed in ways the licence does not cover. The defence is to scope what content the AI has access to, and to ensure that licensed material is either excluded or used only in ways the licence permits.

The interesting strategic question is how to handle the case where the AI provider itself becomes a sub processor under client and supplier agreements. As AI adoption grows, sub processor lists are getting longer and the review process for adding new ones is getting more rigorous. Some Qantas suppliers might object to new AI sub processors being added without consultation. The mature approach is proactive: notify key suppliers of AI processor changes in advance, give them time to raise concerns, and treat sub processor governance as an ongoing discipline.

For AI delivery, the lesson is that IP and confidentiality are contract issues first and technology issues second. The questions to ask are which contracts govern the data being processed, who needs to consent to AI processing, what addenda are needed, and whether some data categories must be excluded entirely. The technology architecture flows from these answers. Skipping this analysis at scoping leads to architectures that have to be reworked after legal review, which is expensive and politically painful.

::: zh

**定义**

当客户数据流向第三方 LLM 提供商时,潜在的关注是 IP (知识产权) 和机密性。IP 关注的是: 该数据是否被用来训练影响竞争对手的模型?机密性关注的是: 我们的数据可以被泄露给第三方吗?这些问题来自每个企业 AI 范围确定阶段。

**为何在交付中重要**

误解这些会破坏 AI 项目在采购阶段。能够清楚地解释 IP 和机密性风险的 PM 可以解决高管担忧,并避免范围确定崩溃在错误的恐惧。理解差异让你聚焦于真正的风险。

**运作原理**

| 关注 | 现实 (2026) | 缓解 |
|---|---|---|
| 我们的数据训练他们的模型 | 默认通过现代企业 API (零保留) 是: 不 | 检查零数据保留是默认的 (Bedrock, Azure OpenAI 是) |
| 我们的数据存储在他们的服务器上 | 通常被零保留协议禁止 | 检查 DPA 语言 |
| 我们的数据被人类提供商雇员审查 | 通常被零保留协议禁止 | 检查 DPA 语言 |
| 我们的数据被他们的内部系统泄露 | 安全姿势取决于提供商 | 检查认证 (SOC 2, ISO 27001) |
| 数据驻留 | 取决于区域选择 | 选择适当区域 |
| 我们的 IP (system prompt, 工作流) 被提供商提取 | 通常不,但谨慎对 prompt 中的真正 IP | 不放敏感秘密在 prompts 中 |

主要提供商关键事实:

- Anthropic: 默认不在 API 调用上训练。零保留可用于企业。
- OpenAI: 默认不在 API 调用上训练。零保留对 Tier 4+ 默认。
- Google Vertex: 默认不在 API 调用上训练。
- Bedrock: 默认不在 API 调用上训练,在客户配置区域。
- Azure OpenAI: 默认不在 API 调用上训练,在 Azure 区域。
- 开源模型 (Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek) 自托管: 数据永远不离开你的环境。

**PM 决策点**

- 我们的数据合同条款是什么,我们具体审查它们?
- 我们处理什么样的数据敏感性,提供商措施足够吗?
- 法律团队同意 DPA 语言,或者我们还在协商它?

**注意事项**

- "我们使用 OpenAI" 不告诉你它是 OpenAI 直接还是通过 Azure (不同的合同)
- 模型提供商定期更新条款,定期重新检查
- 一些客户拒绝任何第三方 LLM 处理; 然后只有自托管 open weights 工作

**要点总结: 通过现代企业渠道, IP 和机密性风险是低的且可管理的。理解合同语言,适当配置区域和保留,沟通清晰的高管。**

**Qantas 案例**

考虑 Qantas 团队中关于将客户数据发送到 Anthropic 的对话。它将以可预测的方式上升。"我们将客户数据发送给第三方 AI 提供商,他们会用我们的数据训练他们的模型吗?他们会泄露我们的数据吗?他们会看到我们的数据吗?"

合适的回答是详细的。Qantas 不直接发送到 Anthropic; 它通过 AWS Bedrock 发送。Anthropic 通过 AWS 不接收或存储数据。AWS Bedrock 在悉尼区域操作。零数据保留是 Bedrock 上的默认配置。Anthropic 不会用 Qantas 数据训练。AWS 不会用它训练。它不会泄露给其他客户。AWS 的 SOC 2 和 ISO 27001 认证适用。

更微妙的关注涉及 IP 风险。一些 Qantas 系统的 system prompt 包含商业敏感方法 (例如,如何应用补偿规则,或客户细分逻辑)。这些 system prompts 流向模型。它们被 Anthropic 或 AWS 提取吗? 通过零数据保留, 不。但实际上, 不在 prompts 中放置真实的商业秘密是更深的保护。竞争 IP 应该存在于工具中或代码中, 不是 system prompts。即使没有零保留, 这种纪律应该应用。

更广泛的对话涉及供应商风险。如果 Anthropic 被收购怎么办?如果 AWS Bedrock 政策改变怎么办?如果 Anthropic 改变它们的零保留默认怎么办?Qantas 应该为这些可能性建模, 而不是假设当前条款永远保持。供应商多样化(在不同模型上使用多个 API,所以没有锁定)是缓解。合同条款应该有变更通知期。Qantas 应该明确同意条款变更, 不是默认接受。

对于真正敏感的工作负载 (例如,涉及内部审计或法律事项的工作负载),自托管 open weights 是适当的回答,即使在 Qantas 规模上工程更复杂。安全性是值得的开销 trade-off,数据从未离开 Qantas 边界。

最微妙的关注涉及"我们的数据是否影响他们的模型"。即使没有训练,我们的数据是否以某种方式塑造他们的模型行为?答案是不: 当前推理调用不更新模型权重。数据流入,响应流出,数据不留下。这与 fine tuning 完全不同, fine tuning 实际上确实将数据嵌入模型 (并且默认情况下,fine tuned 模型是私有的, 不影响他人)。

对 AI 交付来说,启示是 IP 和机密性问题是需要清晰回答的合理高管关注。我们的数据是否训练他们的模型?不,通过零保留默认。我们的数据是否被存储?不,通过零保留默认。我们的数据是否被人类看到?不,通过零保留默认。我们的数据是否离开澳大利亚?不,通过区域选择。能够冷静地走过这些问题的交付从业者解决了上升,使项目继续。无法清楚回答的人在重复的合规审查会议中陷入泥沼。
