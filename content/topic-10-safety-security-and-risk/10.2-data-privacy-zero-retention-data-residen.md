**Definition**

Data privacy in AI involves three distinct issues. Zero retention means the provider does not store or train on the data you send through the API. Data residency means data is processed in specific geographic regions to satisfy local laws. PII handling refers to how personally identifiable information is detected, redacted, masked, or otherwise managed in inputs, outputs, and logs.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Privacy is the single most common procurement blocker in enterprise AI. A great architecture that violates GDPR, the Australian Privacy Act, HIPAA, or APRA standards is dead on arrival. PMs who address privacy in week one keep projects on the path. PMs who treat it as a compliance review at the end discover the architecture has to be rebuilt.

**How it works**

| Privacy concern | What it means | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Provider data retention | Does the provider store inputs/outputs, use them for training? | Enterprise tier with zero retention clauses |
| Data residency | Where is data processed and stored geographically? | Region specific endpoints, cloud hosted models in target region |
| Sub processor risk | What third parties does the provider use? | Review provider's sub processor list, restrict where possible |
| PII in inputs | Sending names, emails, IDs, health info to the model | Redact or tokenise before sending, or use providers with appropriate certifications |
| PII in outputs | Model generating PII from context | Output filtering, never expose more than user is authorised for |
| PII in logs | Observability tools storing PII | Redact at logging layer, encrypt, restrict access |
| Right to deletion | User requests data deletion | Pipeline to purge from all stores including caches and logs |

Enterprise tiers from major providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) offer zero retention and signed BAAs (Business Associate Agreements) for regulated use cases. Default consumer tiers usually do not.

**PM decision points**

- What data classes will the model actually process, personal, health, financial, sensitive corporate?
- Does the provider contract include zero retention, BAA, data residency for the required regions?
- Are PII redaction and detokenisation built into the pipeline, or assumed to be unnecessary?

**Watch outs**

- "Enterprise plan" does not automatically mean compliant, check specific clauses
- Logs, caches, and vector stores are also data, not just the API call itself
- Cross border data flow rules (GDPR, China PIPL, Australia APP) apply to AI providers too

**Takeaway: privacy is contractual, architectural, and operational. Solve all three before scaling, not after.**

**Qantas story**

Data privacy at Qantas, particularly under the Australian Privacy Principles and the specific obligations that come with being a major airline, is the single most consequential design constraint for any AI system that touches customer or operational data.

Consider a hypothetical Qantas customer service AI assistant. The data flowing through it includes passenger names, booking references, flight details, frequent flyer numbers, contact information, and sometimes special service requirements (dietary, medical, mobility). All of this is personal information under the APPs. Some of it is health information under more stringent rules.

The privacy posture is therefore layered. First, the provider relationship must satisfy zero retention. Sending this data to a model that might use it for training would be a serious breach. The enterprise tier from major providers offers zero retention contractually, but the contract needs to be in place before any production data flows.

Second, data residency. The Australian Privacy Act and Qantas's own customer commitments mean that data should be processed in Australia where possible. Bedrock and Azure OpenAI both have Sydney region availability for the major model families, which satisfies this. Provider native APIs in other regions would create cross border data flow concerns that need to be assessed and disclosed.

Third, PII handling within the pipeline. The naive architecture sends full customer records into the model. A more careful architecture redacts or tokenises sensitive identifiers before they reach the model, with detokenisation happening at the application layer. The model sees enough to do its job (the request type, the policy context, the conversation history) but not unnecessary specifics (full credit card numbers, full passport details).

Fourth, observability layer privacy. The logs that capture prompts and responses for debugging and eval purposes contain the same PII as the live conversations. The observability stack needs redaction, access controls, retention policies, and right to deletion processes that match the rest of the data estate. This is often where projects discover gaps months in.

Fifth, downstream processes. Caches, vector stores, eval datasets, and analytics pipelines all hold AI conversation data. Each is a separate data store with its own privacy obligations. A unified data governance approach treats them all as in scope.

The interesting organisational reality is that privacy review for AI systems at large enterprises like Qantas can take months. The contracts need review, the architectures need assessment, the data flows need mapping, the residual risks need acceptance. Teams that engage privacy in week one of the project finish on time. Teams that engage privacy in week ten of the project lose two months waiting for review.

For AI delivery, the lesson is that privacy is not a checkbox at the end of an AI project. It is a design constraint from day one. The discipline is to bring privacy stakeholders into scoping, map data flows before architecting, build redaction into the pipeline from the start, and treat the observability layer with the same privacy rigour as the production system. Clients in regulated industries appreciate this because they have seen projects stall on late privacy review. Clients in less regulated industries sometimes need to be educated about why this matters, and the right approach is to lay out the obligations clearly without lecturing.

**定义**

数据隐私关注的是个人信息 (PII)、受监管数据 (医疗、金融)、用户同意,以及它们怎么跟 AI 系统打交道。PII 包括姓名、邮箱、电话、地址、出生日期,以及任何能识别个人身份的东西。在 AI 里,关键问题是: 哪些 PII 流进了 prompt、检索、日志,它们又流向了哪里。

**为何在交付中重要**

隐私问题是 AI 项目最常踩到的合规雷区。它们经常在交付后期才浮现,因为隐私团队没有早期介入。早期就理解 PII 和同意的 PM,能构建出"满足要求的架构",而不是事后才发现问题。

**运作原理**

| 关注点 | 它意味着什么 | 缓解 |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt 里的 PII | 用户消息、检索内容、工具输出可能含 PII | 合理时做净化,用零留存 API |
| 日志里的 PII | 完整 prompt/completion 日志记录每次交互 | PII 脱敏、访问控制、留存政策 |
| 数据驻留 | PII 不能离开特定司法管辖区 | 选 region API、合适的自托管或云部署 |
| 同意 | 用户必须知道 AI 在处理 | 透明披露、提供 opt-out 选项 |
| 跨境数据 | 在 Qantas 这种场景下,横跨 ANZ 边界 | 选 region、走 Qantas 内部审查 |
| 训练数据使用 | 这份数据会被用来训练模型吗 | 零留存协议、API 设置 |
| 第三方 LLM 处理 | 数据流到外部供应商 | 数据处理协议 (DPA) |
| 被遗忘权 | 用户的数据被删除请求 | 删除工作流、日志清理 |

主流厂商在合适配置下都提供零数据留存 (Anthropic、OpenAI、Google),不用你的数据训练。这在 Bedrock 和 Azure OpenAI 上是默认。

**PM 决策点**

- 哪些 PII 在 AI 系统里流动?在什么权限下?这张图不画清楚,后面所有隐私评审都会卡。
- 日志里的 PII 我们怎么处理?
- 用户有没有同意 AI 处理?是什么样的披露?

**注意事项**

- 日志里的 PII 是最常被忽略的暴露面。
- 单个用户的"被遗忘权"请求,需要把这个用户的日志清掉。
- Region 选择影响 PII 流向。有些 API 端点会路由到全球节点,除非你明确配置。

**要点总结: 隐私不是事后再说。它从第一天就是架构。隐私团队从第一次会就要在场。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想 Qantas 客服 AI 助手里的 PII 流。一次典型的客户交互大概是这样的。客户从自己的会员账号提供姓名 (PII)、订单参考号、邮箱 (PII)、电话 (PII)。检索浮现他们的订单记录,包含特殊餐食需求 (有些可能敏感,比如医疗餐食类型)、座位偏好、同行旅客的姓名 (PII)。AI 的回应可能引用这些里面的任何一项。

每一个 PII 元素都需要明确的数据流核算:

1. 客户消息包含 PII。它流到 LLM API。Bedrock API 配置了零数据留存,所以 PII 不会被拿去训练。Region 设为 ap-southeast-2 (悉尼),所以 PII 永远不离开澳大利亚。
2. 检索回来的订单记录作为 LLM 输入。隐私处理跟上一条相同。
3. LLM 调用被记到内部可观测性栈。这些日志含 PII。访问被控制 (只有授权工程师能看)。留存是 30 天作为标准事故调查需要,然后日志被自动清除。
4. 更低级别的访问日志做 PII 脱敏。比如,展示整体数据流 pattern 的分析仪表板,姓名和电话会被脱敏。
5. 客户的请求被记录在客服系统里,而这些是 Qantas 已经有的隐私合规系统,处理本身已经合规。

需要讨论的还包括跨境数据。如果客户在漫游,他们的位置数据可能跨境。如果一个国际客户接触系统,他们的 PII 仍然在 ap-southeast-2 处理。这是合适的,符合澳洲隐私要求。

模型本身呢?模型是 Anthropic 的 Claude。Qantas 的合同是跟 AWS 签的,不是跟 Anthropic 签的。AWS 处理跟 Anthropic 的关系。Anthropic 的隐私实践 (不在客户数据上训练、通过 Bedrock 的数据不被 Anthropic 存储) 通过 AWS 传递过来。

同意是单独的考虑。Qantas 的隐私政策必须披露 AI 在客户交互中的使用。客户必须有 opt-out 选项 (比如要求转人工坐席而不是 AI)。同意机制要明确,符合澳洲隐私法。

被遗忘权请求需要工作流。如果一个 Qantas 客户行使 GDPR 风格的删除请求 (或澳洲等价的请求),他们的 AI 交互日志需要被识别并清除。这意味着日志里要按用户 ID 索引,删除才能高效。

最后,隐私是个分层的合规问题。Qantas 法务、隐私官、信息安全、工程都需要在需求确定时对齐。这通常要走 Qantas 现有的内部隐私评估流程,适配到 AI 用例的具体情境。

往大了说,这件事给 AI 交付的启示是: 隐私从一开始就在需求确定里。在动工程之前问: "用户数据流到哪里、怎么被处理、怎么被存储、怎么被删除"——不是事故之后才问。能在隐私团队面前拿出一张干净的 PII 流图 (输入、处理、日志、留存、删除) 的交付者,能快速过审。给出含糊答案的人,要在第三轮评审里来回打转。
