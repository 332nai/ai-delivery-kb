**Definition**

Distillation is the practice of training a smaller model to imitate the outputs of a larger, more capable model. The large model generates examples, the small model learns from those examples. The result is a small model that performs much better than its size would suggest, on the specific tasks it was distilled for.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Frontier models are expensive and slow. For high volume production workloads, this becomes a real cost and latency problem. Distillation lets you capture most of the frontier model's quality in a small fast cheap model, often at one tenth or one twentieth the inference cost. For the right use case, distillation is the highest leverage cost optimisation in the AI stack.

**How it works**

The basic flow:

1. Define the task (e.g. classifying support tickets, summarising documents)
2. Run a large model (the teacher) on thousands to tens of thousands of real or synthetic examples
3. Capture the teacher's inputs and outputs as training data
4. Fine tune a small model (the student) on that data using SFT
5. Evaluate the student against the teacher on a held out test set
6. If the student is close enough, deploy it for the task

| Layer | Teacher | Student |
|---|---|---|
| Model size | Large (200B+ params, frontier API) | Small (3B to 70B, often open weight) |
| Cost per call | High | 5x to 50x cheaper |
| Latency | Slow | Often 3x to 10x faster |
| Capability | Broad, handles novel queries | Narrow, only good at distilled tasks |

Distillation works because the small model does not need to be generally intelligent, it just needs to mimic the teacher on the specific task. The teacher's "thinking" is implicitly transferred through the examples.

**PM decision points**

- Is the workload high enough volume to justify the engineering effort of distillation?
- Is the task narrow and well defined, distillation does not work well on open ended tasks?
- Has the team evaluated the student rigorously, distillation can hide subtle quality drops?

**Watch outs**

- Distillation locks in the teacher's mistakes, if the teacher hallucinates, the student learns to hallucinate too
- Some model licences restrict using outputs to train other models, check terms (e.g. OpenAI, some China models)
- The student will not generalise to tasks outside the distilled scope, it is a specialist not a generalist

**Takeaway: distillation is the cost optimisation move once a task is stable and high volume. It trades flexibility for economics.**

**Qantas story**

Distillation is one of the highest leverage cost optimisations available to Qantas for high volume narrow AI tasks. The pattern fits a specific shape of workload, and recognising the shape is the skill.

Consider an example workload: tagging inbound customer service interactions with intent categories, sentiment, and urgency. Volume might run to two million interactions per month. Quality requirements are real but bounded: the intent classification needs to be 95 percent accurate against a human labelled gold standard, sentiment classification needs to be 90 percent accurate, urgency classification needs to be 92 percent accurate. The frontier model can do this work easily but at significant cost.

The distillation approach works as follows. First, run the frontier model (say Claude Sonnet) on a stratified sample of fifty thousand real interactions, capturing inputs and outputs. This costs maybe AUD 800 in one off model spend. Second, fine tune a smaller model (say a Llama 8B variant) on the captured pairs using SFT plus LoRA. Training time on a single GPU is a few hours. Third, evaluate the student model on a held out test set against the teacher. If the student is within an acceptable quality range, deploy it.

The cost economics shift dramatically. The frontier model at full volume might cost AUD 90K per month. The distilled student model self hosted on Bedrock with provisioned throughput might cost AUD 6K per month. The savings pay back the distillation engineering effort within the first month.

The interesting edge cases are worth understanding. Distillation works well when the task is narrow and the teacher's behaviour is consistent. It works poorly when the task is open ended or when the teacher's outputs vary by context in ways the student cannot capture. Distillation also locks in the teacher's mistakes, so if the teacher has a known weakness (say, misclassifying a specific edge case 5 percent of the time), the student will inherit it. Validation against ground truth, not just teacher outputs, is important.

The licensing considerations matter too. Some model providers restrict using their outputs to train competing models. Most closed APIs from major providers allow distillation for the customer's own use but restrict commercial training. The legal team needs to review terms before launching a distillation pipeline at scale.

For AI delivery, the lesson is that distillation is the cost lever that turns a successful pilot into a sustainable production system. When a pilot proves a frontier model can do the work well, the next question is "can we capture that capability in a smaller model at lower cost." If the task is narrow and high volume, the answer is usually yes. Clients are often unaware of this lever, and walking them through it during scoping plants the right seed for future cost optimisation.

**定义**

蒸馏是一种实践: 训练一个较小的模型,去模仿一个更大、能力更强的模型的输出。大模型生成样本,小模型从这些样本里学习。最终结果是,在它被蒸馏过的那个具体任务上,这个小模型的表现比它的"个头"暗示的要好得多。

**为何在交付中重要**

前沿模型既贵又慢。一旦走到高量的生产工作负载上,这两件事就变成真正的成本和延迟问题。蒸馏让你能把前沿模型的大部分质量,捕捉到一个又小、又快、又便宜的模型里——通常推理成本能降到十分之一甚至二十分之一。对合适的用例来说,蒸馏是 AI 栈里杠杆最高的成本优化之一。

**运作原理**

基本流程:

1. 定义任务 (比如分类支持工单、总结文档)
2. 在几千到几万个真实或者合成样本上跑大模型 (老师)
3. 把老师的输入和输出捕捉下来作为训练数据
4. 用 SFT 在这份数据上 fine tune 小模型 (学生)
5. 在留出的测试集上,把学生跟老师对比评估
6. 如果学生模型足够接近,在这个任务上部署它

| 维度 | 老师 | 学生 |
|---|---|---|
| 模型大小 | 大 (200B+ 参数,前沿 API) | 小 (3B 到 70B,通常是 open weight) |
| 每次调用成本 | 高 | 便宜 5 到 50 倍 |
| 延迟 | 慢 | 通常快 3 到 10 倍 |
| 能力 | 通用,能处理新查询 | 窄,只在蒸馏过的任务上好 |

蒸馏之所以能 work,是因为小模型不需要广泛的智能,它只需要在一个具体任务上模仿老师就够了。老师的"思考方式"通过样本隐式地转移过去。

**PM 决策点**

- 工作负载的量够不够大,够不够撑得起蒸馏这一轮工程?这是第一道筛。
- 任务是不是足够窄、足够定义良好?蒸馏在开放式任务上效果不好。
- 团队对学生模型做了严格的评估了吗?蒸馏可能会掩盖一些微妙的质量下降,这件事要警觉。

**注意事项**

- 蒸馏会把老师的错误一起锁进去。老师有幻觉,学生就学会幻觉。
- 有些模型的许可证限制"用它的输出来训练其他模型",条款要看清 (比如 OpenAI、一些中国模型)。
- 学生不会泛化到蒸馏范围之外的任务。它是一个专才,不是通才。

**要点总结: 一旦任务稳定下来且量大,蒸馏就是该出手的成本优化举措。它用灵活性换经济学。**

**Qantas 案例**

蒸馏是 Qantas 在高量、窄任务上能用到的最高杠杆成本优化之一。这个 pattern 适用于一种特定形状的工作负载,识别这种形状是 PM 的功夫。

设想一个工作负载: 给入站的客服交互打标签,标注意图类目、情绪、紧急程度。量可能达到每月两百万次交互。质量要求是真实但有边界的: 意图分类要对着人工标注的金标准达到 95% 准确率,情绪分类要 90%,紧急程度要 92%。前沿模型能轻松搞定这种工作,但成本可观。

蒸馏方法是这么走的。第一,在五万条真实交互的分层样本上跑前沿模型 (比如 Claude Sonnet),把输入和输出全部捕捉下来。这一次性的模型支出可能就 800 澳元。第二,用 SFT + LoRA 在这些捕捉到的"输入-输出对"上,fine tune 一个更小的模型 (比如 Llama 8B 变体)。在单张 GPU 上,训练时间是几小时。第三,在留出的测试集上,把学生模型跟老师对比评估。如果学生模型落在可接受的质量区间内,部署它。

成本的经济学一下就变了。前沿模型按完整量算,可能每月花掉 9 万澳元。蒸馏过的学生模型,在 Bedrock 上自托管 + 预置吞吐,可能每月只花 6000 澳元。省下来的钱在第一个月内就把蒸馏的工程投入收回来了。

值得了解的边缘情况。蒸馏在任务窄、老师行为一致时效果好。它在任务开放式、或者老师输出随上下文以学生没法捕捉的方式变化时,效果差。蒸馏还会把老师的错误一起锁进去——如果老师有个已知的弱点 (比如某个具体边缘情况它 5% 的时候会分错类),学生会原样继承这个弱点。所以评估的时候要对着 ground truth 验证,而不是只对着老师的输出验证。

许可证的考虑同样重要。有些模型厂商明确限制"用它们的输出训练其他模型"。主流厂商的 closed API 大部分允许"客户为自己用途做蒸馏",但限制商业训练。在大规模上线蒸馏 pipeline 之前,法务团队要把条款过一遍。

往大了说,这件事给 AI 交付的启示是: 蒸馏是把一次成功的试点转变成可持续生产系统的成本杠杆。当一个试点证明了"前沿模型能很好地完成这项工作",下一个问题就是: "我们能不能用一个更小、更便宜的模型,捕捉到同样的能力?" 如果任务是窄的、量是高的,答案通常是能。客户经常不知道有这个杠杆,在需求确定阶段把它讲清楚,就为未来的成本优化埋下了正确的种子。
