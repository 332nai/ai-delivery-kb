**Definition**

The "fine tuning trap" is the pattern where a client asks for fine tuning because it sounds like the serious, sophisticated solution, when the actual problem is poor prompting, missing context, bad data, or unclear requirements. Fine tuning becomes a way to look productive without solving the real problem.

**Why it matters in delivery**

This is one of the most common scoping mistakes in enterprise AI. PMs who recognise it can redirect the client toward what will actually work, save the budget, ship faster, and avoid the political damage of a fine tuning project that delivers nothing. The trap is everywhere because fine tuning sounds like real engineering and prompting sounds trivial.

**How it works**

| Client says | Real problem | Right fix |
|---|---|---|
| "We need to train it on our data" | Model lacks access to client data | RAG, not fine tuning |
| "It does not sound like us" | System prompt lacks tone guidance and examples | Better system prompt with few shot |
| "It hallucinates on our terminology" | No grounding in client vocabulary | RAG over glossary, prompt with key terms |
| "It is inconsistent" | Temperature too high, prompt too vague | Lower temperature, tighten prompt |
| "It is not as good as ChatGPT" | Wrong model tier or prompt for the task | Test stronger model, improve prompt |
| "We have lots of historical data" | Solution looking for a problem | Define the use case first, then decide |

The trap usually shows up after a disappointing prototype. Someone says "we should fine tune." Heads nod. Months get burned.

**PM decision points**

- Has the team built a strong system prompt and tested it rigorously before considering fine tuning?
- Is the problem actually a knowledge gap (RAG fixes it) or a behaviour gap (better prompt usually fixes it)?
- What is the specific gap fine tuning would close that nothing else can?

**Watch outs**

- Clients sometimes want fine tuning for the optics, "we trained our own model" is a status line, push back politely
- A weak prompt makes the model look bad and creates demand for fine tuning to fix something prompting would have solved
- If you do fine tune, build the eval first, otherwise you cannot prove it worked

**Takeaway: fine tuning is the last resort, not the first. Most "we need fine tuning" requests are really "we need a better prompt or better data."**

**Qantas story**

The fine tuning trap is alive and well in large enterprises, and Qantas would be no exception. The pattern is so consistent it is almost predictable. A team is excited about AI, has access to "lots of data," and proposes fine tuning as the obvious sophisticated solution. The right delivery response is to slow down and ask a series of careful questions.

Consider a hypothetical Qantas team running customer experience research. They have years of customer feedback comments, NPS verbatims, and complaint letters. They propose fine tuning a model on this corpus "so it understands Qantas customers." The proposal is well intentioned and would absorb six months of engineering effort.

The diagnostic questions surface the real problem. What is the model going to do that prompting cannot? The team's actual need is to summarise themes across thousands of customer comments and identify emerging concerns. That is a prompting and clustering problem, not a fine tuning problem. A strong system prompt with five example comment summaries, plus a clustering step using embeddings, solves the actual problem in two weeks.

Another team wants to fine tune for "Qantas tone" in customer service responses. The diagnostic question is whether they have tried strong few shot prompting first. The honest answer is usually no. Five well chosen example responses in the system prompt typically captures 90 percent of the tone gap. The remaining 10 percent is real but rarely worth a fine tuning project.

The trap is hardest to navigate when the desire for fine tuning is partly strategic rather than technical. Leaders sometimes want to say "we have our own AI model" as a competitive signal. The delivery practitioner cannot just say no; they need to acknowledge the strategic intent and offer a path that delivers it. That might mean fine tuning a model on a narrow well defined task where the technical case is strong, and using that as the story of "Qantas's custom AI capability" while the broader portfolio uses simpler approaches.

The other version of the trap is data driven. A team has built a labelled dataset and feels obligated to "use it for fine tuning" rather than letting it sit. The right response is that good evaluation data is often more valuable as eval data than as training data. Use the dataset to measure prompt quality, not to train a model that might not be needed.

For AI delivery, the broader lesson is that "we need fine tuning" is almost always a hypothesis to be tested, not a conclusion to be acted on. The discipline is to build a strong baseline with prompting and retrieval, measure the gap rigorously, and only then consider fine tuning for the specific residual problem. Most of the time, the baseline is enough. The clients who appreciate this approach are the ones who have been burned previously by fine tuning projects that delivered less than promised. They learn to value a practitioner who can explain calmly why fine tuning is unnecessary for their specific case.

**定义**

"fine tuning 陷阱"是指这样一种 pattern: 客户要求做 fine tuning,因为它听起来"严肃"、"复杂"、是一个像样的方案,但真正的问题其实是 prompt 写得糟、缺上下文、数据质量差、或者需求没说清楚。Fine tuning 在这种情况下变成"看起来有产出"的方式,而不是真正解决问题的方式。

**为何在交付中重要**

这是企业 AI 里最常见的需求确定错误之一。能识别这个陷阱的 PM,可以把客户引导到真正起作用的方向上,省下预算,更快上线,而且避免交付一个"什么都没解决"的 fine tuning 项目带来的政治损耗。这个陷阱到处都是,因为 fine tuning 听起来像"真工程",而 prompting 听起来像"小打小闹"。

**运作原理**

| 客户嘴上说 | 真实问题 | 正确的修复 |
|---|---|---|
| "我们要拿自己的数据训练它" | 模型没法访问客户数据 | RAG,不是 fine tuning |
| "它听起来不像我们" | system prompt 缺语气指导和示例 | 写更好的 system prompt,加 few shot |
| "它在我们的术语上幻觉" | 没有锚到客户的词汇表上 | 在词汇表上做 RAG,prompt 里点出关键术语 |
| "它不稳定" | Temperature 太高,prompt 太模糊 | 降 temperature,prompt 写紧 |
| "它不如 ChatGPT 好" | 模型层级不对,或者 prompt 不对 | 测一下更强的模型,改 prompt |
| "我们手里有大量历史数据" | 在拿着方案找问题 | 先把用例定清楚,再决定方法 |

陷阱通常出现在一次令人失望的原型之后。有人说"我们应该上 fine tuning"。大家点头。然后几个月烧没了。

**PM 决策点**

- 在考虑 fine tuning 之前,团队有没有先把强 system prompt 搭起来、严格测一遍?这一步跳过去,后面所有判断都是建在沙上。
- 这个问题到底是知识缺口 (RAG 能修),还是行为缺口 (好 prompt 通常就能修)?这两件事经常被搞混。
- Fine tuning 要关闭的是哪一个具体差距?其他方案都做不到的那个差距是什么?讲不清,先不动。

**注意事项**

- 客户有时候要 fine tuning 是为了"看起来高级"——"我们训练了自己的模型"是一种身份信号。这种诉求要礼貌地反驳。
- 弱 prompt 让模型看起来差,然后这个"差"被用来证明需要 fine tuning,实际上换个 prompt 就解决了。
- 如果真的要做 fine tuning,先把 eval 搭起来。没有 eval,你没办法证明它有没有效。

**要点总结: Fine tuning 是最后手段,不是第一选项。大部分"我们需要 fine tuning"的请求,真实意思都是"我们需要更好的 prompt 或更好的数据"。**

**Qantas 案例**

Fine tuning 陷阱在大型企业里活得很滋润,Qantas 不会是例外。这个 pattern 一致到几乎可以预测。一个团队对 AI 兴奋起来,手上"有大量数据",于是提议做 fine tuning 作为一个看起来复杂的解决方案。正确的交付应对是: 慢下来,问一系列仔细的问题。

设想一个假设的 Qantas 客户体验研究团队。他们手里有好几年的客户反馈评论、NPS 直接反馈、投诉信。他们提议在这份语料库上 fine tune 模型,"让它了解 Qantas 的客户"。这个提议是出于好意的,而且会吃掉六个月的工程力。

诊断性问题能把真问题挖出来。"模型要做的事情,是 prompting 做不到的吗?" 这个团队的实际需求,是在几千条客户评论上做主题总结,识别新兴关注点。这是一个 prompting + 聚类问题,不是 fine tuning 问题。一份带五个示例评论摘要的强 system prompt,配上一次用 embedding 的聚类步骤,两周之内就能把真问题解决掉。

另一个团队想为"客服回复里的 Qantas 语气"做 fine tuning。诊断性问题是: 他们有没有先试过强 few shot prompting?诚实的答案通常是"没有"。在 system prompt 里精心挑选五个示例回复,通常能覆盖 90% 的语气差距。剩下的 10% 是真的存在的,但很少值得为它启动一个 fine tuning 项目。

陷阱最难绕过的情况,是当 fine tuning 的诉求一部分是战略性、不是技术性的时候。领导有时候希望说"我们有我们自己的 AI 模型",作为一个竞争性信号。这种时候交付者不能只是说不,他需要承认那个战略意图,然后提供一条能交付出这种意图的路径。这可能意味着: 在一个技术上站得住脚的窄任务上做 fine tuning,把它作为"Qantas 的自研 AI 能力"这个故事的支点,而更大的组合里其他用例继续走简单方案。

陷阱的另一种版本是数据驱动的。一个团队搭好了一份标注数据集,感觉有义务"把它用来 fine tuning",而不是让它闲着。正确的应对是: 一份好的评估数据,通常作为 eval 数据比作为训练数据更有价值。用这份数据去度量 prompt 质量,而不是用它去训一个可能根本不需要的模型。

往大了说,这件事给 AI 交付的启示是: "我们需要 fine tuning" 这句话几乎永远是一个待验证的假设,不是一个该立刻执行的结论。纪律是: 用 prompting 和检索搭一个强基线,严格度量差距,只有到这一步才考虑用 fine tuning 解决具体的残余问题。绝大多数时候,基线就够了。会真正欣赏这种做法的客户,通常是之前被一个交付低于预期的 fine tuning 项目烧过的那些客户。他们学会重视那种能冷静解释"为什么 fine tuning 对你这个具体场景并不必要"的交付者。
