**Definition**

Cost per user per month is the unit economic that matters in enterprise AI. It is the projected token, infrastructure, and operational cost divided by the number of active users in a given period. Modelling this before you build determines whether the business case works, what pricing to set, and what design constraints to enforce.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Most AI projects start without a unit economic model and discover in production that the cost per user exceeds the revenue per user. PMs who build the cost model in week one expose unsustainable designs early, redirect scope to affordable patterns, and avoid the awkward conversation where a successful pilot must be killed because it does not scale.

**How it works**

A practical cost model has four inputs:

1. **Interactions per user per month**: how many AI calls does a typical user trigger?
2. **Tokens per interaction**: input plus output, including retrieval and tool overhead
3. **Cost per million tokens**: weighted average across input, output, and model tiers
4. **Other costs**: vector DB, observability, inference infrastructure, support

| Workload example | Interactions per user per month | Tokens per interaction | Model tier | Cost per user per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Internal search assistant | 30 queries | 4,000 in + 300 out | Mid tier | ~ USD 0.60 |
| Sales prospecting agent | 50 tasks, 8 step agent | 30,000 total per task | Flagship | ~ USD 25 |
| Customer support bot | 5 conversations | 6,000 in + 500 out | Tiered | ~ USD 0.30 |
| Code review assistant | 200 reviews | 8,000 in + 1,500 out | Flagship | ~ USD 12 |
| Document summariser | 20 docs | 10,000 in + 800 out | Mid tier | ~ USD 1.20 |

Add 20 to 40 percent overhead for retries, evaluation, observability, and growth in usage.

**Worked example**

Building a sales agent for 200 users:

- Each user runs ~3 agent tasks per workday, ~60 per month
- Each task averages 8 steps with cumulative input growth, totalling ~25K input tokens and ~3K output tokens
- At flagship pricing (USD 3 input, USD 15 output per million), cost per task ~ USD 0.12
- Cost per user per month: 60 x USD 0.12 = USD 7.20
- 200 users: USD 1,440 per month, USD 17,280 per year
- Add 30 percent overhead for evals, retries, vector DB: ~USD 22,500 per year

That number lets the team compare against expected ROI (e.g. hours saved per rep) and decide if the project pencils out before building.

**PM decision points**

- Has the team built a cost per user per month model before scoping, not after?
- Does the model use realistic usage assumptions, not optimistic launch projections?
- What are the levers to bring cost down if the first estimate is too high?

**Watch outs**

- Real usage is often 2 to 5x higher than initial estimates, model for growth
- Power users (top 5 percent) often drive 50 percent of cost, plan for them
- Cost estimates without observability are guesses, instrument early to validate

**Takeaway: model cost per user per month before building. It is the only number that says whether the product can sustainably exist.**

**Qantas story**

Modelling cost per user per month is the discipline that separates AI projects with sustainable economics from projects that look impressive in pilot and become budget crises in production. Qantas would be no exception.

Consider a hypothetical AI capability proposed for the Qantas Frequent Flyer team: a personal AI concierge for Platinum members, helping with everything from booking management to dining recommendations to upgrade strategy. The pitch is exciting and members would love it. The unit economic question is whether it can sustainably exist.

The cost model walks through realistic assumptions. Platinum members number in the tens of thousands. Active usage among that group might run to 30 percent monthly, so active users per month might be in the high single thousands. Of those active users, average engagement might be ten interactions per month for the typical user, with a long tail of power users running into the hundreds. Each interaction is moderately complex, using an agent pattern with three to five tool calls and retrieval over personal account data plus general Qantas content. Average input per interaction is 15,000 tokens, average output is 800 tokens, on the Sonnet tier.

The cost per interaction at typical pricing is about AUD 0.04 to 0.06 once tool overhead and small retries are included. Ten interactions per month per active user makes AUD 0.50 per user per month for the average user. Power users in the long tail might run AUD 5 to 10 per month.

Across the active user base, the total monthly cost is in the range of AUD 4K to 8K. Annual cost in the range of AUD 50K to 100K. The business case needs to compare this to the value generated: higher Platinum retention, increased upgrade purchases, member advocacy. If the value exceeds the cost, the capability is worth building. If not, the scope needs to change.

The interesting design conversations happen when the math does not work. Common levers include tightening the agent step limit (lower cost per interaction), routing simpler interactions to a cheaper tier (lower average cost), introducing usage limits per user per month (caps the long tail), and prompt caching to drive down input costs.

The other interesting consideration is growth. If the capability becomes popular and active users double, costs double too. The capacity for growth needs to be modelled. A pilot that works at 5,000 users might be unsustainable at 50,000 unless the unit economics improve as scale grows. Caching, tiering, and possibly distillation become more attractive at scale.

For AI delivery, the lesson is that cost per user per month is the unit economic that matters in enterprise AI. It exposes whether the product can sustainably exist before the team has spent six months building it. The discipline is to build the model in week one of any project, with realistic assumptions, and revisit it monthly as the design firms up. Clients are often unaware of how to think about AI costs at this level of detail, and walking them through it builds enormous credibility. It also gives the client a framework they can apply to future projects, which is the kind of value that builds long term relationships.

**定义**

每用户每月成本 (CPUM) 是衡量 AI 系统单位经济学是否可持续的最佳单一指标。它把 token 成本、基础设施成本、辅助成本 (vector DB、监控、底层设施) 分摊到每个用户头上,得到一个可比较的数字。如果 CPUM 大于客户每用户付给你的钱,产品在规模上是亏的。

**为何在交付中重要**

很多 AI 项目带着糟糕的单位经济学就发了,因为团队从来没认真算过。八个月后,用户用得开心,功能也工作,然后发现每个免费用户每月成本 20 澳元,而公司每用户只收 9 澳元。CPUM 是从第一天起防止这件事的纪律,客户会真心欣赏这种思考。

**运作原理**

CPUM 公式 (简化):

```
CPUM = 每用户每月 LLM 调用次数 × 每次调用成本
     + 每用户每月固定基础设施成本
     + 每用户每月其他可变成本 (检索、工具、日志)
```

| 成本桶 | 包含什么 | 典型范围 |
|---|---|---|
| LLM 调用成本 | 跨该用户所有调用的输入 + 输出 token | $0.10 到 $5 每用户每月,变化范围大 |
| 基础设施 (托管、vector DB、监控) | 固定成本按用户分摊 | $0.05 到 $0.50 每用户每月 |
| 第三方 API (embedding、reranking、工具) | 看使用量 | $0.02 到 $0.30 每用户每月 |
| 用户支持和事故运营 | 实时支持的工程时间 | 项目特定 |
| CPUM 合计 | 以上全部加起来 | $0.20 到 $7 每用户每月,大部分认真部署都在这个区间 |

**举例**

Qantas 客服 AI,每月 500 万活跃用户,但实际每月跟 AI 交互的只有 20 万:
- 平均每个活跃用户每月 100 次调用
- 每次调用 $0.007 (做了模型分层)
- LLM 成本: 200,000 × 100 × $0.007 = $140,000 每月
- 基础设施 (vector DB、监控、日志): $25,000 每月
- 检索 (embedding 服务调用): $5,000 每月
- 合计: $170,000 每月

每活跃用户: $0.85 / 月。
每注册用户 (即便他们不用 AI): $170,000 / 5,000,000 = $0.034 / 用户 / 月。

这两个数字讲的是不同的故事。每活跃用户成本告诉你工程经济学。每注册用户成本告诉你商业经济学 (如果整个用户基数把 AI 当作免费功能享受)。

**PM 决策点**

- 我们今天的 CPUM 在哪里?我们什么时候会到哪里?
- 这套单位经济学在现在的商业模式下能不能持续?怎么持续?
- CPUM 的主要驱动因素是什么?该先优化哪一项?

**注意事项**

- 早期 CPUM 通常很高,因为固定成本还没摊薄。要看规模化之后的样子。
- 重度用户和轻度用户的均值会把信号平掉,90 分位的重度用户应该单独看是不是健康的。
- 有些用户花的钱比他们带来的价值多。整体经济学跑得通的话,这没问题。

**要点总结: CPUM 是 AI 项目的北极星指标。从第一天就追踪、优化、通过它的视角做架构决策。**

**Qantas 案例**

CPUM 是给 Qantas AI 项目的财务案例提供支撑的基础指标。设想一次真实的财务团队对话。

CFO 团队在评估 AI 投资。他们想知道: 一年花多少钱、能得到什么、什么时候回本。"它会处理 50% 的客服咨询" 这话有用,但不能直接拿去做财务决策。"它一年运营费 200 万澳元,处理 500 万咨询里 50% 的 Tier 1 量,每自动化一次咨询节省 4 澳元,一年产出 500 万澳元节省"——这才是能直接行动的。

CPUM 让第二种对话成为可能。如果年度成本 200 万澳元分摊到 20 万月活 AI 用户,就是每用户每月 1 澳元。如果每个用户每个月因为 AI 处理咨询而省下 20 澳元的运营时间,经济学显然是正的。这种简单数学让财务决策成为可能。

CPUM 也让规模化决策成为可能。如果用户基数增长 50% 会怎样?CPUM 应该相对稳定 (因为大部分成本是可变的),所以总成本增长 50%。如果它增长了 100%,系统里就有非线性的东西需要搞清楚。可能是 (a) 新进来的用户更重度 (每用户更多查询)、(b) 基础设施需要不成比例的升级、(c) 边际成本曲线在某个点突然加速。每种原因对应不同的修复。

CPUM 还让"按使用 pattern 做决策"成为可能。如果重度用户 (前 10%) 贡献 60% 的 CPUM,这是一个数据点。它可能意味着重度用户拿到了不成比例的价值,应该被鼓励 (他们看见了 AI 的价值)。也可能意味着重度用户在过度调 AI (查询不必要或者没优化好)。两种解读对应不同的行动。

CPUM 还让供应商谈判成为可能。Anthropic 涨价 10%,Qantas 该不该关心?CPUM 给精确答案: 从 1 澳元/月变成 1.07 澳元/月,成本增长 7%。鉴于业务价值,这个增长在容忍范围内吗?决策可以快速做出,不靠猜。

CPUM 还能撑起更大的对话——关于增长的对话。如果 Qantas 想把 AI 助手扩展到 1000 万用户、新工作流、新功能,CPUM 是第一件要建模的事情。如果 CPUM 在新工作流里涨到 5 澳元/月,经济学就不再成立——这件事要在规划阶段解决,而不是 8 个月之后被打个措手不及。

往大了说,这件事给 AI 交付的启示是: CPUM 不是一个工程数字,它是一个商业数字,让 AI 投资变得可治理。在需求确定的最早阶段就把它建出来的交付者,会让财务部门开心。他们让财务能清晰地思考 AI 投资,这反过来又能解锁更多 AI 投资。每一个"成本无法解释"的项目都伤害每一个未来项目。每一个有清晰 CPUM 故事的项目,都在为未来积累信任。
