**Definition**

Traditional project management tracks delivery health through burn down charts: how many tasks remain, how many hours of work, how the trend compares to the plan. In AI projects this is necessary but insufficient. AI projects can have a clean burn down while heading toward production failure, because the burn down measures activity, not outcome. A more useful approach is to track five specific health signals weekly, each of which surfaces a different category of risk that burn down misses.

**Why it matters in delivery**

The most expensive failures in AI delivery are not from running out of time, but from running through the time and arriving at something that does not work in production. A team can complete every planned task on time and still ship a system that fails users. Burn down would have shown green throughout. The five signals described here are designed to catch the kinds of problems that burn down does not see: quality drift, scope reality, dependency stalls, team confidence, and stakeholder alignment.

Each signal is a leading indicator, meaning it changes before the underlying problem becomes visible in conventional metrics. Tracking them weekly gives the team and operating sponsor early warning of issues that would otherwise surface late, when the cost to fix them is high.

**How it works**

The five signals are listed below, with what to measure, what changes mean, and what action to take when a signal is bad.

| Signal | What to measure | Good looks like | Bad looks like | Action when bad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eval trend | Pass rate on the agreed eval set, week over week | Steady upward trend, even if slow | Flat, declining, or volatile | Diagnose the category of failure, target the work |
| Scope reality | Number of items in scope, deferred, and in the parking lot | Stable or modest growth in parking lot | Rapid growth in scope, or churn between in scope and deferred | Convene a scope review, prune deliberately |
| Dependency status | Status of the top 3 to 5 external dependencies | All in progress or resolved | One or more blocked, with no movement | Escalate the specific dependency to the named owner |
| Team confidence | Subjective rating from each team member, 1 to 5 | Most at 3 or 4, no 1s sustained | Multiple 1s or 2s, or a downward trend | Investigate cause, address root issue not symptom |
| Stakeholder alignment | Whether sponsor and key stakeholders agree on status, scope, and next steps | Aligned, with documented agreement | Sponsor says one thing, stakeholders behave differently | Surface the misalignment, schedule a joint meeting |

Each signal can be captured in 10 minutes weekly. Together they take less than an hour. The leverage is high.

**The eval trend signal**

The eval trend is the most important single signal. It is the direct measure of whether the actual product is improving. The right way to track it: a single chart, updated weekly, showing eval score over time. Annotate the chart with major changes (model version, prompt iteration, eval set expansion) so trends can be interpreted.

A healthy eval trend climbs steadily, even if slowly. A flat trend means the team is busy but not improving. A declining trend usually means the eval set has been expanded faster than the model has been improved. A volatile trend means the team is making changes without controlling for what is being changed.

What is not useful: tracking eval score as a single number without trend, comparing to a target without showing trajectory, or only reporting the score in the steering committee where it is too late to act.

**The scope reality signal**

Scope reality tracks whether the project's scope is stable. Items in scope (will be delivered), deferred (v2 or v1.5), and in the parking lot (under discussion). The healthy state is roughly stable: a few items move between categories each week, mostly as items are decided.

What is unhealthy: rapid expansion of scope (signs of scope creep not being managed), rapid growth in the parking lot (signs the team is collecting ideas without resolving them), or items oscillating between in scope and deferred (signs of unresolved disagreement).

What to do when scope reality goes bad depends on the pattern. Rapid expansion calls for a scope review with the sponsor. Parking lot growth calls for a review session to either promote or retire items. Oscillation calls for surfacing the underlying disagreement.

**The dependency status signal**

The risk register (from 14.5) lists external dependencies. This signal asks: are the top dependencies moving? Each top dependency has an owner, an expected resolution date, and a current status. The signal is good if all are progressing. The signal is bad if one or more have been at the same status for two or more weeks.

Stalled dependencies are particularly dangerous because they are invisible. The team is doing other work, the dependency is "pending", and weeks pass before anyone notices. The weekly check forces attention on dependency status, which is half the work of resolving them.

**The team confidence signal**

Team confidence is a subjective measure but a valuable one. Each team member rates their confidence in the project on a 1 to 5 scale, weekly, in a short async survey or a one minute check at the standup. The numbers themselves matter less than the trend. A team that was at 4 last week and is at 2 this week is signalling something the PM needs to investigate.

The signal does not always reveal what the issue is. A drop in team confidence might be about technical concerns, about a difficult stakeholder, about a personal issue affecting one team member, or about something the team senses but cannot articulate. The PM's job when this signal goes bad is to investigate, not to optimise for higher numbers. Trying to make the number look good without addressing the underlying issue is one of the worst mistakes a PM can make.

**The stakeholder alignment signal**

Stakeholder alignment is whether the sponsor and the team's understanding of project status, scope, and next steps matches what other stakeholders believe. It is checked by listening: in the weekly check-in with the operating sponsor, does the sponsor describe the project the same way the team does? In conversations with users or blockers during the week, do they describe the project the same way?

When stakeholders describe the project differently, alignment has slipped. The team is still moving, but in a direction not all stakeholders endorse. This signal often goes bad before any other signal, and it catches issues that the others miss.

**Putting the five together**

The five signals do not produce a single number. They are a dashboard. In the weekly check-in with the operating sponsor, the PM walks through each, with the current state and any change from last week. Most weeks, most signals are good. When a signal goes bad, the conversation focuses there. When multiple signals go bad in the same week, the project is in genuine trouble and the conversation goes to the engagement partner.

**PM decision points**

- Have I set up the five signals at project start, or am I only tracking task completion?
- Am I capturing the signals weekly, or are they aspirational?
- When a signal goes bad, do I act on the cause, or do I optimise the metric?

**Watch outs**

- Do not let any single signal dominate. The eval trend is the most direct measure of product quality, but a project with a great eval trend and collapsing team confidence is in trouble.
- Subjective signals (team confidence, stakeholder alignment) require trust. If team members feel their honest answers will be punished, the numbers become useless.
- Reporting the signals to the steering committee can be useful but risky. The committee may try to manage the metrics rather than the underlying reality. Filter for the committee, but keep the full set internal.

**Takeaway: track five weekly delivery health signals beyond burn down: eval trend, scope reality, dependency status, team confidence, stakeholder alignment. Each is a leading indicator. Each takes minutes to capture. Together they surface the categories of failure that burn down misses.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine running a Qantas AI project, an internal tool for the customer experience team that classifies and routes incoming customer complaints. The project is 14 weeks. The team is six people. The PM sets up the five signals in week one.

Each Friday, the PM spends 30 minutes updating the dashboard. The eval trend is taken from the latest automated eval run. Scope reality is the count of items in scope, deferred, and in the parking lot, from the project's shared document. Dependency status is read from the risk register. Team confidence comes from a short Friday morning survey with one question. Stakeholder alignment is the PM's subjective rating based on the week's conversations.

The dashboard is shared with the operating sponsor in the weekly check-in. The conversation each week is short: the PM walks through the five signals in five minutes, the sponsor asks questions, decisions are made on anything that has gone bad.

In week three, the eval trend signal goes bad: the score has dropped from 71 percent to 67 percent. The team had expanded the eval set the previous week and the new examples were harder than the original set. The signal triggered investigation. The drop was real but expected, given the eval set expansion. The team and sponsor agree this is a healthy correction, and the team continues. Without the signal, the drop might have been missed or, worse, misinterpreted as regression.

In week six, the team confidence signal goes bad: two team members rate confidence at 2, one at 1. The PM investigates. The cause is not technical: the team is exhausted from a difficult workshop the previous week with a particularly aggressive operating team member. The PM addresses this directly with the operating sponsor, the workshop format is adjusted, and the next workshop is much smoother. Team confidence recovers within two weeks. Without the signal, the issue might have festered for another month and produced burnout.

In week nine, the stakeholder alignment signal goes bad: in conversation with a senior customer service manager, the manager describes the project as "automating complaint responses", whereas the team and sponsor have been describing it as "classifying and routing complaints to the right human responder". The misalignment is significant: one is about replacing humans, the other is about helping them. The PM raises this in the next steering committee, the misalignment is surfaced, and the sponsor takes responsibility for re-aligning the customer service leadership. Without the signal, this misalignment would have surfaced at launch, when the customer service team realised the system was not what they thought.

In week 12, the dependency status signal goes bad: the integration dependency on the CRM system has been at the same status for three weeks. The PM escalates the named owner directly. The owner has been struggling with competing priorities. With escalation through the engagement partner, the dependency is moved to the front of the queue and resolved in week 13. Without the signal, the dependency might have slipped past launch.

The launch in week 14 goes smoothly. None of the issues that arose during the project caused launch delay or production failure, because each one was caught by one of the signals while it was still cheap to fix.

The interesting comparison is with a different Qantas project that tracked only burn down. The burn down showed green throughout. The launch was delayed by eight weeks because of issues that the five signals would have caught: dependency stall not noticed until late, stakeholder misalignment not surfaced until launch readiness, team confidence drop not addressed until burnout was visible.

The lesson for AI delivery is that activity is not the right thing to measure. Outcome is. The five signals are different views of outcome: is the product getting better, is the scope under control, are the dependencies moving, is the team healthy, are the stakeholders aligned. Each takes minutes to track. Together they protect against the failure modes that burn down does not see.

**定义**

传统项目管理通过 burn down 图追踪交付健康: 还剩多少任务、多少工时、趋势怎么跟计划比。AI 项目里这个必要但不够。AI 项目可以有干净的 burn down 同时朝 production 失败前进, 因为 burn down 衡量活动, 不是结果。更有用的方式是每周追踪五个具体的健康信号, 每个浮出 burn down 错过的不同类别风险。

**为何在交付中重要**

AI 交付里最贵的失败不是来自时间耗尽, 而是来自时间耗完后到达一个在生产里不工作的东西。一个团队能按时完成每一个计划任务, 还交付一个对用户失败的系统。Burn down 全程会显示绿色。这里描述的五个信号被设计来抓 burn down 看不到的问题: 质量漂移、范围现实、依赖停滞、团队信心、stakeholder 对齐。

每个信号是先行指标, 意思是它在底层问题在常规指标里变得可见之前就变化。每周追踪它们, 给团队和运营 sponsor 早期警告 issue, 不然这些 issue 会晚浮出, 那时候修它们的成本高。

**运作原理**

下面列出五个信号, 带 "测什么、变化什么意思、信号差时采取什么行动"。

| 信号 | 测什么 | 好看起来像 | 差看起来像 | 差时的行动 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eval 趋势 | 商定 eval set 上的通过率, 周对周 | 稳定上升趋势, 即使慢 | 平、下降、或波动 | 诊断失败类别, 靶向工作 |
| 范围现实 | 范围内、推迟、parking lot 的项数 | 稳定或 parking lot 适度增长 | 范围快速增长, 或范围内和推迟之间的搅动 | 召集范围 review, 刻意修剪 |
| 依赖状态 | 前 3 到 5 个外部依赖的状态 | 全部进行中或解决 | 一个或多个被阻塞, 没动作 | 把具体依赖升级到具名 owner |
| 团队信心 | 每个团队成员的主观评分, 1 到 5 | 大多数 3 或 4, 没有持续的 1 | 多个 1 或 2, 或下降趋势 | 调查原因, 处理根问题不是症状 |
| Stakeholder 对齐 | Sponsor 和关键 stakeholder 是否同意 status、scope、下一步 | 对齐, 带文档化协议 | Sponsor 说一回事, stakeholder 行为不同 | 浮出错位, 安排联合会议 |

每个信号每周 10 分钟能捕捉。合起来用不到一小时。**杠杆高**。

**Eval 趋势信号**

Eval 趋势是最重要的单一信号。它是 "实际产品是否在改善" 的直接衡量。追踪的对的方式: 一张图, 每周更新, 显示 eval 分数随时间。用主要变化标注图 (模型版本、prompt 迭代、eval set 扩大), 这样趋势能被解读。

健康的 eval 趋势稳定上升, 即使慢。平的趋势意思是团队忙但没在改善。下降趋势通常意味着 eval set 被扩大得比模型被改善得快。波动趋势意思是团队在改东西但没控制改了什么。

**什么没用**: 把 eval 分数当一个数追踪、没显示轨迹就跟目标比较、只在 steering committee 报分数 (太晚没法行动)。

**范围现实信号**

范围现实追踪项目范围是否稳定。范围内的项 (会交付)、推迟的 (v2 或 v1.5)、parking lot 里的 (在讨论中)。健康状态是大致稳定: 每周几项在类别之间移动, 大多是因为项被决定。

**不健康**: 范围快速扩张 (没在管 scope creep 的迹象)、parking lot 快速增长 (团队在收集想法不解决它们)、项在 "范围内" 和 "推迟" 之间摇摆 (未解决分歧的迹象)。

范围现实变差时做什么取决于 pattern。快速扩张需要跟 sponsor 的范围 review。Parking lot 增长需要 review 会议来提升或退役项。摇摆需要浮出底层分歧。

**依赖状态信号**

风险登记表 (来自 14.5) 列外部依赖。**这个信号问**: 前几个依赖在动吗? 每个前依赖有 owner、预期解决日期、当前状态。信号好, 如果都在推进。信号差, 如果一个或多个保持同样状态两周以上。

停滞的依赖特别危险, 因为**它们不可见**。团队在做其他工作, 依赖 "pending", 几周过去没人注意。每周检查强制注意依赖状态, 这是解决它们一半的工作。

**团队信心信号**

团队信心是主观衡量但有价值。每个团队成员对项目的信心打 1 到 5 分, 每周, 在一个短的异步调查或 standup 上一分钟检查里。数字本身没比趋势重要。上周 4、这周 2 的团队在发信号, PM 需要调查。

信号不总揭示 issue 是什么。团队信心下降可能是关于技术关切、难搞 stakeholder、影响一位团队成员的个人 issue、或团队感知到但说不出来的东西。这个信号变差时 PM 的工作是调查, 不是优化更高数字。试图让数字看好但不处理底层 issue, 是 PM 能犯的最糟错误之一。

**Stakeholder 对齐信号**

Stakeholder 对齐是 sponsor 和团队对项目 status、scope、下一步的理解是否匹配其他 stakeholder 相信的。**通过倾听检查**: 跟运营 sponsor 的每周 check-in 里, sponsor 描述项目的方式跟团队一样吗? 本周跟用户或阻断者的对话里, 他们描述项目的方式一样吗?

当 stakeholder 不同地描述项目, 对齐滑了。团队还在动, 但朝一个不是所有 stakeholder 都认可的方向。这个信号经常在任何其他信号之前变差, 它抓住其他错过的 issue。

**把五个合起来**

五个信号不产出单一数字。它们是 dashboard。跟运营 sponsor 的每周 check-in 里, PM 走过每一个, 带当前状态和上周以来的任何变化。大多数周, 大多数信号都好。信号变差时, 对话聚焦那里。当同一周多个信号变差, 项目真的有麻烦, 对话上升到 engagement partner。

**PM 决策点**

- 我**有没有在项目开始时设置五个信号**, 还是**只在追踪任务完成**?
- 我**有没有每周捕捉信号**, 还是**它们是愿望**?
- 信号变差时, 我**对原因行动**, 还是**优化指标**?

**注意事项**

- **不要让任何单一信号支配**。**Eval 趋势是产品质量的最直接衡量, 但有好 eval 趋势同时团队信心崩溃的项目, 在麻烦中**。
- **主观信号 (团队信心、stakeholder 对齐) 需要信任**。**如果团队成员觉得诚实答案会被惩罚, 数字变得无用**。
- **把信号汇报给 steering committee 可能有用但有风险**。**委员会可能尝试管理指标而不是底层现实**。**为委员会过滤, 但保持完整集合内部**。

**要点总结: 追踪 burn down 之外的五个每周交付健康信号: eval 趋势、范围现实、依赖状态、团队信心、stakeholder 对齐。每个是先行指标。每个花几分钟捕捉。合起来浮出 burn down 错过的失败类别。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你跑一个 Qantas AI 项目, 给客户体验团队的内部工具, 分类和路由进来的客户投诉。项目 14 周。**团队六人**。PM 第 1 周设置五个信号。

每个周五, PM 花 30 分钟更新 dashboard。Eval 趋势从最新自动 eval 跑取。范围现实是从项目共享文档来的 "范围内、推迟、parking lot" 的项数。依赖状态从风险登记表读。团队信心来自周五早晨带一个问题的短调查。Stakeholder 对齐是 PM 基于本周对话的主观评分。

Dashboard 在每周 check-in 跟运营 sponsor 共享。**每周对话短**: PM 五分钟走过五个信号, sponsor 问问题, 对任何变差的做决定。

第 3 周, eval 趋势信号变差: 分数从 71% 降到 67%。团队上周扩大了 eval set, 新例子比原集合难。**信号触发调查**。降是真的但预期的, 鉴于 eval set 扩大。团队和 sponsor 同意这是健康的修正, 团队继续。没有信号, 降可能被错过, 或更糟, 被误解为回退。

第 6 周, 团队信心信号变差: 两位团队成员评信心 2, 一位 1。**PM 调查**。原因不是技术的: 团队从上周跟一位特别 aggressive 的运营团队成员的难搞 workshop 里精疲力尽。PM 直接跟运营 sponsor 处理这个, workshop 格式被调整, 下次 workshop 顺得多。团队信心两周内恢复。没有信号, issue 可能再发酵一个月, 产出 burnout。

第 9 周, stakeholder 对齐信号变差: 跟一位资深客服经理的对话里, 经理描述项目为 "自动化投诉回应", 而团队和 sponsor 一直描述它为 "把投诉分类和路由到对的人工回应者"。**错位显著**: 一个是关于替代人, 另一个是关于帮他们。PM 在下次 steering committee 提出, 错位被浮出, sponsor 承担责任重新对齐客服领导层。没有信号, 这个错位会在上线时浮出, 当客服团队意识到系统不是他们以为的。

第 12 周, 依赖状态信号变差: 对 CRM 系统的集成依赖保持同一状态三周。PM 直接升级具名 owner。Owner 一直在跟竞争性优先级搏斗。通过 engagement partner 升级, 依赖被移到队伍前面, 第 13 周解决。没有信号, 依赖可能滑过上线。

第 14 周上线顺利。项目期间出现的任何 issue 都没造成上线延迟或 production 失败, 因为**每个都被信号之一抓住, 在修它还便宜的时候**。

有意思的对照是只追踪 burn down 的另一个 Qantas 项目。Burn down 全程显示绿。上线被推迟八周, 因为五个信号会抓住的 issue: 依赖停滞晚才被注意、stakeholder 错位上线 readiness 才浮出、团队信心降在 burnout 可见之前没被处理。

AI 交付的启示: 活动不是对的衡量东西。**结果是**。五个信号是结果的不同视图: 产品在变好吗、范围在控制下吗、依赖在动吗、团队健康吗、stakeholder 对齐吗。每个花几分钟追踪。合起来防止 burn down 看不到的失败模式。
