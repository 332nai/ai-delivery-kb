# Telling the client what won't make v1: the scope cut conversation

A scope cut is the **most underestimated** kind of bad news in AI projects.

Delay, missing eval, PoC failure — clients carry some expectation of these ("AI projects have risk"). A scope cut is different. The client bought a "full solution" from day one. When you tell them "this, this, and this won't make v1", their first reaction is **"so why did I sign this project in the first place"**.

This piece isn't about preventing scope creep (T13) or writing the v1 will-not-do (T12.5). It's about: **when you have to cut scope, how to deliver it**.

A few decision points below.

---

## Decision 1 — Scope cut vs delay: which to choose

A lot of PMs treat "cut scope" as a substitute for "delay" — can't hit the date, drop some features to save it. Sometimes that's right. Sometimes it's a disaster.

**Scope cut beats delay when**:

- The features being cut are nice-to-haves and don't affect core value
- Those features can come back in v2, technically independent, no architecture rework
- The sponsor is extremely time-sensitive (e.g. they've publicly committed to a launch date)
- After cutting, the core can be fully tested, no production issues

**Delay beats scope cut when**:

- The features in question are sponsor anchors — cutting kills the narrative for them
- The "cut" portion is actually part of the core — cutting it leaves the product incoherent
- After cutting, v1 has no clean demo or business value story
- The sponsor's budget approval is based on full scope and can't be justified otherwise
- The feature list has been communicated to end users; cutting will hurt adoption

**The genuinely worst choice**: **cut and delay at the same time**. PMs slide into this when they originally tried to cut to save timeline, found they still couldn't make it, and end up with "half-cut + half-delayed". The sponsor loses confidence in the project completely. **If you see yourself drifting toward "cut + delay", stop. Re-align with your PL, then have an open conversation with the sponsor to reset both scope and timeline.**

---

## Decision 2 — What to cut, in what order

Not every feature can be cut. Before deciding what, sort all v1 features into 4 categories:

**Category A — non-negotiable core**

Features without which the project doesn't make sense. E.g. "answer customer questions" in a RAG project — cut that and the project has no point. **Never cut these.**

**Category B — high-visibility / sponsor anchor**

Features the sponsor mentally treats as the core reason for signing. Cutting one of these is fatal to their narrative. **Don't cut if you can avoid it. If you must, 1:1 align with the sponsor first.**

**Category C — useful but replaceable**

Has a manual workaround, or another feature covers the use case. E.g. "AI auto-generates monthly report" can temporarily be user-triggered. **The ideal candidate for v1 cuts.**

**Category D — edge / future-proofing**

Built for some hypothetical future scenario. E.g. multi-language support, when all current users are English. **The most cuttable, and the sponsor usually doesn't mind.**

**Tactical advice**

- Ideally a single round of scope cuts touches only D and C — **don't touch B**
- If you must touch B, don't call it a "scope cut", call it "scope phasing" — framing as "v1 → v1.5 → v2" feels psychologically very different to the sponsor
- **Always list 6 to 8 candidates and decide jointly with the sponsor**, never walk in with a "final list of what's being cut"
- When you cut, give a v2 timeline or trigger condition (e.g. "v2 launches after [date], triggered by [specific metric]") — this gives the sponsor a forward picture

---

## Decision 3 — Opening with the sponsor (first 5 minutes)

A scope cut conversation usually runs 45 to 60 minutes. The opener has to establish three things: this is necessary, this isn't panic, there's a forward narrative.

**The worst opener**

> "[Name], we need to cut some scope to make the launch date."

What's wrong: it describes the cut as "sacrificing for timeline" — the sponsor immediately enters zero-sum thinking (what am I losing), and goes defensive.

**The right opener**

> "[Name], 45 minutes please. I want to walk through some adjustments I'm recommending to v1 scope, and why I think these adjustments actually make v1 stronger.

> Framework first, then specifics, then v2 forward path — so what you're seeing isn't 'what got cut', it's 'how do we deliver v1 better + how does v2 work'.

> What I've prepared isn't a final decision, it's 6 candidates. Today we decide together what to cut, what to keep, what to phase into v2."

What this opener does:

- No "cut" — "adjustments" and "phasing"
- "Actually make v1 stronger" — immediately reframes this as not-a-loss
- "Better v1 + v2 plan" — gives the sponsor a forward picture
- "6 candidates, decide together" — sponsor is decision maker, not recipient

---

## Decision 4 — Explaining "why we need to adjust" — not "we can't finish"

The most common mistake: framing the cut as "engineering can't finish" or "we underestimated". Hear that, and the sponsor will immediately ask "so why not add people / add time".

**The right framing — three reasons, in order**:

**Reason 1 — quality bar has risen**

> "Over the course of building v1, we've gotten a clearer read on what 'good enough' actually means.

> Specifically: current scope has [N] features. If we ship all of them, average per-feature reliability is around 75 to 80%. If we phase some to v2 and focus on the core [M] features at 90%+, overall user-perceived quality is much higher.

> This is a trade-off we didn't fully anticipate at PoC stage — feature breadth vs reliability depth. I'd strongly recommend prioritising depth."

**Reason 2 — data / dependency reality**

> "As we've built, we've found that [some features] depend on data / systems / processes that aren't fully ready on [the client's] side. Specifically: [one or two concrete examples].

> Not a blocker — but to force these into v1, we'd spend 30 to 40% of effort working around data quality / system integration, work that wouldn't be needed in v2 (by which time [the relevant prerequisite] will be ready).

> I'd recommend phasing these features into v2, rather than forcing them into v1."

**Reason 3 — post-launch operability**

> "Third reason is what happens after launch. v1 has a 30-day ward rounds period (monitoring + tuning). Lots of features means a large surface area, and when issues come up, root cause is harder to isolate.

> Lean v1 → stabilise quickly after launch → confident to add v1.5 features within 6 to 8 weeks → this is the standard playbook for mature AI projects in industry."

**Key: none of the three reasons is "we can't finish".** All three are "a strategic choice for the project's success". This framing shifts the sponsor from "what am I losing" to "the PM is protecting v1 quality".

---

## Decision 5 — Walking 6 candidates (middle 15 to 20 minutes)

Don't read out a list of "what's been decided to cut". Read out a candidate list, and prioritise together.

**For each candidate**:

> "**Candidate 1: auto weekly report (Category C/D)**
>
> Current scope: Monday morning auto-generated report for managers.
> Recommendation: phase to v2.
> Rationale: This feature is hard with current LLM capability (needs synthesis from multiple sources); users can manage with manual report in the meantime.
> v2 timing: kicks off 2 months after v1 launch, 2 months development, so roughly v1 launch + 4 months.
> v1 workaround: keep existing manual process; we provide an export tool that lets managers generate it in 5 minutes."

Each candidate should carry:
- Current scope (what was it)
- Recommendation (cut / phase / partial)
- Rationale (why this one is OK to cut)
- v2 timing (cut isn't gone, it's phased)
- Workaround (how the user copes after the cut)

**Candidates the sponsor will react strongly to (there will be 1 or 2)**

You can anticipate the sponsor will push back on candidate 3 (say, a feature they care about). **Don't defend — raise it proactively yourself**:

> "I want to flag candidate 3 — I'm guessing you have some attachment to this feature, because it was one of the use cases you emphasised when we scoped the project.

> I've listed it as a candidate not because I've decided to cut, but because I think we need to look at the implementation effort vs actual user value ratio together — and let you decide whether to keep it.

> If you strongly want to keep it, we keep it — but it means candidates 5 and 6 have to be cut; we can't keep everything."

**Key: proactively acknowledge the sponsor's attachment + give them a trade-off instead of a yes/no.** Once the sponsor sees the trade-off forced on them, the chance of a rational decision goes up sharply.

---

## Decision 6 — v2 forward path (last 10 to 15 minutes)

The real closer of a scope cut conversation is the v2 plan. **Without a v2 plan, it's just "loss". With a v2 plan, it's "phasing".**

**The v2 plan needs three things**:

1. **When does v2 start** — not vague "after launch", but specific "v1 launch + 8 weeks to start v2 scoping, launch + 12 weeks to start v2 development"
2. **What's in v2** — take the candidate list from today, tag each as "v2 phase 1", "v2 phase 2", or "indefinitely deferred"
3. **v2 trigger conditions** — what has to be true for v2 to start. E.g. "v1 reaches [adoption metric] + [accuracy metric]; 80% user feedback positive; v2 budget approved"

**Sample script**:

> "Now to v2. What I'd recommend: 4 weeks after v1 launch we run a retrospective based on real user data. After the retro, 2 weeks of v2 scoping (using today's candidate list as starting point), then 6 weeks of development.

> v2 launch target is roughly v1 launch + 4 months. That gives you a firm date you can communicate upward.

> v2 start triggers I'd suggest three: one, at least 70% of target users using v1 (adoption); two, user satisfaction ≥ 4/5; three, you and I (PM) jointly agree v1 is stable enough. All three need to be met before v2 starts.

> I'll write up this v2 plan as a formal document by end of June for you to review, as the follow-up to today's conversation."

**Trigger conditions are the key design.** They do two things: one, the sponsor is reassured (v2 won't be indefinitely deferred); two, you're protected (being pushed into v2 before v1 is stable is the disaster scenario — triggers give you the basis to push back).

---

## Decision 7 — Common sponsor reactions

**Reaction A: "Why not add people / add budget and do all features?"**

> "Fair question. I did evaluate adding people — v1 has 8 weeks left, a new engineer needs at least 2 weeks to onboard, so they only have 6 weeks of productive time, and they'll disrupt the current team's rhythm (I have to spend time onboarding them).

> Even with 2 added people, we can probably deliver 1 more candidate's worth of feature, not 6. The remaining 5 still need to be cut or phased.

> Also — Brooks's law, which you've probably heard: 'adding people to a late project makes it later'. AI projects especially, because evaluation and prompt engineering are highly contextual; new people struggle to contribute fast.

> My strong recommendation is to keep the current team and phase some to v2, rather than add people to push everything."

**Reaction B: "Can you think again — is there any magic to ship everything?"**

The sponsor is hoping you've held back an ace card. **Directly but gently say no**:

> "I wish I had one — but I've been over every possibility. Conclusion: there's no magic. We either phase, delay, or lower the quality bar. Phasing is the most favourable to the project and you.

> I want to hear your gut — is it phase that's uncomfortable, or delay / quality? We'll decide together which trade-off is most acceptable."

Pass the ball back. Let the sponsor express a preference, rather than repeatedly pushing you for a magic answer.

**Reaction C: "Will v2 actually happen, or once we cut is it gone forever?"**

The most common and most genuine fear. **Address it directly**:

> "Fair concern. In industry, many v2s genuinely don't happen — usually because v1 wasn't stable, didn't demonstrate value, or budget didn't continue.

> We handle this with trigger conditions. The three triggers I mentioned (adoption + satisfaction + jointly agreed stable) are the mechanism ensuring v2 happens. If v1 hits these three, your finance team and IT team have no reason not to approve v2. If v1 doesn't hit them, then v2 not happening is also reasonable — because v1 itself still has problems and adding v2 features doesn't help.

> Bottom line: triggers make v2 depend on whether v1 actually worked, not on sponsor goodwill. That's safer for both of us."

---

## A short do / don't

**Do**

- Decide cut vs delay first, don't do both at once
- Sort all features into A/B/C/D, prioritise cuts from C and D
- Use "phasing" with the sponsor, not "cutting"
- List candidates (not a final list) and prioritise together
- Three reasons: quality depth, data reality, post-launch operability — none of them "we can't finish"
- Proactively surface the candidates the sponsor anchors to
- v2 plan three things: start time + scope + trigger conditions
- Trigger conditions are the core design (protect v1 and protect v2)
- Handle "why not add people" with Brooks's Law + specific numbers

**Don't**

- Don't cut and delay at the same time (sponsor's confidence collapses)
- Don't go into a scope cut conversation without a v2 plan
- Don't cut Category A; only touch B after 1:1 align
- Don't bring a "decided to cut" final list — bring candidates
- Don't frame the cut as "we can't finish"
- Don't defend against the sponsor's attachment — surface it yourself
- Don't be vague with "v2 sometime later" — give a specific date and triggers
- Don't surface a scope cut for the first time in a group meeting — always 1:1 first

---

---

Stepping back: a scope cut is the most common and most underestimated thing a PM does mid-flight.

A weak PM treats it as a sign of failure — they hide it, minimise it, drag it out until they have to raise it. Clients see through this and never trust them again.

A medium-strength PM knows to raise early, be transparent, explain the why. But when they explain, they only cover "why we're cutting", and forget "how we're moving forward". The sponsor agrees, but a "is this project failing" worry lingers.

A senior PM frames the cut as **"scope phasing"** — not failure, but strategic sequencing. They bring a candidate list, not a final list. They bring a v2 plan, not just a cut. They bring trigger conditions, not vague promises. The sponsor walks out feeling not deflated, but **"we just made this project better"**.

The PMs who deliver scope cut conversations well end up in a special category in the client's mind — this person isn't just shipping features, they're protecting my project from itself. That kind of reputation is gold, and especially rare in AI delivery.

# 砍范围:跟客户讲哪些 feature v1 上不了

砍范围是 AI 项目里**最被低估难度**的一种坏消息。

延期、评测不达标、PoC 失败,这三种坏消息客户心里多少有预期 ("AI 项目本来就有风险")。砍范围不一样 — 客户从一开始就买了一个"整套方案",你现在跟他讲"这一块、那一块、还有那一块 v1 不做",他第一反应是**"那我当初为什么签这个项目"**。

这一节不讲怎么避免范围蔓延 (T13 讲)、也不讲 v1 不做清单怎么写 (T12.5 讲),这一节讲: **当不得不砍范围的时候,怎么跟客户讲**。

下面拆几个决策点。

---

## 决策点 1 — 砍范围对延期,哪个该选

不少项目经理把"砍范围"当成"延期"的替代品 — 不能按时上,就砍一些功能保时间线。这有时候对,有时候是灾难。

**砍范围比延期好的情况**:

- 砍掉的功能是锦上添花,不影响核心价值交付
- 砍掉的功能在 v2 加回来,技术上独立、不会触发架构重做
- 主办人对时间线极度敏感 (比如对外承诺过某个上线日期)
- 砍掉之后核心部分能完整测试,不会出生产环境问题

**延期比砍范围好的情况**:

- 要砍的功能是主办人心里签项目时的核心功能 — 砍了主办人觉得整个项目失去意义
- "砍"的部分其实是核心的一部分,砍了产品不连贯
- 砍掉之后 v1 没有清晰的演示 / 业务价值故事
- 主办人的预算批准是基于完整范围的,砍了之后他没法解释
- 已经跟最终用户沟通过功能列表,砍了影响采用率

**真正最差的选择**: **既砍范围又延期**。这种项目经理一开始想砍保时间线,后来发现砍了也来不及,最后变成"砍了一半 + 延了一半"。这种主办人听完会直接对项目失去信心。**如果你看到自己滑向"砍 + 延",立刻停,跟项目负责人重新对齐,然后跟主办人开放对话重新设定范围 + 时间线**。

---

## 决策点 2 — 砍什么、怎么排序

不是所有功能都能砍。砍什么之前先把所有 v1 功能分成 4 类:

**A 类 — 不可谈判的核心**

砍了项目就不成立的功能。比如 RAG 项目里"能回答客户问题"这一项,砍了项目没意义。这一类**永远不能砍**。

**B 类 — 高曝光度 / 主办人心理锚点**

主办人心里把"这个功能能做"当成项目签约的核心理由。砍这一类对主办人的叙事是致命的。**能不砍就不砍,要砍必须先一对一跟主办人对齐**。

**C 类 — 有用但可替代**

有人工替代办法或者其他功能能覆盖部分场景。比如"AI 自动生成月度报告",可以暂时让用户自己触发生成。**这一类是 v1 砍最理想的候选**。

**D 类 — 边缘 / 为未来准备**

为了未来某些假设场景做的功能。比如"多语言支持"但当前只有英文用户。**这一类最该砍,而且主办人通常不在乎**。

**战术建议**

- 一次砍范围最好只砍 D 和 C,**不要碰 B**
- 如果你必须砍 B,就不要叫"砍范围",叫"范围分阶段" — 表述成"v1 → v1.5 → v2",主办人心理感受完全不一样
- **永远先列 6 到 8 个候选,跟主办人一起讨论哪些砍**,不要拿一个"我决定砍这些"的最终列表去找主办人
- 砍的同时给一个 v2 时间线或者触发条件 (比如"v2 在 [日期] 之后启动,触发是 [具体指标]") — 让主办人心理有前进路径

---

## 决策点 3 — 跟主办人讲的开场 (前 5 分钟)

砍范围的对话通常 45 到 60 分钟。开场要确立三件事: 这是必要的、不是慌乱、有前进叙事。

**最差的开场**

> "[姓名],我们要砍一些范围才能按时上线。"

错在哪: 把砍范围描述成"为了时间线牺牲" — 主办人立刻陷入零和思维 (我失去了什么),防御模式开启。

**对的开场**

> "[姓名],占用 45 分钟。我想跟你逐项过一遍 v1 范围我建议的几个调整,以及为什么我觉得这些调整反而让 v1 更扎实。

> 我先讲框架,再讲具体调整,然后讲 v2 前进路径 — 这样你看到的不是'砍了什么',是'我们怎么把 v1 交付得更好 + v2 怎么规划'。

> 我准备的不是最终决定,是 6 个候选,我们今天一起决定哪些砍、哪些保留、哪些分阶段到 v2。"

这个开场做了几件事:

- 没用"砍"或"切" — 用"调整"和"分阶段"
- "反而让 v1 更扎实" — 立刻重新定调这不是损失
- "v1 更好 + v2 计划" — 给主办人一个前进画面
- "6 个候选一起决定" — 主办人是决策者,不是被通知

---

## 决策点 4 — 讲"为什么需要调整" — 不要用"我们做不完"当理由

最容易犯的错: 把砍范围的理由讲成"工程师做不完"或者"我们低估了"。这种讲法主办人听完会立刻问"那为什么不加人 / 加时间"。

**对的讲法 — 三个理由,按顺序讲**:

**第一个理由 — 质量标准在变高**

> "我们在做 v1 的过程中,对什么算'够好'的质量标准有了更清晰的判断。

> 具体讲: 当前范围里有 [N] 个功能,如果全部上,平均每个功能的可靠度大概在 75% 到 80% 范围。如果我们分阶段一部分到 v2,集中精力把核心 [M] 个功能做到 90% 以上,整体用户感知质量会高得多。

> 这是一个我们 PoC 阶段没有完全预见到的取舍 — 功能广度对可靠度深度。我现在强烈建议优先深度。"

**第二个理由 — 数据 / 依赖现实**

> "做的过程中我们发现,[某些功能] 依赖的数据 / 系统 / 流程,在 [客户公司] 当前的状态下不完全就绪。具体讲: [一两个具体例子]。

> 这不是阻塞 — 但要在 v1 强行推,我们要花 30% 到 40% 的投入在绕过数据质量 / 系统集成上,而这些绕过办法在 v2 不需要 (因为那时候 [具体的前提] 会就绪)。

> 我建议把这一类功能分阶段到 v2,而不是在 v1 强行做。"

**第三个理由 — 上线后可运营性**

> "第三个理由是上线后。我们 v1 上线之后会有 30 天的巡房期 (监控 + 调优)。功能多意味着这 30 天的覆盖面大,有问题时根本原因难定位。

> 精简 v1 → 上线之后能快速稳定 → 6 到 8 周内有信心加 v1.5 功能 → 这是行业上成熟 AI 项目的标准做法。"

**关键: 三个理由都不是"我们做不完"**。三个理由都是"为了项目更成功的策略选择"。这种定调让主办人从"我失去了什么"转到"项目经理在保护 v1 质量"。

---

## 决策点 5 — 讲 6 个候选 (中间 15 到 20 分钟)

不要列一个"已经决定砍的"列表,列一个"候选"列表,主办人跟你一起排优先级。

**每个候选这样讲**:

> "**候选 1: 自动周报功能 (C / D 类)**
>
> 当前范围: 每周一自动生成给经理的周报。
> 建议: 分阶段到 v2。
> 理由: 这个功能用 LLM 难度高 (需要从多个来源综合),用户当前用手动报告也能管理。
> v2 时机: v1 上线 2 个月后启动,2 个月开发,大概 v1 上线 + 4 个月。
> v1 期间替代办法: 保留现有手动流程,我们提供导出工具帮经理 5 分钟生成。"

每个候选都要带:
- 当前范围 (它本来是什么)
- 建议 (砍 / 分阶段 / 部分)
- 理由 (为什么这个砍可以)
- v2 时机 (砍了不是没了,是分阶段)
- 替代办法 (砍了之后用户怎么办)

**主办人会有强反应的候选 (一定会有 1 到 2 个),怎么应对**

预想到主办人会对候选 3 (假设是某个他看重的功能) 强反应。**不要辩护,主动提出来**:

> "我想提请注意候选 3 — 我猜你对这个功能会有比较强的执着,因为这是当初讨论项目时你强调的几个场景之一。

> 我把它列在候选里,不是因为我决定砍,是因为我觉得我们需要一起看一下: 这个功能的当前实现工作量跟用户实际价值之间的比例,看完之后你来决定要不要保留。

> 如果你强烈想保留,我们保留 — 但意味着候选 5 和 6 必须砍,不能保留全部。"

**关键: 主动承认主办人的执着 + 给取舍而不是是 / 否**。主办人一旦看到必须取舍,他理性决策的概率大幅提升。

---

## 决策点 6 — v2 前进路径 (最后 10 到 15 分钟)

砍范围对话的真正收尾是 v2 计划。**没有 v2 计划就只是"损失",有 v2 计划就是"分阶段"**。

**v2 计划要讲三件事**:

1. **什么时候启动 v2** — 不是模糊的"上线后再说",是具体的"v1 上线 + 8 周后启动 v2 定范围,上线 + 12 周后启动 v2 开发"
2. **v2 包含什么** — 拿出今天砍的候选列表,逐一标"v2 阶段 1""v2 阶段 2""无限期推迟"
3. **v2 触发条件** — v2 启动的前提是什么。例如"v1 达到 [采用率指标] + [准确率指标];80% 用户反馈正向;v2 预算获批"

**示例脚本**:

> "现在讲 v2。我建议这样: v1 上线之后 4 周做复盘,基于真实用户数据。复盘之后 2 周做 v2 定范围 (用我们今天的候选列表当起点),然后 6 周做开发。

> v2 上线目标大概在 v1 上线 + 4 个月。这给你一个确定日期可以传达给上面。

> v2 启动触发我建议设三个: 一,v1 至少 70% 的目标用户在用 (采用率);二,用户满意度 ≥ 4 / 5;三,你和我 (项目经理) 共同同意 v1 已经足够稳定。三个都满足才启动 v2。

> 这个 v2 计划我 6 月底前会写成正式文档给你评审,作为我们今天对话的跟进。"

**触发条件是关键设计**。它做两件事: 一,让主办人安心 (v2 不会无限期推);二,让你保护自己 (v1 还没稳定就被推 v2 是灾难情景,触发条件给你拒绝的依据)。

---

## 决策点 7 — 主办人几种典型反应

**反应 A: "为什么不加人 / 加预算,所有功能都做"**

> "这是一个公平的问题。我评估过加人 — 现在 v1 还有 8 周,新加的工程师起码 2 周上手,实际只有 6 周高产,而且会打乱当前团队的节奏 (我要花时间引入)。

> 即使加 2 个人,我们能多交付的功能大概是 1 个候选,不是 6 个。剩下 5 个还是要砍或分阶段。

> 而且 — 布鲁克斯定律,这一句你可能听过: '给一个晚的项目加人会让它更晚'。AI 项目尤其如此,因为评估和 prompt 工程是高度依赖上下文的,新人很难快速贡献。

> 我的强推荐是保持当前团队,分阶段一部分到 v2,而不是加人推全部。"

**反应 B: "你能不能再想想,有没有奇招让所有功能都上"**

主办人心里在希望你藏了一个王牌。**直接但温和地讲没有**:

> "我希望我有 — 但我把所有可能性都过了一遍。结论是没有奇招。我们要么分阶段,要么延期,要么降低质量标准。分阶段是我评估对项目和你都最有利的。

> 我想听你的直觉 — 是分阶段让你不舒服,还是延期 / 质量让你不舒服?我们一起决定哪个取舍最能接受。"

把球扔回去,让主办人表达偏好,而不是反复推你给奇招答案。

**反应 C: "v2 真的会发生吗,还是说一旦砍就再也不做了"**

最常见也最真实的恐惧。**直接回应**:

> "合理的担忧。行业上很多 v2 确实就没发生 — 通常因为 v1 没稳定 / 没展示价值 / 预算没续。

> 我们用触发条件处理这个: 我刚说的三个触发 (采用率 + 满意度 + 共同同意稳定) 是确保 v2 一定发生的机制。如果 v1 达到这三个触发,你的财务团队和 IT 团队没有理由不批 v2;如果 v1 达不到三个触发,那 v2 不发生也是合理的 — 因为那时候 v1 本身还有问题,加 v2 功能没意义。

> 底线: 触发条件让 v2 不取决于主办人善意,取决于 v1 是不是真的有效。这个对你和我都更安全。"

---

## 一些"该做 / 不该做"速记

**该做**

- 砍对延先决定,不要同时砍同时延
- 把所有功能分 A / B / C / D 4 类,优先砍 C 和 D
- 跟主办人用"分阶段"不用"砍"
- 列候选 (不是最终列表) 跟主办人一起排优先级
- 三个理由: 质量深度、数据现实、上线后可运营 — 都不是"做不完"
- 主动提出主办人心理锚点的候选
- v2 计划三件事: 启动时间 + 范围 + 触发条件
- 触发条件是核心设计 (保护 v1 也保护 v2)
- 应对"为什么不加人"用布鲁克斯定律 + 具体数字

**不该做**

- 不要既砍又延 (主办人信心崩盘)
- 不要不带 v2 计划去开砍范围对话
- 不要砍 A 类,要一对一对齐后才碰 B 类
- 不要列"已经决定砍"的最终列表 — 用候选列表
- 不要把砍讲成"做不完"
- 不要为主办人的执着辩护 — 主动提出来
- 不要模糊"v2 之后再说" — 给具体日期和触发条件
- 不要在大会上第一次提砍范围 — 永远先一对一

## 话术速查:15 条砍范围直接抄走用的句子

### 框架

**1. 砍对延对同时砍延的判断**
> 砍范围比延期好: 砍的功能是锦上添花 / 不影响核心 / v2 能独立加 / 主办人时间线敏感。延期比砍好: 砍的是主办人锚点 / 砍了产品不连贯 / 砍了没演示故事 / 预算批次是完整范围。最差选择: 同时砍同时延,立刻停重置。

**2. 功能 4 分类**
> A — 不可谈判的核心 (永远不砍)。B — 主办人锚点 (一对一对齐才碰)。C — 有用但可替代 (v1 砍最理想候选)。D — 边缘 / 为未来准备 (最该砍)。

### 开场

**3. 45 分钟标准开场**
> "占用 45 分钟。想逐项过 v1 范围我建议的几个调整,以及为什么这些调整反而让 v1 更扎实。先框架,再具体,然后 v2 路径。你看到的不是'砍了什么',是'怎么把 v1 交付更好 + v2 怎么规划'。我准备 6 个候选,我们今天一起决定。"

### 讲为什么 (三个理由)

**4. 理由 1 — 质量深度**
> "做 v1 过程中我们对'够好'的标准有了更清晰判断。全上平均可靠度 75% 到 80%。分阶段一部分到 v2,集中精力把核心 M 个做到 90% 以上,整体用户感知质量更高。这是 PoC 阶段我们没完全预见到的取舍 — 功能广度对可靠度深度。"

**5. 理由 2 — 数据 / 依赖现实**
> "[某些功能] 依赖的数据 / 系统 / 流程在 [客户公司] 当前状态下不就绪。强推 v1 我们 30% 到 40% 投入会花在绕过办法,而这些绕过办法在 v2 不需要 (那时候 [前提] 会就绪)。建议分阶段到 v2 而不是 v1 强行做。"

**6. 理由 3 — 上线后可运营**
> "功能多意味着上线 30 天巡房期覆盖面大,出问题时根本原因难定位。精简 v1 → 快速稳定 → 6 到 8 周内有信心加 v1.5 功能 → 这是行业成熟 AI 项目标准做法。"

### 候选模板

**7. 每个候选 5 件事**
> 当前范围 (本来是什么) + 建议 (砍 / 分阶段 / 部分) + 理由 (为什么可以) + v2 时机 (砍不是没了是分阶段) + 替代办法 (砍后用户怎么办)。

**8. 主动提出主办人锚点候选**
> "我想提请注意候选 3 — 我猜你会有比较强的执着,这是当初讨论项目时你强调的几个场景之一。列在候选里不是我决定砍,是觉得需要一起看实现工作量对价值的比例,看完你决定。如果强烈保留就保留,但意味着候选 5 和 6 必须砍。"

### v2 计划

**9. v2 三件事**
> 启动时间: "v1 上线 + 4 周复盘,+ 6 周定范围,+ 12 周开发,v2 上线在 v1 上线 + 4 个月。"
> 包含什么: 今天砍的候选列表标"v2 阶段 1 / 阶段 2 / 无限期推迟"。
> 触发条件: 三个 (采用率 / 满意度 / 共同同意稳定),三个都满足才启动 v2。

**10. 触发条件表述**
> "触发让 v2 不取决于主办人善意,取决于 v1 是不是真的有效。达到触发,你的财务和 IT 没理由不批 v2;达不到,那 v2 不发生也合理 — v1 本身有问题。底线: 对你和我都更安全。"

### 应对反应

**11. "为什么不加人 / 加预算"**
> "评估过。新人 2 周上手,8 周里只有 6 周高产,而且打乱当前团队节奏 (我花时间引入)。加 2 人能多交付 1 个候选,不是 6 个。布鲁克斯定律 — 给一个晚的项目加人让它更晚 — AI 尤其,评估和 prompt 高度依赖上下文,新人难快速贡献。我强推荐保持当前团队 + 分阶段。"

**12. "你能不能再想想奇招让所有都上"**
> "我希望我有,但都过了一遍,没奇招。要么分阶段,要么延,要么降质量。分阶段是对项目和你都最有利的。听你直觉 — 分阶段让你不舒服,还是延期 / 质量让你不舒服?我们一起决定哪个取舍最能接受。"

**13. "v2 真的会发生吗"**
> "合理的担忧。行业上很多 v2 没发生,通常因为 v1 没稳定 / 没展示价值 / 预算没续。我们用触发条件处理: 三个触发满足才启动 v2。达到触发你财务和 IT 没理由不批;达不到 v2 不发生也合理。触发让 v2 不取决于善意,取决于 v1 是不是有效。"

### 内部 / 后续

**14. 跟你的项目负责人 / 直属老板**
> "[姓名],明天跟主办人谈范围分阶段,不是时间线。准备了 6 个候选,推荐砍 D 类 3 个 + 分阶段 C 类 2 个 + 主动提出 B 类 1 个让主办人自己决定。v2 计划带触发条件。主办人推回概率最大: 加人这一题。先同步,讲完回来复盘。"

**15. 对话之后的跟进邮件**
> "[姓名],谢谢今天 45 分钟。我们对齐的几件事: v1 范围分阶段掉 [X / Y / Z];保留 [A / B / C];候选 3 你最终决定保留,意味着 v1 时间线不变但候选 5 也要砍。v2 计划我会本周写成正式文档,带触发条件,周五前发给你评审。下一步 [具体下一步]。"

---

往大了说,砍范围是项目经理在项目中途最常见但也最被低估的一件事。

低水平的项目经理看砍范围是"失败的标志" — 他试图藏 / 轻描淡写 / 拖到不能再拖才讲。这种项目经理客户看穿之后就再也不信任了。

中等水平的项目经理知道要早讲、要透明、要给原因。但他讲的时候只描述"为什么砍",忘了讲"怎么前进"。主办人听完同意了,但心里萦绕一个"这个项目是不是失败了"的感觉。

高水平的项目经理把砍范围讲成**"范围分阶段"** — 不是失败,是策略性排序。他带着候选列表而不是最终列表,带着 v2 计划而不是只砍,带着触发条件而不是模糊承诺。主办人走出会议室不是失落,是**"我们刚刚让项目变得更好了"**。

砍范围讲得最好的项目经理,客户会在心里把他放到一个特殊的位置 — 这个人不只是交付功能,他在保护我的项目不被自己拖垮。这种声誉是金子,在 AI 这一行尤其稀缺。
