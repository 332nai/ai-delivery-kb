# Telling the client costs are running over: the budget conversation

A cost overrun is the **most financially sensitive** kind of bad news in an AI project.

Delays, eval misses, PoC failure — the sponsor can absorb these internally; they're project-internal matters. A cost overrun is different. Once over budget, the sponsor has to go to their CFO or finance team for more money, which means the bad news escalates to a level they don't control. From the sponsor's perspective, **a cost overrun is a political problem, not just a project problem**.

This piece assumes you've already done cost-side fundamentals (T9.5 estimating CPUM, T9.3 caching, T9.4 batch vs real-time). This piece is about: **when budget inevitably overruns, how to deliver it**.

A few decision points below.

---

## Decision 1 — Sort which of 4 types of overrun this is

Different overrun types have **different root causes and different framings**. Walking in without sorting first, the sponsor will only remember "we went over", not why.

**Type 1: Volume overrun — usage exceeds expectation**

Production launches, user adoption is higher than planned, token consumption exceeds budget. This is the **best kind of overrun** — because it implies success.

Framing: present it as a "positive problem".

**Type 2: Unit cost stable, but per-task token count exceeds estimate**

Each task consumes more tokens than estimated at PoC stage. E.g. context is longer than expected, or reasoning chain is longer than expected. This is the **most technical kind of overrun** — doesn't mean failure, but means your estimate was off.

Framing: present it as "estimation refinement", with data.

**Type 3: Hidden cost blowout — people / infra / third-party SaaS**

T19.1 mentioned that the most underestimated costs in AI projects are people, infra, and compliance. When these overrun, it's usually discovered mid-flight (e.g. vector DB at scale is 5x the PoC unit cost, or a specialist role isn't in the original plan). This is the **hardest kind to talk about** — because it exposes a gap in your PoC estimation.

Framing: present it as a "stage gate" — we've reached a point where we need to recalibrate together.

**Type 4: Genuine runaway**

Engineer didn't monitor cost, API misconfiguration caused looping, someone triggered an unexpected batch job. This is **the most damaging to reputation** — because it exposes that basic ops aren't in place.

Framing: own it, give specific root cause + the controls you've already added, and over-correct (proactively propose monitoring upgrades).

**Self-test**: which type is your overrun? Don't talk to the sponsor before you know. If it's Type 4, fix root cause first, then talk — don't talk while still fixing.

---

## Decision 2 — Type 1 (usage overrun) framing

Type 1 is the most favourable. Framed well, the sponsor walks away not just non-angry, but excited.

**The right opener**

> "[Name], wanted to give you an update — 4 weeks into production, I've got a positive piece of news that triggers a budget conversation.

> Short version: user adoption is 60% above our conservative estimate. MAU has gone from our plan of 200 to 320, daily query from 800 to 1300.

> That's a success signal, but the implication is: token consumption scaled proportionally too. End of Q3 we're projecting roughly [$X] over original budget; on current trajectory, end of Q4 is [$Y] over.

> I want to walk through a few options for how we handle this — expand budget, cost-optimise, or both. What I'm bringing isn't a budget ask, it's trade-offs for you to decide on."

This opener:

- First line sets tone as "positive" — sponsor doesn't go defensive
- Lead with adoption numbers, not cost — sponsor registers success first
- "Success signal, but implications" — natural transition
- "Trade-offs for you to decide" — sponsor is the decision maker

**Trade-off options with concrete numbers**

> "Few options:

> **Option A — expand budget**: at current trajectory, +$[X] by end of Q3, +$[Y] by end of Q4. If budget approval isn't an issue, this is the cleanest path and reinforces the success narrative.

> **Option B — cost-optimise, maintain current adoption**: we've already identified 3 optimisations: caching repeated queries (saves 25%), routing simple queries to a cheaper model tier (saves 15%), batching non-real-time tasks (saves 10%). Conservative estimate is we can absorb current growth without raising budget. Trade-off: 4 to 6 weeks of engineering work, delays planned v1.5 features.

> **Option C — mix**: do caching + tiering immediately (high ROI), and request a smaller budget bump to cover the remaining gap. Personally I lean Option C — long-term cost discipline + short-term no ramp-down of adoption.

> Your preference?"

**Key: don't assume the sponsor defaults to budget expansion.** Many sponsors have pressure from their own finance side, and prefer cost optimisation even at the expense of timeline. Give them options to pick.

---

## Decision 3 — Type 2 (per-task cost exceeds estimate) framing

Type 2 is the most technical. The sponsor probably doesn't follow "per-task token count" — you translate.

**The right opener**

> "[Name], wanted to sync on a refinement to our cost estimation.

> At PoC stage, we estimated each user task consumes around [X] tokens, and built the budget on that figure. 4 weeks into production, we're seeing actual per-task token consumption of [Y] — [Z%] higher.

> Why — nothing to do with user adoption. Two reasons: one, real user queries are more complex than PoC test queries (more nuanced wording, more follow-ups); two, our retrieval system on average retrieves [N]x the context length compared to PoC stage (we found longer context improved accuracy, so this trade-off was deliberate).

> Both factors are positive (real user behaviour + accuracy gains from longer context), but they mean per-task cost is [Z%] above original estimate."

**An analogy the sponsor can grab onto**

> "An analogy — PoC was like measuring a car's fuel economy on ideal road conditions, getting [X] L/100km. Production is real road conditions (traffic, hills, air-con), and actual consumption is [Y] L/100km.

> This isn't the car having a problem, and it's not an estimation error — it's that PoC setup was more ideal than real-world conditions. Now that we have real production data, we re-baseline the estimate. Future budget will be more accurate."

The analogy lets the sponsor grasp it immediately without going defensive. The fuel-economy comparison works because CFOs / sponsors are familiar with "PoC numbers vs production numbers" gaps — it's a common pattern in software / engineering.

**Acknowledge your estimation gap, but as systemic**

> "I have to acknowledge — my PoC-stage cost estimation should have come with a wider range (e.g. ±40%), not a point estimate. AI projects have a wider per-task cost variance than traditional software, this is industry-wide, but as PM I should have built that buffer into the budget.

> I've updated my own estimation methodology. Future milestone cost estimates will come with low / median / high numbers, no more point estimates."

---

## Decision 4 — Type 3 (hidden cost blowout) framing

Type 3 is the hardest. The sponsor's reaction will be "did you not think about the full stack cost at PoC stage".

**Key: align internally before talking to the sponsor, and bring a forward path.** Don't just surface a problem.

**The right opener**

> "[Name], I need 30 minutes to walk through a stage gate moment on the cost side.

> Short version: 2 months into v1 production, I have better visibility into the full cost stack, and I see we underestimated several hidden cost lines at PoC stage — specifically: [vector DB scale cost / SRE headcount / compliance SaaS renewal].

> Total overrun is roughly [X]. I've analysed the root cause and prepared mitigations. Today's goal: one, you understand why we're over; two, we decide together whether to absorb, mitigate, or mix."

**Walking specific buckets, with specifics**

> "Three buckets of overrun:

> **Bucket 1 — Vector DB cost**: PoC estimate was [$X]/month, based on [PoC-scale vector count + query rate]. Production actual is [$Y]/month, because our vector count is [N]x the PoC (real-world documents are much more numerous than samples), and query rate is also above estimate.

> Mitigation: we've identified an alternative vector DB provider (cheaper at scale); migration would save roughly 40%, but takes 6 weeks of engineering.

> **Bucket 2 — SRE / DevOps headcount**: PoC was 0.5 FTE, production reality is 1.5 FTE. Because production monitoring + on-call + tuning is more labour-intensive than expected.

> Mitigation: not reducible short-term; long-term, automation / observability tools can bring it down to 1 FTE.

> **Bucket 3 — Compliance SaaS**: SOC 2 + GDPR-related tools renewed at [Z%] over PoC pricing.

> Mitigation: review the vendor list, 2 to 3 tools are redundant and can be dropped."

**Frame as stage gate, not failure**

> "This pattern of cost exposure 2 to 3 months after AI production launch is industry-standard — not good, but expected. PoC stage will always underestimate the real production cost surface.

> I'd recommend framing today as a stage gate: we've reached the first full cost calibration after v1 production. Based on real data, we update budget projection to [new number], then quarterly review going forward."

**Key: use the phrase "stage gate".** When the sponsor hears "stage gate", it sounds planned, normal — not a failure.

---

## Decision 5 — Type 4 (genuine runaway) framing

Type 4 is the ugliest. Most PMs in this situation try to frame it as Type 2 or Type 3 — **don't**. The sponsor sees through it, and the reputation hit doubles.

**Fix first, talk second**

Before talking to the sponsor, you must:

1. **Stop the bleed** — immediately fix root cause, close the looping / cap the rate limit / kill the misconfigured batch
2. **Identify root cause specifically** — not vague "some issue", but specific "engineer A didn't set max_tokens in the prompt template, and the monitoring alert didn't trigger after [date] because the alert threshold was set wrong"
3. **Add controls to prevent recurrence** — not "we'll be more careful", but specific "daily cost alert at [$X] threshold, SRE checks at 9am daily, hard cap at [$Y]/day"

These three done before you open the conversation. **The sponsor's first question will always be "are we still bleeding?" — you have to answer "no, stopped at [time]" immediately.**

**The right opener**

> "[Name], there's something I have to tell you immediately.

> Last week [date], our production environment triggered an unexpected cost spike due to [one-sentence root cause]. Total impact is $[X] one-time overrun — this is new, not a trend.

> Three things up front:

> One, **the problem fully stopped on [date], no ongoing impact**.
> Two, **root cause is [specific], fixed**.
> Three, **prevention controls in place: [specific controls 1 / 2 / 3]**.

> I'm not here today to ask you to do something — I'm here because this is a material event, and you should hear it from me first."

**Key: one-sentence root cause + three fix actions + framed as material event**

**Own it, don't pass blame**

> "Root cause was [engineer didn't set max_tokens / SRE didn't monitor the alert / I didn't set process for review to catch this].

> As PM I have to own it — this is a process gap, not just an individual mistake. I've added three things: PR review checklist includes cost-impact item; weekly cost burn review added to status report; production changes now require a cost-impact assessment.

> I'm not going to throw [engineer A] under the bus — this is on me as PM for not setting controls."

**Proactively over-correct**

> "I realise this could make you question our overall cost discipline. So beyond the three controls, I'm proactively proposing two over-corrections:

> One, **monthly cost review with finance team** — invite your finance partner to attend, formal review of cost trajectory even when there's no issue.

> Two, **quarterly external audit of cost setup** — have an independent team from [BCG / tomoro] audit our cost monitoring and controls.

> Neither requires your acceptance — but I want to demonstrate I'm taking this seriously and I'm open to oversight."

**Proactively offering oversight on yourself** is the most powerful move in a Type 4 conversation. The sponsor will usually decline (quarterly audits are heavy), but they remember the offer. **Willingness to be overseen** is the real signal of trust.

---

## Decision 6 — The weeks after the conversation

After the budget conversation, the sponsor enters quiet observation mode. The next 4 to 6 weeks are the trust-rebuilding window.

**Do**

1. **Send the sponsor a weekly cost burn snapshot** — single line: "last week burn $[X], MTD $[Y], projection on track / off by [$Z]"
2. **Ping the sponsor within 24 hours on any anomaly** — "Tuesday we saw an unusual spike, root cause [X], contained"
3. **Add a cost section to monthly check-ins** — even with no issue, formal review once. Cost discipline is demonstrated, not stated
4. **If you went with Option B / C (cost optimisation), report actual savings strictly** — don't wait for the sponsor to ask

**Don't**

1. **Don't disappear on the cost topic** — saying it once and going silent is the worst pattern, the sponsor feels you "said it and forgot"
2. **Don't oscillate** — one week on track, next week off, the sponsor feels your estimation is unstable. Stabilise after recalibration
3. **Don't over-promise reductions** — don't say "next quarter we'll cut 30%". Cost reductions depend on multiple factors; empty promises set you up to fail again

---

## A short do / don't

**Do**

- Sort the overrun into Type 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 first — different framings
- Type 1 framed as success signal + budget trade-off
- Type 2 use an analogy (fuel economy) + acknowledge estimation gap + framed as method refinement
- Type 3 framed as stage gate + give specific buckets + per-bucket mitigation
- Type 4 fix first, then talk, with three fix actions + proactive oversight offer
- Any type: bring options for the sponsor to decide, don't dictate
- 4-6 weeks of quiet observation period after, with elevated cost discipline transparency
- Offering oversight on yourself is the senior move in Type 4

**Don't**

- Don't dress Type 4 up as Type 2 or 3 (sponsor sees through it, reputation hit doubles)
- Don't open without sorting the type
- Don't assume the sponsor defaults to budget expansion — many have finance pressure
- Don't dump numbers without mitigation
- Don't throw engineers under the bus
- Don't over-promise cost reductions (e.g. "30% saving next quarter")
- Don't disappear after the budget conversation — continued cost discipline transparency is required
- Don't surface an overrun in a meeting the sponsor isn't prepared for — always 1:1 first

---

---

Stepping back: the budget overrun conversation has a special place in AI delivery — it directly affects the sponsor's standing in their own organisation.

A delay the sponsor absorbs internally. A scope cut the sponsor absorbs internally. But a budget overrun forces the sponsor to go to finance, which means this bad news has an audience much larger than the project itself. What you're really giving the sponsor isn't just a problem statement — it's **how they sell this problem upward**.

A budget overrun conversation delivered well: the sponsor walks out and can immediately articulate three things — why we went over (root cause), what we did (mitigation), how we're controlling going forward (oversight). Those three things are exactly what finance / CFO are sorting through when they hear about a cost overrun.

When you pre-organise these three things for the sponsor, they can reproduce your words verbatim when they go upward. PMs who can do this get categorised in the sponsor's head as **partner, not vendor** — because you helped them navigate internal politics, not just shipped the project.

And that, in AI delivery, is the most valuable kind of reputation.

# 预算超支: 跟客户讲钱不够用

预算超支是 AI 项目里**最财务敏感**的一种坏消息。

延期、评测不达标、PoC 失败,这些主办人可以自己消化 — 都是项目内部的事。预算超支不一样: 一旦超支,主办人必须去找他的 CFO 或者财务团队要更多钱,这意味着这个坏消息会被升级到他自己不能掌控的层级。从主办人的角度看,**任何成本超支都是政治问题,不只是项目问题**。

这一节假设你已经做完了成本端的基本功 (T9.5 怎么估算每用户每月成本、T9.3 缓存、T9.4 批处理对实时) — 这一节讲: **当预算不可避免超支的时候,怎么跟客户讲**。

下面拆几个决策点。

---

## 决策点 1 — 区分 4 种不同类型的超支

不同类型的超支,**根本原因不一样,讲法也不一样**。一上来不分类就讲超支,主办人听完只记得"超了",记不住为什么。

**1 型: 用量超支 — 用量超预期**

生产环境上线之后用户采用率比预期高,token 消耗比预算的多。这是**最好的一种超支** — 因为它意味着成功。

讲法: 主要表述成"正面的问题"。

**2 型: 单位成本稳定但每任务 token 数超出预期**

每次任务消耗的 token 比 PoC 阶段估算的多。比如上下文越来越长,或者推理链比预期长。这是**最技术性的一种超支** — 不意味着失败,但意味着你的估算不准确。

讲法: 主要表述成"估算的细化",带数据。

**3 型: 隐性成本爆发 — 人 / 基础设施 / 第三方 SaaS**

T19.1 提过 AI 项目最被低估的成本是人、基础设施、合规。如果这一块超支,通常是项目中途才发现 (比如向量数据库扩规模起来比 PoC 贵 5 倍,或者需要一个专家没在原计划里)。这是**最难讲的一种超支** — 因为它暴露了你 PoC 估算的缺口。

讲法: 主要表述成"阶段评审" — 我们到了一个需要一起重新校准的点。

**4 型: 真正的失控**

工程师没监控成本、API 设置错了导致死循环、有人触发了一个意外的批处理任务。这是**最影响声誉的一种** — 因为它暴露了基础运维没做好。

讲法: 必须承担它,讲清楚根本原因 + 已经修的控制,而且要矫枉过正 (主动提出监控升级)。

**自检**: 你的超支是哪一种?不知道之前不要跟主办人讲。如果是 4 型,先修完根本原因再去讲 — 不能边修边讲。

---

## 决策点 2 — 1 型 (用量超预期) 怎么讲

1 型是最有利的。表述得好,主办人听完不光不生气,反而会兴奋。

**对的开场**

> "[姓名],想给你一个更新 — 我们生产环境上线 4 周,我有一个正面但需要预算对话的消息。

> 简单讲: 用户采用率比我们保守估计高了 60%。月活用户从我们计划的 200 涨到了 320,日均查询从 800 涨到了 1300。

> 这是成功信号,但影响是: token 消耗也按比例涨了,Q3 末预计会超原预算大概 [X 万],按当前趋势看 Q4 末超 [Y 万]。

> 我想跟你逐项过几个选项怎么处理 — 是扩预算,还是成本优化,还是两个一起。我准备的不是要预算,是给你取舍让你决定。"

这个开场:

- 第一句定调是"正面" — 主办人不会立刻防御
- 用采用率数字开场,不用成本数字 — 主办人先记住的是成功
- "成功信号,但影响" — 自然过渡
- "取舍让你决定" — 主办人是决策者

**取舍选项要带具体数字**

> "几个选项:

> **选项 A — 扩预算**: 按当前趋势,Q3 末 +$[X],Q4 末 +$[Y]。如果预算审批没问题,这是最简单的路径,且强化项目成功叙事。

> **选项 B — 成本优化,保持当前采用率**: 我们已经识别了 [3] 个优化点: 缓存重复查询 (省 25%),把简单查询切到便宜的模型分层 (省 15%),批处理一些非实时任务 (省 10%)。保守估计能消化当前增长不需要加预算。代价: 需要 4 到 6 周工程工作,延后了原 v1.5 功能。

> **选项 C — 混合**: 立刻做缓存 + 分层 (高回报),同时申请一个小一点的预算补充覆盖剩余缺口。我个人倾向选项 C — 长期成本纪律 + 短期不降采用率。

> 你怎么倾向?"

**关键: 不要假设主办人会默认选扩预算**。很多主办人在财务那边其实有压力,他们倾向成本优化即使时间线牺牲。给选项让他选。

---

## 决策点 3 — 2 型 (每任务成本超估算) 怎么讲

2 型是最技术性的一种。主办人通常听不懂"每任务 token 数" — 你要翻译。

**对的开场**

> "[姓名],想跟你同步一下我们成本估算的一个细化。

> 当我们做 PoC 的时候,我们估算每个用户任务消耗大概 [X] 个 token,基于这个数字我建了预算。生产环境上线 4 周之后,我们看到实际每任务 token 消耗是 [Y] 个 — 高了 [Z%]。

> 为什么 — 跟用户采用率没关系。原因有两个: 一,真实用户的查询比 PoC 测试的查询复杂 (用词更微妙、追问更多);二,我们检索系统平均检索的上下文比 PoC 阶段长 [N] 倍 (因为我们发现长上下文提升了准确率,这个取舍是有意做的)。

> 这两个因素都是正面的 (真实用户行为 + 通过更长上下文提升准确率),但意味着每任务成本比原估算高 [Z%]。"

**用主办人能抓住的类比**

> "用一个类比 — 我们 PoC 阶段像是测试一辆车的耗油量,在理想路况上跑出来 [X] 升每百公里。生产环境是真实路况 (堵车、上坡、空调),实际耗油是 [Y] 升每百公里。

> 这不是车有问题,也不是估算错误 — 是我们 PoC 阶段的设置比真实环境理想。我们现在有真实的生产环境数据,重新设定一下估算基线,后面预算会更准。"

类比让主办人立刻抓住而且不会防御。"耗油量"这个比喻好,因为 CFO / 主办人都熟悉"PoC 数字对生产环境数字"的差异 — 这是软件 / 工程的常见模式。

**承认你的估算缺口,但讲成系统性**

> "我必须承认一下: 我 PoC 阶段的成本估算应该带一个更宽的区间 (比如 ±40%),不是单点估计。AI 项目的每任务成本变化范围比传统软件高,这是行业普遍现象,但作为项目经理我应该把这个缓冲建进预算。

> 我已经更新了我自己的估算方法,后续里程碑的成本我会带 [低 / 中位 / 高] 三个数字,不再给单点估计。"

---

## 决策点 4 — 3 型 (隐性成本爆发) 怎么讲

3 型是最难的一种。主办人听完会觉得"你 PoC 阶段是不是没想清楚整个技术栈的成本"。

**关键: 跟主办人讲之前先内部对齐,而且要带一个前进路径**。不要只提出问题。

**对的开场**

> "[姓名],我需要 30 分钟跟你逐项过一个成本端的阶段评审。

> 简单讲: 我们 v1 生产环境跑了 2 个月,我现在对完整成本栈有更好的可见性,而且我看到 PoC 阶段我们低估了几块隐性成本,具体: [向量数据库规模成本 / SRE 人力 / 合规 SaaS 续约]。

> 总超支大概 [X 万] — 我已经分析了根本原因,而且准备好了缓解方案。今天的目标是: 一,你了解为什么超;二,我们一起决定是吸收还是缓解还是混合。"

**讲具体哪几块,要具体**

> "三块超支:

> **第一块 — 向量数据库成本**: PoC 阶段我们估算 $[X]/月,基于 [PoC 规模的向量数 + 查询速率]。生产环境之后实际 $[Y]/月,因为我们的向量数比 PoC 多了 [N] 倍 (真实世界文档比样本文档多得多),而且查询速率也比预估高。

> 缓解: 我们已经识别一个备选向量数据库供应商 (大规模便宜),如果迁移大概省 40%,但需要 6 周工程时间。

> **第二块 — SRE / DevOps 人力**: PoC 阶段 0.5 全时当量,生产环境之后实际 1.5 全时当量。原因是生产环境监控 + 待命 + 调优比预期人力密集。

> 缓解: 短期没法降,长期靠自动化 / 可观测性工具能降到 1 全时当量。

> **第三块 — 合规 SaaS**: SOC 2 + GDPR 相关的工具续约比 PoC 时贵 [Z%]。

> 缓解: 我们评审一下供应商列表,有 2 到 3 个工具冗余可以砍。"

**表述成阶段评审,不是失败**

> "这种成本暴露在 AI 项目生产环境后 2 到 3 个月发生是行业标准 — 不是好,但意料之中。原因是 PoC 阶段一定低估生产环境的真实成本覆盖面。

> 我建议把今天表述成一个阶段评审: 我们到了 v1 生产环境第一次完整成本校准的时候,基于真实数据我们更新预算预测到 [新数字],然后季度评审一次。"

**关键: 用"阶段评审"这个词**。主办人听"阶段评审"觉得这是有计划的、正常的 — 不是失误。

---

## 决策点 5 — 4 型 (真正失控) 怎么讲

4 型最难看。绝大多数项目经理在这种情况下试图把它讲成 2 型或 3 型 — **不要这么做**。主办人会看穿,声誉打击翻倍。

**先修再讲**

跟主办人讲之前必须先:

1. **止血** — 立刻修根本原因,关掉死循环 / 加速率上限 / 关掉配置错的批处理
2. **识别根本原因** — 不是模糊的"某些问题",是具体的"工程师 A 没在 prompt 模板里设最大 token 数,而且监控警报在 [日期] 之后没触发因为警报阈值设错"
3. **加控制防止再发生** — 不是承诺"我们会小心",是具体的"我们加了每日成本告警在 $[X] 阈值,每天上午 9 点 SRE 检查,而且加了硬上限在 $[Y]/天"

这三件事做完才能去找主办人。**主办人第一个问题一定是"现在还在流血吗" — 你必须能立刻答"没有,已在 [时间] 停止"**。

**对的开场**

> "[姓名],我有一件事必须立刻跟你讲。

> 上周 [日期],我们生产环境因为 [一句话根本原因] 触发了一个意外的成本飙升。总影响是 $[X] 一次性超支,这是新增不是趋势。

> 我先告诉你三件事:

> 一,**问题在 [日期] 已经完全停止,目前没有持续影响**。
> 二,**根本原因是 [具体],已经修**。
> 三,**预防我已经加了三个控制: [具体控制 1 / 2 / 3]**。

> 我今天来找你,不是要你立刻做什么 — 是因为这是一个重大事件,你应该从我嘴里第一个知道。"

**关键: 一句话根本原因 + 三件修的事 + 表述成重大事件**

**承担,不甩**

> "这件事根本原因是 [工程师没设最大 token 数 / SRE 没监控警报 / 我没设好流程让评审抓到]。

> 我作为项目经理必须承担 — 这是流程缺口,不只是个人失误。我已经加了三件事: PR 评审清单加成本影响项;每周成本消耗评审加到进展更新;生产环境变更必须有成本影响评估。

> 我不会甩 [工程师 A] — 这是我作为项目经理没设好控制。"

**主动矫枉过正**

> "我意识到这件事可能会让你怀疑我们整体的成本纪律。所以除了上面三个控制,我主动提两件矫枉过正:

> 一,**每月成本评审跟财务团队** — 邀请你的财务对接人参加,即使月度没有问题,正式评审一次成本趋势。

> 二,**季度外部审计成本设置** — 邀请 [BCG / tomoro] 一个独立团队来审计我们的成本监控和控制。

> 这两件你不一定要接受 — 但我想展示我认真对待这件事,而且愿意接受监督。"

**主动提出对自己的监督**是 4 型对话最有力的一招。主办人听完通常会拒绝 (因为季度审计太重),但他会记得你愿意提议。**愿意接受监督**是信任真正的标志。

---

## 决策点 6 — 对话之后的几周

预算超支讲完之后,主办人进入安静观察模式。后面 4 到 6 周是信任重建的关键期。

**该做的**

1. **每周给主办人一个成本消耗快照** — 简单 1 行: "上周消耗 $[X],月度累计 $[Y],预测在轨 / 偏离 $[Z]"
2. **任何异常 24 小时内通知主办人** — "周二我们看到一个异常飙升,根本原因是 [X],已控制住"
3. **每月校准加一个成本部分** — 即使没问题,正式评审一次。成本纪律是要被展示的,不是说一次就够
4. **如果走的是选项 B / C (成本优化),严格汇报实际省了多少** — 不要让主办人自己问

**不该做的**

1. **不要在成本话题上消失** — 讲完一次然后回归沉默是最差的,主办人会觉得你"讲完就忘了"
2. **不要反复** — 一会儿成本在轨,一会儿成本偏离,主办人觉得你估算不稳。校准后要稳定一段时间
3. **不要承诺过度的减少** — 不要说"下个季度一定省 30%"。成本减少取决于多个因素,空头承诺只会再坑

---

## 一些"该做 / 不该做"速记

**该做**

- 先把超支分类 (1 / 2 / 3 / 4 型),不同类型不同讲法
- 1 型表述成成功信号 + 预算取舍
- 2 型用类比 (耗油量) + 承认估算缺口 + 表述成方法细化
- 3 型表述成阶段评审 + 给具体三块 + 每块的缓解方案
- 4 型先修再讲,带三件修的事 + 主动提出监督
- 任何类型,带选项给主办人决定,不要拍板
- 对话之后 4 到 6 周安静观察期,加成本纪律透明度
- 主动提出对自己的监督是 4 型的高级招

**不该做**

- 不要把 4 型包装成 2 型或 3 型 (主办人看穿,声誉翻倍打击)
- 不要不分类直接讲超支
- 不要假设主办人默认选扩预算 — 很多主办人在财务那边有压力
- 不要扔数字不给缓解
- 不要甩工程师
- 不要承诺过度的成本减少 (比如"下季度省 30%")
- 不要讲完预算对话之后消失 — 后续成本纪律透明度是必须
- 不要在主办人没准备的会议上提超支 — 永远先一对一

## 话术速查:16 条预算超支直接抄走用的句子

### 自检 + 分类

**1. 四类超支分类**
> 1 型: 用量超 (采用率超预期),成功信号。2 型: 每任务 token 超 (估算不准),技术细化。3 型: 隐性成本爆发 (人 / 基础设施 / SaaS),阶段评审。4 型: 真正失控 (配置错误 / 死循环),声誉事件。

### 1 型 (用量超)

**2. 开场: 正面定调**
> "想给你一个更新 — 生产环境 4 周,我有正面但需要预算对话的消息。采用率比保守估计高 60% — 月活 200→320,日均查询 800→1300。这是成功,但影响: Q3 末 +$[X],Q4 末 +$[Y]。"

**3. 给三个选项**
> "选项 A: 扩预算,最简单,强化成功。选项 B: 成本优化 (缓存 / 分层 / 批处理) 省 50%,4 到 6 周工程,延后 v1.5 功能。选项 C: 混合 — 立刻做缓存 + 分层 + 小一点的预算补充。我倾向 C — 长期纪律 + 短期不降采用率。"

### 2 型 (每任务超)

**4. 开场: 估算细化**
> "想同步一个成本估算细化。PoC 估算每任务 [X] token,生产环境 4 周实际 [Y] token,高 [Z%]。原因: 真实用户查询比 PoC 复杂;检索上下文比 PoC 长 N 倍 (有意取舍,提升准确率)。两个都是正面但意味着每任务成本高 Z%。"

**5. 用类比让主办人抓住**
> "类比 — PoC 像测车在理想路况上耗油 X 升 / 百公里,生产环境是真实路况 (堵车、上坡、空调),耗油 Y 升。不是车有问题,不是估算错误 — 是 PoC 设置比真实理想。现在有真实生产环境数据,重新设定基线,后面预算更准。"

**6. 承认估算缺口 + 方法更新**
> "我必须承认 — PoC 成本估算应该带更宽区间 (±40%),不是单点估计。AI 项目每任务变化范围比传统软件高,行业普遍。我已经更新方法,后续成本带 低 / 中位 / 高 三个数字,不再单点估计。"

### 3 型 (隐性成本)

**7. 开场: 阶段评审**
> "我需要 30 分钟逐项过一个成本端的阶段评审。V1 生产环境 2 个月,我对完整成本栈有更好可见性,看到 PoC 阶段低估几块隐性成本: [向量数据库 / SRE / 合规 SaaS]。总超支 [X 万]。今天目标: 你了解为什么 + 一起决定吸收 / 缓解 / 混合。"

**8. 拆三块 + 每块缓解**
> "三块: ① 向量数据库成本 — PoC $[X]/月 实际 $[Y]/月 (向量数 + 查询速率都高),缓解迁更便宜的供应商省 40% (6 周工程)。② SRE 人力 — 0.5 全时当量变 1.5 全时当量,长期靠自动化降到 1。③ 合规 SaaS — 续约贵 Z%,缓解评审供应商列表砍冗余。"

**9. 表述成阶段评审而非失败**
> "这种成本暴露在 AI 生产环境后 2 到 3 个月发生是行业标准 — 不是好,但意料之中。PoC 阶段一定低估生产环境真实成本覆盖面。建议把今天表述成阶段评审: v1 生产环境第一次完整成本校准,基于真实数据更新预算预测到 [新数字],季度评审。"

### 4 型 (真正失控)

**10. 三件事先做完再讲**
> 跟主办人讲之前必须做完: ① 止血 (关死循环 / 加速率上限 / 关配置错的批处理)。② 识别具体根本原因 (不是模糊的"某些问题")。③ 加控制 (具体每日告警 / 硬上限)。三件做完才能开口。

**11. 开场: 三件事表述**
> "我有一件事必须立刻跟你讲。上周 [日期] 生产环境因为 [一句话根本原因] 触发意外成本飙升,总影响 $[X] 一次性,新增不是趋势。三件事告诉你: ① 问题 [日期] 完全停止,无持续影响;② 根本原因已修;③ 预防已加三个控制。今天不要你立刻做什么,但你应该从我嘴里第一个知道。"

**12. 承担 + 不甩**
> "根本原因是 [工程师没设最大 token 数 / SRE 没监控 / 我没设流程]。我作为项目经理必须承担 — 这是流程缺口不只是个人失误。我加了: PR 评审清单加成本影响项;每周成本消耗加到进展更新;生产环境变更必须有成本影响评估。我不甩 [工程师] — 是我没设好控制。"

**13. 主动提出监督**
> "我意识到这可能让你怀疑我整体成本纪律。除了三个控制,我主动提两件矫枉过正: ① 每月成本评审跟财务团队 (邀请你财务对接人);② 季度外部审计 (邀请独立团队审计我们成本监控)。你不一定接受,但我想展示认真对待 + 愿意接受监督。"

### 应对主办人反应

**14. 主办人问"现在还在流血吗"**
> "没有,已在 [具体时间] 停止。从那一刻零持续影响。每天的成本消耗我都监控中,过去 [N] 天都在正常范围。"

**15. 主办人问"你怎么确保不再发生"**
> "三个控制: 每日成本告警在 $[X] 阈值,每天上午 9 点 SRE 看;硬上限在 $[Y]/天,触发自动限流;PR 评审加成本影响评估。这三个不是承诺'会小心',是系统级控制,即使个人失误也不会达到这种规模。"

### 跟进

**16. 每周成本快照**
> "本周成本消耗: 上周 $[X],月度累计 $[Y],预测在轨 / 偏离 $[Z]。无异常。" (一句话一周,持续 6 周。成本纪律是被展示的不是被宣称的。)

---

往大了说,预算超支对话在 AI 交付里有一个特殊地位 — 它直接影响主办人在他自己组织里的地位。

延期主办人自己消化,砍范围主办人自己消化,但预算超支主办人必须去找财务,这意味着这个坏消息有一个听众远大于项目本身。你给主办人讲的不只是问题,是**他要怎么去往上汇报这个问题**。

讲得好的预算超支对话,主办人走出会议室之后能立刻表述三件事: 一,为什么超 (根本原因);二,我们做了什么 (缓解);三,前进怎么控制 (监督)。这三件事正好是财务 / CFO 听成本超支时心里在分类的三个问题。

你帮主办人把这三件事预先组织好,主办人上去汇报的时候只需要复读你的话。这种项目经理,主办人会在心里把你定义为**合作伙伴而不是供应商** — 因为你帮他走过了内部政治,不只是交付了项目。

而这,是 AI 交付这一行里值最钱的一种声誉。
