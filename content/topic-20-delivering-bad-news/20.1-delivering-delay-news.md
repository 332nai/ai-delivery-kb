# Delivering delay news: when you have to tell the client the timeline slips

A delay is the most common type of bad news in AI projects, and also the most poorly handled.

The most common cause isn't the technology — it's the PM dragging their feet. You've known for three weeks the timeline is going to slip, but the weekly status still reads "on track", and only in the last week before the deadline do you tell the sponsor "we might need two more weeks". When the sponsor hears it, they're not just annoyed — they start questioning whether every status update before this was accurate.

**A delay on its own doesn't lose the client's trust. A delay plus the fact that you knew and didn't say does.** That's the core of this article.

A few decision points below.

---

## Decision 1 — When to actually open your mouth

PMs stall because of a single thought in their head: "maybe we can pull this back this week — let me wait and see". That thought is always wrong. AI projects have a particular property: once a milestone starts slipping, **the slip accelerates, it doesn't self-correct**. A few reasons:

- Failures to hit eval targets are usually rooted in data or prompt issues, not solvable by another week of tweaking
- Engineers working overtime to catch up introduce more bugs, which then need more debug time later
- External dependencies (legal review, IT provisioning, stakeholder feedback) that have slipped once almost always slip again
- The moment the team starts privately doubting the deadline, velocity drops

So the threshold for raising it needs to be hard. **If any of the following applies, escalate**:

1. The milestone has slipped more than 3 days with no clear plan to recover
2. You've been telling yourself "maybe next week we'll catch up" for more than a week
3. A critical dependency (data, infra, headcount) has an unresolved blocker
4. Your own confidence in the original deadline is below 60%

Anything under 80% confidence is worth surfacing. By the time you're at 50%, you're already late.

---

## Decision 2 — Who hears it first

Don't surprise the sponsor in a group meeting. **Any bad news goes to the sponsor 1:1 first, then gets confirmed in the broader forum.**

The order:

1. **Tell your own manager first** (PL at BCG, someone like Dan at tomoro) — let them know before anyone else so they can have your back if the escalation goes sideways
2. **Then 1:1 with the sponsor** — 15 minutes alone, not by email, not by Slack
3. **Then the IT lead / key technical stakeholder** — because they may need to help you unblock
4. **The status email or wider channel comes last** — that step is confirmation, not announcement

Why this order: what the sponsor hates most is hearing bad news about their own project from someone else. Not from the IT lead, not from one of their reports, not from a status email. They need to hear it from you first.

If your sponsor and the IT lead aren't on good terms (the T15.5 scenario), this ordering becomes even more important — if the sponsor finds out the IT lead heard about the delay first, they'll immediately suspect you've taken IT's side.

---

## Decision 3 — Opening the 15-minute 1:1 with the sponsor

These are the most important 15 minutes of the whole delay conversation. How you open determines how the sponsor responds.

**The wrong opener**

> "[Name], I've got some bad news — our milestone needs to slip by two weeks."

What's wrong: you've handed over the problem without any context, the sponsor's brain immediately enters defensive mode, and nothing you say in the next 14 minutes lands.

**The right opener**

> "[Name], can I grab 15 minutes with you? There's a timeline thing I want to talk through 1:1. I'll give you the bottom line, then the why, then how I'm proposing to handle it — stop me at any point.

> **Bottom line: the milestone originally planned for June 30 — I'd recommend we move it two weeks to July 14**. I made this call two days ago, ran it internally to stress-test before bringing it to you. I'm bringing it now because I'm confident the move is needed."

What this opener does:

- "Grab 15 minutes" — gives the sponsor a defined time window so they don't get anxious
- "Bottom line first" — they know you're not going to ramble
- "I made the call two days ago" — signals you didn't just discover this, you've already pressure-tested internally
- "I'd recommend" — softer than "we need to" — leaves room for the sponsor as the decision maker

**The next 5 minutes — the why**

Don't drop a pile of detail. Three things:

1. What kind of delay this is (size, cause, impact)
2. Why this isn't fixable by another week of overtime
3. What you've already done about it

> "On the why — this isn't an engineering velocity problem. This week's eval came back at 78% accuracy on the production-realistic test set. We'd committed 85% to you.

> The 7 point gap isn't something we can close with prompt tweaks — it's a retrieval recall problem at the architecture level. Put simply, the model isn't finding the documents it should be using. Prompt changes don't fix this; we need to rework the retrieval architecture, which is roughly 8 to 10 working days of work.

> What we've done in the last two weeks: I've put Engineer 1 on a new branch working the retrieval fix, and Engineer 2 holding the existing path as a fallback in case the new approach doesn't pan out. I haven't spread the team thin — both directions have a dedicated person."

What this does:

- Gives a concrete number (78% vs 85%) — the gap is quantified
- Explains why this isn't solvable by working harder — preempting the "can't you just push?" question
- Shows you've already moved on it (plan A + plan B in parallel) — the sponsor sees you in control, not panicking

**The last 5 minutes — how you propose to handle it**

> "Here's what I'd recommend.

> First, I'd reset the timeline publicly to July 14 rather than quietly let it drag. Quiet drift loses the team its anchor, and if more slips compound on top, it looks ugly. One clean reset.

> Second, I'd suggest a go/no-go on July 7, one week ahead of the new deadline, with you and the IT lead. By then we'll have eval data, and we can decide jointly whether to ship or push further.

> Third, this two-week shift has downstream impact — the UAT we set up with the business team needs to move, and finance will need to adjust the Q3 budget release cadence. I'll handle both conversations, but I need backing from you — when they push back, I'll quote your sign-off.

> One last thing — this is my call. I'm not pushing it on engineering or on a vendor. If you need to escalate this up the chain, I'm ready to take the questions."

That last line is the move. **Take ownership, don't dodge.** What sponsors hate most isn't the delay itself — it's the PM looking for somewhere to put the blame. When you say "this is my call", the sponsor's instinct flips to "let me help this person handle it up the chain".

---

## Decision 4 — The sharp questions the sponsor will ask, and how to answer

**Q1: "You said we were on track three weeks ago."**

The hardest question to answer, because the sponsor is questioning the accuracy of your reporting.

**Don't lie. Don't minimise.** Be direct:

> "Fair point. Three weeks ago when I said on track, our eval was based on a small test set and was running at 86%. I assumed that number would hold. When we expanded to the production-realistic test set, it dropped to 78%. Two takeaways from that:

> One, small-test-set metrics can't be trusted — I should have pushed to production-realistic testing earlier.
> Two, I should have flagged that uncertainty to you, not treated 86% as a settled number.

> I'm going to change the status report format to include the test set being used, so the context behind the metric is visible to you."

What this does:

- Acknowledges the error, but anchors it to a specific gap (not a vague "I should have communicated more")
- Uses concrete numbers (the sponsor sees you genuinely understand the issue)
- Proactively proposes a process fix — they see you actually took something away from it

**Q2: "Can we hold the deadline and cut some scope?"**

The standard sponsor move to save the date. The question is whether scope cuts would actually save it. **If they won't, say so directly.**

> "I did think about it. Cutting scope doesn't save the timeline in this case — the blocker is the retrieval architecture, not any one feature. Cutting feature A still leaves us waiting on the retrieval fix.

> But if your priority is having something demo-able on June 30, here's an alternative: we ship a reduced-scope internal demo on June 30 — visible only to you and the sponsor circle — and ship the full version to end users on July 14. From the outside you've held the commitment; internally we still have the buffer to fix the real work.

> I'd suggest this stays between you and me, no need to communicate the gap externally."

What this does:

- Explains why scope cuts don't actually solve the problem (technical grounding)
- Offers a face-saving alternative (internal demo)
- Leaves the sponsor a dignified exit (the external commitment is intact)

**Q3: "Can you commit that July 14 is going to hold?"**

The easiest question to answer wrong. If you say "yes, guaranteed" and slip again, you're finished. If you say "no, I can't guarantee anything", the sponsor wonders how on earth they'll sell this up.

> "Here's where I'd put confidence — July 14 to production is 80% confidence right now. That number will either go up to 95%+ or drop to 60% at the July 7 go/no-go, depending on what eval data we see.

> I won't commit '100% certain' to you, because AI projects always have unknowns. But here are three things I will commit to: **I'll update my confidence number for you weekly; if it drops below 70% I escalate immediately; no more quiet slips — any risk reaches you within 24 hours**."

Confidence levels in place of binary commitments are the best gift you can give a sponsor — they can take that number upward to sell it, and you've protected your own flexibility.

---

## Decision 5 — The weeks after, and rebuilding trust

The delay conversation is just the start. The next 2 to 3 weeks are where trust quietly gets observed.

**What to do**

1. **Tighten status density and depth** — go from weekly to twice-weekly, and each update should be more specific than before (concrete metrics, concrete blockers, concrete next steps)
2. **Small commitments need to be 100% hit** — "I'll send notes tomorrow", "eval results Monday morning" — every one of these has to land on time. The sponsor is calibrating your reliability
3. **Push updates proactively** — don't wait for them to ask. If they haven't asked, it means they're watching whether you'll surface things on your own, not that they've stopped caring
4. **Trigger a confidence check-in a week before the new deadline** — don't make the sponsor anxious enough to chase you

**What not to do**

1. **Don't over-apologise** — own it once, then move on. Apologising every meeting afterwards looks unprofessional
2. **Don't over-promise compensation** — don't say "we'll pull the next milestone in to make up for it". In AI projects you don't know what the next milestone will look like; empty promises set you up to fail again
3. **Don't throw the team under the bus** — if the sponsor asks "what went wrong with engineering", the answer is always "this is on me as PM for not setting expectations correctly". Protect your team
4. **Don't avoid the sponsor** — some PMs unconsciously reduce face time after a delay. Wrong move. Show up more often, but show up with substance

---

## A short do / don't

**Do**

- Escalate when slip > 3 days or confidence < 60%
- Sponsor 1:1 first, group setting second
- Open with: time window + bottom line + my recommendation + interrupt me anytime
- Use confidence levels instead of binary commitments
- Own the call; don't push it to engineering or vendor
- Reset the deadline cleanly, don't let it quietly drift
- Hit every small commitment 100% in the weeks after

**Don't**

- Don't wait until the week of the deadline to raise it
- Don't surprise the sponsor in a group meeting
- Don't minimise or hide behind jargon ("we're in fine-tune iteration")
- Don't over-apologise — own it once and move on
- Don't throw the team or vendor under the bus
- Don't promise "we'll pull in the next milestone to make up"
- Don't avoid face time — show up more, not less

---

### When to actually speak up

**1. The self-check trigger**
> Milestone slipped > 3 days, OR confidence < 60%, OR a critical dependency is blocked, OR you've been saying "maybe we catch up next week" for more than a week — any one of these and you open your mouth.

**14. With your PL / direct manager**
> "[Name], heads up I'm talking to the sponsor about a delay this afternoon. Background is [one line]. Proposing [new deadline + go/no-go]. Syncing with you first so if there's pushback you've got the context to cover me. I'll come back and debrief after."

### Downstream stakeholders (UAT / finance)

**15. With downstream teams**
> "[Name], wanted to align with you: the milestone is moving from [original date] to [new date]. The sponsor knows and supports the move. The impact on you is [UAT shift / budget release cadence change]. I'd like to find time this week to walk through the new rhythm."

---

Stepping back: a delay isn't a sign of PM failure — **mishandling the delay communication is**. AI projects always have unknowns; delays are a probable outcome. What separates a good PM from a bad PM isn't whether they've ever delayed a project. It's whether, in the moment of delay, the sponsor feels they've been navigated through it as a partner, or surprised and then watched the PM hunt for excuses.

Every delay is an opportunity to rebuild trust, provided you raise it early, speak directly, don't push blame, and don't make empty promises. A delay handled well leaves the sponsor with higher trust in you than a project that went smoothly — because now they know you don't hide bad news. That is the single most valuable reputation in AI delivery.

# 传递延期消息:当 timeline 必须往后挪

延期是 AI 项目里最常见、也最被处理得最差的一种坏消息。

最常见的原因不是技术,是 PM 自己拖。明明三周前就知道要延期了,但每周 status 上还在写"on track",直到 deadline 前一周才跟 sponsor 说"我们可能要延两周"。Sponsor 听完不光生气,而且开始怀疑你之前所有的 status 是不是都不准。

**延期本身不会让你失去客户信任,延期 + 你早就知道但没说才会**。这是这一节的核心。

下面拆几个决策点。

---

## 决策点 1 — 什么时候算"必须开口"

PM 之所以拖,通常是因为心里有一个想法:"也许这周能赶回来,先观察一下"。这种想法每次都错。AI 项目的特点是,一旦一个 milestone 开始 slip,**slip 会加速,不会自愈**。原因有几个:

- eval 不达标的问题往往是数据或者 prompt 的根本问题,不是再调一周能解决的
- engineer 加班赶进度会引入更多 bug,后面 debug 时间更长
- 外部依赖 (法务 review、IT 给环境、stakeholder 反馈) 一旦延迟过一次,基本会再延迟
- 团队士气一旦开始怀疑这个 deadline,velocity 直接掉

所以判断标准要硬一点。**满足下面任何一条,就该 escalate**:

1. milestone 已经 slip 超过 3 天,而且没有明确的回赶计划
2. 你心里已经在想"也许下周能赶回来"超过一周
3. 关键依赖 (数据、infra、人) 出了未解决的 blocker
4. 你自己心里对原 deadline 的信心已经低于 60%

不到 80% 信心都该说。等到 50% 你已经晚了。

---

## 决策点 2 — 跟谁说,先说

不要在大会上 surprise sponsor。**任何坏消息都要先 1:1 跟 sponsor 说,然后再在群体场合 confirm**。

顺序:

1. **先告诉你的直属老板** (BCG 是 PL、tomoro 是 Dan 这种角色) — 让他先知情,后面 escalation 出问题他能 cover 你
2. **然后 1:1 sponsor** — 单独 15 分钟,不要邮件,不要 Slack 文字
3. **再是 IT 头 / 关键技术 stakeholder** — 因为他可能要给你 unblock
4. **最后才是 status 邮件或者大群** — 这一步是 confirm,不是 announce

为什么这个顺序: sponsor 最讨厌的是从别人嘴里听到自己项目的坏消息。从 IT 头嘴里、从他下属嘴里、从 status 邮件里都不行。他要从你嘴里第一时间听到。

如果你 sponsor 跟 IT 头关系不好 (T15.5 的情况),先告诉 sponsor 这一条更要严格执行 — sponsor 听到 IT 头比他先知道项目要延期,会立刻怀疑你站队 IT。

---

## 决策点 3 — 1:1 sponsor 那 15 分钟怎么开口

这是整个延期沟通最关键的 15 分钟。开场决定 sponsor 的反应模式。

**错的开场**

> "[Name],我有一个 bad news 要跟你说 — 我们这个 milestone 要延两周。"

错在哪: 一句话给出问题没给出 context,sponsor 大脑直接进入防御模式,接下来你说什么他都听不进去。

**对的开场**

> "[Name],占用你 15 分钟。我有一个 timeline 上的事要单独跟你 align,先讲结论,再讲为什么,最后讲我准备怎么处理 — 你随时打断我。

> **结论是: 原计划 6 月 30 日上线的 milestone,我建议往后挪两周到 7 月 14 日**。这个判断是我两天前做出来的,我先内部 stress test 了一下,confirm 是真的需要挪,所以来跟你讲。"

这个开场做了几件事:

- "占用你 15 分钟" — 给 sponsor 一个时长预期,他不会焦虑
- "先讲结论" — 直接给判断,sponsor 知道接下来你不会绕弯
- "我两天前做的判断" — 表明你不是今天才知道,是经过内部推演才来讲
- "我建议" — 用"建议"不用"我们要" — 留出 sponsor 是决策者的空间

**接下来的 5 分钟讲"为什么"**

不要讲一堆细节。讲三件事:

1. 这是一个怎样的延期 (规模、原因、影响)
2. 为什么不是再加班一周能解决的
3. 你已经做了什么动作

> "为什么 — 不是 engineering velocity 的问题。我们这周 eval 跑出来,production-realistic test set 上的 accuracy 是 78%,我们承诺给你的是 85%。

> 差的这 7 个点不是 prompt 调一下能补的,是底层的 retrieval recall 出了问题 — 简单讲,model 找不到该用的文档。这种问题改 prompt 没用,要改 retrieval 架构,大概需要 8 到 10 个工作日。

> 这两周里我们已经做了什么: 我让 Engineer 1 切了一个新 branch 在调 retrieval,Engineer 2 继续保持原路径作为备选,这样如果新方案不行我们还有 B 方案。我没有把人手摊薄,这两个方向都有专人。"

这段话做了几件事:

- 给了具体数字 (78% vs 85%) — sponsor 知道差距是可量化的
- 解释为什么不是赶一下能解决 — 提前堵住"再加把劲行不行"
- 主动展示已经在处理 (A 方案 + B 方案并行) — 让 sponsor 看到你在掌控,不是在 panic

**最后 5 分钟讲"我准备怎么处理"**

> "我建议这样处理。

> 第一,timeline 我建议公开 reset 到 7 月 14 日,而不是 quietly 拖延。原因是: quietly 拖延会让团队失去 anchor,而且如果后面再有 slip 累积起来很难看。Reset 一次,清清楚楚。

> 第二,我建议在 7 月 7 日 (新 deadline 前一周) 跟你和 IT 头开一次 go/no-go,那时候 eval 数据已经出来,我们可以一起判断是上还是再延。

> 第三,这两周的延期会对下游有影响 — 跟业务团队约的 UAT 要顺延,跟 finance 要调一下 Q3 的预算释放节奏。这两件事我来负责沟通,但需要你给我一个 backing — 当他们 push back 的时候,我引用你的话。

> 最后,我想跟你说一件事 — 这次延期是我的 call,我没有把责任推给 engineer 或者 vendor。如果你需要往上一级 escalate,我准备好被问。"

最后那句话是关键招。**主动承担责任,不躲**。Sponsor 最讨厌的不是延期,是 PM 找借口。你主动说"这是我的 call",sponsor 反而会站在你这边帮你顶上面。

---

## 决策点 4 — sponsor 会问的几个尖锐问题怎么答

**Q1: "你三周前不是说 on track 吗?"**

这一题最难答,因为 sponsor 在质疑你的 status 准确度。

**不能撒谎**,也不能轻描淡写。直说:

> "你说得对。三周前我跟你说 on track,那时候我们的 eval 数据是基于小 test set 的,跑出来 86%。我以为这个数字会 hold,实际我们扩到 production-realistic test set 之后,这个数字降到了 78%。这中间我有两个教训:

> 一,小 test set 上的 metric 不能信,我应该早一点 push 到 production-realistic 上测;
> 二,我应该早跟你 flag 这个不确定性,而不是把 86% 当成确定数字。

> 我会调整 status report 的格式,把 eval 的 test set 描述也带上,这样你能看到 metric 背后的 context。"

这种答法做了几件事:

- 承认错误,但落到具体的点 (不是泛泛的"我应该早点说")
- 用具体数字描述问题 (sponsor 觉得你确实理解了问题在哪)
- 主动提出流程改进 (status 加入 test set context) — 让 sponsor 看到你学到了东西

**Q2: "能不能不延期,scope 砍一砍?"**

这是 sponsor 试图保 deadline 的标准动作。要看砍 scope 是不是真能保这个 deadline。**如果不能,要直说不能**。

> "我考虑过这个选项。但实际上砍 scope 在我们这个 case 不会保住 timeline — 原因是 blocker 是 retrieval 架构,不是某一个 feature。砍掉 feature A 我们还是要等 retrieval 修好。

> 但如果你的优先级是 6 月 30 日有一个东西能 demo,我可以给你一个替代方案: 6 月 30 日上一个缩小版本的内部 demo (只对你和 sponsor 圈),然后 7 月 14 日上完整版给最终 user。这样你对外是按承诺走的,内部我们有 buffer 修。

> 这个我建议你和我决定就好,不必跟外部沟通这个差别。"

这种答法做了几件事:

- 解释为什么砍 scope 不直接解决问题 (技术上的依据)
- 主动给一个保面子的替代方案 (内部 demo)
- 留 sponsor 一个体面退路 (对外承诺保住了)

**Q3: "那你能不能 commit 7 月 14 日一定能上?"**

这一题最容易答错。如果你说"可以保证",后面要是再 slip 你死定了。如果你说"不能保证",sponsor 觉得你信心都没有他怎么往上 sell。

> "我可以告诉你我现在的信心 — 7 月 14 日上 production 是 80% 信心,这个信心会在 7 月 7 日的 go/no-go 那天更新到 95% 以上或者降到 60%。

> 我不会跟你 commit '100% 一定上',因为 AI 项目永远有意外。但我可以承诺三件事: **信心值我每周给你更新;低于 70% 我立刻 escalate;不会再有偷偷的 slip,任何 risk 你 24 小时内知道**。"

用信心值代替二元承诺,是给 sponsor 的最好礼物 — 他可以拿着这个数字往上 sell,你保护了自己的灵活度。

---

## 决策点 5 — 延期之后的几周怎么重建信任

延期讲完只是开始。后面 2 到 3 周是关键 — sponsor 的信任在默默观察。

**该做的**

1. **Status 加密也加深** — 从每周加到每周两次,但每次要比之前更具体 (具体的 metric、具体的 blocker、具体的下一步)
2. **小承诺一定做到** — "明天发给你 notes" "下周一发 eval 结果",这种小事必须 100% 准时。Sponsor 在 calibrate 你的可靠度
3. **主动给 update** — 不要等 sponsor 来问。Sponsor 没问代表他在观察你主不主动,不代表他不关心
4. **新 deadline 之前一周再做一次信心 check-in** — 主动触发,不要让 sponsor 紧张到自己找你

**不该做的**

1. **不要过度道歉** — 延期讲一次,认一次,讲完就翻篇。后面每次见面都说"对不起又延期了"会让 sponsor 觉得你不专业
2. **不要乱许补偿** — 不要说"下个 milestone 我们会提前完成补回来"。AI 项目你不知道下个 milestone 会怎样,空头承诺只会再坑自己
3. **不要甩团队** — Sponsor 问"engineer 那边怎么搞的",答案永远是"这是我作为 PM 没设好预期"。保护你的团队
4. **不要躲见面** — 有些 PM 延期之后会下意识减少跟 sponsor 见面的频率。错。要更频繁出现在 sponsor 面前,但出现的时候带着实质内容

---

## 一些 do / don't 速记

**Do**

- Slip 超 3 天或者信心低于 60% 就 escalate
- 1:1 sponsor 先说,再到群体场合
- 开场: 占用时间 + 结论 + 我建议 + 你打断我
- 主动给信心值代替二元承诺
- 主动承担 call,不甩 engineer / vendor
- Reset deadline 要清清楚楚,不要偷偷拖
- 延期之后小承诺要 100% 准时

**Don't**

- 不要拖到 deadline 前一周才说
- 不要在大会上 surprise sponsor
- 不要轻描淡写或者用术语包装 ("我们在 fine-tune iteration 中")
- 不要过度道歉,讲一次认一次就好
- 不要甩团队 / vendor
- 不要承诺"下次提前补回来"
- 不要躲见面 — 反而要更频繁

## 话术速查:15 条传递延期消息直接抄走用的句子

### 决定要不要开口

**1. 自检触发器**
> Milestone slip 超 3 天,或信心低于 60%,或关键依赖出问题,或你心里"也许下周赶回来"已经超过一周 — 任一满足就开口。

### 1:1 sponsor 开场

**2. 标准开场结构**
> "占用你 15 分钟。我有一个 timeline 上的事要单独跟你 align,先讲结论,再讲为什么,最后讲我准备怎么处理 — 你随时打断我。"

**3. 给出结论 + 表明时间差**
> "结论是: 原计划 [日期] 的 milestone,我建议往后挪 [时长] 到 [新日期]。这个判断是我 [N 天] 前做的,我先内部 stress test 过,confirm 是真的需要挪。"

### 讲为什么

**4. 用具体数字描述差距**
> "我们这周 eval 跑出来,production-realistic test set 上 accuracy 是 [X%],承诺给你的是 [Y%]。差的这 [Y-X] 个点不是 prompt 调一下能补的,是 [retrieval / 数据 / 别的根本问题],大概需要 [N 个工作日] 修。"

**5. 主动展示已经在处理**
> "这两周里我已经让 Engineer 1 切新 branch 调 [问题点],Engineer 2 保持原路径作为备选。我没有把人手摊薄,两个方向都有专人。"

### 讲怎么处理

**6. Reset 而不是偷偷拖**
> "我建议公开 reset deadline 到 [新日期],不要偷偷拖。偷偷拖让团队失去 anchor,而且 slip 累积起来很难看。Reset 一次清清楚楚。"

**7. 提议 go/no-go gate**
> "我建议在 [新 deadline 前一周] 跟你和 [IT 头] 开一次 go/no-go,那时候 eval 数据已经出来,我们可以一起判断是上还是再延。"

**8. 主动承担**
> "这次延期是我的 call,我没有把责任推给 engineer 或者 vendor。如果你需要往上一级 escalate,我准备好被问。"

### 应对尖锐问题

**9. 应对"你之前不是说 on track 吗"**
> "你说得对。三周前我说 on track 是基于小 test set,跑出来 86%。扩到 production-realistic 之后降到 78%。我有两个教训: 小 test set 不能信;我应该早跟你 flag 这个不确定性。我会调整 status 格式,把 test set context 带上。"

**10. 应对"砍 scope 行不行"**
> "我考虑过。砍 scope 在我们这个 case 保不住 timeline,因为 blocker 是 [底层架构],不是某个 feature。但如果你优先级是 [原 deadline] 有东西能 demo,我可以提一个缩小版内部 demo,然后 [新 deadline] 给最终 user。"

**11. 应对"你能不能 commit 一定上"**
> "我可以告诉你信心值 — [新 deadline] 上 production 是 80% 信心,会在 [check-in 日期] 更新到 95% 以上或者降到 60%。我不会跟你 commit 100%,但我承诺三件事: 信心每周更新;低于 70% 立刻 escalate;不会再有偷偷的 slip。"

### 长尾重建信任

**12. 加 status 密度**
> "从今天起 status 改为每周两次,一直到 [新 deadline]。每次会带具体的 metric、blocker、下一步。"

**13. 新 deadline 前主动触发 check-in**
> "[新 deadline 前一周] 我想跟你做一次信心 check-in,这样你不用担心被 surprise — 我们到时候一起看 eval 数据。"

### 内部 (告诉你老板)

**14. 跟你的 PL / 直属老板**
> "[Name],我下午要跟 sponsor 谈一个延期。背景是 [一句话]。我准备 propose [新 deadline + go/no-go]。先 sync 给你,有 push back 你帮我 cover;sponsor 那边讲完我立刻回来 debrief。"

### 给下游 stakeholder (UAT / finance)

**15. 跟下游团队沟通延期**
> "[Name],想跟你 align 一下: 我们 milestone 从 [原日期] 挪到 [新日期]。这个决定 sponsor 已经知道并支持。对你们的影响是 [UAT 顺延 / 预算释放节奏调整],我建议我们这周内对一下新的节奏。"

---

往大了说,延期不是 PM 失败的标志 — **延期沟通失败才是**。AI 项目永远有意外,延期是大概率事件。区分好 PM 和坏 PM 的不是有没有延过期,是延期的时候 sponsor 觉得自己是被一起 navigate 过去,还是被 surprise 之后看 PM 找借口。

每次延期是一次重建信任的机会,前提是你早讲、直讲、不甩锅、不空头承诺。一次延期处理得好,sponsor 后面对你的信任会比项目顺利的时候还高 — 因为他知道了你不会藏坏消息。这是 AI delivery 里最值钱的一种 reputation。
