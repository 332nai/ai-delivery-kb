# When PoC fails: telling the client this approach won't work

PoC failure is the **genuine failure** in AI projects, and most PMs don't know how to deliver it.

Delays and missing eval bars are still in the "the project can be done, just not as well" category. PoC failure means: **the current technical approach won't work, and no amount of additional money or people will fix it**.

The conversation is hard in three ways:

1. You need to communicate failure while preserving your (and your team's) reputation
2. You need the sponsor to understand this isn't a capability issue, it's the risk profile specific to AI projects
3. You need to give them a next step — you can't say "it failed" and walk out

Done well, PoC failure is the biggest trust boost (the sponsor knows you won't push something that doesn't work). Done badly, it's the biggest reputation hit (the sponsor feels you wasted months).

A few decision points below.

---

## Decision 1 — How to tell "PoC actually failed" apart from "needs another push"

This is the hardest call, and the easiest to stall on. Two biases pull on the PM at once: sunk cost ("we've invested so much, maybe one more push") and fear ("telling the sponsor it failed is too uncomfortable, let me hold one more week"). These two combined push the PoC well past the point it should have been killed.

**Signals it should be killed** (any 2 of these and you should be killing it):

1. **Core metric has plateaued in PoC** — not 70% slowly climbing to 72%, but 60% flat for two weeks or more
2. **Failure modes are structural, not occasional** — failures aren't edge cases, they're hitting the core use case in the middle
3. **No clear next thing to try** — you and the engineers have exhausted prompting / retrieval / fine-tuning, and no obvious improvement vector remains
4. **External dependencies aren't in place and won't be** — e.g. the high-quality data needed doesn't exist, or the model capability needed isn't there yet
5. **Even if the PoC numbers hit, production clearly won't work** — e.g. latency is 10x the requirement, or cost per query is too high for anyone to use

**Signals that look like failure but are actually just "stuck"**:

- Engineer is stuck on a specific bug this week — that's normal engineering, not PoC failure
- One specific slice of the use case isn't working but other slices are — that's a scope problem, not PoC failure
- Stakeholder feedback is "doesn't feel good enough" without specific metric backing — that's an expectation calibration problem, not PoC failure
- The gap to industry benchmark is large — but the benchmark setup doesn't match yours — that's a framing problem, not PoC failure

**Self-test**: can you state, in one sentence, "PoC failed because X, and X won't be solved by Y, Z, or W"? If you can't say it cleanly, you haven't actually confirmed failure to yourself yet — don't rush to the sponsor.

---

## Decision 2 — Align internally first

Before talking to the sponsor about PoC failure, **align three people internally first**:

1. **Your own PL / direct manager** — most important. They cover your escalation, and very likely co-deliver to the sponsor with you
2. **Engineering lead (or tech lead)** — to confirm your root cause reading is technically right. The worst case is you tell the sponsor "this isn't technically possible", they ask their IT lead for a second opinion, and the IT lead says "you could try X" — your credibility is gone in one sentence
3. **If there's an internal technical contact the sponsor trusts (their head of data, head of AI), have a 1:1 with that person ahead of time** — not for approval, but so they can confirm your judgement when the sponsor goes to verify

**Internal align script** (with your PL):

> "[Name], I need 30 minutes to align on something. My assessment is the PoC has failed — not a fixable-by-incremental-improvement failure, but the technical path doesn't work.

> Evidence is [three signals, specifically]. I've confirmed root cause with [engineering lead], they agree.

> I'd propose talking to the sponsor next Tuesday. I've prepared a deck — the core message is: PoC current path failed + what we learned + 3 forward paths I'm recommending.

> Before I go to the sponsor, I want to align with you on: one, do you confirm my read on failure; two, can you review the deck; three, will you be in the room when I deliver?

> My preference is you in the room but not speaking — your presence tells the sponsor this escalation is an aligned judgement from BCG / tomoro, not me individually panicking."

The PL will usually do a few things: confirm the judgement, review the deck, decide whether to be in the room, sometimes escalate up their own chain. **Don't skip this step.** A PM telling a sponsor about PoC failure without PL backing — the sponsor's first instinct is "does your firm know you're failing here?". Once that doubt is planted, recovering it is very hard.

---

## Decision 3 — Opening with the sponsor (first 5 minutes)

A PoC failure 1:1 is usually 60 to 90 minutes. The first 5 minutes determine everything.

**The worst opener**

> "[Name], I've got bad news — the PoC didn't succeed, we may need to reconsider the whole project."

What's wrong: a single sentence dumps a huge problem on them, their brain locks down, and the next 50 minutes they're panicking and not listening.

**The right opener**

> "[Name], I need 60 minutes to walk through PoC results and the forward path I'm recommending.

> Bottom line first, then evidence, then options. Stop me at any point with questions.

> **Bottom line: our PoC — the current technical path doesn't work. Specifically: [one-sentence root cause]. My assessment is this path isn't going to be fixed by more iteration, so I'm recommending we pivot rather than keep pushing.**

> I'm also bringing 3 forward options, so this conversation isn't 'PoC failed, what do we do' — it's 'PoC taught us something critical, and we decide together which path forward we take'."

What this opener does:

- "60 minutes" — sets the time investment
- "Bottom line first, then evidence, then options" — gives structure
- One-sentence root cause — the sponsor has a mental model in one second
- "Pivot, not keep pushing" — frames this as strategic, not panicked
- "Bringing 3 forward options" — preempts the "now what" in the sponsor's head
- "Decide together" — you're not dumping a problem, you're navigating together

---

## Decision 4 — Walking the evidence (middle 30 minutes)

After the bottom line, the sponsor needs evidence. How professional this evidence section is determines their final read of you.

**Evidence in three layers**:

**Layer 1 (5 minutes): what we tried**

> "Over the past [N months], the PoC ran in [M] phases. Phase 1 [what we did, result]; phase 2 [what we did, result]; phase 3 [what we did, result].

> In each phase we systematically tried four dimensions — prompting / retrieval / fine-tuning / data. I've prepared a grid for you: dimensions on the horizontal, attempts on the vertical, each cell has metric and conclusion.

> The point of this page is to show you we didn't try one or two methods and give up. We systematically explored the viable directions."

**Key: show the sponsor a systematic grid.** The sponsor's biggest unspoken doubt is "did the PM actually try". The grid kills that doubt in one second — it visibly shows exhaustive exploration.

**Layer 2 (10 minutes): why it doesn't work, specifically**

> "The root cause — let me explain with an analogy. What our use case needs the model to do is [analogy, e.g. 'in a workflow with 50 steps, every step has to be 100% correct to reach the next'].

> The current model capability limit is: 92% accuracy per step, which is the frontier. But chain 50 steps and end-to-end accuracy is 0.92^50 = 1.5%.

> This isn't solvable by fine-tuning — 92% is already at frontier model level. It isn't solvable by retrieval — retrieval doesn't affect the reasoning step. It isn't solvable by multi-agent — each agent still has to do the reasoning step.

> Bottom line: the physical limit of current model capability and what our use case needs are several orders of magnitude apart. That gap isn't closeable by iteration."

**Key: use language the sponsor can grasp to explain root cause.** The analogy (50 steps, 0.92^50) is the heart of this. If the sponsor doesn't walk away with a clear mental model, they won't trust your judgement.

**Layer 3 (5 minutes): why we didn't see this wall earlier**

The most sensitive section. The sponsor is silently thinking: "so why did it take you N months to find this".

> "I have to own this section: we didn't see this wall earlier, for two reasons.

> One, **our early PoC tests were all single-step**. Each step looked fine, we didn't chain end-to-end. That's a PoC design gap.

> Two, **we didn't see case studies in the industry — but I read that as 'novel application'**, when in reality it means everyone hits this wall. I should have challenged that assumption earlier.

> Both of these I own — not pushing them to engineering or vendors."

**Owning it is critical.** The sponsor will be silent for a few seconds, then usually says "fair enough, what's next". If you get defensive or pass blame here, every drop of remaining trust erodes.

---

## Decision 5 — Walking the forward options (last 20 minutes)

Forward is where this conversation actually delivers value. Never finish "PoC failed" and walk out.

**At least 3 options**:

**Option 1 — pivot to an adjacent use case (recommended)**

> "Option 1 is to pivot — take what we've learned and apply it to an adjacent use case whose complexity better fits current model capability.

> Specifically: original use case was [50-step full automation], pivot target is [10 steps + human review]. End-to-end reliability we can get to 90%+, and business value is around 60-70% of original.

> Timeline: rescope 2 weeks + new PoC 8-10 weeks = roughly 3 months. Investment is about 30% less than original plan, because a lot of infra and learning carries over."

**Option 2 — park the project, wait for model capability to catch up**

> "Option 2 is to park — we close the project, write up the learnings into an internal report, wait 18 to 24 months for model capability to catch up.

> Upside is it's honest, no further spend. Downside is no deliverable, and your job selling this upward is harder."

**Option 3 — re-frame as human-AI hybrid, lower the ambition**

> "Option 3 is to keep the project but reset the target — from 'full automation' to 'AI-assist + human-in-the-loop'. Specifically: AI proposes candidate answer + confidence score, human accepts or rejects.

> End-to-end quality is guaranteed, but throughput is about 30-40% of the original plan, because humans are the bottleneck. Also needs long-term commitment of human resource from the business team."

**How to give the recommendation**

> "Personally I recommend Option 1, because: it preserves 60-70% of business value; it leverages the past few months of learning; it gives you a narrative you can declare success on.

> But Options 1 and 3 are both viable, I've prepared detailed plans for both. Option 2 isn't a strawman — in some contexts (e.g. if your strategy has shifted this year), parking is the reasonable call."

**Proactively acknowledge the sponsor's political reality**

> "I want to acknowledge something — this conversation isn't just a technical decision. You need to sell this upward, and to the business team.

> Whichever option you pick, I'll help you draft the communications. You don't navigate this alone this week — we're in it together."

That last line is the single most important sentence of this conversation. **What the sponsor is thinking about isn't just the project — it's how they explain this to their own boss.** When you proactively offer to help with that navigation, the sponsor immediately feels you're a partner, not a vendor.

---

## Decision 6 — Common sponsor reactions and how to handle them

**Reaction A: Sponsor panics — "What about the money we've already invested?"**

> "I understand. What we've invested is sunk cost — no matter which option you pick, the sunk doesn't change, but the forward investment is controllable.

> Let me give you an angle — current sunk is roughly [N], the PoC investment. If we keep pushing the failed path, additional spend is roughly [M] and most likely still fails. Option 1 (pivot) additional spend is [P] and delivers 60-70% of value. On forward economics, Option 1 is net positive.

> Let the sunk go. Focus forward."

**Reaction B: Sponsor doubts — "Are you sure it doesn't work? What about trying X?"**

X is usually a buzzword the sponsor has picked up (multi-agent, a new model, fine-tuning). **Don't dismiss it, engage with it directly.**

> "X is a fair question. We specifically evaluated X — [a specific technical response on why X doesn't solve the root cause in our context].

> But I don't want to say 'no possibility' — if you'd strongly like to try X, I can add 2 weeks for a micro-PoC, see whether X breaks the plateau. Cost is +2 weeks on timeline, and I'll be direct: my prior is X won't fix this specific failure mode, but if you want to try, I support it."

**Proactively offering a small experiment** is the best way to defuse "are you sure". Usually when the sponsor asks this, they're not actually asking you to try it — they're confirming you've seriously considered it. After you offer the micro-PoC, they'll typically say "OK, no need".

**Reaction C: Sponsor gets emotional — "I can't take this to my boss"**

This is the most emotional moment of the conversation. **Don't immediately problem-solve — empathise first.**

> "[Name], I hear you. This isn't easy for you internally, I get that.

> We don't have to decide today how to communicate this upward. Suggested approach: today we align on direction (which option); I draft an internal narrative for you to review this week, core message is [one sentence] with talking points; you read it, then we decide timing.

> Take the week to sleep on it — entirely fine, let's sync Friday."

Proactively proposing a cooling-off period + drafting the upward narrative is the best de-escalation for emotional reactions.

---

## A short do / don't

**Do**

- Judge PoC failure by 5 hard signals — 2 of them triggers process
- Align internally first (PL + engineering lead + sponsor's trusted technical contact)
- Open with: 60 minutes + bottom line + evidence + options + decide together
- Three-layer evidence: what we tried + why it doesn't work + why we didn't see it earlier
- Use an analogy to give the sponsor a mental model
- Proactively own your process gap
- At least 3 forward options with a recommendation
- Proactively offer to help draft the upward narrative
- When sponsor asks "try X", offer a 2-week micro-PoC rather than dismissing

**Don't**

- Don't minimise ("PoC didn't go great")
- Don't wait until sunk cost has piled up too high to raise
- Don't go into the meeting without forward options
- Don't let the sponsor see evidence cold in the meeting (send a preview deck a day before)
- Don't throw engineering / vendor under the bus
- Don't dismiss "try X" — engage with it directly
- Don't immediately problem-solve emotional reactions — empathise first

Stepping back: PoC failure is the most humbling moment in AI delivery, and also where a PM's craft actually shows.

A technical path not working is a fact, not a fault. What separates a good PM from a bad PM isn't whether they've ever had a PoC fail. It's whether, when a PoC fails, the client walks out of the room feeling they were navigated through it, or feeling the PM wasted months of their time.

The highest-craft PoC failure conversation: the sponsor walks out and tells their own boss, "the project didn't work out, but I learned where AI's real capability limit is, and the vendor / team I want for the next attempt is the same one". The PMs who can produce that reaction in a client are rare in this industry — because most PMs either hide failures or pass blame.

Be honest, structured, and forward-looking once — clients remember you for a decade.

# PoC 失败:跟客户讲这条路走不通

PoC 失败是 AI 项目里**真正可能存在的失败**,但绝大多数 PM 不知道怎么讲。

延期和 eval 不达标都还在"项目能做出来,只是没那么好"的范畴里。PoC 失败的意思是: **当前这条路在技术上走不通,不是再投钱再加人能解决的**。

这种对话难在三个地方:

1. 你要表达失败但保住自己 (和团队) 的声誉
2. 你要让 sponsor 知道这不是能力问题,是 AI 项目特有的风险
3. 你要给出下一步 — 不能讲完"失败了"然后走出会议室

讲得好,PoC 失败是信任的最大提升 (sponsor 知道你不会硬推不行的方案)。讲得差,这是声誉的最大打击 (sponsor 觉得你浪费了他几个月)。

下面拆几个决策点。

---

## 决策点 1 — 怎么判断 "PoC 真的失败了" 还是 "需要再 push 一下"

这是最难的判断,也是最容易拖延的地方。PM 心里有两种偏见同时拉扯: 一种是沉没成本 (已经投了这么多,再 push 一下也许行),一种是害怕 (跟 sponsor 讲失败太尴尬,再撑一周)。这两种偏见加起来,PM 会把 PoC 拖远超它该被砍的时间。

**该砍的信号** (满足任意 2 个就该砍):

1. **核心 metric 在 PoC 阶段已经卡在低位平台** — 不是 70% 慢慢爬到 72%,是 60% 不动两周以上
2. **失败模式是结构性的,不是零星的** — 失败 case 不是边缘 case,是核心 use case 的中段就在失败
3. **没有清晰的下一个尝试** — 你和 engineer 已经穷举了 prompting / retrieval / fine-tuning 三条路,都没明显改进空间
4. **依赖的外部条件不到位且不会到位** — 比如需要的高质量数据根本不存在,或者需要的 model 能力还没到
5. **就算 PoC 数字达标,production 也明显不可行** — 比如 latency 是要求的 10 倍,或者每次 query 的 cost 高到没人会用

**容易被误判成失败,实际只是卡住的信号**:

- Engineer 这周卡在一个具体 bug 上 — 这是正常 engineering,不是 PoC 失败
- 一个具体的 use case 切片跑不通 — 但其他切片跑得通 — 这是 scope 问题,不是 PoC 失败
- Stakeholder 给的反馈是"感觉不够好" — 没有具体的 metric 支持 — 这是预期校准问题,不是 PoC 失败
- 跟行业 benchmark 比差距大 — 但 benchmark 用的 setup 跟你的根本不一样 — 这是表述问题,不是 PoC 失败

**自检**: 你能不能用一句话讲清楚 "PoC 失败是因为 X,X 不会通过 Y / Z / W 任何一种方式解决"。如果讲不清,你心里其实还没确认失败,不要急着去找 sponsor。

---

## 决策点 2 — 内部 align,先

跟 sponsor 讲 PoC 失败之前,**先内部 align 三个人**:

1. **你自己的 PL / 直属老板** — 最关键。他 cover 你的 escalation,而且很可能要一起跟 sponsor 讲
2. **engineering lead (or tech lead)** — 让他确认你对根本原因的判断在技术上是对的。最怕的是你跟 sponsor 讲"技术上做不通",sponsor 找他的 IT 头来 second opinion,IT 头一句"其实可以试 X"你立刻信用扫地
3. **如果有 sponsor 信任的内部技术 contact (比如他的 head of data 或者 head of AI),提前 1:1 跟这个人谈** — 不是去要批准,是让他在 sponsor 找他验证的时候,能确认你的判断

**内部 align 的话术** (跟你的 PL):

> "[Name],我需要 30 分钟跟你 align 一件事。我评估我们 PoC 失败了 — 不是小修小补能修的失败,是技术路径走不通。

> 我的依据是 [三个信号,具体讲]。我跟 [engineering lead] 确认过根本原因,他同意我的判断。

> 我想 propose 下周二跟 sponsor 讲。我准备了一个 deck,核心 message 是: PoC 当前路径失败 + 我们学到了什么 + 我建议的 3 个前进路径。

> 在我去找 sponsor 之前,我想先跟你 align: 一,你确认我对失败的判断;二,你帮我看 deck;三,我跟 sponsor 讲的时候,你在场吗?

> 我个人偏好是你在场但不主动讲 — 你的存在告诉 sponsor 这个 escalation 是 BCG / tomoro 内部一致的判断,不是我个人慌乱。"

PL 听完通常会做几件事: 确认判断、review deck、决定要不要在场、有时候要往他自己上一级 escalate。**不要跳过这一步**。PM 跟 sponsor 讲 PoC 失败的时候没有 PL 撑腰,sponsor 第一反应是"你的 firm 知道你在这里 fail 吗" — 这个 doubt 一旦种下你后面很难恢复。

---

## 决策点 3 — 跟 sponsor 讲的开场 (前 5 分钟)

PoC 失败的 1:1 通常 60 到 90 分钟。前 5 分钟决定一切。

**最差的开场**

> "[Name],我有一个 bad news — PoC 没成功,我们可能要 reconsider 整个项目。"

错在哪: 一句话扔出一个巨大的问题,sponsor 大脑直接 lock down,接下来 50 分钟他都在慌听不进去。

**对的开场**

> "[Name],我需要 60 分钟跟你 walk through PoC 的结果 + 我建议的前进路径。

> 我先讲结论,然后讲证据,最后讲选项。如果你想在任何一个点上 stop me 问问题,随时。

> **结论是: 我们这个 PoC,当前的技术路径走不通。具体讲: [一句话核心根本原因]。我评估这条路不是再迭代能修的,所以我建议我们转向,而不是继续 push**。

> 我同时也带来了 3 个前进选项,所以这个对话不是 'PoC 失败,我们怎么办' — 是 'PoC 让我们学到了关键的东西,基于这个学习我们一起决定下一步走哪条路'。"

这个开场做了几件事:

- "60 分钟" — 让 sponsor 知道时间投入
- "先结论再证据再选项" — 给一个结构化的预期
- 一句话讲清根本原因 — sponsor 一秒钟内有心理模型
- "转向,而不是继续 push" — 立刻表明这不是慌乱是策略决定
- "我带来了 3 个前进选项" — 提前回答 sponsor 心里的"现在怎么办"
- "一起决定" — 表明你不是来扔 problem,是来一起 navigate

---

## 决策点 4 — 讲证据 (中间 30 分钟)

Sponsor 听完结论会需要证据才能信。证据部分讲得专业不专业,决定 sponsor 对你的最终信任。

**证据要分三层讲**:

**第一层 (5 分钟): 我们试了什么**

> "过去 [N 个月],我们的 PoC 分了 [M] 个阶段做。阶段一 [做了什么,结果];阶段二 [做了什么,结果];阶段三 [做了什么,结果]。

> 每个阶段我们都系统性地试了 prompting / retrieval / fine-tuning / data 这四个维度。我准备了一个 grid 给你看,横轴是维度,纵轴是尝试,每个 cell 里是 metric 和结论。

> 这一页的目的是让你看到: 我们不是只试了一两种方法就放弃,我们系统性地把可行的方向都试过了。"

**关键: 给 sponsor 看一个系统性 grid**。Sponsor 心里最大的怀疑是"是不是 PM 没努力" — 一个 grid 让他立刻明白这是被详尽探索过的,不是放弃。

**第二层 (10 分钟): 为什么走不通,具体讲**

> "根本原因是这样的 — 我用一个类比讲。我们这个 use case 需要 model 做的事,是 [类比,比如 '在一个有 50 个 step 的工作流里,每个 step 都要 100% 正确才能完成下一个']。

> 当前 model 能力的 limitation 是: 每个 step 我们能做到 92% accuracy,这是当前最高水平。但 50 个 step 串起来,端到端 accuracy 是 0.92^50 = 1.5%。

> 这不是 fine-tuning 能解决的 — 因为 92% 已经是前沿 model 的水平。也不是 retrieval 能解决的 — retrieval 不影响 reasoning step。也不是 multi-agent 能解决的 — 每个 agent 还是要做 reasoning step。

> 底线: 当前 model 能力的物理边界,跟我们 use case 需要的边界之间,差距是几个数量级。这种差距不是迭代能补的。"

**关键: 用 sponsor 能抓住的语言讲根本原因**。类比 (50 个 step、0.92^50) 是这种讲法的核心。如果 sponsor 听完没有一个清晰的心理模型在脑子里,他不会信你的判断。

**第三层 (5 分钟): 为什么我们之前没看到这个墙**

这是最敏感的一段。Sponsor 心里在想: "那你为什么花了 N 个月才发现"。

> "我必须 own 这一段: 我们之前没看到这个墙,有两个原因。

> 一,**PoC 早期阶段我们的测试都是 single-step 的**。每个 step 看起来都很好,我们没串起来跑端到端。这是 PoC 设计的 gap。

> 二,**我们在行业上没看到 case study 是因为别人也没解决这个问题**。我之前以为'没看到'是因为这是新颖应用,实际上'没看到'是因为大家都在这里碰壁了。我应该早一点挑战这个假设。

> 这两个我都 own,不甩给 engineer / vendor。"

**主动 own 是关键**。sponsor 听完会有几秒钟沉默,然后通常会说"fair enough,接下来怎么办"。如果你在这里 defensive 或者甩锅,sponsor 后面所有的信任都会 erode。

---

## 决策点 5 — 讲前进选项 (最后 20 分钟)

讲前进是这个对话真正交付价值的部分。永远不要讲完"PoC 失败"就走出会议室。

**至少 3 个选项**:

**选项 1 — 转向相邻 use case (推荐)**

> "选项 1 是转向 — 我们 take 我们学到的东西,用到一个相邻的 use case 上,这个 use case 的复杂度更适合当前 model 能力。

> 具体讲: 我们原 use case 是 [50 step 全自动],转向后的 use case 是 [10 step + 人工 review]。后者的端到端可靠度我们能做到 90% 以上,而且业务价值大概是原来的 60% 到 70%。

> Timeline: 重新 scope 2 周 + 新 PoC 8 到 10 周 = 大概 3 个月。投资比原计划少 30%,因为很多 infra 和学习能复用。"

**选项 2 — 暂停项目,等 model 能力跟上**

> "选项 2 是暂停 — 我们关掉这个项目,把学到的写成内部 report,等 18 到 24 个月 model 能力赶上。

> 这个选项的好处是诚实,不浪费钱。坏处是没有交付物,你的 sponsor 心里和往上面 sell 会比较难。"

**选项 3 — 重新定义为人机协作,降低 ambition**

> "选项 3 是保留项目,但重新设定目标 — 从'全自动'改成 'AI 辅助 + 人工兜底'。具体讲: AI 给候选答案 + 给置信度,人决定是否接受。

> 这个选项的端到端质量能保证,但吞吐量大概是原计划的 30% 到 40%,因为人是瓶颈。也需要业务团队长期投入人力。"

**给推荐的方式**

> "我个人推荐选项 1,原因是: 它保留了 60% 到 70% 的业务价值;它利用了我们这几个月的学习;它给你一个能宣告成功的叙事。

> 但选项 1 和选项 3 都是 viable,我都准备了详细计划。选项 2 我列出来不是 strawman — 在某些 context 下 (比如你们今年战略已经转向),暂停是合理的。"

**主动 acknowledge sponsor 的政治现实**

> "我也想 acknowledge 一件事 — 这个对话不只是技术决策,你需要往上 sell,需要 sell 到业务团队。

> 不管你选哪个选项,我准备帮你 draft 沟通材料。你这一周不要 alone navigate 这个 — 我们一起。"

最后这句话是这个对话最重要的一句话。**Sponsor 心里在想的不只是项目,是他怎么跟他老板解释**。你主动 offer 帮他 navigate 这个,sponsor 立刻 feel 到你是合作伙伴不是供应商。

## 决策点 6 — 跟 sponsor 不同典型反应怎么应对

**反应 A: Sponsor 慌 — "那我们已经投了的钱怎么办?"**

> "这个我理解。我们已经投入的成本是沉没的 — 不管选哪个选项,沉没成本不会变,但前向投入是可控的。

> 一个角度帮你想 — 当前沉没大概 [N 万],这是 PoC 投入。如果继续 push 失败的路径,额外投入会是 [M 万] 而且大概率仍然失败。选项 1 (转向) 额外投入 [P 万],预期交付 60% 到 70% 价值。从前向经济学看,选项 1 仍然净正向。

> 沉没成本我们让它去,聚焦前向。"

**反应 B: Sponsor 怀疑 — "你确定真的走不通?是不是再试试 X?"**

X 通常是 sponsor 自己听来的某个 buzzword (multi-agent、新模型、fine-tuning)。**不要直接否定,但要正面回应**。

> "X 是一个公平的问题。我们专门评估过 X,具体讲: [一段技术 specific 的回应,讲为什么 X 在我们的 context 不解决根本原因]。

> 但我不想说'完全没可能' — 如果你强烈想试 X,我可以加 2 周做一个 micro-PoC,看 X 是否能突破当前 plateau。代价是 timeline 加 2 周,我也想跟你直说: 我个人先验是 X 不能修这个 specific 失败模式,但你想试我 support。"

**主动 offer 一个 small experiment** 是化解"你确定吗"的最好方式。Sponsor 通常问这种问题不是真的要让你试,是要确认你认真考虑过。Offer micro-PoC 之后他多半会说"好那不用了"。

**反应 C: Sponsor 情绪化 — "这件事我没法跟我老板交代"**

这是对话最 emotional 的时刻。**不要立刻给方案,先共情**。

> "[Name],我听到你了。这个对你内部来说不容易,这一点我清楚。

> 我们今天不必现在就 decide 怎么 communicate 上去 — 我建议这样: 今天先 align 大方向是哪个选项;然后我这周内 draft 一份内部叙事给你 review,核心 message 是 [一句话],带 talking points;你看完之后我们再决定 timing。

> 你需要这周 sleep on 这个决定我完全 okay,我们周五再 sync。"

主动 propose 一个冷却期 + 帮 sponsor draft 沟通材料,是 emotional 反应最好的化解。

## 一些 do / don't 速记

**Do**

- 判断 PoC 失败用 5 个硬信号,满足 2 个再走流程
- 跟 sponsor 讲之前先内部 align (PL + engineering lead + sponsor 信任的技术 contact)
- 开场: 60 分钟 + 结论 + 证据 + 选项 + 一起决定
- 证据三层: 试了什么 + 为什么走不通 + 为什么之前没看到
- 用类比让 sponsor 有心理模型
- 主动 own 自己的流程 gap
- 至少 3 个前进选项,带推荐
- 主动 offer 帮 sponsor draft 内部叙事
- Sponsor "再试试 X" 主动 offer micro-PoC (2 周) 而不是直接否定

**Don't**

- 不要轻描淡写 ("PoC 不太理想")
- 不要拖到沉没成本累积太久才讲
- 不要不带前进选项就去开会
- 不要让 sponsor 在对话中 surprise 看到证据 (deck 提前一天发预览)
- 不要甩 engineer / vendor
- 不要直接否定 sponsor 的"再试试 X" — 正面回应
- 不要立刻给方案 sponsor 的 emotional 反应 — 先共情

## 话术速查:14 条 PoC 失败直接抄走用的句子

### 自检

**1. 判断 PoC 真失败的 5 个信号**
> 满足 2 个以上即考虑砍: ① 核心 metric 卡在低位平台 2 周以上;② 失败模式是结构性的不是边缘 case;③ 没有清晰的下一个尝试 (穷举了 prompting / retrieval / fine-tuning);④ 依赖的外部条件不到位且不会到位;⑤ 即使 PoC 数字达标,production 也明显不可行 (latency / cost)。

### 内部 align

**2. 跟你的 PL 讲**
> "我需要 30 分钟。我评估 PoC 失败,不是小修小补能修。依据是 [3 个信号]。跟 engineering lead 确认过根本原因。我想下周二跟 sponsor 讲,准备了 deck,核心是 PoC 失败 + 学习 + 3 个前进选项。在我去之前: 一你确认判断;二 review deck;三你在场但不主动讲,表明 alignment。"

### 开场

**3. 60 分钟 1:1 标准开场**
> "我需要 60 分钟 walk through PoC 结果和前进路径。先结论再证据再选项,随时打断我。结论: PoC 当前技术路径走不通,具体是 [一句话根本原因]。我建议转向不是继续 push。我带了 3 个前进选项,所以今天不是 'PoC 失败怎么办' 是 '一起决定下一步走哪条'。"

### 证据

**4. 第一层 — 试了什么 (用 grid)**
> "过去 N 个月 PoC 分 M 个阶段,系统性试了 prompting / retrieval / fine-tuning / data 四个维度。我准备了一个 grid: 横轴维度,纵轴尝试,每个 cell 是 metric 和结论。让你看到我们不是只试一两种就放弃。"

**5. 第二层 — 为什么走不通 (用类比)**
> "Use case 需要的是 [50 step 全自动,每 step 都要正确]。Model 当前能力是 92% 每个 step。串起来端到端 = 0.92^50 = 1.5%。这不是 fine-tuning / retrieval / multi-agent 能解决 — 都不影响每个 step reasoning 的上限。"

**6. 第三层 — 为什么之前没看到**
> "我必须 own: 一,PoC 早期我们都是 single-step 测试,没串起来跑端到端,这是 PoC 设计 gap;二,行业上'没看到 case study'我以为是新颖应用,实际是大家在这里碰壁,我应该早一点挑战。这两个我 own,不甩 engineer / vendor。"

### 前进选项

**7. 选项 1 — 转向相邻**
> "选项 1: 转向相邻 use case。原方案是 [50 step 全自动],转向后是 [10 step + 人工 review]。端到端可靠度 90% 以上,业务价值 60% 到 70%。Timeline 3 个月,投资比原计划少 30%,infra 和学习能复用。"

**8. 选项 2 — 暂停**
> "选项 2: 暂停项目,关掉 + 写内部学习 report,等 18 到 24 个月 model 能力赶上。好处诚实不浪费钱,坏处没有交付物,往上 sell 难。"

**9. 选项 3 — 重新定义人机协作**
> "选项 3: 保留项目,重新设定目标从'全自动'到 'AI 辅助 + 人工兜底'。AI 给候选 + 置信度,人接受或拒绝。端到端质量保证,但吞吐量 30% 到 40%,需要业务团队长期投入人力。"

**10. 给推荐**
> "我推荐选项 1: 保留 60% 到 70% 业务价值 + 利用学习 + 给你一个能宣告成功的叙事。选项 3 也 viable。选项 2 不是 strawman — 某些 context 下 (战略转向) 暂停是合理的。"

### 帮 sponsor 往上 sell

**11. 主动 offer 帮 sponsor draft 叙事**
> "这个不只是技术决策,你需要往上 sell,sell 到业务团队。不管选哪个选项,我帮你 draft 沟通材料。这周不要 alone navigate — 我们一起。"

### 应对反应

**12. 应对慌 ("我们投了的钱怎么办")**
> "沉没成本是沉没的,不管选哪个选项都不变,但前向投入可控。当前沉没 N 万,继续 push 失败路径额外 M 万仍失败。转向额外 P 万,交付 60% 到 70% 价值。前向经济学看,转向净正向。沉没成本让它去,聚焦前向。"

**13. 应对"再试试 X" (主动 offer micro-PoC)**
> "X 我们专门评估过 — [一段 specific 推理]。我个人先验是 X 不解决根本原因,但你想试我 support: 加 2 周做 micro-PoC,看 X 是否能突破 plateau。代价 timeline +2 周,我直说我的先验,你来决定。"

**14. 应对 emotional ("没法跟老板交代")**
> "我听到你了,这个对你内部不容易。我们今天不必现在 decide communicate 上去 — 先 align 大方向哪个选项;我这周 draft 内部叙事给你 review,核心 message 是 [一句话] 带 talking points;你看完再 decide timing。你需要 sleep on it 完全 okay,周五再 sync。"

---

往大了说,PoC 失败是 AI delivery 里最令人谦卑的时刻,也是 PM 真正展示功夫的时刻。

技术路径走不通是一个事实,不是一个过错。区分好 PM 和坏 PM 的,不是"有没有 PoC 失败过",是"PoC 失败的时候,客户走出会议室的时候是觉得自己被一起 navigate 过去,还是觉得自己被 PM 浪费了几个月"。

最高级的 PoC 失败对话,sponsor 出门之后跟自己老板讲: "我们今年这个项目没成,但我学到了 AI 的真实能力边界,而且我这个 vendor / 团队下次还会找他们做"。能让客户产生这种判断的 PM,在 AI 这一行里凤毛麟角 — 因为绝大多数 PM 要么藏失败要么甩锅。

你诚实、有结构、面向前进一次,客户记你十年。
