**Definition**

AI engagements typically fall into one of three modes: proof of concept (POC), pilot, or production. The labels are used loosely in conversation, but they imply very different commitments around scope, quality, infrastructure, support, and what "done" means. Confusing them, in either direction, is one of the most expensive mistakes a delivery team can make.

**Why it matters in delivery**

The single most common scope dispute in AI consulting is the gap between what the client thought they bought and what the team thought they sold. The client uses the word "pilot" to mean "the real thing, just smaller". The team uses the same word to mean "a controlled experiment with no production obligations". Both are valid interpretations of the word, and the SOW rarely clarifies which one was meant. The clarification has to happen verbally, in week one, before the team builds the wrong thing.

**How it works**

The three modes differ on a small number of dimensions. The table below is the cheat sheet to walk through in the kickoff meeting.

| Dimension | POC | Pilot | Production |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal | Show it is possible | Test with real users in a constrained way | Run reliably for all intended users |
| Users | The project team and a few demo audiences | A small, named group of real users | The full intended user base |
| Data | Sample, synthetic, or curated | Real data, possibly with safeguards | Real data, full volume |
| Quality bar | Compelling on the demo path | Acceptable on the typical path | Acceptable on the full distribution including edge cases |
| Infrastructure | Whatever runs | Limited but real | Hardened, monitored, on supported platforms |
| Support model | None | Best efforts during business hours | SLAs, on call, incident response |
| Evals | Light, often demo based | Defined, run periodically | Continuous, automated, regression tested |
| Security and compliance | Often deferred | Partially addressed | Fully addressed and audited |
| Handover | Walkthrough deck | Documentation and training | Operational runbooks and support handover |
| Typical fee multiplier | 1x | 2 to 4x | 5 to 10x |

The fee multipliers are illustrative, not law, but the order of magnitude is real. The fact that pilot costs three times POC and production costs five times pilot is a useful sanity check when scoping the next phase.

**What goes wrong**

A few common patterns of confusion, each of which has burned someone:

*POC sold as pilot.* The team built something for the demo audience, the client interpreted "pilot" as "limited real users". When real users showed up, the system did not survive contact with edge cases. The team is asked why "the pilot is so unreliable", and the answer is "because it was a POC", which sounds like an excuse.

*Pilot sold as production.* The team built something that works in the controlled pilot setting. The client expects to "just turn it on for everyone" at the end of the pilot. The team explains that production hardening is a separate phase, with separate effort, separate cost, separate timeline. The conversation is awkward because the SOW did not say this.

*Production scope, pilot timeline.* The team agreed to a 12 week production rollout because the salesperson said "12 weeks is what the client wants to hear". This is not a labelling problem, it is a feasibility problem, and labelling cannot rescue it.

**How to use the table in conversation**

In the kickoff, walk through the table with the client and ask, on each row, "where on the spectrum are we for this engagement?" The answers will not all be the same column. Many real engagements are "pilot quality on data, but POC quality on infrastructure" or "production scope on security, but pilot scope on support model". That is fine, as long as it is made explicit. The trap is letting all 10 rows be ambiguous and discovering in week six that the team and the client filled them in differently.

A practical artifact is a one page "delivery mode matrix" with the 10 rows, the three columns, and a marker showing where this engagement sits on each. Both sides sign off on it. It takes 30 minutes to produce and saves weeks of argument.

**PM decision points**

- Have I made the team's interpretation of POC, pilot, or production explicit in writing, or am I relying on the word in the SOW?
- For each of the 10 dimensions, where does this engagement actually sit? Are there inconsistencies between dimensions?
- If the client wants to upgrade mode mid project, how is that re-priced and re-scoped?

**Watch outs**

- Beware the word "production ready". It almost always means different things to different people. Replace it with specific commitments on each dimension.
- POC results do not project to production performance. A POC at 85 percent demo accuracy is not a production system at 85 percent accuracy, it is a project that has not yet faced edge cases.
- Clients sometimes use "pilot" to manage internal political risk, even when the technical scope is production. Listen for whether "pilot" is a real constraint or a label of convenience.

**Takeaway: POC, pilot, and production are not synonyms. Walk through the 10 dimension table with the client in week one, and get a signed off matrix. The 30 minutes spent here is the cheapest scope insurance available.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine being asked to deliver an AI booking assistant for Qantas. The SOW uses the word "pilot" throughout. The fee is sized for a pilot. The timeline is 14 weeks. The client sponsor uses the word "pilot" too, but in conversation it becomes clear they expect to make the assistant available to all Platinum members at the end of the engagement.

The team's interpretation of pilot is: 200 to 500 selected Platinum members, limited to a defined set of booking flows, business hours support only, security review deferred to a follow on production phase. The client's interpretation is: every Platinum member, all booking flows, 24x7 because Qantas operates 24x7, security signed off before launch.

These two definitions describe two completely different projects. The team's version costs and takes the pilot fee and 14 weeks. The client's version is a production system that would reasonably take 9 to 12 months and four to five times the fee.

The right move in week one is to produce the delivery mode matrix described above, walk through it with the sponsor, and surface the gap politely. The framing is not "you misunderstood us" or "we cannot do what you said". The framing is "we want to make sure we are aligned on what we are building, and we have noticed a few places where the assumptions might differ. Let's go through them together".

In most cases, the gap is resolved by descoping the engagement to a genuine pilot (smaller user group, fewer flows, deferred security) while planning a separate production phase for later. The client is rarely upset by this, because it is presented as protecting the success of the work rather than as backing away from a promise.

If the gap had been left unaddressed, the team would have spent 10 weeks building a pilot, presented results, and then heard "great, now let us turn it on for everyone". At that moment the team would have to explain that the pilot was not designed for that, which is the worst possible moment to have the conversation.

The lesson for AI delivery is that the words POC, pilot, and production are useful only when both sides have written down what they mean by them. The matrix is a 30 minute artifact that prevents the most expensive misunderstanding in this line of work.

**定义**

AI 项目通常落在三种模式之一: POC (proof of concept, 概念验证)、pilot (试点)、production (生产)。这些词在对话里用得很松, 但它们暗示的**范围、质量、基础设施、支持、"完成"的定义**完全不同。把它们搞混 (任何方向上的搞混) 是一个交付团队能犯的最贵的错误之一。

**为何在交付中重要**

AI 咨询里最常见的单一范围争议, 是**客户以为买到的**和**团队以为卖出的**之间的差距。客户说 "pilot" 意思是 "真正的东西, 只是规模小一点"。团队说同一个词意思是 "有控制的实验, 没有生产义务"。两种解读都说得通, SOW 也很少澄清是哪一种。澄清必须**口头发生, 在第一周, 在团队建错东西之前**。

**运作原理**

三种模式在少数几个维度上不同。下面这张表是 kickoff 会议上**应该一行一行走一遍**的备忘单。

| 维度 | POC | Pilot | Production |
|---|---|---|---|
| 目标 | 证明可行 | 在受控范围内用真实用户测试 | 对全部目标用户可靠运行 |
| 用户 | 项目团队和少数 demo 观众 | 一小群有名字的真实用户 | 完整的目标用户群 |
| 数据 | 样本、合成、或挑过的 | 真实数据, 可能有保护措施 | 真实数据, 完整量 |
| 质量要求 | demo path 上有说服力 | 典型 path 上可接受 | 包括边缘情况的完整分布上可接受 |
| 基础设施 | 能跑就行 | 受限但真实 | 加固、有监控、在受支持的平台上 |
| 支持模式 | 无 | 工作时间内尽力支持 | SLA、on call、事故响应 |
| 评估 (eval) | 轻量, 通常基于 demo | 定义清楚, 定期跑 | 持续、自动化、有回归测试 |
| 安全与合规 | 通常推迟 | 部分处理 | 完整处理且审计过 |
| 移交 | 一份讲解 deck | 文档加培训 | 运营 runbook 加支持移交 |
| 典型费率倍数 | 1x | 2 到 4x | 5 到 10x |

费率倍数只是示意, 不是定律, 但**数量级是真实的**。Pilot 比 POC 贵三倍、Production 又比 Pilot 贵五倍, 这件事在估下一阶段费率时是一个有用的 sanity check。

**会出什么岔子**

几种常见的混淆模式, 每一种都烧伤过人:

*POC 当 pilot 卖。* 团队给 demo 观众建了东西, 客户把 "pilot" 理解成 "受限的真实用户"。真实用户出现时, 系统在边缘情况上撑不住。团队被问 "为什么这个 pilot 这么不可靠", 答案是 "因为它是个 POC", **听起来像借口**。

*Pilot 当 production 卖。* 团队建了一个在受控 pilot 环境下能跑的东西。客户期待 pilot 结束时**"对所有人开个开关就行"**。团队解释生产化加固是一个单独的阶段、单独的工作量、单独的成本、单独的时间线。对话很尴尬, 因为 SOW 里没说这件事。

*Production 范围, pilot 时间线。* 团队同意了一个 12 周的生产上线, 因为销售说 "12 周是客户想听到的"。**这不是标签问题, 是可行性问题, 标签救不了它**。

**怎么用这张表对话**

在 kickoff 上, 跟客户**一行一行**走过这张表, 在每一行问: "对于这次合作, 我们落在频谱上的哪个位置?" 答案不会全部落在同一列上。很多真实项目是 "数据上是 pilot 质量, 但基础设施上是 POC 质量", 或者 "安全上是 production 范围, 但支持模式上是 pilot 范围"。这没问题, 只要**写明白**就行。陷阱是让 10 行全部模糊, 然后第六周发现团队和客户填的不一样。

一个实用的产物: 一页 "交付模式矩阵", 包含这 10 行、3 列、以及标记出本次合作在每一行上的位置。双方签字。做这张表花 30 分钟, 能省下几周的争吵。

**PM 决策点**

- 团队对 POC、pilot、或 production 的理解, 我有没有**写下来**明确化, 还是只靠 SOW 里那个词?
- 这 10 个维度上, 这个项目实际落在哪? 维度之间有没有不一致?
- 如果客户中途想升级模式, **怎么重新定价、重新定范围**?

**注意事项**

- 警惕 "production ready" 这个词。它几乎总是对不同的人意味着不同的东西。**用 10 个维度上的具体承诺取代它**。
- POC 的结果**不能外推到 production 表现**。85% demo 准确率的 POC, 不是一个 85% 准确率的生产系统, 而是一个**还没遇到过边缘情况的项目**。
- 客户有时用 "pilot" 是为了管理**内部政治风险**, 哪怕技术范围其实是 production。听一下 "pilot" 是真的范围约束, 还是方便用的标签。

**要点总结: POC、pilot、production 不是同义词。第一周跟客户一行一行过那张 10 个维度的表, 拿到双方签字的矩阵。这 30 分钟是市面上最便宜的范围保险。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你被要求给 Qantas 交付一个 AI 订票助手。SOW 通篇用 "pilot" 这个词。费率按 pilot 定的。时间线 14 周。客户 sponsor 也用 "pilot" 这个词, 但对话里逐渐清楚, 他期待**这次合作结束时, 这个助手对所有 Platinum 会员可用**。

团队对 pilot 的理解是: 200 到 500 个挑选过的 Platinum 会员、限定在一组定义清楚的订票流程、只在工作时间支持、安全审查推到后续 production 阶段。客户的理解是: 所有 Platinum 会员、所有订票流程、7x24 (因为 Qantas 是 7x24 运营)、上线前安全已经签字。

这两个定义描述的是**两个完全不同的项目**。团队的版本花 pilot 的费率、14 周。客户的版本是一个 production 系统, 合理估计要 9 到 12 个月、四到五倍的费率。

第一周正确的动作是把上面那张交付模式矩阵做出来, 跟 sponsor 一行一行走一遍, 把差距**礼貌地浮出水面**。措辞不是 "你们理解错了" 或 "我们做不到你说的"。措辞是: "我们想确保我们在建什么这件事上对齐, 我们注意到几处假设可能不同。我们一起过一遍。"

大多数情况下, 这个差距会通过把项目降级成一个真正的 pilot 来解决 (更小的用户群、更少的流程、推迟安全), 同时**为后续单独规划一个 production 阶段**。客户很少会因此不开心, 因为这件事被呈现为**保护工作的成功**, 而不是**从承诺里后退**。

如果这个差距没被处理, 团队会花 10 周建一个 pilot, 汇报结果, 然后听到 "很好, 现在给所有人开开关吧"。在那个瞬间, 团队不得不解释 pilot 不是为那个设计的, 而那是这场对话能发生的最糟糕的时机。

AI 交付的启示: POC、pilot、production 这三个词, 只有在双方都把各自的理解写下来时, 才是有用的。那张矩阵是一个 30 分钟的产物, 能防住这一行最贵的误解。
