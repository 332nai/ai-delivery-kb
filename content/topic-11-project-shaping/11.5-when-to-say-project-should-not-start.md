**Definition**

Some AI projects, by week one or two, show signals that they will not succeed in their current form. The honest move at that point is to tell the client that the project should pause, descope, or be reshaped, rather than push forward and produce a predictable failure. This is also the move that is least often taken, because it feels like commercial self harm, and because the team's incentive is to keep the engagement running.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Failed AI projects damage relationships, careers, and the broader appetite for AI in the client organisation. A project that should have been paused but was pushed through hurts the client more than a project that was honestly reshaped at week two. The hard part is that the financial incentives, in the short run, push the other way: the team is paid by the week, the partner has revenue targets, the salesperson has commission. Saying "stop" requires overriding all of these. The work of this item is to make that override easier by giving the team a clear set of signals, a clear decision process, and a clear way to communicate it.

**How it works**

There are five signals that, individually, are warnings, and collectively, are grounds to recommend pausing or reshaping.

*Signal 1: The data is not there and cannot be there in time.* The use case depends on data that the client does not have, cannot get within the engagement timeline, or has but cannot legally share with the team. This is the single most common project killer. It is rarely visible in the SOW.

*Signal 2: There is no real user.* The sponsor describes the project in terms of strategic intent, but cannot name a specific person or team who is waiting for this tool and would change their workflow to use it. A project without a user has nothing to test against and nothing to optimise for.

*Signal 3: The success criteria are undefined or politically contested.* Different stakeholders have different expectations, and there is no shared definition of "done". The team will produce something, and whatever they produce will fail to meet at least one stakeholder's expectations.

*Signal 4: The technical approach is wrong for the problem.* The client has committed to a technical pattern (often the one they read about in a recent announcement) that is the wrong fit. Forcing the project through means delivering an inferior solution.

*Signal 5: The political situation in the client makes success structurally unlikely.* The sponsor is leaving. There is an organisational restructure pending. A competing initiative is funded that overlaps with this one. The project would land in soil where nothing grows.

If two or more of these are present, the project needs reshaping before it proceeds. If three or more are present, the honest recommendation is to pause.

**How to raise it internally first**

Before saying anything to the client, the team has to be aligned internally. The conversation goes to the engagement partner, with the following structure:

1. *What I am seeing.* The specific signals, with evidence, not opinion.
2. *What I think the consequence is.* The likely outcome if the project continues unchanged.
3. *What I think we should do.* Pause, reshape, descope, or proceed with explicit risk acceptance.
4. *What I need from you.* Air cover to raise it with the client, or a counter argument I had not considered.

The partner may push back. The partner has more context, including commercial pressures the team does not see. But the partner is also better placed to raise the issue with the client at the right level. The job of the team member who first sees the problem is to surface it, not to fix it.

**How to raise it with the client**

If the internal alignment is reached, the client conversation has a specific shape. It is not "we are quitting". It is "we owe you our honest read, and our honest read is that this project will not succeed in its current form. Here is what we recommend, and we want your input on which path to take."

A useful structure for the conversation:

1. *Acknowledge what is working.* There are always things that are working. Start there.
2. *Name the concern, with specifics.* "We have noticed that the data we need to build X is not available in the form we expected. Here is what we have tried, here is what we have found."
3. *Lay out two or three options.* Pause, reshape with a different scope, proceed with explicit risk acceptance, or pivot to an adjacent use case.
4. *State a recommendation.* The team has a view. Stating it is professional, not presumptuous.
5. *Ask for the client's input.* They know their organisation better than the team does.

The hardest part is the first two minutes, after which the client almost always appreciates the honesty. Clients have been on the receiving end of consultants who pushed bad projects through. They remember the consultants who pulled the handbrake.

**PM decision points**

- Have I named the signals I am seeing in writing, or am I keeping them in my head?
- Have I aligned with the engagement partner before raising it with the client?
- What is my actual recommendation, not just my list of concerns?

**Watch outs**

- The first reaction inside the team will be "we cannot say this, the client will be furious". The client is usually not furious. The salesperson and the partner sometimes are, until they think it through.
- "Pause and reshape" is almost always more saleable than "stop". Frame it as the former when possible.
- Do not raise the issue on email. This is an in person conversation, or at least a video call. Email creates a paper trail that calcifies positions.

**Takeaway: a small number of signals, present together, mean a project will not succeed in its current form. The job is to name them, recommend a reshape, and do it before too much money has been spent to make the conversation politically possible.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine being three weeks into a Qantas AI project to build a personalised marketing content generator for the frequent flyer program. The SOW promised a working tool, integrated with the marketing automation platform, delivering personalised email content at scale, in 16 weeks.

The signals are accumulating. The personalisation depends on customer behaviour data that lives in three systems, one of which is owned by a vendor and contractually cannot be shared with an external team. The marketing automation platform integration requires sign off from the platform owner, who is in the middle of a separate replatforming project and has no bandwidth. The sponsor is enthusiastic but has not been able to identify a marketing manager who will operationally own the content output. Two of the five signals are clearly present, and a third (the platform owner constraint) is on the edge.

The internal conversation goes to the engagement partner. The team member raising it presents the signals as evidence, not opinion. The likely consequence is that in 12 weeks the team will deliver a content generator that produces good copy in a sandbox but cannot reach a real customer, because the integration and the data have not been resolved. The recommendation is to pause for two weeks, work with the client to either resolve the data and integration constraints or reshape the scope to a smaller, fully achievable version.

The partner pushes back briefly. There is revenue pressure. There is a salesperson who closed the deal recently. But the partner is also a senior consultant who has been burned by exactly this pattern before, and after a 20 minute conversation the partner agrees that the issue needs to be raised with the client at the next steering committee.

The steering committee conversation is led by the partner, not the team member. The partner opens with what is working. The partner then names the data and integration constraints with specifics. The partner offers two options: a four week reshape of the scope into a smaller standalone use case that does not depend on the marketing automation platform, or a two week pause to work jointly on resolving the constraints. The partner states a recommendation: the reshape, because the constraints look unlikely to resolve in the engagement timeline.

The client sponsor is initially uncomfortable, because the original scope was something the sponsor advocated for internally. After 30 minutes, the conversation turns. The sponsor agrees that the reshape is the right move. The sponsor also says, in private to the partner afterwards, that the willingness of the team to surface the issue rather than push on has noticeably increased trust.

The project goes on to deliver the reshaped scope on time. The original integration problem is taken up by Qantas internally, separately, six months later, when the platform owner has bandwidth. The relationship deepens, and the team is invited to bid on the follow on work.

The lesson for AI delivery is that the act of saying "this project will not succeed in its current form" is not commercial self harm in the medium term. It is the move that protects the client, protects the team, and most of the time, protects the next engagement too. The hard part is that the medium term benefits are invisible at the moment of the conversation, and the short term costs (an awkward 30 minutes) are very visible. The discipline is to weight them correctly.

::: zh

**定义**

有些 AI 项目, 在第一两周就显示出**以当前形态不会成功**的信号。诚实的做法是在那个时候告诉客户**项目应该暂停、缩范围、或重新塑形**, 而不是推下去, 产出一个**可预见的失败**。这也是**最少被采取**的做法, 因为它感觉像商业自残, 而且团队的激励是让项目继续跑下去。

**为何在交付中重要**

失败的 AI 项目会损害关系、职业, 以及客户组织里**对 AI 的整体胃口**。一个本该暂停却被推下去的项目, 对客户的伤害大过一个第二周就被诚实重塑的项目。难的地方是**短期内财务激励指向相反方向**: 团队按周收费, partner 有营收指标, 销售有提成。说 "停" 需要**压过所有这些**。这一节的工作就是让那个 "压过" 更容易, 通过给团队一组清晰的信号、一个清晰的决策流程、一种清晰的沟通方式。

**运作原理**

有五个信号, 单独看是警告, 合起来看是**建议暂停或重塑的依据**。

*信号 1: 数据不在那里, 而且来不及在时间内拿到。* 这个 use case 依赖的数据, 客户没有、或在合作时间线内拿不到、或拥有但法律上不能给团队。**这是最常见的项目杀手**。SOW 里几乎看不到。

*信号 2: 没有真实用户。* Sponsor 用战略意图描述项目, 但**说不出**某一个具体的人或团队**在等这个工具**, 并愿意改自己的工作流去用它。**没有用户的项目没有可测试的对象、也没有可优化的目标**。

*信号 3: 成功标准没定义, 或在政治上有争议。* 不同的 stakeholder 有不同的期待, 没有共享的 "完成" 定义。团队会产出某个东西, 而**无论产出什么, 都至少有一位 stakeholder 不满意**。

*信号 4: 技术方案对问题来说是错的。* 客户承诺了一个特定的技术 pattern (通常是他们最近在某个公告里读到的那一个), 但它**不是合适的选择**。把项目强推下去意味着交付一个次优方案。

*信号 5: 客户内部的政治形势让成功在结构上不太可能。* Sponsor 要走了。组织在等一次重组。**有另一个跟你重叠的项目也拿了预算**。这个项目会落在**什么都不生长的土壤里**。

两个或更多信号同时存在, 项目需要在继续之前重塑。三个或更多同时存在, 诚实的建议是**暂停**。

**先在内部提出来**

跟客户说之前, 团队内部必须先对齐。这个对话走向 engagement partner, 用以下结构:

1. *我看到的。* 具体的信号, 带证据, 不是观点。
2. *我认为后果是什么。* 不改变继续推, 大概率结果是什么。
3. *我认为我们该做什么。* 暂停、重塑、缩范围、或带着明确的风险接受继续。
4. *我需要你做什么。* 给我空中掩护去跟客户提, 或给我一个我没想到的反驳。

Partner 可能会 push back。Partner 知道更多上下文, 包括团队看不见的商业压力。但 partner 也更适合在合适的层级上跟客户提。第一个看到问题的团队成员的工作是把它浮出水面, 不是修它。

**怎么跟客户提出来**

内部对齐之后, 跟客户的对话有特定的形状。不是 "我们要撤"。是 "我们欠你一个诚实的判断, 我们的诚实判断是这个项目以当前形态不会成功。下面是我们的建议, 我们想听你对该走哪条路的意见。"

对话的实用结构:

1. *承认在跑通的部分。* **永远有在跑通的部分**。从那里开始。
2. *指出担忧, 带具体证据。* "我们注意到, 我们建 X 需要的数据, 不是我们以为的那种形态。下面是我们已经尝试的、下面是我们已经发现的。"
3. *摆出两到三个选项。* 暂停、用不同范围重塑、带明确风险接受继续、或转向相邻的 use case。
4. *给出一个建议。* 团队有看法。**说出来是专业, 不是僭越**。
5. *邀请客户的意见。* 他们比团队更了解他们的组织。

最难的是**前两分钟**, 之后客户**几乎总是欣赏这种诚实**。客户**被那些把烂项目推下去的咨询顾问伤害过**。他们记得**那些拉手刹的咨询顾问**。

**PM 决策点**

- 我看到的信号, 我**写下来**了, 还是只放在脑子里?
- 在跟客户提之前, 我跟 engagement partner 对齐了吗?
- 我真正的建议是什么, 不只是一份**担忧清单**?

**注意事项**

- 团队内部的第一反应会是 "我们不能这么说, 客户会大怒"。**客户通常不会大怒**。销售和 partner 有时候会, 直到他们想清楚。
- **"暂停并重塑" 几乎总是比 "停止" 更好卖**。能这么框架化的时候就这么框架化。
- **不要用邮件提出这件事**。这是一个面对面对话, 至少是视频通话。**邮件留下的纸面记录会让立场僵化**。

**要点总结: 少数几个信号同时存在, 意味着一个项目以当前形态不会成功。工作是把它们指出来, 建议重塑, 在已经花掉太多钱、政治上不可能再谈这件事之前把它做了。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你做 Qantas AI 项目第三周, 在给 frequent flyer 项目建一个个性化营销内容生成器。SOW 承诺一个能跑通的工具, 集成营销自动化平台, 大规模交付个性化邮件内容, 16 周交付。

信号在累积。个性化依赖的客户行为数据散在三个系统里, 其中一个由一家供应商拥有, 合同上不能分享给外部团队。营销自动化平台的集成需要平台 owner 签字, 但他正在做一个单独的换平台项目, 没有带宽。Sponsor 很热情, 但**说不出哪个 marketing manager 会运营层面拥有这些内容产出**。五个信号里**两个清楚地在**, 第三个 (平台 owner 约束) 在边缘。

内部对话走向 engagement partner。提出问题的团队成员把信号作为**证据**呈现, 不是观点。可能的后果是: 12 周后团队会交付一个内容生成器, 它**在沙箱里能产出好文案, 但触达不了真实客户**, 因为集成和数据没解决。建议是**暂停两周**, 跟客户一起要么解决数据和集成约束, 要么把范围重塑成一个**更小、完全做得到**的版本。

Partner 短暂 push back。有营收压力。有一个刚签下这个单子的销售。但 partner 也是一个**正好被这个 pattern 烧伤过**的资深咨询顾问, 20 分钟对话之后, partner 同意这件事需要在下次 steering committee 上跟客户提。

Steering committee 上的对话**由 partner 主讲, 不是团队成员**。Partner 先开口讲在跑通的部分。然后 partner **用具体证据指出**数据和集成约束。Partner 摆出两个选项: 一个四周的重塑, 把范围缩成一个**不依赖营销自动化平台**的小独立 use case; 或者两周暂停, 跟客户一起解决约束。Partner 给出建议: **走重塑**, 因为约束**看起来在合作时间线内不太可能解决**。

客户 sponsor 一开始不舒服, 因为原始范围是 sponsor 自己在内部推过的。30 分钟之后, 对话转向。Sponsor 同意重塑是对的。Sponsor 之后**私下跟 partner 说**: 团队**愿意把问题浮出来而不是硬推**, 这件事**明显增加了信任**。

项目继续, 用重塑后的范围**按时交付**。原始的集成问题在六个月后被 Qantas 内部单独捡起来, 那时平台 owner 有带宽了。关系加深, 团队被邀请投标后续工作。

AI 交付的启示: "这个项目以当前形态不会成功" 这句话, 中期来看不是商业自残。它是**保护客户、保护团队、大多数时候也保护下一个项目**的动作。难的地方是**中期收益在那场对话发生的瞬间不可见, 短期代价 (30 分钟的尴尬) 非常可见**。纪律就是把这两件事的权重赋对。
