**Definition**

The Statement of Work (SOW) is the document that defines what you are paid to deliver. In AI projects it is almost always shaped by sales people who closed the deal, not the people who will deliver it. Reading a SOW well means reading what is missing, ambiguous, or quietly impossible, and surfacing those issues before you sit in front of the client for the first time.

**Why it matters in delivery**

The first week of an AI project is when leverage is highest and political cost is lowest. After kickoff, every renegotiation feels like you are walking back a promise. Before kickoff, you are just clarifying. Most delivery disasters in AI consulting trace back to a SOW that was signed without anyone on the delivery side reading it carefully. By the time the team is three weeks in and discovers that "production deployment" was promised but data access has not been arranged, the conversation is already painful.

**How it works**

Read the SOW three times, each time looking for a different category of problem.

*First pass: what is being promised.* Highlight every verb and every noun that describes a deliverable. "Deploy a production agent", "integrate with the CRM", "achieve 95% accuracy". These are the commitments. Note the ones that sound easy but are actually hard.

*Second pass: what is missing.* AI SOWs typically skip the unglamorous prerequisites. Look for whether the following are explicitly addressed:

| Category | Questions to ask of the SOW |
|---|---|
| Data | Who provides it, in what format, by when, with what quality guarantees? |
| Access | Who provisions cloud accounts, model API keys, internal system credentials? |
| Environments | Where does this run in dev, staging, production? Whose infrastructure? |
| Evaluation | How is "done" defined? Who signs off, against what criteria? |
| Change | What happens if scope changes mid project? Time and materials, or fixed price? |
| Handover | What happens at the end? Who maintains the system? |

*Third pass: what is ambiguous.* Phrases that should make you pause: "best efforts", "industry standard accuracy", "production ready", "scalable architecture", "integrate where applicable". These are diplomatic placeholders for "we did not have time to figure this out". Each one is a future argument waiting to happen.

**Red flags to look for**

A short, non-exhaustive list of things that have ended badly more than once:

- Fixed price contract with vague scope (you will pay for every uncosted assumption)
- Accuracy targets without a defined test set (impossible to either pass or fail)
- "Production deployment" mentioned without IT or security as named stakeholders
- Timeline based on demo, not on production hardening
- "AI strategy" listed as a deliverable without a definition of what the artifact looks like
- Acceptance criteria that read like marketing copy

**PM decision points**

- Have I read the SOW myself, or only the summary deck?
- Which of the commitments would I personally bet my reputation on hitting in the stated timeline?
- What three things would I most want to clarify before kickoff, and with whom?

**Watch outs**

- The salesperson who closed the deal is not your enemy, they are your guide to what was actually said in the room. Talk to them before you talk to the client.
- Do not dump every concern on the client in week one. Triage. Some issues you raise in kickoff, some you raise in the first steering committee, some you simply manage around quietly.
- Silence is also a signal. If the SOW says nothing about data, it does not mean data is sorted, it means nobody asked.

**Takeaway: read the SOW three times, for promises, gaps, and ambiguity. Surface the worst issues before kickoff, when the cost of correction is still political, not contractual.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine inheriting a SOW for a Qantas AI project that was sold as a "GenAI powered customer service transformation, 12 week proof of value, leading to production rollout in the following quarter". The deck looks great. The fee is healthy. The salesperson is delighted.

A careful first read turns up the usual issues. Data access is not addressed at all. The SOW assumes the team will have access to historical customer service transcripts within the first two weeks. There is no clause about how those transcripts are de-identified, who owns the de-identification work, and how long Qantas Legal and Privacy take to approve external access. In practice, that approval can take six weeks on its own.

Accuracy is described as "match or exceed current human agent quality". There is no definition of what current human agent quality is, no benchmark dataset, and no agreed evaluation methodology. Whoever is judging this at the end of 12 weeks holds enormous discretion, and the team has no protection if the judge moves the goalposts.

Production rollout is mentioned in the SOW, but no Qantas IT person is named in the governance section. The implication is that an external team can deploy AI into a Qantas production environment in three months without IT engagement. Anyone who has worked with a major airline IT function knows this timeline is fantasy.

The polite move is not to wave these issues at the kickoff meeting like a list of grievances. The polite move is to walk through them privately with the engagement partner and the salesperson first, agree on which ones become explicit clarifications in week one, which ones get raised at the first steering committee, and which ones the delivery team manages around without making it the client's problem.

The lesson for AI delivery is that the SOW is rarely written by people who have shipped a production AI system. It is written by people who have sold a vision. The delivery person's job in week one is to translate the vision back into reality without anyone losing face. The earlier this is done, the less it costs everyone.

**定义**

SOW (Statement of Work, 工作说明书) 是定义你被付钱交付什么的合同文件。AI 项目里的 SOW 几乎总是由签下单子的销售人写的, 不是后续交付的人写的。读懂一份 SOW 意味着读出它**遗漏了什么、含糊在哪里、什么地方静悄悄地不可能**, 并在第一次面对客户之前把这些问题浮出水面。

**为何在交付中重要**

AI 项目的第一周是你**杠杆最大、政治成本最低**的时候。kickoff 之后, 任何重新谈判都像是在收回承诺。kickoff 之前, 你只是在澄清。咨询界大多数 AI 交付灾难都能追溯到一份没有人在交付侧仔细读过的 SOW。等到团队进入第三周, 发现"生产部署"已经被承诺、但数据访问权限还没安排时, 那个对话已经很痛苦了。

**运作原理**

读 SOW 读三遍, 每遍找不同类别的问题。

*第一遍: 到底承诺了什么。* 标出每一个描述交付物的动词和名词。"部署一个生产级 agent"、"集成 CRM"、"达到 95% 准确率"。这些是承诺。其中哪些**听起来容易、实际很难**, 单独标出来。

*第二遍: 缺了什么。* AI 项目的 SOW 通常会跳过那些不光鲜但关键的前置条件。检查以下是否被明确写进 SOW:

| 类别 | 要追问 SOW 的问题 |
|---|---|
| 数据 | 谁提供、什么格式、什么时候到、质量保证是什么? |
| 访问权限 | 谁负责开通云账户、模型 API key、内部系统凭证? |
| 环境 | dev、staging、production 在哪运行? 谁的基础设施? |
| 评估 | "完成"怎么定义? 谁验收, 对什么标准? |
| 变更 | 项目中途范围变化怎么办? T&M 还是 fixed price? |
| 移交 | 结束时是什么状态? 谁后续维护这个系统? |

*第三遍: 哪里含糊。* 让你必须停下来的措辞: "best efforts"、"行业标准准确率"、"production ready"、"可扩展架构"、"在适用情况下集成"。这些都是"我们当时没时间想清楚"的外交辞令。每一条都是一场未来的争吵在等着发生。

**Red flag 清单**

下面是一份不完整但非常实用的清单, 这些都曾经让项目走得不好:

- Fixed price 合同 + 模糊范围 (每一个没成本化的假设你都要自己付钱)
- 准确率目标 + 没定义测试集 (既不可能通过也不可能失败)
- 提了"生产部署"但 stakeholder 里没出现 IT 或安全
- 时间线基于 demo 算的, 不是基于生产化所需的工程量
- "AI 战略"作为一个交付物, 但没定义"战略"长什么样
- 验收标准读起来像营销文案

**PM 决策点**

- 我自己读过 SOW 吗, 还是只看了销售给的总结 deck?
- 在承诺的时间线内, 哪几个承诺我**愿意用个人声誉去赌**能做到?
- kickoff 前, 我最想澄清的三件事是什么, 找谁澄清?

**注意事项**

- 签单的销售不是你的敌人, 他/她是知道"客户当时在房间里到底说了什么"的人。先跟销售谈, 再跟客户谈。
- 不要在第一周把所有担忧一股脑倾倒给客户。**分级处理**。有些在 kickoff 会上提, 有些在第一次 steering committee 提, 有些干脆自己默默管理掉、不让客户为之操心。
- 沉默也是信号。SOW 里**没提**数据, 不代表数据搞定了, 代表**没人问过**。

**要点总结: 读 SOW 读三遍, 分别找承诺、缺漏、含糊。在 kickoff 之前把最严重的问题浮出来, 那时候纠正的代价还只是政治成本, 不是合同成本。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你接手了一份 Qantas 的 AI 项目 SOW, 卖的卖点是"GenAI 驱动的客户服务转型, 12 周价值证明, 下一季度生产部署"。Deck 看起来很棒。费率很健康。销售很开心。

仔细读第一遍, 一些典型问题就浮上来了。

**数据访问完全没提**。SOW 默认团队能在头两周拿到历史客服对话记录。但没有任何条款说这些记录怎么去识别化、谁负责去识别化的工作、Qantas Legal 和 Privacy 批准外部访问需要多久。实践中, 这个审批自己就能跑六周。

**准确率被描述为"达到或超过当前人工客服质量"**。没定义什么是当前人工客服质量, 没基准数据集, 也没商定评估方法。12 周结束时谁来评判这件事, 谁就握有巨大裁量权, 而团队对"评判方移动目标"完全没有合同保护。

**SOW 里提到生产部署, 但治理章节里没有 Qantas IT 的人**。隐含意思是外部团队可以在三个月内把 AI 部署到 Qantas 的生产环境, 而不需要 IT 参与。任何跟大型航空公司 IT 部门打过交道的人都知道这个时间线是幻想。

**得体的做法不是在 kickoff 会上把这些问题像一张投诉清单那样挥舞**。得体的做法是先私下跟 engagement partner 和销售把这些问题过一遍, 商定哪几条在第一周作为明确澄清提出来、哪几条留到第一次 steering committee、哪几条交付团队自己默默管理掉、不让客户难堪。

AI 交付的启示是: SOW 几乎从来不是由"真正上线过生产 AI 系统"的人写的。它是由"卖过愿景"的人写的。交付者在第一周的工作就是把愿景翻译回现实, **不让任何人丢面子**。这件事做得越早, 对所有人的代价都越小。
