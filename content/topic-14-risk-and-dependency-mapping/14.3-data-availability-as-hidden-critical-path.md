**Definition**

The critical path of a project is the sequence of dependent activities that determines its minimum duration. In AI projects, the published critical path is usually built around model selection, prompt design, evaluation, and integration. The real critical path is almost always data access: getting the data the system needs, in the form it needs it, with the legal and security clearances required, into the team's hands fast enough to build against. This item is about recognising this hidden critical path early and treating it as the primary scheduling driver, not as a side concern.

**Why it matters in delivery**

The most common reason AI projects slip is not technical difficulty. It is data not being available when it was assumed to be available. The team plans week 1 to 3 for model selection and prompt design, weeks 3 to 8 for build, and discovers in week 4 that the data export request takes six weeks. The project is now two weeks behind before the build even properly starts, and the team is squeezing later phases to compensate. By the time anyone says "we have a data problem", the time to address it cheaply has passed.

The deeper reason data is the hidden critical path: it sits at the intersection of three slow-moving things, each of which has its own queue. Engineering teams that own the data source have their own roadmap. Legal and privacy teams reviewing the access have their own backlog. Security teams approving the connection have their own approvals process. Each one alone takes time. Together, they often take longer than the entire AI build that depends on them.

**How it works**

The data critical path has, in most projects, five sequential steps. Each step has its own typical duration, and each one is usually owned by a different team.

| Step | What happens | Typical duration | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify | Confirm what data exists, where it lives, what schema and quality | 1 to 2 weeks | Data engineering or data platform |
| Authorise | Get legal, privacy, and security approval for the AI team to access | 2 to 8 weeks | Legal, privacy, security |
| Provision | Set up the actual access (credentials, connection, sample) | 1 to 3 weeks | Data engineering or IT |
| Validate | Confirm the data delivered matches what was expected | 0.5 to 2 weeks | Delivery team |
| Iterate | Address quality issues, request additional fields, re-validate | 1 to 4 weeks, often more | Delivery team and data engineering |

Adding these up: best case is roughly 5 weeks, typical case is 8 to 12 weeks, worst case is 6 months. For a 12 week project, this means the data critical path is the project. Every other activity must be planned around it.

**What this implies for project planning**

Three concrete practices follow from recognising data as the critical path.

*Practice 1: Start the data path in week 1.* Not week 2, not after the kickoff settles down. Week 1, day 1 if possible. The identify and authorise steps are slow regardless of how urgently they are pushed, so any delay in starting them propagates directly to project end.

*Practice 2: Plan the build against the projected data availability date, not the wished-for date.* If data realistically arrives in week 8, plan the build to use weeks 1 to 8 for everything that can be done without data (evals on small samples, prompt design with synthetic data, infrastructure, eval harness, etc.) and the actual model work for weeks 8 onwards. Trying to start the model work in week 3 when the data is not there until week 8 wastes effort and demoralises the team.

*Practice 3: Maintain a parallel synthetic data fallback.* In the worst case, the data does not arrive in time. The team needs a synthetic fallback that allows demos and basic evals to happen without the real data. This is not a substitute for real data, it is a parachute. Building it in week 1 is cheap. Trying to build it in week 8 under pressure is expensive.

**How to surface the data critical path to the client**

Many clients underestimate the data critical path because their own organisations have hidden the complexity from them. The client's data team sees data access as a routine request. The client's executive sponsor has never had to wait 8 weeks for it. The client's procurement process measured the project against the build phase only.

A useful visual is a Gantt chart that puts data activities on the top line and build activities below, with the build activities shown as gated by data milestones. When the client sees that build cannot meaningfully start until week 8 because data is not provisioned until week 7, the conversation shifts. The client now has incentive to accelerate the data path. The data team gets attention from senior leaders that it would not otherwise get. The project schedule becomes a tool for unblocking, not just for reporting.

A second useful artifact: a one page memo that lists every data dependency, its current status, who owns it on the client side, and what the team is doing to keep it moving. Update this weekly. The client sees it as a sign of professionalism, and the data teams that are owning the work see themselves named on the critical path, which often is enough to accelerate them.

**PM decision points**

- Have I confirmed, with the client's data team in week 1, the actual timeline for each data dependency, or am I working from optimistic estimates?
- For each data dependency, do I know the specific person on the client side who owns the next step, and have I asked them directly?
- Have I planned the build around realistic data availability dates, or am I assuming the data will appear on time?

**Watch outs**

- Beware of clients who say "we have lots of data, it should not be a problem". This statement almost always reflects optimism about access, not actual data accessibility. Push for specifics.
- Synthetic data is useful for some things and dangerous for others. It is fine for building infrastructure, eval harnesses, and demos. It is dangerous for tuning prompts (because tuning to synthetic data overfits to assumptions that may not hold) and for accuracy claims to the client (because performance on synthetic is not predictive of performance on real).
- The data team owning the dependency may not see themselves as on the critical path of an AI project. Making them visible (with their leadership's awareness) is part of the work.

**Takeaway: data availability is the real critical path of most AI projects. The five step process (identify, authorise, provision, validate, iterate) often takes 8 to 12 weeks, longer than many project timelines assume. Start the data path on day one, plan the build around realistic data dates, and maintain a synthetic fallback. Surfacing the path to the client is half the work.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine starting a Qantas AI project to build a customer service agent that handles questions about flight credits and travel vouchers. The SOW promises a working agent in 14 weeks. The team is keen, the model selection seems straightforward, the prompt design is clear. The temptation in week 1 is to focus on the technical work, which feels productive.

The discipline is to focus week 1 on the data critical path instead. The team and the operating sponsor map out what data the agent will need: customer account records to check identity, credit balance data to answer balance questions, voucher terms and conditions to answer eligibility questions, and customer interaction history to provide context. Four data sources, each owned by a different team within Qantas.

The PM contacts each owning team in the first three days. The conversations reveal the actual timelines. Customer account records are managed by the loyalty platform team, who can provide a sample in 2 weeks and full access in 4 weeks. Credit balance data is managed by the finance systems team, who require a formal data access request with privacy review, expected duration 6 to 8 weeks. Voucher T&Cs are document-based and available immediately. Customer interaction history is in the contact centre platform, requires privacy review and security approval, expected duration 8 to 12 weeks.

The longest chain is interaction history at 8 to 12 weeks. The project is 14 weeks. The PM presents the critical path analysis to the operating sponsor in week 2. The conversation reveals two important things. First, the sponsor had not realised that interaction history access was that slow, because she had never personally requested it. Second, there is a possibility to descope the interaction history dependency: the agent can be useful without it, just less contextual. The team and sponsor agree to two parallel actions: pursue the interaction history access aggressively (escalating to the contact centre director), and build the v1 agent to function without interaction history, treating it as a v1.5 enhancement.

The PM also negotiates with the loyalty platform team for an accelerated sample, agreeing to specific data sharing constraints in exchange for a week 2 delivery rather than a week 4 delivery. With the loyalty data available in week 2, the team can start meaningful prompt design and eval set construction earlier than originally planned.

The build phase proceeds. Credit balance data arrives in week 7 as projected. Interaction history access is granted in week 10, faster than the worst-case estimate but slower than what would have been needed for v1 inclusion. The v1.5 enhancement is scoped for delivery six weeks after v1 launch. The v1 launches on schedule at week 14.

The interesting counterfactual is what would have happened if the team had focused week 1 on technical work and discovered the data timelines in week 4. By that point, the contact centre data access would have started 3 weeks late, the credit balance review would have started 3 weeks late, and v1 launch would have been delayed by approximately 4 to 6 weeks. The technical work in weeks 1 to 3 would have been useful but not critical-path-shifting. The data work was the only thing that could have moved the project forward in week 1.

The lesson for AI delivery is that data availability is almost always the critical path, even when it is not labelled as such. The team's first week of work should be on the data dependencies: identifying them, mapping their timelines, engaging the owning teams, and starting the slow approval processes immediately. The technical work matters, but it is rarely the bottleneck. Treating the data path as primary, and planning every other activity around it, is one of the highest leverage decisions in AI delivery scheduling.

**定义**

一个项目的关键路径, 是决定项目最短工期的那一串相互依赖的活动。AI 项目里, 公开的关键路径通常围绕模型选型、prompt 设计、评估、集成。真正的关键路径几乎总是数据访问: 拿到系统需要的数据、以它需要的形态、带着法务和安全的许可、足够快地到团队手上, 才能开始建造。这一节是关于早期识别这条隐藏关键路径、把它当作主要排程驱动因素来对待, 而不是边角小事。

**为何在交付中重要**

AI 项目滑期最常见的原因不是技术难度。是假设该可用的数据没准备好。团队计划第 1 到 3 周做模型选型和 prompt 设计、第 3 到 8 周做建造, 第 4 周发现数据导出请求要六周。项目在建造甚至正式开始之前就已经晚了两周, 团队在挤压后续阶段补偿。等到有人说 "我们有数据问题" 时, 便宜解决的时间已经过去了。

数据是隐藏关键路径的更深原因: 它处在三个慢速移动的东西的交叉口, 每一个都有自己的排队。拥有数据源的工程团队有他们自己的路线图。审阅访问的法务和隐私团队有他们自己的 backlog。批准连接的安全团队有他们自己的审批流程。任何一个单独都要时间。合起来, 它们经常比依赖它们的整个 AI 建造还久。

**运作原理**

大多数项目里, 数据关键路径有五个顺序步骤。每一步有典型时长, 每一步通常由不同团队拥有。

| 步骤 | 发生什么 | 典型时长 | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 识别 | 确认有什么数据、住在哪、什么 schema 和质量 | 1 到 2 周 | 数据工程或数据平台 |
| 授权 | 拿到法务、隐私、安全批准, 让 AI 团队访问 | 2 到 8 周 | 法务、隐私、安全 |
| 配置 | 设置实际访问 (凭证、连接、样本) | 1 到 3 周 | 数据工程或 IT |
| 验证 | 确认交付的数据符合预期 | 0.5 到 2 周 | 交付团队 |
| 迭代 | 处理质量问题、请求额外字段、重新验证 | 1 到 4 周, 经常更多 | 交付团队和数据工程 |

**加起来**: 最好情况大约 5 周, 典型情况 8 到 12 周, 最坏情况 6 个月。对一个 12 周的项目, 这意味着数据关键路径就是项目。所有其他活动都必须围绕它规划。

这对项目规划意味着什么

识别数据为关键路径, 引出三条具体实践。

*实践一: 第 1 周就开始数据路径*。**不是第 2 周, 不是 kickoff 安顿下来之后**。**第 1 周, 第 1 天如果可能**。**识别和授权步骤不管多急都慢, 所以任何延迟启动都直接传导到项目末**。

*实践二: 把建造规划在预期的数据可用日期上, 不是希望中的日期上*。**如果数据现实地在第 8 周到, 把建造规划为第 1 到 8 周做所有不需要数据的事 (小样本上的 evals、用合成数据做 prompt 设计、基础设施、eval harness 等), 真正的模型工作从第 8 周开始**。**在数据要到第 8 周才到的情况下试图第 3 周开始模型工作, 浪费精力且打击士气**。

*实践三: 维护一份并行的合成数据 fallback*。**最坏情况, 数据来不及**。**团队需要一份合成 fallback, 允许 demo 和基本 evals 在没有真实数据时发生**。**这不是真实数据的替代品, 是降落伞**。**第 1 周建它便宜**。**第 8 周在压力下建它贵**。

怎么向客户浮出数据关键路径

很多客户低估数据关键路径, 因为**他们自己的组织对他们隐藏了复杂度**。客户的数据团队把数据访问看作例行请求。客户的 executive sponsor 从未亲自等过 8 周。客户的采购流程只对建造阶段衡量项目。

一个有用的视觉: 一份 Gantt 图, 把数据活动放在顶行, 建造活动放在下面, 建造活动展示为被数据里程碑 gated。当客户看到建造在数据到第 7 周才配置之前没法有意义地开始, 对话转变。客户现在有动机加速数据路径。数据团队拿到原本不会有的高层关注。项目排程变成解封锁工具, 不只是汇报工具。

第二个有用产物: 一份一页的 memo, 列每个数据依赖、当前状态、客户侧 owner、团队正在做什么让它前进。**每周更新**。客户视为专业性的信号, 拥有这份工作的数据团队看到自己被点名在关键路径上, 经常就足以加速他们。

**PM 决策点**

- 我**有没有在第 1 周跟客户的数据团队确认过每个数据依赖的实际时间线**, 还是基于乐观估计在工作?
- 对每个数据依赖, 我**知不知道客户侧拥有下一步的具体人**, 我**直接问过他/她吗**?
- 我**有没有围绕现实的数据可用日期规划建造**, 还是假设数据会按时出现?

**注意事项**

- **警惕说 "我们数据很多, 应该不是问题" 的客户**。**这句话几乎总是反映对访问的乐观, 不是实际的数据可访问性**。**追问具体细节**。
- **合成数据对某些事有用、对某些事危险**。**用来建基础设施、eval harness、demo 是 fine 的**。**用来调 prompt 危险 (因为调到合成数据会过拟合到可能不成立的假设)**, **用来对客户做准确率宣称危险 (因为合成上的表现不预测真实上的表现)**。
- **拥有依赖的数据团队可能不把自己看作在 AI 项目的关键路径上**。**让他们可见 (以及他们的领导知道), 是工作的一部分**。

**要点总结: 数据可用性是大多数 AI 项目的真正关键路径。五步流程 (识别、授权、配置、验证、迭代) 经常 8 到 12 周, 比很多项目时间线假设的长。第 1 天就开始数据路径, 围绕现实的数据日期规划建造, 维护合成 fallback。把这条路径浮给客户, 是一半的工作。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你启动一个 Qantas AI 项目, 建一个客服 agent, 处理关于航班积分和旅行 voucher 的问题。SOW 承诺 14 周内有一个能跑的 agent。团队有干劲, 模型选型看起来直接, prompt 设计清晰。第 1 周的诱惑是聚焦在感觉有产出的技术工作上。

纪律是把第 1 周聚焦在数据关键路径上。团队和运营 sponsor 画出 agent 会需要什么数据: 核查身份的客户账户记录、回答余额问题的积分余额数据、回答资格问题的 voucher 条款、提供上下文的客户交互历史。四个数据源, 每个由 Qantas 内不同团队拥有。

PM 在前三天联系每个拥有团队。对话揭示实际时间线。客户账户记录由忠诚度平台团队管, 他们 2 周内能给样本, 4 周内全访问。积分余额数据由金融系统团队管, 他们要求正式的数据访问请求加隐私 review, 预计 6 到 8 周。Voucher 条款是文档形式, 立即可用。客户交互历史在联络中心平台, 需要隐私 review 和安全批准, 预计 8 到 12 周。

最长的链是交互历史 8 到 12 周。项目 14 周。PM 在第 2 周向运营 sponsor 呈现关键路径分析。对话揭示两件重要的事。**第一**, sponsor 没意识到交互历史访问这么慢, 因为她自己从未亲自请求过。**第二**, 存在去掉交互历史依赖的可能: agent 没有它也能有用, 只是 less contextual。团队和 sponsor 同意两个并行动作: 激进追求交互历史访问 (升级到联络中心总监), 把 v1 agent 建成没有交互历史也能跑, 把它当作 v1.5 enhancement。

PM 还跟忠诚度平台团队谈判加速样本, 同意特定的数据共享约束以换取第 2 周交付而不是第 4 周交付。忠诚度数据第 2 周可用后, 团队能比原计划更早开始有意义的 prompt 设计和 eval set 构建。

**建造阶段推进**。积分余额数据按预期第 7 周到。交互历史访问第 10 周被批准, 比最坏估计快, 但比 v1 纳入所需的慢。v1.5 enhancement 被 scope 为 v1 上线后六周交付。v1 第 14 周按时上线。

**有意思的反事实**是**, 如果团队第 1 周聚焦在技术工作上、第 4 周才发现数据时间线, 会发生什么**。到那时, 联络中心数据访问会晚 3 周开始, 积分余额 review 会晚 3 周开始, v1 上线会延迟大约 4 到 6 周。第 1 到 3 周的技术工作会是有用的, 但不是关键路径变动。数据工作是第 1 周唯一能让项目往前推动的事。

AI 交付的启示: 数据可用性几乎总是关键路径, 即使没被标记为关键路径。团队的第一周工作应该在数据依赖上: 识别它们、画出它们的时间线、engage 拥有团队、立刻启动慢的批准流程。技术工作重要, 但它很少是瓶颈。把数据路径当作主要的、把所有其他活动围绕它规划, 是 AI 交付排程里杠杆最高的几个决策之一。
