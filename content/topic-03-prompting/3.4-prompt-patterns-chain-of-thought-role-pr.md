**Definition**

Prompt patterns are reusable structures for instructing models on different kinds of tasks. The three most useful in delivery are chain of thought (asking the model to reason step by step), role prompting (assigning the model an expert persona), and decomposition (breaking a hard task into smaller subtasks).

**Why it matters in delivery**

Most prompt quality improvements come from picking the right pattern, not from clever wording. A team that knows three or four patterns and applies them consistently will outperform a team that writes long bespoke prompts from scratch every time. These patterns are the bread and butter of working with LLMs.

**How it works**

| Pattern | What it does | Example use case | Trade off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain of thought | Asks model to show working before answering | Math, multi step reasoning, decision making | Longer output, higher cost and latency |
| Role prompting | Assigns expert identity ("You are a senior tax accountant") | Domain specific tone, expertise framing | Can over commit model to a stereotype |
| Decomposition | Splits one prompt into a chain of smaller prompts | Complex tasks, agentic workflows | More API calls, more orchestration |
| Self critique | Model checks its own output before finalising | Quality sensitive output | Doubles cost and latency |
| Plan then execute | Model writes a plan first, then carries it out | Long horizon tasks | Plans can be wrong, harder to debug |

**Worked examples**

Chain of thought:

"A customer bought 3 items at AUD 24 each with a 15 percent discount and 10 percent GST added. What did they pay? Think step by step before giving the final answer."

Without CoT, models sometimes get this wrong. With CoT, accuracy jumps significantly because the model commits to intermediate calculations.

Role prompting:

"You are a senior contract lawyer reviewing an MSA. Identify clauses that expose the buyer to unlimited liability."

The role frames vocabulary, depth, and what to flag, all without writing detailed instructions.

Decomposition:

Instead of "Read this 50 page report and recommend three actions," split it: (1) extract key findings, (2) cluster findings by theme, (3) propose actions per cluster. Each step is a separate prompt, each is easier to evaluate.

**PM decision points**

- Has the team tried obvious patterns before writing bespoke prompts?
- Is the task simple enough for one prompt, or should it be decomposed?
- Are we trading off latency and cost for quality, and is that trade off worth it?

**Watch outs**

- Chain of thought is unnecessary for simple tasks, it just burns tokens
- Role prompts can cause the model to fabricate, e.g. "as a doctor, I would prescribe..." for queries it should refuse
- Decomposition creates more failure points, each subtask needs its own eval

**Takeaway: pick the pattern that matches the task. Chain of thought for reasoning, roles for tone, decomposition for complexity.**

**Qantas story**

A good Qantas example of prompt pattern selection comes from disruption recovery analysis. Imagine an AI tool helping operations controllers think through recovery options when a major disruption hits, say a runway closure at Sydney affecting outbound flights for six hours.

A naive single shot prompt ("here is the disruption, suggest recovery options") produces shallow output. The model recommends generic options without engaging with the specific constraints: aircraft positioning, crew duty time limits, downstream rotational impact, customer mix sensitivity (high status customers vs leisure travellers), and slot availability at affected airports.

Applying decomposition transforms the output. The prompt is restructured as a sequence: first, identify the affected flights and downstream rotations; second, classify each flight by customer mix and revenue impact; third, list candidate recovery options for each affected service (cancel, delay, swap aircraft, swap crew, divert); fourth, evaluate each option against constraints (crew duty, aircraft availability, slot rules); fifth, recommend a prioritised recovery plan with rationale.

Each step is a separate prompt call. Each is easier to evaluate independently. The model performs better on each sub task because it is not trying to do everything at once.

Adding planning makes it stronger again. Before executing, the model writes a plan: "I will analyse 14 affected flights, prioritise by revenue at risk, propose recovery options, and rank them." The plan creates accountability, and if the model deviates, the controller can see why.

Adding chain of thought on the harder steps (the constraint evaluation) catches errors that single shot reasoning misses, like a proposed crew swap that would breach duty time rules.

For AI delivery, the practical lesson is that prompt patterns are not academic constructs, they are practical tools that map onto specific kinds of work. Decomposition for tasks with clear sub steps. Chain of thought for tasks involving constraint satisfaction. Planning for tasks that benefit from upfront structure. Role prompting for tasks where domain framing matters. The skill is recognising which pattern fits which task, not knowing every pattern in detail. Most production AI work uses two or three patterns well, applied consistently, rather than every pattern in the literature.

**定义**

Prompt pattern 是为不同类型的任务"引导模型"的可复用结构。在交付里最常用的三种是: chain of thought (要求模型一步步推理)、role prompting (给模型分配一个专家角色)、decomposition (把困难任务拆成更小的子任务)。

**为何在交付中重要**

大部分 prompt 质量的提升,来自"选对 pattern",而不是"措辞写得巧"。一个团队掌握三四种 pattern 并一致使用,会胜过另一个每次从头写长定制 prompt 的团队。这些 pattern 是跟 LLM 协作最基本的工具。

**运作原理**

| Pattern | 它做什么 | 用例 | 取舍 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain of thought | 让模型在给答案前展示推理过程 | 数学、多步推理、做决策 | 输出更长,成本和延迟都上去 |
| Role prompting | 分配一个专家身份 ("你是一位资深税务会计") | 领域特定语气、专业视角的框架 | 模型可能滑向刻板印象 |
| Decomposition | 把一个 prompt 拆成更小 prompt 的链 | 复杂任务、agentic 工作流 | 更多 API 调用,更多编排 |
| Self critique | 模型先检查自己的输出,再定稿 | 对质量很敏感的输出 | 成本和延迟翻倍 |
| Plan then execute | 模型先写计划,然后执行 | 长视野任务 | 计划本身可能错,debug 更难 |

**举例**

Chain of thought:

"一位顾客以每件 24 澳元买了 3 件商品,享受 15% 折扣,再加 10% GST。他总共付了多少?在给最终答案之前,一步一步思考。"

不加 CoT 的话,模型有时候会算错。加上 CoT,准确率明显上升——因为模型先把中间计算承诺出来了。

Role prompting:

"你是一位资深合同律师,正在 review 一份 MSA。识别出那些会让买方承担无限责任的条款。"

角色一定下来,词汇、深度、要标记什么,都被框架住了,不用再写一堆详细指令。

Decomposition:

不要写"读这份 50 页报告,推荐三个行动"。而是拆开: (1) 提取关键发现;(2) 把发现按主题聚类;(3) 在每个聚类里提出一个行动。每一步是一个单独的 prompt,每一步更容易评估。

**PM 决策点**

- 团队在动手写定制 prompt 之前,有没有先把这些明显的 pattern 试一遍?
- 这个任务真的简单到一个 prompt 能搞定,还是应该拆开?这个判断错了,后面所有 prompt 优化都白干。
- 我们在用延迟和成本换质量,这个换法值不值?这件事要算清楚再做。

**注意事项**

- 简单任务不需要 chain of thought,加了只是在烧 token。
- Role prompt 可能让模型编造——比如"作为一名医生,我会开这个处方……"这种本该拒答的查询,被角色给绕过去了。
- Decomposition 创造更多失败点。每一个子任务都需要自己的 eval。

**要点总结: 选跟任务匹配的 pattern。Chain of thought 用于推理,角色用于语气,decomposition 用于复杂度。**

**Qantas 案例**

Prompt pattern 选择,在 Qantas 一个很好的例子来自中断恢复分析。设想一个 AI 工具,在重大中断 (比如悉尼跑道关闭 6 小时,影响出港航班) 发生时,帮运营控制员推演恢复方案。

天真的单一 prompt ("这是中断情况,建议恢复方案") 产出的东西是浮于表面的。模型会推荐一堆通用选项,但根本没有触及具体约束: 飞机定位、机组值勤时长限制、下游轮换的连锁影响、客户构成的敏感性 (高级会员 vs 休闲旅客)、相关机场的时刻可用性。

应用 decomposition 之后,输出就变了。Prompt 被重组成一个序列: 第一步,识别受影响的航班和下游轮换;第二步,按客户构成和收入影响,把每个航班分类;第三步,给每个受影响的航班列候选恢复选项 (取消、延误、换飞机、换机组、改道);第四步,把每个选项跟约束 (机组值勤、飞机可用性、时刻规则) 对照评估;第五步,推荐一份带优先级、有理由的恢复方案。

每一步都是一次单独的 prompt 调用,每一步都更容易独立评估。模型在每一个子任务上的表现都更好,因为它不是在试图一次性把所有事情都做完。

再加上 planning,效果还能再强一档。执行之前,模型先写一份计划: "我会分析 14 个受影响航班,按 revenue at risk 排优先级,提出恢复选项,并给它们排序。" 计划创造了问责机制——模型如果跑偏了,控制员能看出来为什么。

在较难的步骤上 (约束评估那一步) 再加上 chain of thought,能抓住单次推理会错过的错误,比如某个拟议中的机组调换,实际上会违反值勤时间规则。

往大了说,这件事给 AI 交付的启示是: prompt pattern 不是学术概念,它们是映射到具体工作类型的实用工具。Decomposition 用于有清晰子步骤的任务。Chain of thought 用于涉及约束满足的任务。Planning 用于"先有结构会更好"的任务。Role prompting 用于"领域框架很重要"的任务。技能在于"识别哪个 pattern 配哪个任务",而不是"把每个 pattern 都背得滚瓜烂熟"。大部分生产级 AI 工作,用的就是两三种 pattern,用得一致、用得好,而不是把文献里的每一种 pattern 都搬出来用一遍。
