**Definition**

There are four main ways to evaluate AI output quality, each with different cost, accuracy, and use cases. Deterministic evals check for measurable properties (exact match, format validity, presence of citations). LLM as judge uses another LLM to grade output. Human review uses people to grade output. A/B testing compares versions in production with real users.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Picking the wrong eval method wastes money or produces misleading numbers. A team that uses human review for everything spends a fortune and moves slowly. A team that uses LLM as judge blindly trusts numbers that may be wrong. PMs who can match the eval method to the question being asked design eval systems that actually drive decisions.

**How it works**

| Eval type | What it measures | Speed | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deterministic | Exact match, format, schema, regex, keyword presence | Instant | Free | Structured output, classification, fact lookup |
| LLM as judge | Quality, relevance, tone, factual accuracy (with caveats) | Seconds | Cheap (LLM cost per eval) | Open ended generation, summaries, tone |
| Human review | Anything, gold standard for ambiguous quality | Hours to days | Expensive (USD 0.50 to 10 per item) | Calibration, ground truth, sensitive domains |
| A/B testing | Real user behaviour (clicks, completions, retention) | Days to weeks | Cheap per user, slow to learn | Production decisions, ROI proof |

The right mix is layered. Use deterministic evals for what they can catch (it is free). Use LLM as judge for subjective quality on large samples. Use human review on a smaller calibration set to verify the LLM judge is reliable. Use A/B testing in production for the final word.

**Worked example**

For a customer support chatbot:

- Deterministic: Does every response contain a greeting? Is the response under 200 words? Does it cite a knowledge base article?
- LLM as judge: Is the response factually consistent with the cited article? Is the tone empathetic? Did it answer the user's question?
- Human review: 50 sampled conversations per week, rated by a support lead for trust and accuracy
- A/B testing: Does v2 of the system reduce escalation rate vs v1 in production?

Each layer catches different problems. Deterministic catches format breaks. LLM judge catches quality drift. Human review calibrates the LLM judge. A/B tells you what users actually do.

**PM decision points**

- For each eval question, what is the cheapest reliable method?
- Has the team validated LLM as judge against human review on a calibration set?
- Are deterministic checks running on every output in production, not just in testing?

**Watch outs**

- LLM as judge is biased, judges tend to favour their own model family and verbose responses
- Human review fatigues, reviewers get less reliable after 50+ items per session
- A/B testing without sufficient traffic produces noisy results, calculate sample size needed

**Takeaway: match the eval method to the question. Deterministic for the cheap catches, LLM judge for scale, humans for ground truth, A/B for real world proof.**

**Qantas story**

At Qantas scale, no single eval method is sufficient. The smart approach layers multiple methods based on what each does well.

Consider a hypothetical AI assistant for the Frequent Flyer team. The eval stack might look like this. Deterministic evals run on every response in real time, checking that the output follows expected schema, contains required disclosures, never mentions competitor airline names, and never reveals other members' data. These are free to run, fast, and catch hard failures immediately.

LLM as judge evals run on a sampled subset of production traffic, maybe 5 percent. The judge model evaluates response quality, factual accuracy against retrieved sources, tone consistency with brand guidelines, and helpfulness. The cost is modest per evaluation, the throughput is high, and the results give an ongoing signal of quality drift.

Human review runs on a smaller sample, maybe 1 percent of production traffic, with senior Frequent Flyer team members grading interactions weekly. This is expensive and slow but provides the gold standard against which the LLM judge gets calibrated. If the LLM judge starts diverging from human grades, it needs to be retuned or replaced.

A/B testing happens at the feature level, comparing two versions of the assistant on production traffic. Metrics include user satisfaction ratings, escalation rate to human agents, task completion rate, and follow up question rate. The A/B test gives the business level signal that justifies (or kills) feature releases.

Each layer catches different problems. Deterministic catches schema breaks and policy violations. LLM judge catches quality drift on individual interactions. Human review catches subtle quality issues the LLM judge misses and keeps the judge honest. A/B testing catches whether changes actually improve user outcomes.

The integration is where it gets interesting. The LLM judge runs continuously and surfaces metrics on dashboards. Human reviewers see the highest variance and lowest confidence cases first, focusing their limited time. A/B test results trigger automatic rollback when key metrics regress. Deterministic violations block releases entirely.

The organisational reality is that this stack takes real investment. Many enterprise AI projects ship without it and then struggle in production. The teams that invest in eval infrastructure early ship slower initial versions but improve faster over time, because they actually know when changes help and when they hurt.

For AI delivery, the lesson is that the eval stack is not optional for serious enterprise AI work. The investment pays for itself in faster iteration, fewer production incidents, and clearer evidence to leadership that the system is working. Clients sometimes resist the eval investment because it does not produce visible features. The right framing is that eval infrastructure is what makes future feature work possible. Without it, every change is a guess.

**定义**

AI 项目里实战中常用的三种主要 eval 类型。Golden dataset eval 是把输出跟人工挑过的"正确"答案做比较。LLM-as-judge eval 是用第二个 LLM 给第一个的输出打分。人类偏好 eval 是让真实人类对真实使用情况下的输出质量打分。每一种解决不同的问题,成本也不一样。

**为何在交付中重要**

不同用例需要不同的 eval 类型。客户问"我们怎么度量 AI 有没有在工作?",答案不是一种方法,是多种方法的组合,具体看你在测什么。能把正确的 eval 类型匹配到正确的问题上的 PM,能让团队不从一套昂贵的 eval 流程里读出错误的信号。

**运作原理**

| 类型 | 怎么工作 | 适合什么 | 成本 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Golden dataset | 一份静态的"正确答案"语料库,自动给输出打分 | 分类、提取、结构化任务 | 低,可反复跑 |
| LLM-as-judge | 用第二个 (通常更强的) LLM 给输出评分 | 开放式生成、语气、主观维度 | 中,每次 eval 调用有成本 |
| 人类偏好 | 真实用户或者评估者对输出打分或两两对比 | 真正的质量度量、A/B 测试 | 高,慢,但是是金标准 |
| 启发式 / 基于规则 | 简单的代码检查 (格式有效、引用存在、延迟低于阈值) | 不可妥协的基础检查 | 几乎不要钱 |
| 在线 eval | 在生产输出上监控特定信号 | 漂移检测、真实世界质量追踪 | 一次性投入,持续运行成本不高 |

实践中,生产系统全都在用。启发式做入站门 (输出本身是否合法?)。Golden dataset 用于回归测试。LLM-as-judge 处理细微差别。人类偏好留给重大版本里程碑、或者在拿不准的时候用。

**举例**

任务: AI 写客服回复。

启发式: 回复是不是低于 500 字?有没有用允许的语气词汇?有没有签名?

Golden dataset: 对这种类型的工单,回复有没有引用对的政策章节? (可以自动检查,因为政策引用是结构化的。)

LLM-as-judge: 回复是不是符合品牌指南 (温暖、直接、不卑躬屈膝)? (用第二个 LLM 在 1 到 5 的尺度上给每条回复打分。)

人类偏好: 我们真正的客服坐席评估了 50 条回复,他们把新模型的输出在 73% 的时候排在旧模型之上。

**PM 决策点**

- 这个任务有客观的真值 (用 golden dataset),还是主观的质量 (用 LLM-as-judge 或人类)?这个判断错了,后面整个 eval 体系都偏。
- 我们能在 eval 基础设施上投多少?哪里 ROI 最高?
- 我们怎么防止 eval 数据泄漏进训练或者开发数据? 这件事一旦发生,所有 eval 数字都失去意义。

**注意事项**

- LLM-as-judge 可以做成多数投票: 几个不同的评判模型一起,比单一一个更可靠。
- 人类偏好贵且慢。把它留给"如果搞错代价不可接受"的高风险变更。
- Golden dataset 会随时间漂移: 内容变了、真值变了。每年至少做一次更新预算。

**要点总结: 没有一种 eval 方法包打天下。把类型匹配到问题,在不同维度上组合方法。**

**Qantas 案例**

对像 Qantas 这种航空公司来说,组合里的不同 AI 用例,在召唤不同的 eval 方法。我们逐个看几个,以及哪种 eval 类型适合哪个。

给客服工单做意图分类是 golden dataset 的地盘。任务是客观的 (每个工单有一个真实的意图标签),量是高的,真值可以一次性以适度成本构建出来、然后定期演化。Eval 是确定性的: 模型预测匹配标签的比例是多少。每次改模型或者改 prompt 之后,回归马上就能被检测到。

给延误通知生成面向客户的邮件,是 LLM-as-judge 加人类偏好的地盘。这里不存在"唯一正确答案"。质量是关于语气、清晰度、品牌一致性、合适的道歉、附上相关的下一步。基于规则的检查可以抓基础项 (低于字数上限、提到航班号、避免推卸语言)。LLM-as-judge 可以在多个维度上给输出打分 (语气合适度、清晰度、完整性)。人类偏好,通过小规模坐席审查小组,提供真正的质量度量,但太贵跑不起持续运行。三种方式在不同时间尺度上组合起来。

代码共享伙伴政策的检索是混合场景。"我用 BA 持有人身份在 SYD-LHR 上飞 Qantas,能拿到什么?"——这种问题有真实答案 (来自代码共享协议),所以 golden dataset eval 跑得很好。但回复有没有让客户读得懂 (哪怕技术上正确),这就是 LLM-as-judge 的问题。两种类型并行跑。

对客服 agent 的评估是更难的地盘。Agent 完成任务了吗?用对工具了吗?在该升级时升级了吗?事实是不是正确?它需要一个分解过的复合 eval: 任务完成 (启发式 + golden dataset)、工具使用准确性 (golden dataset)、回复质量 (LLM-as-judge)、合理升级 (启发式)。光看一个数字告诉不了你这个 agent 准备好了没有。

有意思的运营点是: 不同的 eval 类型映射到不同的运营节奏。启发式和 golden dataset 在 CI 里每次提交都跑。LLM-as-judge 在每个候选版本上夜间跑。人类偏好在重大版本之前跑一次 (太贵,跑不起持续运行)。在线 eval 在每个生产回复上,针对特定信号持续跑。每个时间尺度抓不同种类的问题。

往大了说,这件事给 AI 交付的启示是: Eval 设计是 AI 项目的一个工程学科,跟系统设计一样要刻意去做。"我们会测试它"不是一份 eval 策略。具体的策略要回答: 要回答什么问题、用什么方法、运营节奏怎么走。从客户对话的早期就带着这种具体性的交付者,会赢得信任,而且通常能避免那种"我们以为它会工作"的惊喜在后期把项目搞死。
