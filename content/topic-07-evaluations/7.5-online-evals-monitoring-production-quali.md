**Definition**

Online evals are quality measurements done on live production traffic, as opposed to offline evals run on a fixed dataset. They include sampling and grading real user interactions, tracking proxy metrics (escalation rate, thumbs down, retry rate), and detecting drift over time. Online evals are how you know the system is still working, not just how it was working at launch.

**Why it matters in delivery**

AI systems degrade in production for reasons offline evals do not catch: user behaviour shifts, content changes upstream, the model gets a quiet provider update, retrieval index grows or stales. Without online evals, you find out about quality problems from customer complaints, which is too late. PMs who set up online evals catch problems in days instead of months.

**How it works**

| Online eval method | What it catches | Cost | Latency to signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Implicit feedback (clicks, completions, escalations) | Real user behaviour, business impact | Free | Days |
| Explicit feedback (thumbs, ratings, comments) | User perceived quality | Free, but low capture rate | Days |
| LLM as judge on sampled traffic | Quality drift on real distribution | LLM cost per sample | Hours |
| Human review on sampled traffic | High fidelity ground truth | Expensive | Days |
| Distribution monitoring (latency, cost, refusals) | System health, cost blowouts | Free with logging | Minutes |
| Output validation (schema, format, length) | Format regressions | Free | Real time |

A mature online eval setup combines several layers: real time deterministic checks, hourly LLM judge sampling, daily human review on a small sample, and weekly business metric review.

The point is not to grade every interaction. It is to sample enough to detect drift, with a fast feedback loop when something breaks.

**PM decision points**

- What are the leading indicators (escalation, thumbs down, refusal rate) that signal quality problems?
- Who owns the online eval dashboard, and how often is it reviewed?
- What is the threshold that triggers investigation, and who responds?

**Watch outs**

- Sampling bias, if you only review the easy interactions, you miss the hard ones
- Alert fatigue, set thresholds that are meaningful, not just sensitive
- Privacy and compliance, production data in eval pipelines often crosses legal lines, design for it

**Takeaway: offline evals tell you what shipped. Online evals tell you what is still working. Both are required.**

**Qantas story**

Online evals are how Qantas, or any enterprise, would actually know its production AI system is still working. Offline evals tell you what shipped. Online evals tell you what is happening right now.

Consider a hypothetical Qantas customer service assistant six months into production. Offline evals are still passing. The team has not made any prompt or model changes. By every measure they look at directly, the system is fine. And yet escalation rates from the AI to human agents have crept up over the past three weeks from 14 percent to 19 percent. The team only notices because someone glances at the dashboard and the trend line catches their eye.

Investigation reveals that the underlying frequent flyer program had recently changed several benefit rules, and the AI's knowledge base had not been updated. Retrieval was returning stale content that conflicted with what customers were now told elsewhere. The system was confidently giving wrong answers, and customers were escalating to humans to get the correct ones.

This is exactly the kind of problem that online evals catch and offline evals miss. The offline eval set was built before the benefit change. It still passes because it tests against the old (and now incorrect) ground truth. The problem only shows up in production because customers ask questions that depend on the updated rules.

The online eval stack that catches this kind of drift looks like the following. Real time deterministic checks (every response follows schema, never mentions disallowed content). Hourly LLM as judge sampling on a percentage of production traffic, scoring quality and flagging anomalies. Daily human review on a smaller sample, with reviewers focusing on the lowest quality automated grades. Weekly review of business metrics (escalation rate, satisfaction scores, repeat contact rate) at the topic level so trends become visible.

The dashboard that surfaces all of this is critical. If the data is collected but nobody looks at it, the system is no better than not collecting it. The discipline is that someone is responsible for reviewing the dashboard at a defined cadence, with documented triage paths when something looks off.

The interesting design question is how to surface signals without alert fatigue. Set thresholds too sensitive and the team gets paged constantly for noise. Set them too loose and real problems slip through. The right approach is tiered. Hard limits trigger immediate alerts (cost overrun, policy violations). Trend changes trigger weekly review (escalation rate creeping up, quality score drifting down). Aggregate health metrics inform monthly leadership review.

For AI delivery, the lesson is that production AI systems are not static, and the monitoring discipline needs to reflect that. Content changes upstream. User behaviour evolves. Model provider updates ripple through. Without online evals, the team learns about problems from customer complaints, which is slow and expensive. With online evals, the team catches drift in days and addresses it before customers feel the impact. Clients sometimes resist the investment because it does not produce visible features. The right framing is that online evals are how the team protects the investment in features they have already built.

**定义**

在线 evals 是衡量实际生产输出质量的连续测量,与衡量精选数据集上质量的离线 evals 形成对比。在线 evals 包括延迟、成本、用户反馈、行为信号 (用户重新表达问题?升级到人类?接受响应?) 和样本驱动的人类审查。

**为何在交付中重要**

离线 evals 告诉你系统的能力。在线 evals 告诉你它实际上正在做什么。两者都需要,因为生产分布与 eval 数据集不同,生产行为漂移,真实用户互动揭示了 eval 数据从未捕捉的失败模式。

**运作原理**

| 在线信号 | 它衡量什么 | 它如何被收集 |
|---|---|---|
| 延迟分布 (p50, p95, p99) | 用户感知的速度 | API 仪器 |
| 成本每请求和每用户 | 实际经济学 | 跨 LLM 调用的 token 跟踪 |
| 用户反馈 (拇指,评分) | 显式质量信号 | UI 提示 |
| 行为信号 (重新表达,放弃) | 隐式质量信号 | 会话分析 |
| 升级率 | 系统何时挣扎 | 人工切换检测 |
| 拒绝率 | 系统拒绝多频繁 | 在响应中模式匹配 |
| 模型置信度分布 | 检测漂移到困难案例 | 日志记录 logprobs 在可用时 |
| 样本人类审查 | 真实质量度量 | 随机抽样 + 标注 |

样本审查是大多数昂贵的金标准信号。1% 的生产输出每周由人类审查者评分。趋势浮现在仪表板中。新失败模式被发现并添加到 eval 套件。这就是生产闭环到离线 eval 改进的方式。

**PM 决策点**

- 我们今天在生产中测量什么,什么是空白?
- 我们如何处理用户反馈,它实际上塑造决策吗?
- 谁审查样本输出,他们的发现如何在产品中行动?

**注意事项**

- 拇指向下数据非常嘈杂,使用模式,不是单个事件
- 行为信号 (重新表达,放弃) 解释起来微妙但常常比显式评分更有信息
- 完整事件日志记录是 GDPR 和数据保留考虑,提前与隐私设计

**要点总结: 在线 evals 关闭离线 evals 和真实生产之间的循环。两者都需要,他们衡量不同的事情。**

**Qantas 案例**

在线 eval 在 Qantas 设置中以揭示离线 eval 错过的事情的方式有价值。考虑客户服务 AI 助手在头几个月的生产中。离线 evals 看起来很棒。延迟在预算内。系统通过它的回归测试。但客户服务运营领导报告"事情感觉不对"。代理沮丧。客户调查显示满意度下降。

在线 evals 揭示离线套件错过的内容。延迟分布在某些时间窗口期间显示长尾,与峰值时段相关,在那里客户被烦恼的等待时间影响。每客户成本比模型显示得更高,因为某些用例迭代多次以解决,模型从未估计的事情。升级率比预期高,因为系统在它本应处理的案例上礼貌地拒绝。拒绝率比预期高,因为模型对模糊但合法的请求被过度警惕。

每个发现来自在线信号,不是离线评估。每个反馈到产品改进。延迟修复涉及在峰值时段路由到更便宜的模型层级。每客户成本修复涉及在缓存中失败时改进检索回退。升级率修复涉及在边缘情况上更宽松的 prompt。拒绝率修复涉及澄清 prompt 中适当的拒绝边界。

样本人类审查程序是更不舒服的纪律。每周 1% 的随机抽样面向客户响应被高级代理审查,在五点量表上评分。评分按用例类别报告。它揭示真实生产质量随时间的演变。

有趣的洞察来自将在线信号与离线 eval 分数相关联。当用户报告满意度下降时,样本审查显示什么?这些发现是否被反映在离线 evals 中?如果不是,套件需要更新以包括类似的案例。如果是,离线 evals 是预测性的,变更应该已经被识别。

第三种纪律是仪表板。在线 evals 不能只存在于日志中。它们必须存在于产品所有者每天看到的仪表板中。延迟、成本、用户反馈、升级率、拒绝率、按类别质量评分 - 所有都可见,所有都按时间趋势,所有都按用例细分。仪表板对生产 AI 与对其他生产系统一样必要。

对 AI 交付来说,要点是 evals 是分层的运营纪律。离线 evals 是更改控制层。在线 evals 是真实质量层。结合它们,他们让团队信心地运营生产 AI。单独,他们留下大空白。建立这两层的客户在六个月内看到改进的轨迹。只有一个的客户看到不可解释的波动,通常将其归因于"AI 不可靠"而不是不完整的衡量。
