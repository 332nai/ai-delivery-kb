**Definition**

The RAG pipeline is a sequence of six steps that turn a user question into a grounded answer. Each step is a distinct technical component and each has its own failure modes. Understanding the pipeline is what separates PMs who can scope and triage RAG projects from those who cannot.

**Why it matters in delivery**

When a RAG system gives a bad answer, the cause is almost always in one specific step. "The model is wrong" is rarely the right diagnosis. PMs who know the pipeline can ask the right diagnostic question ("did we retrieve the right chunk?") instead of vague complaints to the engineering team.

**How it works**

The flow:

1. **Chunking** breaks documents into smaller pieces (a paragraph, a section) because models cannot read entire 500 page documents at once
2. **Embedding** converts text into numerical vectors so that semantically similar text is mathematically close
3. **Vector store** holds those vectors and lets you search them fast
4. **Retrieval** finds the K most relevant chunks for the user's question
5. **Reranking** uses a smarter (slower) model to reorder those chunks by true relevance
6. **Generation** sends the user question plus the top chunks to the LLM, which writes the answer

**PM decision points**

- Is the team measuring retrieval quality separately from answer quality?
- Can the team replay a bad answer and see exactly which chunks were retrieved?
- Where in the pipeline is the most likely failure for this domain?

**Watch outs**

- Most RAG demos skip reranking, most production systems need it
- Each pipeline step adds latency, a slow RAG system is usually slow because of cumulative latency, not the LLM
- The default settings of frameworks like LangChain are rarely optimal for any specific use case

**Takeaway: RAG is a pipeline, not a model. Quality depends on every step working, and debugging requires knowing which step failed.**

**Qantas story**

The RAG pipeline at Qantas scale has interesting choke points at every stage. Consider a knowledge assistant over the Qantas Sales Data Support content (which covers fare rules, ticketing procedures, refund policies, and a hundred other operational topics for travel agents and customer service teams).

Ingestion is non trivial. The source content lives in multiple systems, with multiple formats (PDFs, HTML pages, Word documents, structured database content). Each format needs its own extraction pipeline. PDFs with tables, especially fare rule tables with conditions and exceptions, are notoriously hard to chunk correctly.

Chunking is where most quality lives. Naive 500 token chunks shred fare rule tables, splitting the conditions away from the exceptions. The fix is structural: chunk by section, preserve tables whole, attach the section heading as metadata. This sounds simple and is genuinely fiddly to get right across hundreds of document types.

Embedding choice matters more than people expect. A general purpose embedding model handles most queries well, but airline jargon (fare basis codes, ticketing acronyms, sector specific terminology) is poorly represented in general embeddings. The retrieval miss rate on specialised terminology is noticeably higher than on plain language queries. Fine tuning embeddings on airline content, or switching to a domain specific embedding model, is a real lever.

Vector store choice at Qantas scale is rarely about the vector DB itself. It is about access control integration with existing identity systems, region constraints (Sydney availability for AWS or Azure), and operational maturity (can the team running it operate it at airline reliability standards). Pinecone, Qdrant, pgvector, and the cloud native options all work technically. The decision is operational.

Retrieval needs both semantic and keyword paths. A query for "FBN code" needs exact matching, not semantic similarity. A query for "what happens when a passenger changes their mind about a non refundable ticket" needs semantic reasoning. Hybrid retrieval covers both.

Reranking is the cheap quality win. A reranker over the top 30 candidates typically lifts answer quality by another 10 to 15 percent at modest latency cost. Most production systems include it. Most prototypes do not, which is part of why prototypes underdeliver against production.

For AI delivery, the takeaway is that RAG is a six step pipeline and each step can break the system. When answers are wrong, the diagnostic question is "which step failed," not "is the model bad." Teams that instrument each step with its own metrics debug in minutes. Teams that treat the pipeline as a black box debug in days.

**定义**

RAG pipeline 是把"用户的一个问题"变成"有出处的答案"的一条六步流水线。每一步都是一个独立的技术组件,每一步都有自己的失败模式。把这条 pipeline 搞清楚,是"能给 RAG 项目定范围和做问题分类的 PM"和"做不到的 PM"之间的分水岭。

**为何在交付中重要**

当一个 RAG 系统给出烂答案的时候,原因几乎总是出在某一个具体的步骤上。"模型错了"这种诊断很少是对的。一个理解 pipeline 的 PM 能提出正确的诊断问题 ("我们检索到对的 chunk 了吗?"),而不是对工程团队抛出一个模糊的抱怨。

**运作原理**

流程:

1. **Chunking** 把文档切成更小的片段 (一段、一节),因为模型没法一次读完一份 500 页的文档。
2. **Embedding** 把文本转成数值向量,让语义上接近的文本在数学上也接近。
3. **Vector store** 存这些向量,而且让你能快速搜索它们。
4. **Retrieval** 针对用户问题,找出 K 个最相关的 chunk。
5. **Reranking** 用一个更聪明 (也更慢) 的模型,按真实相关性把这些 chunk 重新排一次序。
6. **Generation** 把用户问题和最相关的几个 chunk 一起发给 LLM,由 LLM 写出答案。

**PM 决策点**

- 团队有没有把"检索质量"跟"答案质量"分开度量?这两件事不分开,就没办法定位失败在哪一步。
- 一个烂答案出来之后,团队能不能复现出来"当时具体检索到了哪几个 chunk"?这是 debug 的基础能力。
- 在这个领域里,pipeline 最可能失败的环节是哪一步? 这个问题不在项目早期回答,后面就会一遍遍重蹈覆辙。

**注意事项**

- 大多数 RAG demo 跳过了 reranking,但大多数生产系统都需要它。
- Pipeline 每一步都在加延迟。慢的 RAG 系统,慢的原因通常是延迟在 pipeline 里累积,而不是 LLM 本身慢。
- LangChain 之类框架给的默认配置,几乎从来不是某个具体用例的最优解。

**要点总结: RAG 是一条 pipeline,不是一个模型。质量取决于每一步都能工作,debug 取决于知道是哪一步失败了。**

**Qantas 案例**

Qantas 规模上的 RAG pipeline,每一步都有有意思的瓶颈。设想一个知识助手,数据源是 Qantas Sales Data Support 内容 (覆盖票价规则、票务程序、退款政策,以及给旅行社和客服团队的一百多个其他运营主题)。

接入这一步就不平凡。源内容散落在多个系统里,格式五花八门 (PDF、HTML 页面、Word 文档、结构化数据库内容)。每种格式都需要自己的一条提取 pipeline。带表格的 PDF,尤其是带条件和例外的票价规则表,是出了名的不好 chunking。

Chunking 是大部分质量上下浮动的地方。天真的 500 token chunk 会把票价规则表切碎,把条件和例外分到两个 chunk 里。修复要靠结构: 按节 chunk、保持表格完整、把节标题作为元数据附进 chunk。听起来简单,但在几百种文档类型上一个一个做对,真的是细活。

Embedding 的选择比一般人想的更重要。通用 embedding 模型对大部分查询表现都还行,但航空行话 (票价基础代码、票务首字母缩写、行业特定术语) 在通用 embedding 里表征得很差。专业术语查询的检索失败率,明显比日常语言查询高。在航空内容上 fine tune embedding,或者切换到一个领域特定的 embedding 模型,是真正的杠杆所在。

在 Qantas 规模上,Vector store 的选择很少是关于 vector 数据库本身的。它是关于跟现有身份系统的访问控制集成、region 约束 (AWS 或 Azure 的悉尼可用性)、以及运营成熟度 (跑这个系统的团队能不能按航空业的可靠性标准跑得起来)。Pinecone、Qdrant、pgvector、各家云原生方案,技术上都能用。决策是运营层面的。

检索需要语义和关键词两条路并行。"FBN code" 这种查询需要精确匹配,不需要语义相似度。"乘客买了不可退款票之后又改主意了怎么办" 这种查询需要语义推理。Hybrid retrieval 把两条路都覆盖。

Reranking 是性价比极高的质量提升。在前 30 个候选 chunk 上加一个 reranker,通常能在适度的延迟代价下,把答案质量再拉高 10 到 15%。大部分生产系统都包含 reranking,大部分原型都没有——这就是为什么原型上线之后表现比 demo 差一档的原因之一。

往大了说,这件事给 AI 交付的启示是: RAG 是一条六步 pipeline,任何一步都可能把整个系统拖垮。答案错的时候,正确的诊断问题是"哪一步失败了",不是"模型是不是变差了"。给每一步都加上独立指标的团队,debug 在几分钟内完成。把 pipeline 当黑盒的团队,debug 要花几天。
