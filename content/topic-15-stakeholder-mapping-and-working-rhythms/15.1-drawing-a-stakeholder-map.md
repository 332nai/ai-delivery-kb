**Definition**

A stakeholder map is a one page artifact that shows every person whose opinion or action can affect the project, grouped by the role they play. The four roles that matter most for AI projects are: sponsors (who fund and authorise), users (who will operate the system), blockers (who can stop the project even without authority to start it), and influencers (who do not decide but shape the views of those who do). The map is produced in week one, updated as the project progresses, and used to plan engagement.

**Why it matters in delivery**

The first failure mode in stakeholder management is treating the org chart as the stakeholder map. The org chart shows formal authority. The real influence on an AI project is rarely confined to the org chart. A junior data analyst with strong opinions about model accuracy may have more practical influence than a senior executive who never attends a working session. The org chart misses this, and the team that plans engagement against the org chart over-engages with formal authority and under-engages with practical influence.

The second failure mode is treating stakeholder mapping as a one-time exercise. Stakeholders change throughout a project. Someone who was an influencer becomes a blocker when their priorities shift. Someone who was a peripheral observer becomes critical when their team is asked to integrate. The map needs to be a living document, not a week one slide that nobody returns to.

**How it works**

The map has four quadrants, organised by the role the stakeholder plays. Each stakeholder goes in one quadrant (occasionally two), with their name, role title, and a one line note on what they care about.

| Role | Definition | Typical examples | What they need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor | Authorises the project, owns the outcome at executive level | Operating sponsor, executive sponsor, sometimes a steering committee chair | Confidence the project will deliver, visibility into progress, defensibility to their peers |
| User | Will operate the system day to day, or whose work it will change | Operating team members, frontline staff, supervisors | Confidence the tool will actually help them, not threaten their role or add work |
| Blocker | Cannot start the project but can stop it | Legal, security, privacy, IT architecture, regulatory affairs, sometimes finance | Confidence that risks are managed, that constraints are respected, that no surprises are coming |
| Influencer | Shapes the views of decision makers without holding decision authority | Trusted advisors to the sponsor, senior individual contributors, vocal team members | Confidence that their expertise is being considered, that their views are heard |

The four roles are not mutually exclusive. A senior data engineer might be both a user (will operate the system) and a blocker (can refuse to provide data access). A senior executive might be both a sponsor and an influencer (formally authorises one project, informally shapes the views of sponsors of related projects). The mapping should reflect this complexity.

**How to produce the map**

The first version of the map is produced by the delivery team in week one, based on the SOW, the kickoff conversations, and any pre-engagement context. It is then reviewed with the operating sponsor in week two. The operating sponsor will reveal stakeholders the team did not know existed, especially influencers and blockers who are not formally in the project governance.

A useful question to ask the operating sponsor when reviewing the draft map: "if you wanted to kill this project, whose call would you make?" The answer is almost always the list of blockers and influencers the team has not identified.

A second useful question: "in our weekly steering committee, who is in the room but is mostly listening to one specific other person?" The person being listened to is an influencer worth knowing.

**Maintaining the map**

The map is updated every two weeks, alongside the risk register review. Three kinds of updates are typical:

*New stakeholders.* Someone joins the project who was not on the original map. They get added to the appropriate quadrant.

*Role changes.* A stakeholder's role in the project shifts. A user becomes an influencer because they have started speaking publicly about the project. An influencer becomes a blocker because their position has hardened against an aspect of the design.

*Departures.* Stakeholders leave or change roles in their own organisation. The map is updated to reflect this, and the team plans how to engage the replacement.

The total time to maintain a stakeholder map across a 14 week project is roughly two hours, spread over the project. The leverage is high.

**What to do with the map**

The map is not an artifact for its own sake. It exists to inform engagement decisions. Three practical applications:

*Engagement planning.* For each quadrant, the right engagement intensity is different. Sponsors need regular structured updates. Users need workshops and prototypes. Blockers need early constraints conversations and steady reassurance. Influencers need to feel their views are being considered. Mapping the stakeholders by quadrant immediately tells the team how to allocate their engagement time.

*Pre-meeting preparation.* Before any meeting with a new stakeholder, the map tells the team what role this person plays, what they care about, and what kind of conversation will land well. Without the map, every new conversation starts from scratch.

*Risk surfacing.* Risks in the risk register often map to specific stakeholders. The map makes the connection visible. A risk of "legal review delaying launch" maps to specific named legal stakeholders, with engagement gaps that can be addressed.

**PM decision points**

- Have I produced a stakeholder map in week one, or am I relying on the org chart?
- Have I reviewed the map with the operating sponsor, including the "who would kill this project" question?
- Am I updating the map every two weeks, or has it become a stale week one artifact?

**Watch outs**

- Resist the temptation to map only senior people. The blockers and influencers who matter are often two or three levels below the executive sponsor.
- The stakeholder map is not a political document. It is an engagement tool. Avoid language that characterises stakeholders as obstacles or allies. The neutral framing (role, what they care about) holds up better and is safer if the map is seen by someone outside the team.
- A map that lists 40 people is not a map, it is a directory. Aim for 10 to 15 stakeholders, the ones whose engagement actually affects the project.

**Takeaway: a stakeholder map is a one page artifact showing sponsors, users, blockers, and influencers, with what each cares about. Produce it in week one, review it with the operating sponsor, update it every two weeks. Use it to plan engagement, prepare for meetings, and surface risks.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine running a Qantas AI project to build an internal tool for the cargo operations team: a system that helps cargo schedulers match incoming shipment requests to available cargo capacity across the network. The SOW lists three formal stakeholders: the head of cargo operations as executive sponsor, a senior cargo manager as operating sponsor, and a representative from IT.

The delivery team produces an initial stakeholder map in week one based on this. The map looks thin. The PM reviews it with the operating sponsor in week two, with the "who would kill this project" question.

The operating sponsor's answer is revealing. The formal list misses several critical stakeholders. First, the head of network planning, who owns the data on available cargo capacity, has been skeptical of AI projects in general and could withhold cooperation on data access. He is a blocker, formally not on the project, with the authority to slow data access if he chose. Second, a senior cargo scheduler with 20 years of experience is highly respected by peers and has been openly skeptical of automation. She is an influencer, not in formal governance, who could shape the views of the daily users. Third, the cargo finance manager will need to sign off on the cost case before scaling, and has a different perspective on ROI than the operating sponsor. She is a blocker for scaling phases. Fourth, the cargo customer relationship managers, who interact with shippers daily, will be affected by any changes to scheduling priorities and could escalate to their executive if they feel disadvantaged. They are users (indirectly) and influencers (their feedback shapes executive view).

The map is updated. The four quadrants now contain: the executive sponsor and operating sponsor (sponsors), the daily cargo schedulers and indirectly the customer relationship managers (users), the head of network planning and the cargo finance manager (blockers), and the experienced cargo scheduler and the customer relationship managers (influencers).

The engagement plan is built against this updated map. The head of network planning gets a 45 minute introductory meeting in week two, where the team listens to his concerns about data access and AI more broadly. This conversation reveals that his concerns are about transparency rather than capability, and he agrees to data access in exchange for a clear documentation of how the model uses his data. The experienced cargo scheduler is invited into the requirements workshops, not as a token presence but as a substantive contributor. She raises edge cases that nobody else has thought of, several of which become eval rows. The cargo finance manager gets a brief in week four, before financial decisions need to be made.

The interesting test comes in week 10. The model produces a recommendation that the experienced cargo scheduler disagrees with. She raises it loudly in a working session. The team's response is to take her concern seriously, walk through the model's reasoning, and identify the gap (a specific edge case the eval set had not captured). The model is updated, the eval row is added, and the scheduler becomes an active advocate for the tool because her input was respected. Without the map identifying her as a key influencer, the team might have dismissed the objection as one user's preference.

In parallel, the head of network planning continues to provide data access without friction, because the early conversation established trust. The cargo finance manager, briefed in week four, supports the financial case at week 14 without surprise. The customer relationship managers are surveyed in week 12 and most of their concerns are accommodated in the v1 design.

The launch goes smoothly. The relationships that mattered, beyond the formal three stakeholders, were all identified, engaged, and managed.

The lesson for AI delivery is that the org chart is a starting point for stakeholder mapping, not the answer. The real influencers and blockers in an AI project are often people the SOW does not mention. Producing a map that captures them, reviewing it with someone who knows the organisation, and updating it over time, is one of the most leverage-rich practices in project management. The cost is small, the protection against political surprise is large.

**定义**

干系人地图是**一份一页的产物, 展示每一位其意见或行动能影响项目的人, 按他们扮演的角色分组**。AI 项目里最重要的四个角色是: sponsor (出钱并授权)、用户 (将操作系统)、阻断者 (没有启动权但能停下项目)、影响者 (不决定但塑造决策者的观点)。地图在第 1 周产出, 项目推进时更新, 用来规划 engagement。

**为何在交付中重要**

干系人管理的第一种失败模式, 是**把组织架构图当作干系人地图**。组织架构图展示正式权威。AI 项目的真实影响很少局限在组织架构图里。一个对模型准确率有强烈意见的初级数据分析师, 可能比一个从不出席工作会议的资深高管有更多实际影响。组织架构图错过这点, 而对着组织架构图规划 engagement 的团队会对正式权威过度 engage、对实际影响不足 engage。

第二种失败模式是把干系人地图当一次性练习。Stakeholder 在整个项目过程中会变。一个影响者在他们的优先级转变时变成阻断者。一个外围观察者在他们团队被要求做集成时变成关键人物。地图需要是活文档, 不是第 1 周的一张没人回去看的 slide。

**运作原理**

地图有四个象限, 按 stakeholder 扮演的角色组织。每位 stakeholder 进一个象限 (偶尔两个), 带名字、职位、一行说明他们在意什么。

| 角色 | 定义 | 典型例子 | 他们需要什么 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor | 授权项目, 在高管层级拥有结果 | 运营 sponsor、executive sponsor、有时 steering committee 主席 | 项目会交付的信心、对进度的可见性、在同侪面前的可辩护性 |
| 用户 | 日复一日操作系统, 或其工作会被改变 | 运营团队成员、一线员工、主管 | 工具会真的帮他们、不威胁他们的角色、不加负担的信心 |
| 阻断者 | 无法启动项目但能停下它 | 法务、安全、隐私、IT 架构、监管事务、有时财务 | 风险被管理、约束被尊重、没有意外的信心 |
| 影响者 | 不持有决策权, 但塑造决策者的观点 | Sponsor 的可信顾问、资深个人贡献者、声音大的团队成员 | 他们的专业被考虑、他们的观点被听到的信心 |

四个角色不互斥。一位资深数据工程师可能既是用户 (将操作系统) 又是阻断者 (能拒绝提供数据访问)。一位资深高管可能既是 sponsor 又是影响者 (正式授权一个项目, 非正式地塑造相关项目 sponsor 的观点)。地图应该反映这种复杂性。

**怎么产出地图**

地图的第一版由交付团队在第 1 周产出, 基于 SOW、kickoff 对话、任何 engagement 前的上下文。然后在第 2 周跟运营 sponsor review。运营 sponsor 会揭示团队不知道存在的 stakeholder, 尤其是不在正式项目治理中的影响者和阻断者。

Review 草图时问运营 sponsor 一个有用的问题: "如果你想杀掉这个项目, 你会找谁?" 答案几乎总是团队没识别的阻断者和影响者清单。

第二个有用的问题: "我们每周的 steering committee 里, 谁在房间里但大部分时候在听某一位具体的其他人?" 被听的那个人是值得知道的影响者。

**维护地图**

地图每两周更新一次, 跟风险登记表 review 并列。典型有三种更新:

*新 stakeholder*。**有人加入项目, 之前不在原始地图上**。**他们被加到合适的象限**。

*角色变化*。**Stakeholder 在项目里的角色转变**。**一位用户因为开始公开谈论项目而变成影响者**。**一位影响者因为对设计某方面立场硬化而变成阻断者**。

*离开*。**Stakeholder 在自己的组织里离开或换岗**。**地图被更新反映, 团队规划怎么 engage 接替者**。

一个 14 周项目维护干系人地图的总时间大约两小时, 分散在项目里。**杠杆很高**。

**怎么用地图**

地图不是为自己存在的产物。它存在是为了告知 engagement 决定。**三个实际应用**:

*Engagement 规划*。**每个象限的合适 engagement 强度不同**。**Sponsor 需要规律的结构化更新**。**用户需要 workshop 和原型**。**阻断者需要早期约束对话和稳定的安抚**。**影响者需要感觉他们的观点被考虑**。**按象限 map stakeholder, 立刻告诉团队怎么分配 engagement 时间**。

*会前准备*。**任何跟新 stakeholder 的会议之前, 地图告诉团队这个人扮演什么角色、他们在意什么、什么样的对话会 land**。**没有地图, 每次新对话都从零开始**。

*风险浮出*。**风险登记表上的风险经常映射到具体 stakeholder**。**地图让这种连接可见**。**"法务 review 拖延上线" 这种风险映射到具体的法务 stakeholder, 带可以处理的 engagement 缺口**。

**PM 决策点**

- 我**有没有在第 1 周产出干系人地图**, 还是**靠组织架构图**?
- 我**有没有跟运营 sponsor review 过地图**, 包括 **"谁会杀掉这个项目" 的问题**?
- 我**有没有每两周更新地图**, 还是**它变成了一个陈旧的第 1 周产物**?

**注意事项**

- **抵抗只 map 资深人员的诱惑**。**真正重要的阻断者和影响者经常在 executive sponsor 之下两三层**。
- **干系人地图不是政治文档**。**它是 engagement 工具**。**避免把 stakeholder 描述为障碍或盟友的语言**。**中性框架 (角色、在意什么) 更经得起检验**, **如果地图被团队外的人看到也更安全**。
- **列 40 个人的地图不是地图, 是通讯录**。**瞄准 10 到 15 位 stakeholder, 那些 engagement 真的影响项目的人**。

**要点总结: 干系人地图是一份一页产物, 展示 sponsor、用户、阻断者、影响者, 以及每位在意什么。第 1 周产出, 跟运营 sponsor review, 每两周更新。用它规划 engagement、为会议准备、浮出风险。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你跑一个 Qantas AI 项目, 给货运运营团队建一个内部工具: 一个系统帮货运调度员把进来的货运请求匹配到网络上可用的货运容量。SOW 列了三位正式 stakeholder: 货运运营负责人作为 executive sponsor、一位资深货运经理作为运营 sponsor、一位 IT 代表。

交付团队第 1 周根据这个产出初始干系人地图。地图看起来很薄。PM 在第 2 周跟运营 sponsor review, 带 "谁会杀掉这个项目" 的问题。

运营 sponsor 的答案有揭示性。正式清单漏了几位关键 stakeholder。**第一**, 网络规划负责人, 他拥有可用货运容量的数据, 一般对 AI 项目持怀疑态度, 可能在数据访问上不合作。他是阻断者, 正式不在项目里, 但有授权减慢数据访问如果他选择这么做。**第二**, 一位有 20 年经验的资深货运调度员, 同侪高度尊重她, 她公开对自动化持怀疑态度。她是影响者, 不在正式治理里, 但能塑造日常用户的观点。**第三**, 货运财务经理在扩展之前需要给成本案签字, 对 ROI 有跟运营 sponsor 不同的视角。她是后续阶段的阻断者。**第四**, 货运客户关系经理们, 每天跟托运人交互, 任何调度优先级的变化都会影响他们, 如果他们感觉受损会升级到自己的高管。他们是 (间接) 用户和影响者 (他们的反馈塑造高管视角)。

**地图被更新**。四个象限现在包含: executive sponsor 和运营 sponsor (sponsor)、日常货运调度员和间接的客户关系经理 (用户)、网络规划负责人和货运财务经理 (阻断者)、有经验的货运调度员和客户关系经理 (影响者)。

Engagement 计划针对这份更新过的地图建造。网络规划负责人第 2 周得到一个 45 分钟的介绍会议, 团队在那听他对数据访问和更广泛的 AI 的关切。这次对话揭示他的关切是关于透明度而非能力, 他同意提供数据访问, 以换取关于模型怎么使用他的数据的清晰文档。有经验的货运调度员被邀请进需求 workshop, 不是作为象征性出席而是作为实质贡献者。她提出没人想到的边缘情况, 其中几个变成 eval 行。货运财务经理第 4 周拿到一份简报, 在需要做财务决定之前。

有意思的测试出现在第 10 周。模型产出一个有经验的货运调度员不同意的推荐。她在工作会议上大声提出来。团队的回应是认真对待她的关切, 走一遍模型的推理, 识别出差距 (一个 eval set 没捕捉的具体边缘情况)。模型被更新, eval 行被加入, 调度员变成工具的主动倡导者, 因为她的输入被尊重。没有地图识别她是关键影响者, 团队可能把这个反对当作一位用户的偏好打发。

平行地, 网络规划负责人继续无摩擦地提供数据访问, 因为早期对话建立了信任。第 4 周被简报过的货运财务经理, 在第 14 周不带意外地支持财务案。客户关系经理们在第 12 周被调研, 他们的大部分关切在 v1 设计里被容纳。

**上线很顺**。重要的关系, 在正式三位 stakeholder 之外, 都被识别、engage、管理了。

AI 交付的启示: 组织架构图是干系人 map 的起点, 不是答案。AI 项目里真正的影响者和阻断者经常是 SOW 没提到的人。产出一份捕捉他们的地图、跟了解组织的人 review、随时间更新, 是项目管理里杠杆最高的实践之一。成本小, 对政治意外的保护大。
