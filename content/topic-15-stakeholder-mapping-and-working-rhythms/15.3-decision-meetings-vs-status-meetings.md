**Definition**

Every meeting on an AI project serves one of two primary purposes: making a decision, or sharing status. The two are different in their agenda, attendees, pre-work, and what good looks like at the end. The most common dysfunction in project meetings is letting them blur: status meetings that drift toward attempted decisions, decision meetings that get derailed into status updates. Each blur produces a worse version of both meeting types. The discipline is to be clear, in advance, which type each meeting is, and to defend the distinction in the room.

**Why it matters in delivery**

A status meeting that turns into a decision meeting damages decisions. The people who needed to be there to decide are not all present. Pre-work has not been done. The framing of the decision is improvised. A decision made in this state is often wrong, and even if right, lacks the buy-in that a properly framed decision would have.

A decision meeting that turns into a status meeting damages momentum. The people gathered to decide spend their time hearing updates instead of acting. The decision that needed to be made gets deferred. The senior people in the room conclude that the meeting was unproductive, and become reluctant to attend again.

Both failures are common because both feel natural. Stakeholders default to status: "what is happening" is the easiest topic to fill time with. Decisions require effort and discomfort. Without active steering, every meeting drifts toward status. The PM's job is to steer.

**How it works**

The two meeting types have distinct characteristics. Recognising them in advance and designing each meeting accordingly is the discipline.

| Aspect | Status meeting | Decision meeting |
|---|---|---|
| Primary purpose | Inform a group about progress, surface issues for visibility | Resolve a specific question that requires choice |
| Attendees | Whoever needs awareness | Only the people whose input or authority is needed to decide |
| Pre-work | A status document or summary | A decision memo with options, recommendation, and trade offs |
| Agenda structure | Topics in order of importance, time-boxed | Single decision, framed at start, with options reviewed and choice made |
| Output | A shared mental model of project state | A documented decision with owner and date |
| Length | 30 to 60 minutes typically | 30 to 45 minutes if pre-work is good |
| Cadence | Regular (weekly, bi-weekly) | Ad hoc, when a decision is needed |
| Failure mode | Drifting into attempted decision | Drifting into general status update |

**Designing a good status meeting**

The right structure for a status meeting:

1. Pre-read sent 24 hours before, containing the substantive content. The meeting itself is for clarification and questions, not first transmission of information.
2. A short opening (5 minutes) that names the top three things to know this week, in plain language.
3. Time for questions from attendees, structured (one topic at a time) rather than open-ended.
4. A closing summary of any action items or follow-ups, with owners.

What makes a status meeting bad: reading aloud from slides that everyone could have read, no pre-read so people are absorbing information for the first time, going around the room for updates from each person regardless of relevance.

**Designing a good decision meeting**

The right structure for a decision meeting:

1. A decision memo sent 48 hours before, with: the decision being made, the options, the recommendation with reasoning, the trade offs of each option. The memo is one to two pages, no more.
2. A short opening (5 minutes) that restates the decision and confirms everyone has read the memo.
3. The body of the meeting (20 to 30 minutes) is structured around the options, not around free discussion. Each option gets discussed in turn. Trade offs are surfaced. Questions are taken.
4. A closing decision (5 minutes), with the decision named clearly, the owner of execution named, and the date.

What makes a decision meeting bad: no pre-read so people learn the issue in the meeting, no recommendation so the conversation drifts, no time discipline so the decision is deferred, no clear close so people leave unsure of what was decided.

**The blur, and how to prevent it**

The most common pattern of blur: a status meeting where someone raises a substantive question that needs a decision. The instinct is to engage with it then and there. This is usually wrong.

The right move: acknowledge the question, note that it deserves a proper decision conversation, schedule that conversation with the right attendees and pre-work, and continue with the status meeting. This feels rude in the moment ("we are not going to discuss this important thing right now"), but it produces better decisions and better status meetings.

The framing that helps: "this is an important enough question that I want us to make the decision properly, not in passing. Can I follow up with a memo this week and schedule a 30 minute decision meeting next week?"

The reverse blur is also common: a decision meeting where someone uses the opportunity to give a status update on their part of the project. The right move is to redirect: "thanks, let me note that for the status next week. For this meeting, can we focus on the decision in front of us?" Done politely, this works without offence.

**When the two genuinely combine**

There are some meetings where status and decision legitimately mix. The most common is the steering committee, which usually does both: project health status (10 to 15 minutes) followed by specific decisions needed (the remainder). This works because the structure is clear in advance: the status portion is bounded, the decision portion is framed.

The principle: combination is fine when it is designed. Drift is not. The agenda should make explicit which parts of the meeting are status and which are decision, and the time allocated to each.

**PM decision points**

- For each meeting on the cadence, have I defined it as primarily status or primarily decision?
- For decision meetings, am I sending a memo 48 hours in advance, with options and recommendation?
- When a status meeting drifts toward decision, am I redirecting, or letting the drift happen?

**Watch outs**

- Some senior people prefer decisions to be made informally in passing because it is faster for them. Letting this happen damages decision quality and creates accountability gaps. Push back politely.
- Be careful with the word "discussion". A discussion that does not end with a decision is a status meeting. If a decision is the goal, frame the agenda around the choice, not around the discussion of it.
- The blur often happens because pre-work was not done. The lazy version of every meeting is "let us talk about it". Defend the pre-work.

**Takeaway: every meeting is primarily status or primarily decision. The two have different attendees, pre-work, structure, and outputs. Mixing them produces worse versions of both. Define the meeting type in advance, prepare accordingly, and defend the distinction in the room.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine running a Qantas AI project, an agent for the operations control centre that helps duty managers respond to weather-related disruptions. By week six, the project has a steady cadence: a weekly working session with two duty managers, a weekly check-in with the operating sponsor, and a bi-weekly steering committee. Things are running smoothly. Then a specific issue arises that needs to be decided: the model is occasionally generating outputs that recommend communicating with affected customers about weather causes, and the customer experience team has raised concerns about whether this is appropriate, given the legal sensitivity of attributing service failures to weather rather than airline operations.

The temptation is to raise this at the next weekly check-in with the operating sponsor and decide it there. The PM resists. The issue involves three stakeholders: the operating sponsor, the customer experience lead (who raised the concern), and legal. None of them are routinely in the same room, and a decision made without all three would lack legitimacy.

The PM frames it as a decision meeting. A memo is drafted: the question (should the model attribute disruption causes to weather, or use neutral framing), the options (attribute to weather as a service to customers, use neutral framing for legal safety, hybrid approach with attribution only when externally verified), the recommendation (hybrid approach), the trade offs of each. The memo is one page. It is sent to the three stakeholders 48 hours before a 30 minute decision meeting.

The meeting runs as designed. The three stakeholders have read the memo. The PM opens by restating the decision and confirming the read. The body of the meeting walks through each option, surfaces trade offs (the customer experience lead has concerns about the neutral option, legal has concerns about the attribution option), and lands on the hybrid recommendation with small modifications agreed in the room. The decision is documented in a one paragraph follow up email. The meeting ends at 28 minutes, ahead of schedule.

In parallel, the weekly status meeting that week proceeds normally, focused on progress against the eval set, the latest prompt iteration, and a minor data issue. The status meeting does not get derailed by the disruption attribution question, because that conversation happened separately.

The contrast with what would have happened if the decision was attempted in the weekly check-in: the customer experience lead was not present, legal was not present, the operating sponsor would have either committed without their input (creating risk) or deferred the decision (creating delay). The proper decision meeting produced a clean outcome with full alignment.

Three weeks later, a similar pattern plays out in reverse. The bi-weekly steering committee has a long agenda, and the operating sponsor tries to use it to give an update on prompt iteration progress. The PM redirects: "thanks, I will include that in the weekly summary I send tomorrow. For this meeting, can we focus on the launch readiness decision in front of us?" The operating sponsor accepts the redirect, the launch readiness decision gets the full attention it needs, and the steering committee ends on time with a clear decision.

The interesting general observation: across the project, the team produces about a dozen formal decision memos for specific decisions. Most are resolved in 30 minute meetings. The total time investment is small. The decision quality is high. The status meetings stay focused on awareness, the decision meetings stay focused on choices, and the project benefits from the discipline.

The lesson for AI delivery is that meetings have two primary purposes, and conflating them damages both. The discipline is to know which type each meeting is, prepare accordingly, and defend the distinction when others try to blur it. The cost of the discipline is small. The cost of the blur is missed decisions, damaged momentum, and senior stakeholders who lose interest in attending the project's meetings.

**定义**

AI 项目里每一次会议服务两个主要目的之一: 做决策, 或分享 status。两者在议程、参加者、预工作、结束时 "好" 长什么样上不同。项目会议里最常见的功能障碍是让它们混: status 会议漂向尝试决策、决策会议被偏离成 status 更新。每一次混都产出两种会议类型的更差版本。纪律是事先清楚每次会议是哪种类型, 并在房间里捍卫这个区分。

**为何在交付中重要**

一个变成决策会议的 status 会议, 损害决策。需要在场决定的人不全在。**预工作没做**。决策的框架是即兴的。这种状态下做出的决策经常是错的, 即使对, 也缺少正确框架化的决策会有的认同。

一个变成 status 会议的决策会议, 损害动量。聚来决定的人花时间听更新而不是行动。需要做的决策被推迟。房间里的资深人物结论是会议没产出, 变得不愿再次出席。

两种失败都常见, 因为两种都感觉自然。Stakeholder 默认 status: "在发生什么" 是最容易填充时间的话题。决策需要努力和不适。没有主动操纵, 每次会议都漂向 status。PM 的工作是操纵。

**运作原理**

两种会议类型有不同特征。事先识别它们、相应设计每次会议, 就是纪律。

| 方面 | Status 会议 | 决策会议 |
|---|---|---|
| 主要目的 | 让一组人知会进度、为可见性浮出问题 | 解决一个需要选择的具体问题 |
| 参加者 | 需要觉知的任何人 | 只有决策需要其输入或权威的人 |
| 预工作 | 一份 status 文档或总结 | 一份决策 memo, 带选项、建议、trade off |
| 议程结构 | 按重要性排列的话题, 限时 | 单一决策, 开头框架化, review 选项, 做出选择 |
| 产出 | 项目状态的共享心智模型 | 一份文档化的决策, 带 owner 和日期 |
| 长度 | 典型 30 到 60 分钟 | 预工作好的话 30 到 45 分钟 |
| 节奏 | 规律 (每周、双周) | Ad hoc, 当需要决策时 |
| 失败模式 | 漂向尝试决策 | 漂向一般 status 更新 |

**设计一次好的 status 会议**

Status 会议的正确结构:

1. **预读 24 小时前发, 包含实质内容**。**会议本身是为澄清和问题, 不是信息的初次传递**。
2. **简短开场 (5 分钟), 用大白话说出本周三件最重要的事**。
3. **结构化的提问时间** (**一次一个话题, 而不是开放式**)。
4. **收尾总结任何行动项或后续, 带 owner**。

什么让 status 会议坏: 大声读所有人都能读的 slide、没预读所以人们第一次吸收信息、不管相关性轮流让每个人更新。

**设计一次好的决策会议**

决策会议的正确结构:

1. **决策 memo 48 小时前发, 包含**: **正在做的决策、选项、带理由的建议、每个选项的 trade off**。**Memo 是一到两页, 不超过**。
2. **简短开场 (5 分钟), 重述决策, 确认所有人都读过 memo**。
3. **会议主体 (20 到 30 分钟) 围绕选项结构化, 不是围绕自由讨论**。**每个选项轮流讨论**。**Trade off 浮出**。**问题被处理**。
4. **收尾决策 (5 分钟), 决策被清晰命名, 执行的 owner 被命名, 带日期**。

什么让决策会议坏: 没预读所以人们在会议里学习问题、没建议所以对话漂移、没时间纪律所以决策被推迟、没清晰收尾所以人离开时不确定决定了什么。

**混淆, 以及怎么防止**

最常见的混淆 pattern: 一次 status 会议, 有人提出一个需要决策的实质问题。本能是当场 engage。**这通常错**。

**对的动作**: 承认问题、note 它值得一次合适的决策对话、用对的参加者和预工作安排那次对话、继续 status 会议。这在那一刻感觉粗鲁 ("我们现在不会讨论这件重要的事"), 但产出更好的决策和更好的 status 会议。

**有帮助的措辞**: "这是一个足够重要的问题, 我想让我们正式做出决策, 而不是顺带做。我能不能本周写一份 memo, 下周安排一次 30 分钟的决策会议?"

反向混淆也常见: 一次决策会议, 有人利用机会给自己那部分项目做 status 更新。对的动作是引导: "谢谢, 让我把那个 note 下来放到下周 status 里。这次会议, 我们能聚焦在面前的决策上吗?" 礼貌做, 这奏效, 不冒犯。

**当两者真的合在一起**

有些会议里 status 和决策合法地混。最常见的是 steering committee, 通常两者都做: 项目健康 status (10 到 15 分钟), 然后是需要的具体决策 (其余)。这奏效, 因为结构事先清晰: status 部分有界, 决策部分有框架。

**原则**: 当组合被设计时, 组合是 fine 的。**漂移不是**。议程应该明确会议哪些部分是 status、哪些是决策, 以及分配给每个的时间。

**PM 决策点**

- 节奏上的每个会议, 我**有没有把它定义为主要 status 或主要决策**?
- 决策会议, 我**有没有 48 小时前发 memo, 带选项和建议**?
- 当 status 会议漂向决策时, 我**有没有引导, 还是让漂移发生**?

**注意事项**

- **有些资深人偏好顺带非正式做决策, 因为对他们更快**。**让这个发生损害决策质量, 制造责任缺口**。**礼貌 push back**。
- **小心 "讨论" 这个词**。**不以决策结束的讨论是 status 会议**。**如果目标是决策, 把议程围绕选择框架化, 不是围绕讨论它**。
- **混淆经常发生是因为预工作没做**。**每次会议的懒版本是 "我们来谈谈"**。**捍卫预工作**。

**要点总结: 每次会议主要是 status 或主要是决策。两者有不同的参加者、预工作、结构、产出。混它们产出两者的更差版本。事先定义会议类型、相应准备、在房间里捍卫区分。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你跑一个 Qantas AI 项目, 一个给运营控制中心的 agent, 帮值班经理应对天气相关中断。到第六周, 项目有稳定的节奏: 跟两位值班经理的每周工作会议、跟运营 sponsor 的每周 check-in、双周 steering committee。**事情跑得顺**。然后一个需要决策的具体 issue 出现: 模型偶尔生成的输出, 建议跟受影响客户沟通天气原因, 客户体验团队对这是否合适提出关切, 鉴于把服务失败归咎于天气而非航司运营的法律敏感性。

诱惑是下次跟运营 sponsor 的每周 check-in 提这件事, 当场决定。**PM 抵抗**。问题涉及三位 stakeholder: 运营 sponsor、提出关切的客户体验负责人、法务。他们没有一位常规在同一房间里, 没有三位都在的情况下做出的决策会缺少合法性。

PM 把它框架成决策会议。一份 memo 起草: 问题 (模型应该把中断原因归咎于天气, 还是用中性框架)、选项 (作为服务给客户归咎于天气、为法律安全用中性框架、混合方式只在外部验证时归咎)、建议 (混合方式)、每个选项的 trade off。Memo 一页。它在 30 分钟决策会议之前 48 小时发给三位 stakeholder。

会议按设计运行。三位 stakeholder 都读过 memo。PM 开场重述决策、确认读过。会议主体走过每个选项, 浮出 trade off (客户体验负责人对中性选项有关切, 法务对归咎选项有关切), 落在带小修改的混合建议上, 修改在房间里同意。决策在一段后续邮件里文档化。会议在 28 分钟结束, 提前。

平行地, 那周的每周 status 会议正常进行, 聚焦在 eval set 进展、最新 prompt 迭代、一个小数据问题上。Status 会议没被中断归咎问题偏离, 因为**那次对话单独发生**。

跟 "如果决策在每周 check-in 上尝试" 的对照: 客户体验负责人不在场, 法务不在场, 运营 sponsor 要么不带他们输入做出承诺 (制造风险), 要么推迟决策 (制造延迟)。合适的决策会议产出一个有完整对齐的干净结果。

三周后, 类似的 pattern 反向播出。双周 steering committee 有长议程, 运营 sponsor 试图用它给 prompt 迭代进展做更新。**PM 引导**: "谢谢, 我会把那个放到明天发的每周总结里。这次会议, 我们能聚焦在面前的上线 readiness 决策上吗?" 运营 sponsor 接受引导, 上线 readiness 决策得到它需要的完整关注, steering committee 按时以清晰的决策结束。

有意思的一般观察: 整个项目, 团队为具体决策产出大约一打正式决策 memo。大多数在 30 分钟会议里解决。**总时间投入小**。**决策质量高**。Status 会议保持聚焦在觉知上, 决策会议保持聚焦在选择上, 项目得益于这种纪律。

AI 交付的启示: 会议有两个主要目的, 混它们损害两者。纪律是知道每次会议是哪种类型、相应准备、在他人试图混淆时捍卫区分。**纪律的成本小**。混淆的成本是错过的决策、被损害的动量、对参加项目会议失去兴趣的资深 stakeholder。
