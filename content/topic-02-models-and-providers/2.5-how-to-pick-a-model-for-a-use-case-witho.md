**Definition**

Model selection is the process of choosing which model to use for a specific workflow or sub task. It is a decision driven by quality requirements, cost budget, latency needs, data constraints, and existing contracts, applied to the family of models available at the time.

**Why it matters in delivery**

This is one of the most frequent PM decisions in an AI project, and one of the most overthought. Teams waste weeks debating models when the answer is usually "start with the strongest available, optimise down later." Knowing a simple decision framework lets you make defensible choices quickly and revisit them when something changes.

**How it works**

A practical decision sequence:

1. **Constraints first.** What is on the approved list? What can the data touch? Eliminate models that fail these gates before anything else.
2. **Quality threshold.** Pick the strongest model available within constraints. Validate it solves the problem at all. If it cannot, no cheaper model will.
3. **Build evals.** Run 30 to 100 real examples through the strongest model. Establish a baseline.
4. **Optimise down.** Test smaller and cheaper models against the same eval. Accept the cheapest one that hits the quality bar.
5. **Route by task.** Different subtasks within the same workflow can use different models. Hard reasoning uses the flagship, simple extraction uses the small one.

| Use case | Reasonable starting model |
|---|---|
| Document Q&A over a knowledge base | Mid tier (Sonnet, Gemini Flash, GPT mid) |
| Complex coding or agentic work | Flagship (Opus, GPT large, Gemini Pro) |
| High volume classification or extraction | Small (Haiku, Gemini Flash Lite, GPT nano) |
| Sensitive content requiring tight refusals | Anthropic models tend to do well |
| Cost sensitive at very high volume | Open weight, self hosted or on Bedrock |
| Real time chat with latency under 1 second | Small to mid tier with streaming |

**PM decision points**

- Have we documented the constraints (approved list, data residency, contracts) before debating models?
- Do we have an eval set to drive the decision, or are we arguing from opinion?
- Have we tested cheaper alternatives, or just assumed the flagship is needed?

**Watch outs**

- Provider release cycles are 3 to 6 months, today's right answer is next quarter's stale answer
- Benchmarks and leaderboards are starting points, not final answers
- Switching models late in delivery has a real cost in prompt tuning and eval revalidation, build the decision in early

**Takeaway: start with the strongest model that meets constraints, then optimise downward against your own evals. The model choice is a number, not a debate.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine a Qantas team building a new internal knowledge assistant for the cabin crew network, helping crew quickly look up procedural questions during turnarounds and layovers. The initial scoping conversation defaulted, predictably, to "use the best model" and the team had Claude Opus pencilled into the architecture.

A more disciplined model selection process would have walked through the decision framework deliberately. First, constraints: the model needs to be accessible via the existing AWS Bedrock agreement, response needs to come back in under three seconds for crew on a turnaround clock, and the data residency requirement points to the Sydney region. That eliminates several options before quality considerations even enter.

Second, quality threshold. The task is structured retrieval over a defined corpus of cabin crew procedures, safety manuals, and operational bulletins. With strong retrieval and clear prompting, the task does not require flagship reasoning. The team built an eval of 80 real cabin crew questions with verified correct answers and tested across Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus on the same retrieval pipeline. Opus scored 96 percent. Sonnet scored 94 percent. Haiku scored 88 percent.

Third, the optimisation step. Sonnet at AUD 0.003 per query was the right answer. The two percentage point quality gap to Opus did not justify the 5x cost multiplier for a high volume workload. Haiku would have been the cost winner but the eight point quality drop was material for safety adjacent content.

The result, decided in a week with a working eval set, was Sonnet via Bedrock with a strong retrieval pipeline. Trying to make that decision through architecture debate alone would have taken months and probably landed on Opus by default.

For AI delivery, the broader takeaway is that model selection is the easiest place to demonstrate value as an AI delivery practitioner. The technical work is not hard, the framework is straightforward, and the client gets a defensible, data driven choice they can explain to their leadership. Walking a client through this kind of selection in week one of a project builds enormous credibility and saves real money. It also sets a precedent that future decisions in the project will be made the same way: with evals, not opinions.

**定义**

模型选择是为特定工作流或子任务选择使用哪个模型的过程。这是一个由质量要求、成本预算、延迟需求、数据约束,以及现有合同驱动的决策,应用于当时可用的模型家族。

**为何在交付中重要**

这是 AI 项目中最频繁的 PM 决策之一,也是最容易过度思考的之一。团队浪费数周辩论模型,而答案通常是"从可用的最强者开始,稍后优化下降"。了解一个简单的决策框架让你能快速做出可辩护的选择,并在情况变化时重新审视它们。

**运作原理**

一个实用的决策序列:

1. **约束优先**。批准清单上有什么?数据可以触及什么?在其他一切之前消除未通过这些门槛的模型。
2. **质量阈值**。在约束内选择可用的最强模型。验证它能解决问题。如果它不能,没有更便宜的模型可以。
3. **构建 evals**。通过最强模型运行 30 到 100 个真实示例。建立基线。
4. **优化下降**。针对同一个 eval 测试更小、更便宜的模型。接受达到质量门槛的最便宜的。
5. **按任务路由**。同一工作流中的不同子任务可以使用不同的模型。困难推理使用旗舰,简单提取使用小型。

| 用例 | 合理的起始模型 |
|---|---|
| 知识库上的文档问答 | 中层 (Sonnet、Gemini Flash、GPT mid) |
| 复杂编码或 agentic 工作 | 旗舰 (Opus、GPT large、Gemini Pro) |
| 高量分类或提取 | 小型 (Haiku、Gemini Flash Lite、GPT nano) |
| 需要严格拒绝的敏感内容 | Anthropic 模型往往做得很好 |
| 在非常高量下成本敏感 | Open weight,自托管或在 Bedrock 上 |
| 低于 1 秒延迟的实时聊天 | 带流式的小到中层 |

**PM 决策点**

- 在辩论模型之前,我们是否记录了约束 (批准清单、数据驻留、合同)?
- 我们是否有 eval 集驱动决策,还是从观点出发争论?
- 我们是否测试了更便宜的替代方案,或者只是假设需要旗舰?

**注意事项**

- 提供商发布周期是 3 到 6 个月,今天的正确答案是下个季度的陈旧答案
- 基准和排行榜是起点,不是最终答案
- 在交付后期切换模型在 prompt 调整和 eval 重新验证方面有真实成本,尽早将决策构建进来

**要点总结: 从满足约束的最强模型开始,然后针对你自己的 evals 向下优化。模型选择是一个数字,不是一场辩论。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想一个 Qantas 团队为机组网络构建新的内部知识助手,帮助机组在周转和过夜停留期间快速查找程序问题。最初的需求确定对话可预测地默认为"使用最佳模型",团队将 Claude Opus 暂时填入架构。

更有纪律的模型选择过程会有意识地走完决策框架。首先,约束: 模型需要通过现有的 AWS Bedrock 协议访问,机组在周转计时下响应需要在三秒内返回,数据驻留要求指向悉尼区域。这在质量考虑甚至进入之前消除了几个选项。

第二,质量阈值。该任务是在已定义的机组程序、安全手册和运营公告语料库上的结构化检索。通过强检索和清晰的 prompting,该任务不需要旗舰推理。团队构建了一个 80 个真实机组问题的 eval,带经验证的正确答案,并在相同的检索 pipeline 上跨 Haiku、Sonnet 和 Opus 进行测试。Opus 得分 96%。Sonnet 得分 94%。Haiku 得分 88%。

第三,优化步骤。每个查询 0.003 澳元的 Sonnet 是正确答案。对 Opus 的两个百分点质量差距不能为高量工作负载证明 5 倍成本倍数。Haiku 本来是成本赢家,但八点质量下降对安全相邻内容是实质性的。

结果,在一周内用一个工作的 eval 集决定,是通过 Bedrock 的 Sonnet,带强检索 pipeline。仅通过架构辩论做出该决策将花费数月时间,可能默认到 Opus。

对 AI 交付来说,更广泛的要点是模型选择是作为 AI 交付从业者展示价值的最容易地方。技术工作不难,框架直截了当,客户得到一个可辩护的、数据驱动的选择,可以向他们的领导层解释。在项目第一周引导客户完成这种选择建立了巨大的信誉并节省了真金白银。它还为未来的决策建立了先例: 用 evals 而非观点。
