**Definition**

Not every change to scope during an AI project is bad. Some are unwanted additions that should be resisted (scope creep). Some are legitimate updates because the team or client learned something new (scope evolution). And some are necessary fixes because the original scope was wrong (scope correction). Confusing the three is one of the most expensive mistakes in scope management. Each requires a different response, and treating them the same way produces the worst outcome: either becoming the team that says no to everything, or becoming the team that lets the project drift.

**Why it matters in delivery**

The PM's instinct, once burned by scope creep, is to harden against all change. This is the wrong reaction. Some change is necessary, some is helpful, some is dangerous. The PM's job is to triage incoming requests into the right category, and then handle each category with the right tools. Without this triage, the team either becomes rigid and ineffective, or flexible and overbudget. Neither outcome serves the client.

In AI projects this triage is especially important because the technology genuinely changes faster than the project does. A new model released in week six can legitimately invalidate a decision made in week two. That is not scope creep, it is scope correction driven by reality. Treating it as creep would be a category error. Treating actual creep as correction would be self-deception.

**How it works**

Every incoming request to change scope falls into one of three categories. The diagnostic test is the source of the new information.

| Category | Diagnostic question | Right response |
|---|---|---|
| Scope creep | "What new information made this come up?" Answer: nothing, the stakeholder just thought of it | Park or defer, use the v1 will-not-do list as anchor |
| Scope evolution | "What new information made this come up?" Answer: we learned something useful as the project progressed | Negotiate a change, document the trade off, adjust v1 if warranted |
| Scope correction | "What new information made this come up?" Answer: we discovered the original scope was wrong | Fix the scope, often without renegotiation, treat as bug fix |

The diagnostic is not always clean. A request that looks like creep often turns out to be evolution once you ask why it came up. A request that looks like evolution sometimes is creep dressed up as new learning. The PM's skill is sitting with each request long enough to find the real source.

**Worked examples**

A few illustrative cases to calibrate the categories.

*"Can the assistant also handle Mandarin?"* If the request comes from a sponsor who just thought of it, this is creep. If it comes because the team discovered in user research that 30 percent of customers in the target segment use Mandarin and the original scope underestimated this, this is evolution. If it comes because the original SOW said "multilingual support" but the team scoped it to English only without checking, this is correction.

*"Can we use the new model that was just released?"* Almost always evolution. A new model that meaningfully changes capability or cost is genuine new information. The right response is to evaluate the impact and adjust the plan if warranted, not to refuse on grounds of original scope.

*"We need to add a compliance check before the AI responds."* If compliance has always been required and was missed, this is correction. If compliance was discussed and deferred and is now being added back, this is creep. If a new regulation was published mid-project that genuinely applies, this is evolution.

*"Can the agent also recommend products after answering the question?"* Usually creep, especially if the original scope was specifically answering questions. The request expands the verb (see 12.1). The right response is to park it, not to absorb it.

**How to communicate the distinction to the client**

Clients sometimes hear "scope creep" as the team being uncooperative. The framing that works is: "we have a way of categorising changes that helps us be responsive to the important ones and not get distracted by the others. We try to identify which type each change is, and we want your input on the classification." This positions the team as thoughtful rather than rigid, and invites the client into the triage process.

Maintaining a one page document that lists recent change requests, their category, and what was done with each, is one of the most underrated artifacts in scope management. The document is reviewed every two weeks. Clients who see it usually become better at categorising their own requests, because they internalise the framework.

**PM decision points**

- For each incoming change request, have I asked the diagnostic question, or am I reacting on instinct?
- Am I tracking requests by category, so I can show the client how each was handled?
- When I disagree with the client on a category (they say evolution, I say creep), have I made the disagreement explicit and resolvable, rather than letting it simmer?

**Watch outs**

- "Scope creep" is the wrong term to use to the client. Use "out of scope" or "deferred" in client-facing language. "Creep" implies the client is being unreasonable.
- The same request can be creep early in the project and evolution later, as the team learns more. The categorisation is context-dependent, not absolute.
- Beware of declaring something correction when it is really creep. Correction means the team got the original scope wrong, which is sometimes unflattering to admit. The temptation is to call it evolution to save face. Resist.

**Takeaway: not all change is creep. The diagnostic question is "what new information made this come up". The answer puts the request in one of three categories, each with a different right response. Skip the diagnostic and you become either rigid or porous.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine running the build phase of a Qantas AI project, a content drafting tool for the loyalty marketing team. The v1 scope is signed, the v1 will-not-do list is locked. Three change requests arrive in the same week. The instinct of a less experienced PM is to push back on all three uniformly. The instinct of a less disciplined PM is to absorb all three to keep the client happy. Both responses are wrong.

Request one comes from the marketing director. "Can the tool also draft content for the international segment, not just domestic?" The diagnostic question reveals the source: the director has been in meetings about international expansion and is suddenly aware of that audience. There is no new technical or operational information. The original scope was deliberately domestic-only because international content has different regulatory requirements. This is creep. The right response is to park it, log it in the v2 backlog, and refer to the will-not-do list.

Request two comes from the IT integration lead. "We have realised that the marketing automation platform changed its API last month, and the integration we scoped in week one will not work as planned." The diagnostic question shows clear new information: an external system changed, the original integration plan no longer applies. This is correction. The right response is to fix the scope, often without commercial renegotiation if the change is roughly cost-neutral, and treat it as the team's responsibility to absorb new reality.

Request three comes from the content team lead, who has been testing early outputs. "We have noticed the model produces really good content for our high-value segments but mediocre content for everyday members. Can we adjust the scope to focus on high-value segments where the quality is reliable, and defer everyday-member content to v2?" The diagnostic shows new information generated by the project itself: the team has learned that quality is uneven across segments. This is evolution. The right response is to negotiate the change, document the trade off (narrower v1 scope but better quality), and adjust the eval set accordingly.

Three requests, three different categories, three different responses. The PM walks the engagement partner and the client through the categorisation in the next steering committee. The categorisation is shown on a one page artifact, with the diagnostic question and the agreed response for each. The client sees that the team is being thoughtful, not reflexively defensive or reflexively accommodating.

The interesting consequence comes six weeks later, when the marketing director asks for another change. Before describing the change, the director says: "I think this is probably evolution because we just got results from a customer research study, but I want your view." That single sentence shows the framework has been internalised. The triage now happens collaboratively, not adversarially.

The lesson for AI delivery is that scope management is not a matter of being strict or being flexible. It is a matter of categorising each change correctly and responding to each category appropriately. A team that does this well becomes the team that the client trusts to make good judgments about scope, which is one of the deepest forms of trust in this work.

**定义**

AI 项目过程中, 不是每一次范围变化都是坏的。有些是不该收的添加 (scope creep, 范围蔓延)。有些是因为团队或客户学到了新东西的合理更新 (scope evolution, 范围演化)。还有些是必须修的, 因为原始范围本来就错了 (scope correction, 范围更正)。把这三种混为一谈, 是范围管理里最贵的错误之一。每一种需要不同的回应, 当作同一种对待会产出最差的结果: 要么变成对什么都说不的团队, 要么变成让项目漂移的团队。

**为何在交付中重要**

被 scope creep 烧过一次之后, PM 的本能是**对所有变化都硬起来**。这是错误的反应。有些变化是必要的, 有些是有帮助的, 有些是危险的。PM 的工作是**把进来的请求分类, 然后用对应类别的工具处理**。没有这层分类, 团队要么变得僵硬而无效, 要么变得灵活而超支。两个结果都不为客户服务。

AI 项目里这种分类**尤其重要**, 因为**技术变化真的比项目本身快**。第六周发布的一个新模型, 可以合理地推翻第二周做的决定。那不是 scope creep, 那是由现实驱动的 scope correction。把它当 creep 处理就是分类错误。把真正的 creep 当 correction 处理就是自欺。

**运作原理**

每一个进来的范围变更请求, 都属于这三类之一。诊断测试是 "新信息的来源"。

| 类别 | 诊断问题 | 对的回应 |
|---|---|---|
| Scope creep | "什么新信息让这件事出现?" 答: 没有, stakeholder 刚想到 | Park 或推迟, 用 v1 不做清单作为锚 |
| Scope evolution | "什么新信息让这件事出现?" 答: 项目推进过程中我们学到了有用的东西 | 谈判一个变更, 记录 trade off, 必要时调整 v1 |
| Scope correction | "什么新信息让这件事出现?" 答: 我们发现原始范围本来就错 | 修范围, 通常不重新谈判, 当作 bug fix 处理 |

诊断不总是干净的。看起来像 creep 的请求, 问一下 "为什么出现" 之后, 经常变成 evolution。看起来像 evolution 的请求, 有时是 creep 化了妆。PM 的技艺是在每个请求上多坐一会, 找到新信息的真实来源。

**实际案例**

几个用来校准类别的例子。

*"助手能不能也处理中文?"* **如果这个请求来自一个刚想到的 sponsor, 这是 creep**。**如果来自团队在用户研究中发现目标群体里 30% 客户用中文、原始范围低估了这个比例, 这是 evolution**。**如果来自原始 SOW 写的就是 "多语言支持"、团队没核对就 scope 成了只有英文, 这是 correction**。

*"我们能用刚发布的新模型吗?"* **几乎总是 evolution**。**一个有意义地改变能力或成本的新模型, 是真正的新信息**。**对的回应是评估影响, 必要时调整计划, 而不是以 "原始范围不含" 为由拒绝**。

*"在 AI 回应之前我们需要加一个合规检查。"* **如果合规一直就是必需的、被漏掉了, 这是 correction**。**如果合规被讨论过、被推迟、现在又被加回来, 这是 creep**。**如果项目中间发布了真的适用的新法规, 这是 evolution**。

*"agent 在回答问题之后能不能也推荐产品?"* **通常是 creep**, **尤其当原始范围明确是回答问题的时候**。**这个请求扩大了动词** (见 12.1)。**对的回应是 park, 不是吸收**。

**怎么跟客户沟通这种区分**

客户有时把 "scope creep" 听成 **团队不合作**。**有效的措辞是**: "我们有一个分类变更的方法, 帮助我们对重要的变更有反应, 不被其他变更分心。我们尝试识别每个变更属于哪一类, 也想听你对分类的意见。" 这把团队定位成深思熟虑而非僵硬, 同时邀请客户进入分类过程。

维护一份一页的文档, 列**近期的变更请求、它们的类别、每一个的处理结果**, 是范围管理里最被低估的产物之一。这份文档每两周 review 一次。看到它的客户通常会更擅长分类自己的请求, 因为**他们内化了这个框架**。

**PM 决策点**

- 对每一个进来的变更请求, **我有没有问过那个诊断问题**, 还是按本能反应?
- 我**有没有按类别追踪请求**, 这样我能给客户展示每个是怎么处理的?
- **当我跟客户在分类上不同意时** (客户说 evolution, 我说 creep), 我**有没有把分歧明确化并解决**, 还是让它发酵?

**注意事项**

- **"Scope creep" 是对客户用错的词**。**面向客户的语言用 "超出范围" 或 "推迟"**。**"Creep" 暗示客户在不讲理**。
- **同一个请求**, **项目早期可能是 creep, 项目后期可能是 evolution**, **随着团队学到更多**。**分类是上下文相关的, 不是绝对的**。
- **小心把其实是 creep 的东西宣布成 correction**。**Correction 意味着团队原始范围搞错了, 承认起来有时不光彩**。**诱惑是叫它 evolution 来挽回面子**。**抵抗**。

**要点总结: 不是所有变化都是 creep。诊断问题是 "什么新信息让这件事出现"。答案把请求放进三类之一, 每一类有不同的对的回应。跳过诊断, 你要么变僵硬, 要么变多孔。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你在跑一个 Qantas AI 项目的建造阶段, 给忠诚度营销团队的内容起草工具。v1 范围签字了, v1 不做清单锁定了。同一周到了三个变更请求。经验不足的 PM 的本能是均匀地 push back 三个。纪律不够的 PM 的本能是吸收三个让客户开心。**两个反应都错**。

请求一来自营销总监。"工具能不能也起草国际市场的内容, 不只是国内?" 诊断问题揭示来源: 总监刚开过一系列关于国际扩张的会, 突然意识到那个受众。没有新的技术或运营信息。原始范围有意只覆盖国内, 因为国际内容有不同的监管要求。这是 creep。对的回应是 park 它, 记录在 v2 backlog 上, 引用不做清单。

请求二来自 IT 集成负责人。"我们意识到营销自动化平台上个月改了 API, 我们第一周 scope 的集成方案按原计划不工作了。" 诊断问题显示清晰的新信息: 一个外部系统变了, 原始集成计划不再适用。这是 correction。对的回应是修范围, 如果变更大致成本中性, 通常不商业重谈, 当作团队责任去吸收新现实。

请求三来自内容团队负责人, 他一直在测试早期输出。"我们注意到模型对高价值细分群体能产出真的好的内容, 但对普通会员的内容就一般。我们能不能调整范围, 聚焦在质量可靠的高价值细分, 把普通会员内容推到 v2?" 诊断显示**项目本身产生的新信息**: 团队学到了不同细分间质量不均。这是 evolution。对的回应是谈判变更, 记录 trade off (更窄的 v1 范围, 但更好的质量), 相应地调整 eval set。

三个请求, 三种不同类别, 三种不同回应。PM 在下次 steering committee 上带 engagement partner 和客户**走一遍分类**。分类在一份一页的产物上展示, 每一项带诊断问题和商定的回应。客户看到团队是深思熟虑的, 不是反射性防御也不是反射性配合。

六周后出现一个有意思的后果, 当营销总监又要求一个变更时。在描述变更之前, **总监说**: "我觉得这大概是 evolution, 因为我们刚拿到一项客户研究的结果, 但我想听你的意见。" 那一句话显示框架已经被内化。分类现在是协作发生的, 不是对抗发生的。

AI 交付的启示: 范围管理不是 "严" 或 "松" 的问题。它是 "把每一个变更分对类、按类别合适地回应" 的问题。做得好的团队, 会变成 "客户信任他们对范围作出好判断" 的团队, 而那是这一行最深的几种信任之一。
