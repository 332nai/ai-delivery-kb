**Definition**

The hardest scope conversation in AI delivery is when a senior executive on the client side, often above the operating sponsor, requests an addition to scope. The phrase used here is "managing down" because the immediate problem is not the executive (who is often easy to deal with directly), it is what happens with the operating sponsor and the team afterwards. The operating sponsor feels exposed, the team feels squeezed, and the decision dynamics get tangled. The skill is to engage with the executive's request without leaving the operating sponsor undermined.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Operating sponsors are the people the team works with day to day. They are the ones who agreed to the scope, signed off on the v1 will-not-do list, and have to live with the consequences. When an executive above them adds scope, the operating sponsor faces a difficult position: they can either back the team and push back on their own boss, or accept the addition and lose face for not anticipating it. Either is uncomfortable.

The team that mishandles this moment damages two relationships at once: with the executive (by being seen as inflexible) and with the operating sponsor (by being seen as accommodating the executive at their expense). The team that handles it well preserves both relationships and converts a difficult moment into a chance to build trust.

In AI projects this dynamic is especially common because executives are often the people most excited about the technology, most exposed to vendor pitches and conference talks, and most likely to read about a new capability and ask for it. The volume of executive-initiated scope additions is meaningfully higher in AI than in traditional software delivery.

**How it works**

The right response has four moves. The order matters.

*Move 1: Acknowledge the executive's request seriously, in front of the operating sponsor.* When the executive raises the addition, the response is engagement, not pushback. "That is an interesting capability, let me understand what you have in mind." This validates the executive without committing to anything. Critically, this is done with the operating sponsor present, so the operating sponsor is not surprised later.

*Move 2: Defer the decision explicitly, with a stated reason.* The response continues: "I want to think through what this would involve, including the impact on the current scope. Can I come back to you and [operating sponsor's name] together with our analysis later this week?" This achieves several things: it postpones the decision (avoiding an on-the-spot yes), it includes the operating sponsor in the decision making (protecting their position), and it positions the team as thoughtful rather than reactive.

*Move 3: Re-engage with the operating sponsor first, privately.* Before going back to the executive, have a private conversation with the operating sponsor. Two purposes: ensure the operating sponsor and team have aligned on the recommendation, and give the operating sponsor the chance to share information you might not have (e.g. "the executive has been wanting this for a while, this is not a new idea", or "the executive often raises ideas in meetings that they do not actually want pursued").

*Move 4: Return to the executive with a structured recommendation, with the operating sponsor present.* The recommendation has the same structure as 13.2: take the request seriously, walk through the cost, offer paths forward. But this time, the operating sponsor is in the room and is a co-author of the recommendation, not a bystander.

The key insight: the operating sponsor must never be excluded from the conversation. Excluding them, even if it seems faster, damages the relationship and creates a politics that infects the rest of the project.

**When the executive insists**

Sometimes the executive insists on the addition despite the cost analysis. The right response is not refusal but commercial structuring. The framing: "we can absolutely include this. There are three ways to fit it in: extend the timeline by [X weeks], descope [Y] of equivalent effort, or expand the fee by [Z]. Which path works for you?"

This response has two properties. It says yes to the addition. And it surfaces the trade off explicitly. Most executives, when presented with a structured trade off, will engage with the trade off. A few will pick a path immediately. Those who refuse to engage with any trade off are revealing something useful about how they expect the engagement to go, and that information is worth having early.

**What not to do**

A few patterns that damage relationships, all common.

*Agreeing to the executive on the spot to avoid friction.* This makes the team look responsive in the moment but is interpreted by the operating sponsor as betrayal. It also creates an absorbed scope drift that the operating sponsor will be blamed for later.

*Pushing back on the executive in front of the operating sponsor.* This makes the operating sponsor look like they have not managed their executive. Even if the team is right, the political cost is high.

*Going to the operating sponsor after the executive meeting and saying "I think we have a problem".* If the operating sponsor was present in the meeting, they already know there is a problem. Coming back with this framing is paternalistic. Better framing: "what is your read on what was said, and how do you want to handle the follow up together".

*Letting the operating sponsor be the one to push back to the executive without team support.* The operating sponsor is often more junior than the executive, and pushing back alone exposes them. The team should be willing to be the visible voice of "this has trade offs", so the operating sponsor does not have to be.

**PM decision points**

- When an executive raises a scope addition, do I instinctively defer the decision, or do I commit on the spot?
- Am I including the operating sponsor in the decision making, or am I going around them to the executive?
- Have I prepared the operating sponsor for these conversations in advance, or do they only learn the team's view in front of the executive?

**Watch outs**

- Executives sometimes raise ideas without expecting them to be pursued. They are thinking out loud. Treating every executive utterance as a directive overburdens the project. Read the room.
- The operating sponsor's relationship with the executive is information you do not fully have. Ask discreetly. The same words can mean very different things in different reporting relationships.
- Never let the executive feel undermined either. The respect goes in both directions. The skill is preserving the dignity of both stakeholders, not picking a side.

**Takeaway: when an executive adds scope, the response is structured: acknowledge seriously, defer explicitly, re-engage with the operating sponsor privately, return with a joint recommendation. The operating sponsor must never be excluded from the conversation, even when the executive's request seems clear. Mishandling this moment damages two relationships at once.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine running a Qantas AI project, a content drafting tool for the loyalty marketing team. The v1 scope is signed off by the operating sponsor, a senior marketing manager who has been thoughtful and engaged throughout. In a quarterly business review, the marketing director (the operating sponsor's boss, two levels above them) is sitting in. The director listens to the project update, looks engaged, and at the end says: "this is good. Can you also extend it to handle our enterprise sales communications? Same kind of drafting, different audience. Should not be too much work."

The operating sponsor visibly tenses. The team's PM, who has been leading the update, has a choice.

Move one: the PM acknowledges the request seriously. "That is interesting, I want to make sure I understand the scope. Are you thinking of the same kind of customer communications, but targeted at enterprise prospects, or are you thinking of full proposal drafting?" The director clarifies they were thinking of customer communications, not proposals.

Move two: the PM defers the decision. "Let me come back to you and [operating sponsor's name] with an analysis of what this would involve and how we might fit it in. Can we set up a meeting next week with the three of us?" The director agrees. The meeting is set.

Move three: after the quarterly review ends, the PM walks with the operating sponsor to their office. "That was unexpected. What is your read on what just happened?" The operating sponsor reveals useful information: the director has been pushing the enterprise audience for several quarters, has not gotten traction, and is increasingly impatient. The operating sponsor was not surprised by the request, but had hoped to address it in a planned v2 rather than as an in-scope addition. The PM and operating sponsor align on a recommendation: include a limited enterprise capability in v1 (capturing the director's interest) with a clear scope boundary, and plan a fuller enterprise capability in v2.

Move four: the meeting the next week has the PM, the operating sponsor, and the director in the room. The PM walks the director through the analysis. The proposed limited inclusion is described concretely: it would cover three specific enterprise communication types out of the seven the director ultimately wants, would require two weeks of incremental work, and would be deliverable within the current v1 timeline by removing one of the lower-priority current capabilities. The director engages with the trade off. The operating sponsor speaks up, comfortably, to suggest which current capability to defer. The director agrees. The decision is documented as a change request memo, signed by both director and operating sponsor.

The interesting outcome is the strengthened relationship between the operating sponsor and the director. The operating sponsor, who had been on the back foot in their relationship with the director, was able to demonstrate operational judgment in a high-visibility meeting. The director felt their input was acted on. The team handled the moment in a way that protected the operating sponsor and gave the director what they wanted in a workable form.

The lesson for AI delivery is that the dynamics around executive-initiated scope additions are not primarily about scope. They are about relationships and dignity. The PM's job is to engage with the executive seriously, preserve the operating sponsor's position, and produce a recommendation that both parties can support. Done well, these conversations strengthen the project's foundation. Done poorly, they sow distrust that lasts the engagement.

**定义**

AI 交付里最难的范围对话, 是**当客户侧一位资深高管 (经常在运营 sponsor 之上) 要求加东西时**。这里用的词是 "向下管理", 因为直接的问题不是高管 (跟高管直接打交道经常容易), 而是之后跟运营 sponsor 和团队会发生什么。运营 sponsor 感觉暴露, 团队感觉被挤, 决策动力学被搅乱。技艺是 engage 高管的请求, 同时不让运营 sponsor 处于被削弱的位置。

**为何在交付中重要**

运营 sponsor 是**团队日复一日打交道的人**。他们是签字同意范围的人、签 v1 不做清单的人、要承担后果的人。当一位他们之上的高管加范围时, 运营 sponsor 面临一个困难位置: 要么支持团队、push back 自己的老板, 要么接受添加、为没有预见到这件事丢面子。任何一个都不舒服。

没处理好这一刻的团队同时损害两个关系: 跟高管 (被看作不灵活) 和跟运营 sponsor (被看作以他们的代价配合高管)。处理好的团队保留两个关系, 把一个困难时刻转换成建立信任的机会。

AI 项目里这个动力学**尤其常见**, 因为**高管经常是最对技术兴奋、最暴露于供应商推销和会议演讲、最可能读到一个新能力就要求加入的人**。AI 项目里 "高管发起的范围添加" 的数量, 比传统软件交付有意义地更高。

**运作原理**

对的回应有四步。**顺序重要**。

*第一步: 在运营 sponsor 面前认真承认高管的请求*。**高管提出添加时, 回应是 engage, 不是 push back**。**"那是一个有意思的能力, 让我理解你想的是什么。"** **这确认高管, 不承诺任何东西**。**关键是, 这件事是在运营 sponsor 在场时做的**, **这样运营 sponsor 不会以后被惊吓**。

*第二步: 明确推迟决策, 带说明的理由*。**回应继续**: **"我想想这会涉及什么, 包括对当前范围的影响。我能不能本周晚些时候带分析回来找你和 [运营 sponsor 名字] 一起谈?"** **这做到几件事**: **它推迟决策 (避免当场是)、它把运营 sponsor 纳入决策制定 (保护他们的位置)、它把团队定位为深思熟虑而不是反应性**。

*第三步: 先私下跟运营 sponsor 重新 engage*。**在回去找高管之前, 跟运营 sponsor 有一次私下对话**。**两个目的**: **确保运营 sponsor 和团队在建议上对齐, 给运营 sponsor 分享你可能不知道的信息的机会** (**例如**, **"高管想要这个已经一段时间了, 这不是新想法"**, **或** **"高管经常在会议上提他自己其实不想被追究的想法"**)。

*第四步: 带着结构化建议回到高管面前, 运营 sponsor 在场*。**建议有跟 13.2 同样的结构**: **认真对待请求、走一遍成本、提供前进的路径**。**但这次, 运营 sponsor 在房间里, 是建议的共同作者, 不是旁观者**。

**关键洞察**: 运营 sponsor 永远不应该被排除在对话之外。**排除他们**, 即使看起来更快, 会损害关系, 并制造感染项目其余部分的政治。

**当高管坚持时**

有时候高管在看到成本分析后仍然坚持添加。对的回应不是拒绝, 而是商业重构。**措辞**: "我们绝对可以纳入这个。有三种把它装进去的方式: 延长时间线 [X 周]、descope 工作量相当的 [Y]、或者把费率扩大 [Z]。哪条路对你 work?"

这种回应有两个属性。**它对添加说是**。它明确浮出 trade off。大多数高管, 当被呈现一个结构化的 trade off 时, 会 engage trade off。少数会立刻选一条路。那些拒绝 engage 任何 trade off 的, 在揭示一些关于他们预期合作怎么走的有用信息, 早点知道那个信息是值得的。

**不要做什么**

几个损害关系的 pattern, 都很常见。

*为了避免摩擦, 当场对高管说是*。**这让团队在那一刻看起来反应灵敏, 但运营 sponsor 会解读为背叛**。**这也制造一个被吸收的 scope 漂移, 之后会怪在运营 sponsor 头上**。

*在运营 sponsor 面前 push back 高管*。**这让运营 sponsor 看起来没管好自己的高管**。**即使团队是对的, 政治成本高**。

*高管会议之后去找运营 sponsor 说 "我觉得我们有问题"*。**如果运营 sponsor 在那场会议上, 他们已经知道有问题**。**带这个措辞回来是居高临下的**。**更好的措辞**: **"你对刚才说的那些有什么看法, 你想怎么一起处理后续?"**

*让运营 sponsor 在没有团队支持的情况下 push back 高管*。**运营 sponsor 经常比高管更资浅, 单独 push back 暴露他们**。**团队应该愿意成为 "这有 trade off" 的可见声音, 这样运营 sponsor 不必这么做**。

**PM 决策点**

- 当高管提出范围添加, **我本能地推迟决策**, 还是**当场承诺**?
- 我**有没有把运营 sponsor 纳入决策制定**, 还是**绕过他们去找高管**?
- 我**有没有提前为这些对话准备运营 sponsor**, 还是**他们只在高管面前才学到团队的看法**?

**注意事项**

- **高管有时候提想法时并不期待被追究**。**他们在出声思考**。**把每一句高管的话当指令对待会压垮项目**。**读房间**。
- **运营 sponsor 跟高管的关系是你不完全掌握的信息**。**谨慎地问**。**同样的话, 在不同的汇报关系里意味着非常不同的东西**。
- **永远不要让高管也感觉被削弱**。**尊重是双向的**。**技艺是保留两位 stakeholder 的尊严, 不是选一边**。

**要点总结: 当高管加范围, 回应是结构化的: 认真承认、明确推迟、私下重新跟运营 sponsor engage、带共同建议回来。运营 sponsor 永远不应该被排除, 即使高管的请求看起来清楚。没处理好这一刻, 会同时损害两个关系。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你跑一个 Qantas AI 项目, 给忠诚度营销团队的内容起草工具。v1 范围由运营 sponsor 签字, 那位是一位资深营销经理, 全程深思熟虑且 engaged。在一次季度业务 review 上, 营销总监 (运营 sponsor 的老板, 高两层) 坐在那里。总监听项目更新, 看起来 engaged, **最后说**: "这不错。你能不能也扩展到处理我们的企业销售沟通?同一类起草, 不同受众。应该工作量不大。"

运营 sponsor 明显紧张。正在带这次更新的团队 PM, 有一个选择。

**第一步**: PM 认真承认请求。"那有意思, 我想确保我理解范围。你想的是同一类客户沟通, 但针对企业潜在客户, 还是想的是完整的提案起草?" 总监澄清说他想的是客户沟通, 不是提案。

**第二步**: PM 推迟决策。"让我带回来一份关于这会涉及什么、我们怎么装进去的分析, 找你和 [运营 sponsor 名字] 谈。我们能不能下周三个人开一次会?" **总监同意**。**会议定下来**。

**第三步**: 季度 review 结束后, PM 跟运营 sponsor 走到她办公室。"刚才那个不在预期内。你对刚才发生的事怎么看?" 运营 sponsor 透露有用的信息: 总监推企业受众已经几个季度了, 一直没拿到牵引力, 越来越没耐心。运营 sponsor 对那个请求并不惊讶, 但希望在规划好的 v2 里处理, 而不是作为范围内的添加。PM 和运营 sponsor 在一个建议上对齐: 在 v1 里包含一个有限的企业能力 (捕捉总监的兴趣), 带清晰的范围边界, 并在 v2 规划一个更完整的企业能力。

**第四步**: 下周的会议, PM、运营 sponsor、总监在房间里。PM 带总监走一遍分析。被提议的有限纳入被具体描述: 它会覆盖总监最终想要的七种企业沟通类型里的三种, 会需要两周增量工作, 通过移除一项当前优先级较低的能力, 能在 v1 当前时间线内交付。总监 engage trade off。运营 sponsor 舒服地发言, 建议哪一项当前能力推迟。**总监同意**。决定作为 change request memo 文档化, 总监和运营 sponsor 都签字。

有意思的结果是运营 sponsor 跟总监之间被加强的关系。运营 sponsor 在跟总监的关系里之前一直处于守势, 得以在一场高可见度的会议上展示运营判断。总监感觉他的输入被采纳。团队处理这一刻的方式, 保护了运营 sponsor, 也以一种可行的形式给了总监他想要的东西。

AI 交付的启示: 高管发起的范围添加, 周围的动力学主要不是关于范围。它是关于关系和尊严。PM 的工作是认真 engage 高管、保留运营 sponsor 的位置、产出一个双方都能支持的建议。做好了, 这些对话加强项目的根基。做差了, 它们播下能持续整个合作的不信任。
