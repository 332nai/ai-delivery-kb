**Definition**

A parking lot is a shared, visible list of ideas, suggestions, and possible features that have been raised during the project but not committed to. It is distinct from the v1 will-not-do list (which is signed off and explicit) and from the change request log (which contains decisions). The parking lot is the holding area: ideas live here once they have been heard but before they have been judged. The discipline is that every raised idea goes onto the list, no idea is silently ignored, and the list is reviewed regularly so nothing simply disappears.

**Why it matters in delivery**

Stakeholders raise ideas for two reasons: they have a real suggestion to contribute, or they want to feel heard. Both are legitimate. The PM's failure mode is to treat ideas as either "we will do it" (which absorbs scope) or "we will not do it" (which makes the stakeholder feel dismissed). The parking lot creates a third option: "we have heard it, it is on the list, we will come back to it". This is often the response the stakeholder actually wanted in the first place.

In AI projects, where ideas are easy to generate (every meeting produces three new "what if we also...") and hard to implement (every addition is real engineering), the parking lot is especially valuable. It absorbs the social need to be heard without imposing the engineering cost of being done.

**How it works**

A parking lot has the following structure. It is deliberately lightweight, because it should be easy to add to and easy to read.

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Idea | One line description of the idea, in the words of the person who raised it |
| Raised by | Name of the stakeholder, date, context |
| Theme | A loose category, useful for sorting (capability, integration, content type, etc.) |
| Status | Active, promoted (moved to change request), declined, deferred to v2 |
| Last reviewed | Date of last review, useful for tracking attention |

Note what is not in the list: there is no impact assessment, no recommendation, no commercial implication. Ideas in the parking lot are not yet decisions. They are inventory. The work of converting an idea to a decision happens in change request memos (13.3) or in v2 planning. The parking lot is upstream of that work.

**How to introduce the parking lot to the client**

The introduction is light. In the first or second steering committee, the PM says: "ideas will come up throughout the project that we may not act on immediately. To make sure we capture them all and can revisit, we will maintain a shared parking lot. Everyone can see it. We will review it every couple of weeks. Nothing on it is being committed to or refused, it is just being held."

This framing achieves two things. It signals openness (ideas are welcome, nothing is dismissed). And it signals discipline (the list is reviewed, items move, nothing rots). Most clients respond positively because the framing matches their underlying preference: be heard, not committed to too early.

**How to use the parking lot in conversation**

When an idea arises in a meeting and is not in scope, the natural response is: "good idea, let me add that to the parking lot and we can come back to it in the next review". This response has three properties:

1. It validates the idea (it goes on the list).
2. It defers the decision (we will come back to it).
3. It does not commit to either action or inaction.

What this avoids is the immediate "yes we will do it" or "no we will not", both of which are conversation enders that can produce regret later. The parking lot keeps the conversation open in a structured way.

**The bi-weekly review**

The parking lot becomes useful only if it is reviewed regularly. The cadence is typically every two weeks, often as part of the steering committee or a separate scope review. In the review, each item gets one of four dispositions:

| Disposition | When applied |
|---|---|
| Keep on parking lot | Idea still interesting, but no new information to act on |
| Promote to change request | Idea now has enough specificity and importance to evaluate formally |
| Defer to v2 | Idea is interesting but will not be acted on in v1 |
| Decline and remove | Idea is no longer relevant, or has been explicitly decided against |

The review is fast: 10 to 15 minutes for a list of 20 items. The PM walks through each, the client confirms or revises the disposition, the list is updated. The key practice is that items move. A parking lot where items only ever get added becomes a graveyard and loses credibility. Items must either move forward (to change request or v2) or be removed.

**Boundaries with related artifacts**

The parking lot is sometimes confused with three other artifacts. Distinguishing them clearly is important.

| Artifact | Purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Parking lot | Hold ideas not yet evaluated | No decision |
| Change request | Document evaluated decisions | Each item has a disposition |
| v1 will-not-do list | Define explicit v1 boundary | Signed off, definitive |
| v2 backlog | Items planned for the next phase | Committed to revisit |

Ideas flow: raised → parking lot → either declined and removed, or promoted to change request, which becomes either an in-scope change or a v2 backlog item. Without this flow, ideas accumulate without direction.

**PM decision points**

- Do I have a single shared parking lot, or are ideas scattered across emails, Slack threads, and meeting notes?
- Am I running the bi-weekly review, or has the parking lot become a list nobody looks at?
- For each item on the list, can I name the next step (promote, defer, decline, hold)? If not, the item is rotting.

**Watch outs**

- The parking lot should not be used to dismiss ideas. The framing is "we are holding this", not "we are not doing this". Stakeholders can tell the difference.
- Avoid letting the parking lot grow unbounded. Above 30 items it stops being useful. Force movement.
- Some ideas are better declined immediately than parked. If an idea is clearly out of bounds (e.g. asks for something that is illegal, unethical, or wildly out of scope), say no with reason. Parking it is a polite lie.

**Takeaway: a parking lot is the third option between "yes" and "no". It captures ideas, validates the stakeholder, defers the decision, and forces regular review. Without one, every casual idea becomes a binary decision in the moment, which produces worse outcomes for both sides.**

**Qantas story**

Imagine running a Qantas AI project to build a knowledge assistant for the airport operations team: a tool that helps duty managers find policy answers quickly during operational events. The sponsor is a senior operations executive, the daily users are duty managers across multiple airports, and the steering committee has six members from different parts of operations.

In the first month of the project, ideas flow constantly. One duty manager suggests the assistant should also alert when policy changes are published. Another wants it to integrate with the rostering system. The sponsor wonders if it should provide briefing summaries before each shift. A ground operations specialist asks whether it could help with disruption checklists. Without a parking lot, the PM would have to either commit to or refuse each idea on the spot, which would create either scope creep or friction.

The parking lot is introduced in the first steering committee. The PM frames it as a shared inventory: "we want to make sure no idea gets lost, so we will keep a list everyone can see. Items on the list are not being committed to or refused. We will review it every two weeks and move things forward or off the list."

The list grows steadily. By week six it has 22 items. The themes range from new capabilities to integrations to content additions to user interface ideas. In each bi-weekly review, the PM walks through the list. Of the 22 items at the week six review, the dispositions are: 14 stay on the parking lot (still interesting, not yet enough to act on), 3 are promoted to change request memos (these will be evaluated for v1 inclusion or v2), 4 are moved to the v2 backlog directly (interesting and deferred), 1 is declined and removed (a request that was specific to a duty manager who has since moved roles, no longer applicable).

The most useful moment comes from a duty manager who joined the project mid-way. In her second steering committee, she suggests an idea, and the sponsor immediately says: "that is a good one, let me check whether it is on the parking lot already". It turns out a similar idea had been raised by another duty manager two months earlier. The new duty manager is shown the existing item, agrees it captures her idea, and asks for it to be promoted to change request. The PM does so. Without the parking lot, the new duty manager's idea would have been treated as fresh, possibly absorbed without comparison, and the project would have been managing two near-identical scope additions in parallel without realising.

The parking lot also serves a softer function. The sponsor mentions, in a private conversation, that the existence of the list has changed how duty managers engage with the project. Previously, they would either avoid raising ideas (assuming nothing would happen) or push hard for immediate inclusion. With the list, they raise ideas freely and trust that they will be considered fairly. The list has become a small piece of cultural infrastructure, not just an admin tool.

At project end, the list has 31 entries across its history. 18 of these were promoted to change requests at some point, of which 6 became in-scope changes and 12 became v2 backlog items. 8 were declined and removed for various reasons (no longer relevant, decided against, out of bounds). 5 are still active and carried into v2 planning. The audit trail is clean: every idea raised by any stakeholder appears in the record, with its disposition.

The lesson for AI delivery is that the parking lot is one of the lowest cost, highest value scope management tools available. It absorbs the social need to be heard, defers premature decisions, and creates an audit trail that protects both sides. The discipline is to maintain it: review it regularly, force movement, and never let it become a graveyard. Done well, it changes how the project feels to stakeholders, from "every idea is a fight" to "every idea is captured and will be considered". That cultural shift is worth more than the artifact itself.

**定义**

Parking lot 是**一份共享的、可见的清单**, 记录项目中被提出但还没承诺的想法、建议、可能的 feature。它跟 v1 不做清单不同 (那个是签过字、明确的), 也跟 change request log 不同 (**那个含决策**)。Parking lot 是保留区: 想法被听到之后、被评判之前住在这里。纪律是每一个被提的想法都进清单, 没有想法被默默忽略, 清单定期 review 以免任何东西就此消失。

**为何在交付中重要**

Stakeholder 提想法有两个原因: 他们有一个真正的建议要贡献, 或者他们想感觉被听到。**两个都合理**。PM 的失败模式是把想法当成 "我们会做" (这吸收 scope) 或 "我们不会做" (这让 stakeholder 感觉被打发)。Parking lot 创造第三个选项: "我们听到了, 它在清单上, 我们会回头看"。这经常是 stakeholder 一开始真正想要的回应。

AI 项目里, 想法很容易生成 (每次会议产出三个新的 "如果我们也...?") **而难以实现** (每次添加都是真实的工程), parking lot 尤其有价值。它吸收 "被听到" 的社交需求, 同时不强加 "被做" 的工程成本。

**运作原理**

Parking lot 有以下结构。**故意是轻量的**, 因为**它应该容易添加、容易阅读**。

| 字段 | 写什么 |
|---|---|
| 想法 | 一行描述, 用提出者的原话 |
| 提出者 | Stakeholder 名字、日期、场合 |
| 主题 | 一个松散的类别, 排序有用 (能力、集成、内容类型, 等等) |
| 状态 | Active、提升 (移到 change request)、拒绝、推到 v2 |
| 最近一次 review | 上次 review 的日期, 对追踪关注度有用 |

注意清单里没有什么: 没有影响评估、没有建议、没有商业含义。Parking lot 里的想法还不是决策。**它们是库存**。把想法转换成决策的工作发生在 change request memo (13.3) 或 v2 规划里。Parking lot 在那个工作的上游。

**怎么向客户引入 parking lot**

**引入要轻**。在第一或第二次 steering committee 上, PM 说: "项目过程中会出现一些我们不会立刻行动的想法。为了确保我们都捕捉到、能回头看, 我们会维护一份共享 parking lot。所有人都能看。我们每两周 review 一次。清单上的东西不是被承诺也不是被拒绝, 只是被持有。"

这个措辞做到两件事。**它传递开放性** (想法欢迎, 没有什么被打发)。**它传递纪律** (清单被 review, 项目移动, 没东西腐烂)。大多数客户回应正面, 因为措辞匹配他们的底层偏好: 被听到, 但不被过早承诺。

**对话里怎么用 parking lot**

当一个想法在会议上出现、不在范围内时, 自然的回应是: "好主意, 让我加到 parking lot, 下次 review 时我们回头看"。这种回应有三个属性:

1. **它确认想法** (**它进清单**)。
2. **它推迟决策** (**我们会回头看**)。
3. **它不承诺行动或不行动**。

它避免的是立即的 "是, 我们会做" 或 "不, 我们不会", 两个都是对话终结句, 都可能产生事后遗憾。Parking lot 以结构化的方式让对话保持开放。

**双周 review**

Parking lot 只有在定期 review 时才有用。节奏通常是每两周一次, 常作为 steering committee 的一部分或单独的 scope review。Review 里, 每一项得到四种处置之一:

| 处置 | 何时适用 |
|---|---|
| 留在 parking lot | 想法仍然有意思, 但没有新信息可以行动 |
| 提升到 change request | 想法现在有足够的具体度和重要性, 可以正式评估 |
| 推到 v2 | 想法有意思, 但 v1 不会行动 |
| 拒绝并移除 | 想法不再相关, 或被明确决定不做 |

Review 很快: 20 项的清单 10 到 15 分钟。PM 走过每一项, 客户确认或修订处置, 清单更新。关键实践是项目要移动。一份只增不减的 parking lot 变成墓地, 失去可信度。项目必须要么往前移动 (到 change request 或 v2), **要么被移除**。

**跟相关产物的边界**

Parking lot 有时被跟另外三个产物搞混。清楚区分它们很重要。

| 产物 | 目的 | 状态 |
|---|---|---|
| Parking lot | 持有还没被评估的想法 | 没决策 |
| Change request | 文档化被评估过的决策 | 每项都有处置 |
| v1 不做清单 | 定义明确的 v1 边界 | 签过字, 最终 |
| v2 backlog | 计划在下一阶段处理的项目 | 承诺会回头看 |

**想法的流动是**: 被提出 → parking lot → 要么被拒绝并移除, 要么被提升到 change request, change request 再变成范围内变更或 v2 backlog 项目。没有这个流动, 想法就累积没方向。

**PM 决策点**

- 我**有没有一份单一共享的 parking lot**, 还是**想法散落在邮件、Slack 线程、会议笔记里**?
- 我**有没有跑双周 review**, 还是 **parking lot 变成了没人看的清单**?
- 清单上的每一项, **我能不能说出下一步** (**提升、推迟、拒绝、保持**)? **如果不能, 这一项在腐烂**。

**注意事项**

- **Parking lot 不应该被用来打发想法**。**措辞是 "我们正在持有这个", 不是 "我们不做这个"**。**Stakeholder 能感觉出区别**。
- **避免让 parking lot 无边界增长**。**超过 30 项就不再有用**。**强制移动**。
- **有些想法立即拒绝比 park 更好**。**如果一个想法明显出界** (**例如, 要求违法、不道德、或大幅超出范围**), **带理由说不**。**Park 它是一个礼貌的谎言**。

**要点总结: parking lot 是 "是" 和 "不是" 之间的第三个选项。它捕捉想法、确认 stakeholder、推迟决策、强制定期 review。没有它, 每一个随口想法都变成当场的二元决策, 对双方都产出更差的结果。**

**Qantas 案例**

设想你在跑一个 Qantas AI 项目, 给机场运营团队建一个知识助手: 一个工具帮值班经理在运营事件中快速找政策答案。Sponsor 是一位资深运营高管, 日常用户是多个机场的值班经理, steering committee 有六位来自运营不同部分的成员。

项目第一个月, 想法不断。一位值班经理建议助手在政策变更发布时也应该 alert。另一位想让它跟排班系统集成。Sponsor 想知道它是否应该在每个班次前提供简报摘要。一位地面运营专员问它能不能帮中断 checklist。没有 parking lot, PM 不得不当场承诺或拒绝每个想法, 这会制造 scope creep 或摩擦。

Parking lot 在第一次 steering committee 上被引入。PM 把它框架成共享库存: "我们想确保没有想法丢掉, 所以我们会保持一份所有人能看的清单。清单上的项目没被承诺也没被拒绝。我们每两周 review, 把东西移动到下一步或移出清单。"

**清单稳定增长**。到第六周有 22 项。主题从新能力到集成、内容添加、UI 想法。每次双周 review, PM 走过清单。第六周 review 的 22 项, 处置是: 14 项留在 parking lot (仍然有意思, 还没足够东西可以行动)、3 项被提升到 change request memo (这些会被评估纳入 v1 或 v2)、4 项直接被移到 v2 backlog (有意思且推迟)、1 项被拒绝并移除 (一位已经换岗的值班经理特有的请求, 不再适用)。

最有用的瞬间来自一位中途加入项目的值班经理。在她第二次 steering committee 上, 她建议一个想法, sponsor 立刻说: "好主意, 让我看看 parking lot 上是不是已经有了"。结果两个月前另一位值班经理提过类似想法。新值班经理看了现有项, 同意它捕捉了她的想法, 要求提升到 change request。PM 这么做了。没有 parking lot, 新值班经理的想法会被当成新鲜的, 可能在没有对照的情况下被吸收, 项目会在不知情的情况下平行管理两个几乎相同的范围添加。

Parking lot 还服务一个更软的功能。Sponsor 在一次私人对话中提到, 这份清单的存在改变了值班经理对项目的 engage 方式。之前, 他们要么避免提想法 (假设什么也不会发生) 要么硬推立即纳入。有了清单, 他们自由地提想法, 信任它们会被公平考虑。清单变成了一小块文化基础设施, 不只是一个管理工具。

项目结束时, 清单的历史有 31 项。其中 18 项在某个时刻被提升到 change request, 其中 6 项变成范围内变更、12 项变成 v2 backlog 项目。8 项被拒绝并移除, 各种原因 (不再相关、被决定不做、出界)。5 项仍然 active, 带入 v2 规划。审计轨迹是干净的: 任何 stakeholder 提的任何想法都出现在记录里, 带它的处置。

AI 交付的启示: parking lot 是市面上最低成本、最高价值的范围管理工具之一。它吸收 "被听到" 的社交需求、推迟过早的决策、创造保护双方的审计轨迹。**纪律是维护它**: 定期 review、强制移动、永不让它变成墓地。做得好, 它改变了项目对 stakeholder 的感觉, 从 "每个想法都是一场战斗" 到 "每个想法都被捕捉、会被考虑"。那种文化转变, 比产物本身更值钱。
