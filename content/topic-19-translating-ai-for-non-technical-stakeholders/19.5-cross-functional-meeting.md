# Speaking to a cross-functional room: CFO, legal, business, IT all at once

The previous four pieces (T19.1-T19.4) were each about a single audience: CFO, legal, sponsor, business — each with their own language system. But the meeting you'll most often face isn't a 1:1. It's **a room of people — CFO, legal, business, IT, sponsor all present — asking you for a project update**.

This kind of meeting is hard mode. Not because the content is complex, but because the people in the room have **completely different mental models** of the AI project:

- CFO is listening for cost / ROI / unit economics
- Legal is listening for risk / liability / governance
- Business is listening for "when can we use it" and "will my team be affected"
- IT is listening for architecture / deployment / operational burden
- Sponsor is listening for timeline and KPI

You have 30 minutes, and every sentence is being scanned through 5 lenses simultaneously.

The two most common failure modes for PMs in this kind of meeting:

**Mode 1 — trying to please everyone**: every sentence tries to cover everyone's concern, and 30 minutes in, every person feels "I didn't hear what I came to hear".

**Mode 2 — following the loudest voice**: whoever is most active gets your attention. The whole meeting becomes a 1:1 between you and the CFO while the other four sit watching, feeling their time is being wasted.

How to actually run this kind of meeting so every person hears what they came to hear, below.

---

## Decision 1 — Frame the agenda up front so each person knows "when my topic is up"

The single most important move in a cross-functional meeting is **structure**. If you don't impose structure proactively, the meeting collapses into "loudest voice takes all the airtime".

**The most effective opener: map the agenda to each stakeholder's concern**

> "30 minutes today, I'll walk through 4 blocks. Let me flag up front who each block matters most to, so you know when to listen actively:

> - First 5 minutes — project status overview, all of you
> - Next 10 minutes — unit economics and ROI, [CFO name] this block is for you
> - Next 10 minutes — risk / governance update, [legal name] most relevant to you
> - Final 5 minutes — deployment and operational impact, [IT name] and [business name] most relevant to you

> I'll pause for Q at the end of each block. If you've got a question on a different block, hold it — we'll get there."

What this does:

- You've given a timed agenda (no one is surprised)
- You've explicitly named the "primary audience" of each block (everyone feels remembered)
- You've set a discussion protocol (raise it in the right block)
- You've preempted "loudest voice" — the CFO might be itching to ask about cost, but they know cost is in block 2 and will wait

**What happens if you skip this step**

- The CFO interrupts with a cost question at minute 3, you get pulled into a cost discussion, the other blocks get squeezed
- Legal realises by the half hour their topic hasn't come up yet, starts disengaging
- Business sits there watching CFO and you ping-pong, and walks out remembering "cost ran over" — but didn't actually hear the deployment timeline they came for

Proactively framing the agenda is 70% of whether this meeting works — far more impactful than the actual content.

---

## Decision 2 — At each block opener, use the "this matters to you because" structure

When you enter each block, **re-anchor what this block means for each person**. Don't assume they'll connect the dots themselves.

**How it sounds (using the unit economics block as an example)**

> "OK, into unit economics. What this block means for each of you is different — let me anchor first:

> - [CFO]: you're looking at whether the cost structure is sustainable, and how unit cost evolves at scale
> - [Legal]: not directly your block, but I'll cross-reference vendor cost when we get to risk, so worth a half-ear
> - [Business]: for you it's about what share of the ROI model your use case represents
> - [IT]: the infrastructure cost line is yours — it sits in your operational scope

> Now, into the numbers."

Why this anchoring works:

- Everyone re-engages (they heard their name)
- You've explicitly told each person what this block means for them — they don't have to translate
- You've demonstrated you thought about each stakeholder — which is itself a trust signal

If a block genuinely has nothing for someone in the room, say so directly:

> "[Legal name], this block is low direct relevance for you, feel free to relax through it — there's a 5-minute block on governance later, that's your active section."

Acknowledging legal explicitly — even when it's not their block — beats letting them feel ignored.

---

## Decision 3 — When one sentence has to land for multiple stakeholders

Sometimes you need a single sentence to speak to multiple people. Hardest mode, but there's a way.

**Method: pack the sentence with multiple lenses, and explicitly tag each lens to its owner**

Wrong version (trying to please everyone):

> "Project is on track for Q3 launch, unit cost will be around 1/5 of current, full governance framework in place, business onboarding takes about 4 weeks."

Each person catches one phrase, but no one hears the full answer. The CFO wants "how did you get to 1/5". Legal wants "what's in the framework". Business wants "is 4 weeks minimum or actual". No one is satisfied.

Right version (layered, with explicit tags):

> "Let me hit a few things in one breath, then expand 5-10 seconds on each — take what's yours:

> One, timeline — Q3 launch (sponsor's question).
> Two, unit economics — 1/5 of current, I'll send the CFO the breakdown after this.
> Three, governance — framework in draft, legal you've seen v0.1, we'll walk through separately.
> Four, business onboarding — 4 weeks, but the first 2 can start in parallel, I'll give business a prep checklist.
> Five, technical — deploys on our existing stack, no new infra, IT overhead is 5-8% of current ops capacity.

> There are follow-ups in-block, let me keep going for now."

Why this works:

- Every stakeholder heard their key number
- You've explicitly said "after this" or "in-block" for follow-up — they know their detail has a slot
- You've kept momentum (no one stakeholder pulled the meeting into a deep dive)

---

## Decision 4 — Handling the "loudest voice"

The CFO is often the loudest voice (CFOs feel entitled to interrupt by default). The sponsor too sometimes. How do you handle them without letting the meeting get hijacked?

**Loudest voice interrupts in the wrong block**

> CFO interrupts at minute 3: "Hold on — what's the total budget for this project..."

Wrong response (going with it):

> "Sure, let me walk you through that now..."

(And the next 20 minutes become a cost discussion, everyone else disengages.)

Right response (firm but polite):

> "[CFO name], the cost block I'll walk through specifically, about 4 minutes from now. Can I finish the project status — 2 more minutes — and then we'll hit cost? I've got detail prepped for the cost block, it'll have more substance than what I could improvise right now."

Key phrases:

- "Can I finish" — politely assert agenda control
- "More substance than improvising" — give the CFO a reward (the detail was prepped for them)
- A specific time ("4 minutes from now") — CFO knows it's not indefinite

90% of CFOs say "OK, keep going". The remaining 10% insist; you've done what you can, you yield, but the rest of the room remembers you tried to maintain structure.

**Loudest voice keeps dominating**

If a stakeholder (not necessarily the CFO — sometimes a senior IT lead) keeps pulling conversation into their territory, you need to redirect proactively:

> "[Name], I think we've covered your block pretty thoroughly in the last 5 minutes. [Business name] I saw you wanted to raise something on deployment — want to go first, and [Name] if you have follow-up we come back to you?"

Passing the mic to a quieter stakeholder is the core move for keeping balance. The loudest voice usually doesn't fight you on this, because they themselves realise they were dominating.

---

## Decision 5 — When someone in the room hasn't said anything

A failure mode in cross-functional meetings: one stakeholder stays silent the whole time, and only after the meeting does it emerge that they disagree — they just didn't say so in the room.

**Proactively invite the silent ones**

At transitions (between blocks), invite them explicitly:

> "[Name], I haven't seen you raise a concern in this block. Let me ask directly — anything I've said that doesn't match your understanding? Even small, it's easier to change now than later."

This matters. Silent stakeholders fall into two types:

- Actually fine (they'll say "no concern, looks good")
- Holding back but not wanting to confront (now you've invited them, they raise something mild)

Either way you win — type one confirms alignment, type two surfaces disagreement early.

**The stakeholder most likely to stay silent**

Usually IT or ops. Culturally they don't like jumping into cross-functional meetings (too much business and finance language in the room), but their operational concerns are often what makes or breaks the project.

Carve out time in every meeting to ask IT/ops directly:

> "[IT name], on the timeline I'm planning to ship — can you absorb the capacity? Any dependency I should raise with the sponsor right now?"

That question makes IT feel taken seriously, and they'll start telling you the operational reality unprompted.

---

## Decision 6 — Before closing, give every stakeholder a takeaway

The last 3 minutes are the most critical. **Every stakeholder needs to walk out of the room with a sentence.**

**How it sounds**

> "Before we wrap, let me make explicit what each of you should expect next:

> - [Sponsor]: timeline unchanged, Q3. Next update [specific date].
> - [CFO]: I'll send cost detail to your finance team, you reconcile the model together, end of this week.
> - [Legal]: governance v0.2 next week, let's book 30 minutes to walk through.
> - [Business]: onboarding prep checklist this week, please align your team internally.
> - [IT]: capacity needs internal confirmation with your ops lead, yes/no to me by Wednesday.

> Anything inaccurate? Anything to add to the follow-up list?"

What this section does:

- Each stakeholder hears one sentence of commitment (specific deliverable + timeline)
- You explicitly invite them to confirm — if any misalignment, surface now, not later
- The meeting shifts from "info dump" to "set of mutual commitments"

Everyone walks out remembering not "lots of information just passed through me", but "I'm doing X, the PM is doing Y" — which is the mark of a meeting worth having.

---

## A short do / don't

**Do**

- Open with a timed agenda mapped to stakeholders
- Anchor "this matters to you because" at each block opener
- When a sentence covers multiple stakeholders, explicitly tag each line to its owner
- Politely defer wrong-block interruptions, give a specific in-block slot
- Proactively invite silent stakeholders
- Before closing, list a takeaway per stakeholder

**Don't**

- Don't let the loudest voice take disproportionate airtime
- Don't try to please everyone in a single sentence
- Don't assume a silent stakeholder agrees
- Don't let IT / ops stay silent throughout
- Don't close without a takeaway for everyone
- Don't go more than 2 consecutive minutes on technical detail in a cross-functional room

---

## Phrasebook: 14 lines to use directly in a cross-functional meeting

### Opening framing

**1. Timed agenda mapped to stakeholders**
> "30 minutes today, I'll walk through 4 blocks. Up front who each block matters most to: status (everyone) → unit economics (CFO) → risk/governance (legal) → deployment (IT + business). I'll pause for Q at the end of each block. Hold cross-block questions until we get there."

### Block anchoring

**2. Anchor at block opener**
> "OK, into [block name]. This block means different things for each of you: [CFO] looks at [angle], [legal] looks at [angle], [business] looks at [angle], [IT] looks at [angle]."

**3. Acknowledge people whose block isn't this one**
> "[Name], this block is low direct relevance for you, feel free to relax through it — there's a 5-minute block on [your block] later, that's your active section."

### Multi-stakeholder sentences

**4. Layered hits**
> "Let me hit a few things in one breath, then expand 5-10 seconds on each — take what's yours: [1. sponsor's piece] [2. CFO's piece, detail after] [3. legal's piece, separate] [4. business's piece, with checklist] [5. IT's piece, 5-8% capacity overhead]."

### Handling loudest voice

**5. Politely defer wrong-block interruption**
> "[Name], that question I'll walk through in the [block] section, about X minutes from now. Can I finish these 2 minutes, then we hit yours? I've prepped detail for that block, it'll have more substance than improvising now."

**6. Redirect when someone keeps dominating**
> "[Name], I think we've covered your block thoroughly. [Another quieter name] I saw you wanted to raise [something] — want to go first, and [original dominator] if you have follow-up we come back?"

### Inviting the silent

**7. Proactive invite**
> "[Name], I haven't seen you raise a concern. Let me ask directly — anything I've said that doesn't match your understanding? Even small, easier to change now than later."

**8. Targeted at IT / ops**
> "[IT name], on the timeline I'm planning to ship — can you absorb the capacity? Any dependency I should raise with the sponsor now?"

### Closing

**9. Takeaway per stakeholder**
> "Before we wrap, what each of you should expect next: [sponsor one line] [CFO one line] [legal one line] [business one line] [IT one line]. Anything inaccurate?"

### Common difficulties

**10. Two stakeholders start debating in front of you**
> "[A] and [B], I see you've got different views. I'd suggest we don't resolve this here — I don't have enough information to weigh in. Let's schedule 30 minutes for the three of us to align, then broadcast back to the group."

**11. Asked an out-of-scope question**
> "Fair question, but out of scope for today. We're focused on [project X]; what you raised belongs in [other context]. Can I take that offline with you separately?"

**12. One stakeholder tries to commit another**
> "[Speaker], that commitment touches [the person being committed]'s capacity. I'd suggest we don't commit in this meeting — give [them] 24 hours to confirm internally, then we finalise."

**13. Meeting drifts, you need to reset**
> "Let me pause 30 seconds. The conversation is getting a bit fragmented — let me reframe. The outcome we want from today is [specific outcome]. Working backwards, the most important topic for the next 20 minutes is [X]. Aligned to continue?"

**14. Time's up but substantive items remain**
> "Time's up — there are 2 substantive items we haven't covered. Suggestion: one, I send a written brief covering both after this; two, if anyone has strong concerns wanting a live discussion, I can schedule a 15-minute follow-up — but attendees opt in. Preference?"

---

Stepping back: the hardest part of a cross-functional meeting isn't the content, it's **balancing different people's attention and commitments**. Everything you say can be technically correct, and if 5 stakeholders walk out with 5 different versions of the meeting, the meeting was net negative.

A PM's real job in this kind of meeting isn't "presenting" — it's **conducting**. You're like an orchestra conductor: each instrument needs to sound at the right moment, the whole has to sound like a coherent piece, not 5 people playing their own parts.

That capability is called **room management**. It's the biggest gap between a junior and a senior PM — a junior PM can explain content clearly, a senior PM can manage the attention of a room.

There's one way to practice it: after every cross-functional meeting, ask yourself what each of the 5 stakeholders walked out with. If anyone walked out with "I think something was said but I'm not sure", that meeting needs debugging.

# 在跨职能会议上对所有人同时讲

前面四篇 (T19.1-T19.4) 都是单一对象的对话: CFO、法务、sponsor、业务方,每一个都有专属的语言系统。但实际项目里你最常面对的不是这种 1-on-1,是**一屋子人,CFO、法务、business、IT、sponsor 全在场,让你 update 项目状态**。

这种会议是地狱难度。原因不是会议本身复杂,是这屋子里的人对 AI 项目的 mental model **完全不一样**:

- CFO 在听 cost / ROI / unit economics
- 法务在听 risk / liability / governance
- Business 在听"我们什么时候能用"和"我团队会不会被影响"
- IT 在听 architecture、deployment、operational burden
- Sponsor 在听 timeline 和 KPI

你只有 30 分钟,你说的每一句话都会被 5 个 lens 同时审视。

PM 在这种会议上最常见的失败模式有两种:

**模式 1 — 讨好所有人**: 每一段话都试图覆盖所有人的关心点,结果 30 分钟讲下来,每个人都觉得"没听到我关心的"。

**模式 2 — 跟着 loudest voice 走**: 谁最 active 你就回应谁。结果整个 meeting 变成你跟 CFO 单独对话,其他 4 个人坐在那里看着,觉得自己时间被浪费。

下面拆怎么真正在这种会议上让每个人都"听到自己想听的"。

---

## 决策点 1 — 议程先 frame 给所有人,让他们知道"什么时候轮到我的话题"

跨职能会议最重要的第一动作是 **structure**。如果你不主动 structure,会议会自然 collapse 成"loudest voice 拿到所有 airtime"。

**最有效的开场:把议程映射到每个 stakeholder 的关心点**

> "今天 30 分钟我想 walk through 4 个 block,我提前讲一下每一块谁特别关心,这样你们可以知道什么时候听最 active:

> - 前 5 分钟,project status 总览 — 所有人
> - 接下来 10 分钟,unit economics 和 ROI — [CFO name] 这一块特别想听
> - 接下来 10 分钟,risk / governance 更新 — [法务 name] 这一块对你最相关
> - 最后 5 分钟,deployment 和 operational impact — [IT name] 和 [业务方 name] 这一块对你们最相关

> 每个 block 结束我会停一下问 Q,但如果你心里在想另一个 block 的事,可以先 hold,我们到那一块再深入。"

这段话做了几件事:

- 你给了一个 timed agenda (没人 surprise)
- 你 explicitly 点名了每个 block 的"主要听众" (每个人知道自己被记得了)
- 你 set 了讨论 protocol (在对的 block 提问)
- 你 preempt 了"loudest voice"问题 (CFO 早就想问 cost,但他知道 cost block 在第二段,他会等)

**没做这一步会发生什么**

- CFO 在 5 分钟时就插 cost 问题,你被迫 walk into cost discussion,其他 block 时间被压缩
- 法务发现自己关心的 risk 没被讨论到一半就已经过半小时,start to disengage
- 业务方坐在那里看 CFO 和你 ping-pong,30 分钟之后只记得"成本超预算" — 但他真正关心的 deployment timeline 没听到

主动 frame agenda 是这种会议 70% 的成败 — 比你后面讲的具体内容重要得多。

---

## 决策点 2 — 每个 block 的开头,用 "this matters to you because" 句式

进入每个 block 时,**重新 anchor 这个 block 对每个人意味着什么**。不要假设大家自动 connect。

**讲法 (用 unit economics block 举例)**

> "好,我们进 unit economics block。这一块对你们每个人意味的事不一样,我先 anchor 一下:

> - [CFO]: 你看到的是成本结构是不是可持续、scale 之后 unit cost 怎么变
> - [法务]: 你不直接相关,但 risk 那一块我会 cross-reference 到 vendor 成本,所以你可以稍微留意一下
> - [Business]: 你听这一段主要是看 ROI 模型里你的 use case 占多少 share
> - [IT]: 你看 infrastructure cost 那一栏 — 这是你 operational scope 里的一部分

> OK,现在开始讲数字。"

这种 anchor 的好处:

- 每个人重新 engage (听到自己的名字)
- 你 explicitly 告诉每个人"这一段对你的意义",他们不需要自己翻译
- 你 demonstrate 你 thought about 每个 stakeholder — 这本身就是 trust signal

如果某个 block 跟某个人确实不相关,也直接说:

> "[法务 name],这一块跟你直接相关性低,可以放松一下听 — 但有一个 5 分钟我会专门 walk through governance,那时候你 active。"

让法务感觉被 acknowledge 而不是被忽略 — 哪怕这一块不是他的。

---

## 决策点 3 — 一句话同时对多个 stakeholder 讲,怎么做

有时候你必须用一句话同时讲给多个 stakeholder。这是地狱难度,但有办法。

**方法: 一句话里包含多个 lens,明确点出每个 lens 是给谁的**

错的版本 (试图讨好所有人):

> "这个项目预计在三季度上线,unit cost 大概是当前的 1/5,有完整的 governance framework,业务方的 onboarding 时间预计 4 周。"

每个人听到一句,但没人听到完整的回答。CFO 想知道 "1/5 是怎么算的",法务想知道 "governance framework 包含什么",业务方想知道 "4 周是 minimum 还是 actual"。没有一个人 satisfied。

对的版本 (分层讲,但 explicitly 标记):

> "我用一句话 hit 几件事,然后每一件我会 expand 5-10 秒,你们各取所需:

> 一,时间线 — 三季度上线 (sponsor 关心)。
> 二,unit economics — 当前的 1/5,会后我给 CFO 算法 detail。
> 三,governance — framework 在 draft,法务这边我已经发过 v0.1,后面单独走。
> 四,Business onboarding — 4 周,但前 2 周可以并行准备,我会给 business 一个 prep checklist。
> 五,Technical — deployment 在 our 现有 stack,不需要新 infra,IT 这边的 overhead 是现有 ops capacity 的 5-8%。

> 这里有 follow-up 我们一会 in-block 讨论,现在我先继续。"

这种讲法的好处:

- 每个 stakeholder 都听到了自己的 key 数字
- 你 explicitly 说了 "会后" 或 "in-block" follow up,大家知道自己关心的细节有时间讨论
- 你保留了 momentum (没让任何一个 stakeholder 立刻 deep dive 卡住会议)

---

## 决策点 4 — 怎么 handle "loudest voice"

CFO 经常是这种会议的 loudest voice (因为 CFO 默认 entitled to interrupt)。Sponsor 也可能是。怎么 handle 不让会议被 hijack?

**Loudest voice 在错误时间打断**

> CFO 在 minute 3 打断:"等等,我先想问一下这个项目的总预算……"

错的回应 (顺着):

> "好的,这个我现在跟你 walk through……"

(接下来 20 分钟全部跟 CFO 讨论 cost,其他人 disengage。)

对的回应 (firm 但 polite):

> "[CFO name],你这个问题 cost block 我会专门 walk through,大概再 4 分钟。我能不能先 finish project status 这 2 分钟,然后我们 hit cost?cost 那一块我准备了 detail,会比现在临时讲更有 substance。"

关键 phrase:

- "我能不能先 finish" — politely assert agenda control
- "比现在临时讲更有 substance" — 给 CFO 一个 reward (你的 detail 是为他 prep 的)
- 给具体时间 ("再 4 分钟") — CFO 知道不是无限期等

90% 的 CFO 会说 "OK, 继续"。剩下 10% 的硬要插,你已经做了你能做的,只能 give in,但其他 stakeholder 会记得你 try 过 maintain structure。

**Loudest voice 持续 dominate**

如果某个 stakeholder (不一定是 CFO,可能是 senior IT lead) 持续把对话拉到他的领域,你需要主动 redirect:

> "[Name] 我想我们这 5 分钟 cover 你这一块够 thorough 了。但 [Business name] 我看你想 raise 一些 deployment 的事,要不你先来,然后 [Name] 你如果有 follow-up 我们回来?"

把麦克风主动交给一个 quieter 的 stakeholder — 这是 maintain balance 的核心动作。Loudest voice 通常不会跟你 fight,因为他们也意识到自己 dominate 了。

---

## 决策点 5 — 房间里有人没参与,怎么办

跨职能会议的一个 failure mode 是: 某个 stakeholder 全程沉默,会议结束之后才发现他根本 disagree,但当时没说出来。

**主动 invite 沉默的人**

在 transition 的时候 (block 之间) explicit invite:

> "[Name] 你这一块我没看到你 raise concern,我想 explicit 问一下 — 我刚讲的有没有跟你之前的理解 misalign 的?哪怕是 minor,raise 出来现在改比后面发现简单。"

这一招很重要。沉默的 stakeholder 通常有两种:

- 真的 OK (那他会说 "no concern, looks good")
- 心里有保留但不想 confront (那他被你 invite 了,会 raise 一个 mild concern)

不管哪一种,你都赢了 — 第一种你 confirm alignment,第二种你提前 surface 了 disagreement。

**最容易沉默的 stakeholder**

通常是 IT 或者 ops。他们 culturally 不喜欢在 cross-functional 会议上插话 (太多 business 和 finance 的人在场),但他们手上的 operational concern 经常是项目成败的关键。

每次会议主动 carve out 时间问 IT/ops:

> "[IT name] 我下面要 ship 这个 timeline,你 capacity 上能 absorb 吗?有没有什么 dependency 上的事我需要现在 raise 给 sponsor?"

这个问题让 IT 感觉被 take seriously,他们后面会主动告诉你 operational reality。

---

## 决策点 6 — 会议结束之前的 closing,要给每个 stakeholder 一个 take-away

最后 3 分钟最关键。**每个 stakeholder 走出会议室时,要带走一句话**。

**讲法**

> "在我们 wrap 之前,我想 explicit 一下每个人接下来要 expect 什么:

> - [Sponsor]: timeline 没变,三季度。下次 update 是 [具体日期]。
> - [CFO]: cost detail 我会发给你的 finance team,你们一起 reconcile 模型,本周末之前。
> - [法务]: governance v0.2 我下周给你,我们约 30 分钟过一遍。
> - [Business]: onboarding prep checklist 这周内给你,你 internal 也 align 一下 team。
> - [IT]: capacity 那一块你需要 internal 跟 ops lead 确认,周三之前给我一个 yes/no。

> 有没有任何一条不准确?或者你们想加任何 follow-up 的?"

这一段做了几件事:

- 每个 stakeholder 听到一句对他的 commitment (具体 deliverable + timeline)
- 你 explicit 让他们 confirm — 如果有任何 misalign,现在 surface 不要等
- 整个会议从 "info dump" 变成 "set of mutual commitments"

每个人走出会议室带的不是"刚才信息很多",是 "我接下来要做 X,PM 接下来要做 Y" — 这是 meaningful meeting 的标志。

## 一些 do / don't 速记

**Do**

- 开场先 frame timed agenda,把每个 block 映射到 stakeholder
- 每个 block 开头 anchor "this matters to you because"
- 一句话同时讲多个 stakeholder 时,explicit 标记每条对应谁
- Politely defer 错时间的打断,给具体的 in-block 时间
- 主动 invite 沉默的 stakeholder
- 会议结束前 explicit 列出每个 stakeholder 的 take-away

**Don't**

- 不要让 loudest voice 拿到 disproportionate airtime
- 不要试图用一句话同时讨好所有人
- 不要假设沉默的 stakeholder 是 agree
- 不要让 IT / ops 一直沉默
- 不要 close 会议不给每个人 takeaway
- 不要在跨职能会议讲技术细节超过 2 分钟连续

## 话术速查:14 条跨职能会议直接抄走用的句子

### 开场 framing

**1. Set timed agenda mapped to stakeholders**
> "今天 30 分钟我 walk through 4 个 block,我提前讲一下每一块谁特别关心: status (所有人) → unit economics (CFO) → risk/governance (法务) → deployment (IT + business)。每个 block 结束我停一下问 Q,但如果你想问另一个 block 的事,我们到那一块再深入。"

### 每个 block 的 anchor

**2. Block 开头 anchor**
> "好,我们进 [block 名]。这一块对你们每个人意味的事不一样: [CFO] 看 [角度], [法务] 看 [角度], [Business] 看 [角度], [IT] 看 [角度]。"

**3. 对没直接相关的人 acknowledge**
> "[Name],这一块跟你直接相关性低,可以放松一下听 — 但有一个 5 分钟我会专门讲 [你的 block],那时候你 active。"

### 一句话讲多个 stakeholder

**4. 分层讲多条**
> "我用一句话 hit 几件事,然后每件 expand 5-10 秒,你们各取所需: [1. sponsor 关心的] [2. CFO 关心的,会后 detail] [3. 法务关心的,单独走] [4. business 关心的,有 checklist] [5. IT 关心的,5-8% capacity overhead]。"

### Handle loudest voice

**5. Politely defer 错时间的打断**
> "[Name],你这个问题 [对应 block] 我会专门 walk through,大概再 X 分钟。我能不能先 finish 这 2 分钟,然后我们 hit 你这个?那一块我准备了 detail,比现在临时讲更有 substance。"

**6. 持续 dominate 时 redirect**
> "[Name] 我想我们这 5 分钟 cover 你这一块够 thorough 了。[另一个 quieter name] 我看你想 raise [某事],你先来,然后 [原 dominator] 如果有 follow-up 我们回来?"

### Invite 沉默的人

**7. 主动 invite**
> "[Name] 你这一块我没看到你 raise concern,我想 explicit 问一下 — 我刚讲的有没有跟你之前的理解 misalign 的?哪怕 minor,现在 raise 比后面发现简单。"

**8. 专门问 IT / ops**
> "[IT name] 我下面要 ship 这个 timeline,你 capacity 上能 absorb 吗?有没有 dependency 上的事我需要 raise 给 sponsor?"

### Closing

**9. 给每个人 takeaway**
> "在我们 wrap 之前,explicit 一下每个人接下来要 expect 什么: [Sponsor 一句] [CFO 一句] [法务 一句] [Business 一句] [IT 一句]。有没有任何一条不准确?"

### 应对常见困难

**10. 两个 stakeholder 在你面前开始 debate**
> "我看 [A] 和 [B] 你们俩有不同 view。我建议我们这里不 resolve,因为我没足够信息 weigh in — 我们会后 schedule 30 分钟你们俩 + 我,把这个 align 清楚再 broadcast 给整个 group。"

**11. 被问到一个完全 out-of-scope 的问题**
> "这个问题 fair 但 out of 这个会议 scope。我们今天 focus 在 [项目 X],你这个 belongs to [另一个 context]。我会后 separately offline 你?"

**12. 一个 stakeholder 试图 commit 别的 stakeholder**
> "[Speaker] 这个 commitment 涉及 [被 commit 的 person] 的 capacity。我建议我们不在这场会议上 commit,先给 [那个人] 24 小时 confirm internal,然后我们再 finalize。"

**13. 会议失控,你需要 reset**
> "我们 pause 一下 30 秒。我感觉对话有点 fragmented,我想重新 frame 一下 — 我们今天 walk out 想要的是 [具体 outcome]。从这个 outcome 倒推,接下来 20 分钟最重要的话题是 [X]。我们 align 这个再继续?"

**14. 时间到了但还有 substantive 没讲完**
> "时间到了 — 我们还有 2 个 substantive items 没 cover。我建议: 一,我会后发一个 written brief cover 这 2 个; 二,如果有 strong concern 想 live 讨论,我可以 schedule 一个 follow-up 15 分钟,但要 attendee 自愿。哪个 prefer?"

---

往大了说,跨职能会议最难的不是 content,是 **balance 不同人的 attention 和 commitment**。你讲的所有内容都正确,但如果 5 个 stakeholder 走出会议室带的是 5 个不一样的理解,这场会议就是 net negative 的。

PM 在这种会议上的真正工作不是 "演讲",是 **conducting** — 你像 orchestra conductor,要让每个 instrument 在正确的时间发声,听上去是一个 coherent piece,而不是 5 个人各自演自己的。

做这件事的能力,叫 **room management**。这是 senior PM 跟 junior PM 最大的差距 — junior PM 能讲清楚内容,但 senior PM 能管理一屋子人的注意力。

练这件事的方法只有一个: 每一次跨职能会议结束之后,问自己 5 个 stakeholder 各带了什么走。如果有人带的是"刚才好像讲了什么但我不确定",这场会议你需要 debug。
